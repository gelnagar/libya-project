[Text] Tripoli--'Abd al-Halim Khaddam, deputy prime minister and foreign minister, 
today met with 'Abd as-Salam Jallud, member cf the General Secretariat of the General 
People's Congress. Issues of common interest were discussed at the meeting. 


'Abd al-Halim Khaddam has left Tripoli for Algeria. In a statement prior to his de- 
parture from Tripoli airport, Mr Khaddam said; It is natural for consultations to be 
held between us and the Libyan brothers on all matters of mutual concern to us all. 
Maj *Abd as-Salam Jallud and I reviewed the current Arab situation and developments in 
the Arab area. We assessed the situation. Our viewpoints were in agreement on all 
issues discussed.

Federation of Chambers of Commerce and Industry:

be permitted (by God) in a certain period could become sacrilegious in another period or 
for another nation. Thus is man's march forward, and his discovery of new paths which 
concern him," 


The revolution!s leader added: "Members of the commercial and contracting sector depend 
on their commercial activities for their living and for supporting their families. They 
are sons of this nation. And no one can deny that many of them were strugglers, they

The revolution's leader affirmed: "Commerce is an exploitative phenomenon, and the 
commercial sector is a consuming, non-producing sector." He added: "I do not mean the 
Libyan traders, but I am referring to commerce wherever it exists in the world. For 
the merchant's basic aim, wherever he is, is to consume without producing." 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi went on to say: "Merchants will come to accept that free commerce 

will come to an end because it is an exploitative phenomenon. During my elaboration on 

the Green Book, to university students, revolutionary committees and political education 
camps, I explained the nature of commerce, and everyone was convinced that commerce was 

an exploitative phenomenon." 


He added: "Any people who allows free enterprise is ignorant. The consumer who succumbs 
to the mercnant!s control over his needs is also ignorant." He said: "The masses have 
awoken to this fact and have started to correct this situation by ridding themselves from 
the yoke of exploitation." 


Colonel al-Gadhdhafi affirmed: "The eradication of exploitation is a historical choice 
for the revolution. The whole world is being swept by a progressive revolution against 
all aspects of exploitation." 


He added: "Despite the shah of Iran's authority and might, his pictures are being 
trampled on in the streets..the people have awoken and will not submit except to their 
own free will, and any attempt made against historical and revolutionary circles, and 
fundamental transformations, are desparate attempts doomed to failure." 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi said: "The great lst September revolution has been developed in 
the Jamahiriyah through stages, so change has occurred peacefully." He added: "Present 
transformations in the Jamahiriyah are taking place in an atmosphere of tranquility, 
peace and contentment," 


Referring to the producers! revolution and the workers marches which accompanied it, 
Colonel al-Qadhdhafi said that he had urged the workers to rise in revolution, by ex- 
plaining to them the exploitative relationship existing between the worker and the em- 
ployer, illustrating it with figures. The workers were convinced after they found that 
they were exchanging their production which takes 10 hours of their day in return for 
wages which were much less than what they should be rightly paid, and which certainly 
gets added to the employers! profits. 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi said that he could convince the merchant and the contractor that 

the enslavement of workers, activity on other peoples! shoulders, and the hiring of hands 
is not permitted (by God). "This persuation could be accomplished easily and without 
compulsion," 


He cited an example of this, from the pre-Islamic Arab society where the burying of 
newborn girls alive, alcoholism and idolatry were permissible, along with other sinful 
acts which Islam had rightfully forbidden through persuation. 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi affirmed; "the process of buying and selling simply means the trans- 
fer of money of the toiler in the factory or the farm, to the merchant or contractor 
sitting on his chair without producing. This process is still going on because it 
embodies the nature of the relationship existing between the worker and the consumer on 
one hand, and the contractor and merchant on the other."

"So long as commerce and contracts are permissible," he further said, "the new direc- 

tions of the revolution are irreversible and the guarantor of their victory and continui- 
ty as the revolutionary forces across the country, as well as those outside the Jamahiriyah 
where these directions are widely met with positive responses from the world's revolu- 
tionary forces which are determined to attain their aspirations through popular revolution." 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi added: "These directions are based on faith in man!ts freedom as a 
general cause, for the great lst of September revolution is a radical, civilizational 
and rational revolution which has high goals to attain. The directions it has taken 
were neither based on a grudge against any specific faction nor on a concern for the 
individual interests of anybody." 


He went on to say: "We are about to embark on an onslaught against all aspects of ex- 
ploitation and their destruction so that we can build the new society which is free 
from exploitation and political and social diseases, and which will be of benefit to all." 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi indicated that "the masses march towards the attainment of this 
dream is based on their desire for a happy, free life, in a society free from explitation 
and slavery." 


He added: "Time might transform she rich into poor, so that what applies to the latter 
now would apply to the former. This means that the issue does basically consist in the 
victory of freedom, that is the liberation of your needs, power and will... for freedom 
is a general cause and anybody who stands against the new directions stands against 
freedom and works for the entrenchment of slavery. This means that if we stand by the 
revolution, it will attain victory and realize prosperity for all, while the reverse 
would occur if we stood against the revolution, for we would, in that case, contribute 
to bringing harm to ourselves and to the future generations." 


"We are struggling now not for the slaves to attain victory and dominate their masters, 
but rather for all to remain masters. We are struggling for bringing the downfall of 
exploitation in all its forms and for negating the deployment of exploitation against 
any one of us." 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi said: "Should any one of us align himself with exploitation, there 
might come a day when that person will become a victim of exploitation. He will there- 
fore lose and become a victim of the very thing he had supported." 


He said: "The high principles posed by the revolution have made the sons of merchants 
and contractors, students, intellectuals and everyone who has understood these problems, 
join the revolution and revolutionary committees... as a result of this, a contradiction 
petween children and their parents has come about, since the parents had lived under the 
shadow of exploitation, and had sanctified their personal interests forgetting the causes 
of right, justice and equality." 


He said: "Free commerce is an exploitative phenomenon which should be eradicated, 

and people working in that field should be transformed into production... in order to 
participate in building the new society and eradicate backwardness which necessitates 

the mobilisation of all capabilities." He added; "The revolution, being radical, civi- 
lizational and rational, does not recognise hesitation and gives to everything and 
everyone what they deserve. There is, therefore, no way in which the revolution could 

pe blamed for these transformations, especially when in the last few years the revolution 
had allowed contractors and merchants to work in the great projects realised by the 
revolution,"

"These people should appreciate the 10 years opportunity which the revolution had granted 
in order that they would not join the camp opposing it, not withstanding the fact that no 
one has tried to rob them of what they had gained. 


All what will happen, will be the transformation of these merchants and contractors into 
productive forces participating in the creation of wealth." 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi said: "Contractors have realised large profits after the revolution 
as a result of massive projects, since the country had earlier been devoid of develop- 
mental activities in all fields. The 10-year period given by the revolution to merchants, 
contractors and others, for making gains, was also sufficient for the maturation of ideas 
which led to the appearance of the Green Book and its theses, There could be no truce 
after this period." 


He added that the new advances did not achieve any personal interests either for him or for 
those who had joined him in bringing about the revolution, ‘The advances, he said, were 
for the benefit of the entire Libyan-Arab people. 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi affirmed the necessity of abolishing free commerce, wages and rents 
"which are all exploitative phenomena that are bound to vanish so that no person will 
be a parasite on another. This way, justice, equality, security, freedom and happiness 
will be achieved." 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi referred to the people's congresses! resolution concerning the 
increase in the production capacity by saying: "This means that everyone working in the 
consumption sector should change to the productive one in a bid to eradicate vackwardness 
by mobilising all capabilities and potentialities to build the new socialist society." 


In relation to this point, he said that large consuming sectors were being transformed 
into productive ones. 


On the dictum "the house belongs to its tenant" Colonel al-Qadhdhafi said: "The renting 
tenant lives a turbulent life for many reasons; one of them being the constant threat of 
eviction at any time, another being rent increases with or without justification. There- 
fore it is essential that a house inhabited by an occupant and his family should be his 
personal property. This also applies to vehicles of transportation which should be under 
the private property of individuals and not means of exploiting others." 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi then referred to the consumers cooperative societies, by saying 
"these societies, in their present conditions, are capitalist societies and are not the 
societies which are required, They will vanish just like free commerce if they do not 
change their nature." 


He pointed out that cooperative societies and public markets, correctly understood, are 
not based on profit, but on offering services to the people and selling consumer goods 
to them at cost price. 


He said: "The Federation of Chambers of Commerce and Industry can play a role by guiding 
the commercial sector and pushing it towards production, and transforming it from a con- 
suming to a productive sector." 


He said: "Anyone who had money could change his activities and carry out, with other 
members of the public, productive socialist work, because ownership in the new socialist 
society differed from any other existing ownership in the world,"

He added: "The new socialist ownership is a great task which one individual or a small 
group could not solely carry out. 


This could only be achieved by every individual carrying out productive work such as in 
big factories. These factories are socialist establishments where people who produced 
in them are not hired hands but have a share in production and where the remaining 
production goes to the people who own the factory, capital, and raw materials. 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi said: "There is another sacred form of ownership, which is pro- 
ductive. It is sacred because its owner works for himself in order to satisfy himself 
and live from his work. This form of work is permitted (by God)." 


He added: "Productive work is that which multiplies the value of the commodity produced, 
and not work which multiplies its price, something which only benefits the merchant who 
raises the commodity's price and therefore benefits society in no way." Colonel 
al-Qadhdhafi gave a few examples of productive work such as plumbing, painting, clock- 
repairing, radio maintenance, car maintenance, and other forms of maintenance which 
maintained or elongated any instrument's life, or mltiplied its productivity. 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi urged the Federation of Chamber of Commerce and Industry to parti- 
cipate fully in the revolution, because it was not the sole property of any group but 
basically depended on personal initiative emanating from the self like faith which can- 

not be withdrawn from the revolutionary like a position or delegation of responsibility 

can be withdrawn. [sentence as received] He said: "You can therefore revolt by 
transforming tens of thousands of merchants into producers instead of their being consumers." 


He said: "If merchants are now described as being bourgeois, exploiters, consumers and 
non=producers, then they can revolt and change these descriptions the same way as idolators 
had earlier ceased worshipping their idols and destroyed them, abstained from drinking 
alcohol, and freed slaves after taking up Islam which fought all these habits which they 
had held onto. Their stand was a revolt against themselves, and thus their lives were 
corrected and became pure and clean." 


He said: "Merchants can today carry out a useful revolution for this country and for 
history and the renaissance of a new civilisation in this society, by transforming tens 
of thousands of merchants from consumers into producers," 


He added: "Libyan soil is the property of all Libyans, and every person has the right of 
exploiting this Libyan soil with his own private effort, until all his-personal needs are 
satisfied." 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi pointed out that the problem of employers and employees had ended and 
that employers were now entitled to become producers anc participate with other producers 
according to their abilities. "This," he said, "is the new society which we are calling 
for, a society in which every individual is a partner in socialist ownership and production 
by himself, or any productive work carried out to satisfy individual needs or present a 
public service rendered to the people where the people, in (?return) would provide this 
individual with his needs," 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi clarified the task of social security in the new socialist order by 
saying: "The people will provide the disabled, who are unable to either work or produce, 
any service to satisfy their needs through the venue of social security."

He said: "Any productive person who is able to work is liable to become disabled, and 
that if this should happen, society will provide any such person with his needs." "This," 
he said, “is integration and solidarity, or in other words, social security." 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi urged the commerce sector to make revolution and said that "they 
could declare [words indistinct] it into an agricultural land, with every one of them

of production. He who has any productive craft other than agriculture is free to work 
in it and facilities could be offered to him as well." 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi indicated that at one point the commercial stores would be closed 
down and "therefore we should consider the case of those merchants so that they, and 
their families after them, would not be harmed as the revolution will not accept this 
situation, for it cannot give salaries to people without work. We have time to consult 
and discuss the way of transforming this sector into a productive one which deserves to 
have assistance and facilities."
