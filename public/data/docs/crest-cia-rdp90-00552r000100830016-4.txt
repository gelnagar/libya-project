Summary (AI): This 1981 New York Times article reprints the final Justice Department report on Billy Carter's dealings as an alleged agent of the Libyan government. It covers President Carter's sworn interview, his knowledge of his brother's 1978 and 1979 trips to Libya, and the use of Billy Carter to contact Libyan diplomats over the Iran hostages.
• Justice Department Order No. 907-80, dated Aug. 1, 1980, directed the Counsel on Professional Responsibility to investigate Billy Carter's activities as an alleged agent of the Libyan Government.
• A Status Report was forwarded to the Solicitor General on Oct. 29, 1980.
• The Justice Department was precluded from interviewing high-level Libyan officials because of the state of US-Libya relations.
• Billy Carter was hospitalized for alcoholism in Americus, Georgia, in February 1979.
• Billy Carter was reportedly not an official representative of the US government.
• 1978: Billy Carter's first trip to Libya; the President says he learned of it from his secretary after Billy was already en route
• 1979: Billy Carter's second trip to Libya, which the President says he learned of only after it was publicly announced
• 1979-11: Billy Carter used to initiate contacts with Libyan diplomats to help secure release of American hostages in Iran; he arranged a meeting between Dr. Brzezinski and the Libyan chargé in Washington
• 1980-12-19: President Jimmy Carter interviewed under oath at the White House as part of the Justice Department investigation
