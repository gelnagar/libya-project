LIBYA

‘TENS OF THOUSANDS’ RELEASED FROM ARMED FORCES 


LD141910 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 0810 GMT 14 Dec 82 


[Text] Tripoli, 14 Dec -- Informed sources told JANA yesterday that tens of thousands 
of soldiers were released from the regular Libyan Arab Armed Forces last week after 
having completed their military service. 


This, these sources point out, affirms Libya's march forward toward the final abolition 
w 


of the regular military establishment and its replacement with the armed people “so 
that self-defense will take the place of the traditional army." 


The JANA political editor, commenting on this, reports: There is no longer any meaning 
for the traditional military establishment in a society that is run by popular con- 
ferences and committees. He added that the creation of the armed people will bolster 
freedom and will mean that Libyans will defend themselves should Libyan or any Arab 
soil be the object of external aggression.

PDRY CULTURE MINISTER THABIT ARRIVES 11 DEC 


LD121012 Tripoli JANA in English 0845 GMT 12 Dec 82 


[Text] Tripoli, Safar 26, Dec 12, Jamahiriyah News Agency -- Mr. Rashid Muhammad 
Thabit, member of the Central Committee and minister of culture and tourism, and Mr 
Abdelwahid Abdallah Ayyad, the education's deputy minister in the Democratic Republic 
of Yemen, arrived here yesterday evening. 


The Yemeni minister expressed to JANA's correspondent his happiness to visit the 
Jamahiriyah and said his visit is part of the joint cooperation and coordination 
in the cultural and information fields and stressed the necessity to promote and 
consolidate relations in all fields in order to create a firm basis for the 
realisation of unionist action between the two brotherly peoples. 


MOROCCAN STUDENTS REPORTED ON STRIKE IN OUJDA 


LD141532 Tripoli Voice of Arab Homeland in Arabic 0215 GMT 14 Dec 82 


[Text] It was learned from Rabat yesterday evening that secondary schools and 
university students at Oujda have been on strike since the beginning of last week 
and that bloody clashes have taken place between students and Moroccan repression 
forces which resulted in the injury of some students. 


It was learned that the strikes began at the Birkan secondary school and spread from 
there to other schools at Oujda. The Moroccan information media have reported nothing 
about the strikes which have been going on for over 10 days. 


MAP today carried a brief report about these strikes claiming they were caused by 
some problems particularly in connection with curricula. However the reports affirm 
that the students’ are on strike because of campaigns of repression, 

oppression and beatings by the Moroccan repression forces against the students. 

This is in addition to the bad economic and political conditions in the country.
