LIBYA

AL-QADHDHAFI DISCUSSES RELATIONS WITH EGYPT, ATOM BOMB 


LD072250 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1920 GMT 7 Jun 80 


{Interview by Alberto Marina Antoni, correspondent of the Italian magazine PANORAMA 
with Libyan leader Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi--date, place not given] 


[Excerpts] Question: To what do you attribute the lack of success which "crowned" your 
policy toward Tunisia and Egypt? 


Answer: To the fact that conditions in the arena we move in are not ripe yet. 


Question: Regarding the failure you met in your dealings with Tunisia and Egypt, do 
you feel that you have failed or that they have failed to understand you? 


Answer: I do not consider them to be a failure. These steps were essential and they 
bring the date of unity never... They are imperative steps. I believe that any action 
that takes place cannot fail because it becomes part of history and will be successful 
in the end. 


Question: What is your definition of politics? 

Answer: Politics mean the tackling of interests. 

Question: Because of its oil deposits, your country is one of the richest in the world. 
yet, in spite of your important income from o11 development and sales, your society 


is still on the road of development. To what do you attribute this economically 
conflicting situation? What remedy can save your society? 


Ve. 9 Jun 80 I 3 NORTH AFRICA 


Answer: Money alone is not a means for progress. Indeed, quite often it acts against 
progress. In fact, the rich Arab states are the backward ones. They are the states 

were illiteracy is rampant and there is little interest in education, In these states, 
man acquites no vocation or productive skill and productive action is always threatened. 

In these countries, man is not creative because he does not need to be as he is compensated 
with money. Thus, if we were to get rid of our oil, it would be better for us in the 
future, for then we would depend on our knowledge, skill and creativity and make progress. 
Then we would advance, 


Furthermore, the absence of Arab unity plays its role in the continuation of backwardness 
and the failure to attain progress. But the real historical reason is that imperialism 
had placed us in such a backward state. 


Question: Inflation in your country is about to devour all the achievements you made in 
the past 10 years. 


Answer: We are not suffering from this problem. On the contrary, this is a problem of 
the capitalist countries. Inflation will threaten capitalism. But we, in our new society, 
will eliminate all the causes of such problems, [words indistinct] 


Question: All the economic sectors in you country suffer from a shortage of manpower in 
terms of mumbers and quality. 


Answer: We realize this fact and this is the rationale behind everyone working to achieve 
Arab unity. 


Question: Why aren't the doors open for the immigration of revolutionaries to Libya since 
you say that unity belongs to all. 


Answer: This might happen. 


Question: During the past 10 years in the life of the Libyan revolution, you rarely used 
the weapon of intimidation against the opposition. On the contrary, you chose to ignore it. 


Answer: First of all, we did not face any political opposition, as there can be no political 
opposition since there is no goverrmment or ruling party or president. What we are facing 
are remnants of an old society, a society of exploitation, enslavement, dictatorship and 
classes. Now the social revolution has begun and will end in the triumph of the new 

society over the old society. Hence, the class which will stand in the way of change will 


end. 


Question: Some Western press circles say that yourPtough attitude toward the opposition 
abroad was caused by the gemiine difficulties in the country. 


Answer; On the oontrary, I feel that the revolution is now crowning its historic 
successes by liquidating the remnants of the old society. 


Questions: According to reports circulating in Europe, 11 June has been fixed as the 
final date for the return of the opposition from abroad. If this is so, what will 
happen to them after that? 


Answer: I only fixed that date in order to save them from the revenge of the revolutionary 
committees. I cannot protect anyone who remains away after that date, from the revenge of 
these committees. What I mean is that I have no control over the revenge of the revolu- 
tionary committees against the remnants of the exploitive society. I have told them that 
unless they come back by 11 June, they will face liquidation and they will be liquidated 
by the revolutionary committees and I shall not be able to protect them, 


Ve. 9 Jun 80 NORTH AFRICA 


Question: Then all will die. 


Answer: I believe that the revolutionary committees will contimue to carry out their pro- 
grams. Nobody has any control over the work of the revolutionary committees; the revolu- 
tionary committees have their own convictions. They believe that the forces of exploita- 
tion and dictatorship in the world mist by physically liquidated and then instigate the 
masses everywhere to destroy that class. 


Question: Can you describe to us one example of opposition, who they are and what is 
the threat they pose? 


Answer: The question is not one of individuals but one of an exploitive class which is 
hostile to the people. It is not hostile to the regime because there is no regime. It 
is hostile to the liberation of the workers now that they have become partners. It is 
also against the principle that the house belongs to the one living init. It is against 
the principle that land should belong to all. It is against the authority of the people. 
Question: According to a recent interview I had with the Arab League Secretary General 
Chadli Klibi, it seems that all the parties, namely, the POLISARIO, Algeria and Morocco 
are now seeking a solution to the problem in that part of Africa through negotiations. 
What do you think about this? 

Answer: I do not believe that Algeria is a party to this issue. Furthermore, there 
will be no negotiations between the POLISARIO and the King [Hassan of Morocco] until he 
concedes to their right of self-determination. 

Question: What about the events at Qafsah? 


Answer: They ended with the fall of the Hedi Nouira government and the formation of a 
new government by a new prime minister, 


Question: After President as-Sadat's decision on 15 May to terminate negotiations with 
Israel, how do you see the situation in the area? Will there be a new war? 


Answer: War between whom and whom? 

Question: Between the Arabs and Israel. 

Answer; Which Arabs? 

Question: Iraq, Syria, Libya and Algeria. 

Answer: The state of war will contime until the liberation of Palestine. 


Question: Do you believe that As-Sadat had good intentions when he suspended the nego- 
tiations? 


Answer: As-Sadat is a failure and consequently the measures he takes are worthless. 


Question: What do you think of the statements of the French president which he made 
during his recert tour of the Gulf? 


Answer: Politically, they were positive, coming from a European government. But they 
do not change the situation. The Palestinians mist contime to fight until they liberate 
their land. 


Question: What do you think of the interview ‘Arafat has given to Radio Free Europe No 1 
in which he said that Europe mist make a choice or the Arab countries will be forced to 
use the oil weapon. What is your reaction to this statement? 


V. 9 Jun 80 hae NORTH AFRICA 


Answer; What he said is correct. But he did not come up with it himself but said it 
based on the Arab stand. 


Question: In one of your speeches you declared that the Western countries which partici- 
pated in the World War, namely, Germany, Britain and Italy must pay compensation for the 
damage Jamahiriyah suffered during World War II. 


Answer: We have raised this matter at the United Nations and we have won full support 
from the UN General Assembly. We have also submitted the matter to a mumber of interna- 
tional organizations which support us, too. The last was the resolution adopted by the 
Islamic foreign ministers conference held in Islamabad last month, 

Question: What is the value of this damage? 


Answer: Very mich and amounts to thousands of billions from each country. We can never 
abandon this matter. 


Question: How much approximately? 

Answer: Thousands of billions from each country. 

Question: The English-language magazine EIGHT DAYS has recently reported that Libya will 
withdraw its deposited assets in Britain and that these amount to about $7.8 billion--a 
matter which will cause the bankruptcy of several British banks. Do you intend to do this? 
Answer: Yes, we have such an intention. 

Question: Shortly? 

Answer: I said we have such an intention. 

Question; Reports are rife in Europe lately that Libya has obtained a muiclear bomb. Can 
you confirm this report or is the whole matter no more than a fantasy as was stated in the 
novel, the FIFTH KNIGHT, or is the report an abstract fact as many affirm? 

Answer; I have repeatedly affirmed that this was a completely reactionary attempt intend- 
ed to impede developing countries and prevent them from using the atom for peaceful pur- 
poses and progress. We do not think at all about muclear armaments. Like the rest of the 
world, we work and think about using the atom forpeaceful purposes. We do not have to use 
atomic weapons. We have defense capabilities which do not make it necessary for us to use 
muclear weapons, We are not faced with a danger so that we may think about using the atom, 
Thank God, all our neighbours are weak. Nevertheless, we are signatories to agreements 
which ban the proliferation of miclear weapons. 

Question: But you have not answered my question. Do you or do you not have an atom bomb? 
Answer; We have not thought at all about the atom bomb.
