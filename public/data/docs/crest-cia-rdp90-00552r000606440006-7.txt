Summary (AI): A New York Times article reports on British pilot Anthony Stubbs, who says he was dismissed by Libya after refusing to fly a transport mission into N'Djamena, Chad, and later flew covert night flights from Libya into Chad for a company controlled by Edwin P. Wilson. He says the C-130 type transports carried bombs, fuel and wounded, and that he saw small arms unloaded by men in unmarked combat uniforms.
• Stubbs was hired in London late 1980 with an expected salary of $48,000 a year; he was paid $15,000 a month per pilot supplied to the Libyan Air Force by Wilson's company.
• Stubbs worked in Libya just under three months.
• Wilson's company allegedly failed to pay Stubbs $33,000 in back wages and warned him not to divulge information.
• Stubbs told Scotland Yard's Secret branch about his experiences soon after returning to London; he later also went to the British authorities in February 1981.
• The article says the Libyan Air Force was assisted by Soviet equipment.
• 1981: Stubbs refused to fly a Libyan Air Force transport into a contested airfield in Chad and was then deported by the Libyan government.
• 1981: Stubbs flew covert night transport missions from Libya to desert strips in Chad for a company controlled by Edwin P. Wilson.
