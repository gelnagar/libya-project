RECRUITING AMERICANS TO AID QADDAFI

Summary (AI): A 1981 Washington Post column reports that American businessmen, ex-CIA agents and retired military officers were recruited by Edwin Wilson and Frank Terpil to work for Libya's Qaddafi, including explosives supplier Jerome Brower and retired Air Force general Joseph Cappucci. It describes the Libyan regime's support for terrorism only in general terms.
• Brower was fined $5,000 and ordered to serve four months of a five-year prison term.
• Wilson reportedly had dozens of companies; Mulcahy said about 90 of 100 deals were legitimate.
• Federal investigators found no evidence of criminal activity by Cappucci and dropped the case.
• CIA involvement in the recruiting could not be ruled out, though hard evidence was lacking.
• 1978-05: Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms investigators were told by an informant that one of Cappucci's employees was trying to obtain a large quantity of arms for export to Libya.
