Summary (AI): A New York Times article (23 July 1980, reproduced in a CIA reading room file) reports U.S. intelligence reports on a Libyan plan to win influence in the United States, including through Billy Carter's dealings with Charter Oil. It describes Libyan efforts to obtain aircraft held back by a U.S. export ban and to use oil allocations as leverage.
• Libya sought release of military and civilian aircraft bought but withheld by a State Department export ban.
• The U.S. had no ambassador in Tripoli for two years and no official representative there since May 1979 (per article).
• Billy Carter acknowledged receiving $220,000 from Libya, described as a loan; the Justice Department called it payment for propaganda work and commercial intermediary service.
• Libya reportedly considered independent oil companies other than Charter as targets, but they apparently were not included.
• The article gives no hard evidence of covert payments or other illegal activity to date.
• 1980-03: Intelligence reports on a Libyan plan to cultivate American friends and interest groups were widely circulated within the intelligence community and executive branch in March 1980.
