LIBYA

JANA COMMENTS ON NATURE OF KISSINGER TRIP 


LD281641 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 28 Dec 80 
[From the “News and Dimensions" program prepared by JANA political affairs department] 


[Text] At a time when the Arab area is being prepared for a new explosion to be detonat- 
ed by the capitulation and liquidation movement on the road of the Palestine cause, 
former U.S. Secretary of State Henry Kissinger leaves Washington on a business trip 

which will take him to Egypt, occupied Palestine, Saudi Arabia, Oman and Morocco. 


The fact that Kissinger's visit has coincided with the start of the new American 
administration has prompted observers to link it with the new line of American policy 
toward the Arab cause or more precisely the Arab-Zionist conflict. 


If we view Kissinger as the observers do, in his capacity as architect of American 
foreign policy, we find outselves faced with an era of an extreme American rush toward 
bolstering the Zionists and consecrating their occupation of the Arab territories, if 
not to say enabling them to expand. 


This conclusion stems from the rature of the Kissinger policy which stands for linking 
domination of oil resources to the strength of the Zionists, or as TIME magazine said 
in one of its issues in March 1974 following U.S. President Nixon's assignment of the 
Middle East issue to Kissinger, the new American policy wants to tie the security of 
Arab oil to a strong Israel, through the settlement plans, that is, the step-by-step 
policy which was pursued then. 


A look at the dossier of Kissinger, the architect of the American policy under 
ex-President Nixon, highlights three elements which could almost delineate the features 
of that policy: 


1. When Kissinger, who was then national security adviser to U.S. President Nixon, 
assumed responsibility for the Middle East question, a new trend manifested itself in 
American policy and was reflected in assurances by Americans, headed by former President 
Nixon, that there is no likelihood of Mideast peace in the near future. 2. That 
political line was coupled with an unreserved declared American readiness to continue 

to supply the Zionist enemy with all its military and economic needs. 3. On the other 
hand was the line parallel to that of the American stand: the Zionists' occupation of 
three Arab islands at Bab El Mandeb at the entrance to the Red Sea. 


At the time observers considered these three elements as the features characterizing 
Kissinger's way of tackling the Palestine question. It is a way organically linked with 
the American strategy which stands for establishigg an American hegemony in the Arab 
area and the Middle East in general and which depends to a great extent on the strength 
and effectiveness of the Zionist occupation in subverting Arab resistance. 


One who follows the capitulatory steps since Kissinger's assumption of responsibility 
for American policy in the Middle East and the Arab area in 1974--from the military 
disengagement steps and the kilometer 101 tent to the complete fall of Egypt and the 
current developments in Lebanon and other events and falls that might follow--can 
readily realize how far things are moving in the direction drawn up by Kissinger in his 
capacity as architect of the strategy contained in the reports of U.S. President Nixon 
and which made the establishment of peace in the area, according to their terms, 
conditional on the establishment of a new system in the Middle East founded on 
reorganizing internal conditions in the area.

With the passage of time the theory of the new system seems to be consolidated. It is a 
system which is completely and directly subservient to American militarism. Thus we see 
that Egypt, Somalia, Oman and the Arab Gulf up to the shores of the Arabian Sea have 
become a lively sphere for movement by the American fleets and other American forces. 


Kissinger is not the genius of his time, nor is he a miracle maker for Arrica, the 
America of hegemony, imperialism and influence. For he has failed in Vietnam, and so 
did his two-edged weapon: the military edge which depends on using force to the maximum 
and the political edge which depends on exploiting his adversaries’ weak points. 


Yet we see Kissinger return today tempted by the weak points in the Arab field and the 
American military expansion in the Arab area. He has come again to ride the wave of the 
American imperialist tide. 


But we must not forget to mention here that Henry Kissinger, former U.S. secretary of 
state and former national security adviser under Nixon, was the first American national 
security official whose political tasks were linked with two of the most grave, crucial, 
and sensitive issues: the issue of Palestine and neighboring areas of the Middle East, 
as they call it, and the issue of energy or the energy crisis, as they claim. That was 
in March 1974, when he first began to tackle the issue and the area. 


Because of that assignment, the experiences of recent years, and the step-by-step 
method, Kissinger comes today to an Arab area which looks as if it had been prepared by 


events to face a new explosion on the road of the cause and destiny. 


LIAISON SECRETARY ATTACKS U.S., EGYPTIAN POLICIES 


LD271330 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 27 Dec 80 


[Text] At a press conference he held in Madrid the secretary of the Foreign Liaison 
Office underlined the hostile American role toward the Arab nation. He said: The 
American policy is totally partial toward the Zionists, and is exercising pressures on 
the European states to support it in its hostility and aggressive plans against the 
Arab nation. He said: The great 1 September revolution knows well the path for the 
liberation of Palestine. 


He called on the Arab nation to use all its potentials and weapons to liberate the 
occupied Arab territory and to regain its usurped rights. He added: Relations between 
the Arab homeland and Europe are threatened because of the increasing American military 
presence in a number of countries in the Arab region and the Indian Ocean. 


The secretary of the Foreign Liaison Office criticized the provocations of the American 
fleets in the Mediterranean Sea and the military concentrations of the Egyptian regime 
along the border of the Jamahiriyah. He said: This constitutes a grave threat to world 
peace and security. He praised the realistic and positive role of the Spanish Govern- 
ment and people for comprehending fully the case of the Palestinian people.
