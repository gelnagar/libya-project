AL-QADHDHAFI RECEIVES GRENADA PRIME MINISTER 


LD161438 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 16 Jun 82 


[Text] The brother leader this afternoon received Grenada Prime Minister Maurice 
Bishop, who arrived in the jamahiriyah at noon today, upon arriving at Tripoli Interna- 
tional Airport, he and the accompanying delegation were welcomed by Brig Gen Abu Rakr 
Yunis Jabir, commander in chief of Libyan Arab Armed Forces, and secretaries of the 
general people's committee. 


In a statement to a JANA correspondent, Bishop emphasized that his visit comes within 
the framework of strengthening bilateral relations in various spheres. He explained 
this visit also will deal with present international political developments, especially 
the barbaric Zionist invasion against the Palestinian and Lebanese peoples.

WEEKLY RAPS HAIG STATEMENT ON MIDDLE EAST 


PM160827 London AL-ZAHAF AL-AKHDAR in English 11 Jun 82 p 2 


(Unattributed article: "Now Is America's Moment in the Middle East"] 


[Text] In a recent issue of AL-ZAHAF AL-AKHDAR it was revealed that the American 
administration was about to launch a new "peace initiative" in the Middle East. This 
has now happened and it comes as no surprise that 'peace' in fact simply means 
imperialist hegemony. 


In what the press termed "important" policy statement to the Chicago council on foreign 
relations, Alexander Haig, the US secretary of state, let the cat out of the bag yet 
again. So that there could be no misunderstanding as to what America's aims are, he 
said: "The United States has to shape events in the Middle East if it is to continue 

to hope for a more peaceful international order." This means clearly that 'peace' can 
only be American-imposed. That is to say, as long as any country in the area refuses to 
recognise US hegemony, there will be war.

This was reinforced by the fact that Reagan and Haig chose a specific date to make their 
new strategies public. It must be remembered that while Haig was speaking in Chicago, the 
Zionist minister of war, Ari'el Sharon, was in the United States drumming up support for 
the Zionist “cause” and helping to reactivate the strategic alliance. 


The other aspect of the timing of the statement that is relevant is that it comes only a 

few weeks before super-terrorist Menahem Begin starts an official state visit to the United 
States. These two facts alone take away all credibility concerning "peaceful" plans by 

the Americans to solve the crisis. df 

Haig went on to delineate what the 'new' US strategy will be. He announced three [apparent 
line drop] the region's main trouble spots -- the Iran-Iraq border, the Israeli-occupied 
West Bank, and south Lebanon. 


He went on to say that "each of the three big conflicts in the region is characterised by a 
mixture of danger and opportunity. If we are to succeed in advancing our goals throughout 
the region, then we must coordinate our approach to all of them. 


In other words, the imperialist US onslaught on the Arab homeland is no longer going to be 
piecemeal action, but a well-coordinated attack. Clearly the White House felt the initiative 
in the Arab homeland had been slipping from its hands over the past six months or so. 


This is especially visible in the stick and carrot approach it has been using to get its most 
faithful ally in the region, the Zionist entity, back into line. Begin has been chomping 

at the bit to realise his dream of a "greater Israel" as fast as possible and has thrown 
caution to the wind in his attempts to bring the Palestinians and the Lebanese to their 
knees. 


Although American aims are identical, Washington would have preferred a more covert approach. 
Preferably, an approach that would not result in the sort of international outcry that was 
raised after the annexation of the Golan and the recent assassination of unarmed schoolgirls 
on the West Bank. 


The stick was the temporary "suspension" of the strategic alliance -- though that is about to 
be reactivated with a vengeance -- while the carrot was handed to Sharon last week with the 
signing for the delivery of 75 F-16 fighters to the Zionist entity. 


In terms of the Camp David agreements, the Haig speech indicated that US policy was exactly 
what it had always been, except that he was gearing up the diplomatic circus by sending Philip 
Habib, the shuttle diplomat, back into the area in an attempt to solve the "problem" which 
recently stalled the talks on "autonomy", namely the place where the talks should continue. 


Mubarak, in an attempt to sound pro-Arab, has refused to go to Jerusalem, which is where 
Begin of course wants to hold the talks. But the Americans have found the solution -- and 
it is a fitting one: Habib is to entice Mubarak to visit Washington while Begin is there 
later this month. The talks can then be held in the very den of the imperialist benefactor. 
Maybe Reagan will even take them to Camp David itself.... 


Haig's speech -- which had been billed in advance as the Reagan administration's most compre- 
hensive policy declaration on the Middle East -- contained little that was new. However, 

the Americans have made more noise than usual about the Iran-Iraq conflict. Haig tried to 
sweet-talk the issue, saying that the US would "remain neutral, but this does not mean we 
are indifferent to the outcome.”

In fact, it has been confirmed in the United States that Washington is to sell six C-130 
Hercules transport planes to Iraq, which the London GUARDIAN speculated "could be used 
to ferry supplies of military equipment from friendly countries in the region, including 
Jordan and the Gulf states of Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Bahrain to Iraq." 


The sale is a clear indication that America, far from staying neutral in the Iran-Iraq 
conflict, has finally decided to throw in its lot with a regime it has previously 
described as "terrorist" -- but of course, it has always used the same terminology 
when describing the Iranian revolution. 


Haig was for once crystal clear when he defined the reasons for this new coordinated 
imperialist onslught on the Middle East: "We have friends and interests that are 
endangered by the continuation of hostilities. We are committed to defend our vital 
interests in the area." 


That, in fact, is a simple declaration of imperialist war on the progressive countries 
of the region. All in all, Haig's "major policy speech" did not tell us anything we 
did not already know. It simply reaffirmed America's aim to impose its hegemony on 
the Arab world, with the help of its Zionist ally, at all costs. 


PAPER ACCUSES HASSAN OF TAKING 'AS-SADAT ROAD' 


PM160835 London AL-ZAHAF AL-AKHDAR in English 11 Jun 82 p 2 
[Unattributed article: "Hassan's Treason"] 


[Text] With the signing of the agreement between Morocco and the United States which 
will allow the U.S. so-called "transit" facilities in Morocco, King Hassan has abandoned 
all pretence of being the leader of a non-aligned country and he has clearly taken the 
As-Sadat road. 


The agreement, signed between Hassan and the Americans on May 27th, allows the U.S. 
access to Moroccan air bases in the event of an "emergency" in the Middle East. A 
possible such "emergency" was mentioned as being any threat to the Western world's access 
to Arab oil fields. 


Although Hassan was at pains during the build-up to the signing of the agreement to 
assure Arab states that this agreement was not directed against them, the immediate 
condemnation which greeted his action proved that the Arab nation is not allowing itself 
to be fooled by Hassan's insincere reassurances. 


For it is quite clear that the facilities extended to the Americans are open for use 

by the Rapid Deployment Force -- as much was openly admitted by the Americans -- and it 
is this force which constitutes such a grave threat to the sovereign independence of 

so many Arab states. 


The agreement signed between U.S. Secretary of State Alexander Haig and Moroccan minister, 
M'hamed Boucetta, ostensibly only provides the U.S. with refuelling facilities for its 
planes and U.S. officials maintained that no U.S. troops would be stationed in Morocco. 


Bases Available 


The truth is that a lot more than just refuelling facilities will be open to the 
Americans. It is inconceivable that all the diplomatic effort which the Americans have 
put into this relationship with Hassan was merely supposed to yield refuelling facili- 
ties for U.S. aircraft.

The visits paid to Moroccan ports by warships of the United States Sixth Fleet, combined 
with the build-up of U.S. personnel involved in training and supporting Moroccans operating 
new U.S. military equipment such as F-5 fighters and M-60 tanks, all suggest that America 
is back in Morocco to stay. 


For of course the Americans have been in Morocco before. Until a few years ago, they 
maintained a communications facility at Kenitra, while amongst the first U.S. troops to

arrive in Africa or Europe during the Second World War were those sent to Morocco in 1942. 


Now with the Moroccan forces pinned down by Polisario guerrillas in the Western Sahara, 
it is clear that the Reagan administration has thrown its full weight behind Hassan's 
regime, a regime which is not above using the most ruthless tactics against its own 
internal political opponents, as the crushing of the Casablanca rising demonstrated. 


The publicly revealed details of the U.S.-Moroccan agreement will mean that the airborne 
elements of the Rapid Deployment Force, specifically the two U.S. Army airborne divisions 
assigned to it together with the assigned U.S. Air Force units, will be able to use 

Moroccan bases on their way to anywhere in the Middle East. With bases in Egypt and elsewhere, 
the Americans will now be able to increase their threat against the Libyan Jamahirivah. 


Threat to Libya 


There is no doubt that threatening the jamahiriyah is the main purpose of the U.S. bases 

in Morocco. The Americans will be able to base planes and military personnel there -- claiming 
of course that they are only “in transit" -- and these will be ready for quick deployment 
against Libya in the event of renewed U.S. aggressive action against Libya's people. 

Of course, the U.S.-Moroccan agreement also threatens the rest of the Arab nation. Most 
immediately threatened are the fighters of Polisario, the armed forces of the Sahrawi 
Arab Democratic Republic, which are engaged in a grim struggle with U.S.-equipped 
Moroccan forces in the Western Sahara. With U.S. arms supplies to Hassan's forces being 
stepped up, it is by no means inconceivable that U.S. personnel may become directly 
involved in this conflict. 


Considering all these factors and the implications of the agreement he has signed with 
the United States, Hassan's action constitutes a clear act of treason against the Arab 
nation. Hassan has turned his back on Morocco's historic commitment to the Arab nation, 
as a key Arab state of the Maghreb. By opening his borders to U.S. planes and troops, 
he has threatened every Arab state in the region.
