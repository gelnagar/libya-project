Summary (AI): A New York Times article from January 1986 reports growing discontent in Libya's armed forces under Qaddafi. It describes the reported killing of Colonel Hassan Ishkal in November 1985, with one widely cited account saying he was shot at the Bab al-Aziziya barracks in Tripoli.
• Colonel Ishkal was reportedly 47 years old and his wife was Egyptian.
• Qaddafi cancelled the annual 1 September military parade for the first time, and gave a major speech in Sebha to Revolutionary Guards and Committees.
• Revolutionary Guards were estimated at 1,000 to 2,000 young men with light weapons and reportedly controlled distribution of live ammunition at bases.
• Libyan income reportedly fell from $22 billion in 1980 to between $8 billion and $9 billion in the last year.
• 1985-11-23: Colonel Hassan Ishkal, head of the Sirte military region, reportedly killed; official account was a car accident, other versions claimed suicide or shooting at the barracks, and a post-mortem reportedly showed he was shot at least six times.
• 1964-05-08: Twelve men attacked the Bab el-Azziziya barracks in an attempt to kill Colonel Qaddafi; the 12 were killed and revolutionary guards and committee members reportedly arrested some 2,000 people afterward. (12 killed)
