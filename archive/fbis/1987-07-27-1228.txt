State Council Declares State of Emergency 
EA252030 Omdurman Domestic Service in Arabic 
1903 GMT 25 Jul 87 


[Declaration issued by the State Council and signed by Ahmad 
al-Mirghani, chairman of the State Council, on 25 July] 


[Text] In view of the anarchy prevailing in the markets, repre- 
sented by artificial shortages of certain essential commodities 
brought about by the black market’s illegal hoarding and spec- 
ulation in foreign currencies and noncompliance with announced 
legal prices; the spread of smuggling in all its forms; the lack of 
security as represented by armed robberies in some areas and the 
criminal gangs that exist in certain towns which have terrorized 
citizens, depriving them of their lives and property and dishon- 
oring their women; and the illegal infiltration of weapons into 
parts of the country — due to all this and in an effort to ensure 
the security of the domestic front in facing the enemies of 
democracy and the homeland, the State Council has decided, 
after consulting with the Council of Ministers on the basis of the 
power vested in it in accordance with Article 134 of Sudan’s 
transitional Constitution of 1985, to declare a state of emergency 
throughout the country for | year effective from the date of this 
declaration. 


This state of emergency has been imposed to achieve the follow- 
ing: 


1. To limit transactions in foreign currencies to legal channels 
and to wage war against foreign currency brokers and traders; 


2. To control anarchy in the market and to combat harmful 
hoarding and black marketeering; 


3. To control the prices of commodities and to compel those 
involved to comply with the announced legal prices; 


4. To wage war against all forms of smuggling; 


5. To end obstructions in public service that affect the citizens’ 
security and comfort; 


6. To stop armed robberies and to deter criminal gangs; 


7. To eliminate the possession of unlicensed firearms and to 
expose their caches; 


8. To ensure the security of citizens’ lives and property and the 
honor of women. 


9. To ensure a secure internal front to face the enemies of 
democracy and the homeland. 


God grant us success. 
Issued on 25 July 1987. 


[Signed] Ahmad al-Mirghani, chairman of the State Council, on 
behalf of the State Council

Council of Ministers Approval 
JN261853 Khartoum SUNA in Arabic 1740 GMT 26 Jul 87 


[Text] Khartoum, 26 Jul (SUNA) — Ahmad ‘Ali al-Mirghani, 
chairman of the Sudanese State Council, signed the state of 
emergency law for 1987 today in accordance with Article 134 of 
Sudan’s transitional Constitution. The Council of Ministers 
approved the state of emergency law today. This measure will 
now be referred to the Constitutent Assembly for proclamation. 


Dr Adam Madibu, minister of energy and mining, said that the 
state of emergency was declared to eliminate the obstacles facing 
the implementation of measures to facilitate the citizens’ liveli- 
hood. 


Madibu added that the crisis of price increases is fabricated and 
premeditated. He declared that the moves made by the emblems 
of the defunct May regime to undermine the democratic system, 
as well as the deteriorating state of security affairs, have dictated 
the imposition of the state of emergency. Madibi said that the 
Council of Ministers has empowered the regional governors and 
the Khartoum commissioner with implementing the state of 
emergency measure. 


Madibu denied that the declaration of a state of emergency is 
politically motivated or directed against citizens. He said that 
workers have a legitimate right to stage strikes if these are carried 
out in accordance with the law. Otherwise, strikes would be 
illegal even if a state of emergency had not been declared. 


A legal source told SUNA that the state of emergency declared 
by the State Council chairman is an exceptional measure that 
the authorities resort to as a means to counter any threat endan- 
gering the nation’s unity or security. Due to this measure, some 
liberties stipulated in the Constitution such as the right to 
assemble, demonstrate, and strike, as well as certain press free- 
doms, are suspended. 


The legal source also told SUNA that declaring a state of 
emergency is usually followed by the issuance of a state of 
emergency law defining the measures that must be taken. 


The legal source announced that the state of emergency law will 
be referred to the Constituent Assembly, which will approve or 
reject it. The source added that the Transitional Military Council 
once vested all powers in its hands before announcing a state of 
emergency and that this state of affairs ended with the Transi- 
tional Military Council. 


It is worth noting that the state of emergency has been rejected 
by large sectors of the population, which view it as a civilian 
dictatorship. 


Islamic Front Rejects Measure 
JN261833 Khartoum SUNA in Arabic 1744 GMT 26 Jul 87 


[Text] Khartoum, 26 Jul (SUNA) — The Islamic National Front 
[INF] has declared its rejection of the state of emergency pro- 
claimed by the State Council yesterday. INF Secretary General 


V. 27 Jul 87 


Al-Turabi declared that the front will resist the proclamation 
through all democratic means. 


In a statement to the press today, Al-Turabi announced: If the 
government has made this decision as a premise to imposing a 
sectarian dictatorship, then we will take it as a premise to rebel 
against such a dictatorship. He declared that the proclamation 
of an emergency is a despicable measure that was adopted in a 
manner reflecting political impulsiveness and legal naivete. 


Al-Turabi remarked that it is strange for a state of emergency to 
be proclaimed in the absence of the prime minister and without 
consultations with the opposition. He added: It is known that 
the text of the Constitution stipulates that a state of emergency 
is proclaimed if great danger threatens national unity or the 
safety of the homeland and its economy. 


He noted that the proclamation said that the state of emergency 
will be in force for 1 full year, thus ignoring the need to refer it 
within 2 weeks to the Constituent Assembly, which defines the 
period for which the state of emergency remains in force. 


Al-Turabi declared that the real reasons for proclaiming a state 
of emergency is the government's emotional reaction and politi- 
cal frustration caused by its complete failure to manage services 
and maintain security, as well as its emotional reaction to the 
increase of popular opposition. 


Al-Turabi asserted that lifting the restrictions of the [word 
indistinct] law and the return to the May security measures were 
done at the behest of illegal security quarters. The government 
wants a free hand and extraordinary powers to carry out its 
political designs against the opposition. 


Al-Turabi added that ordinary legal (?means), if used effectively, 
are adquate to combat smuggling and armed robbery, to exercise 
control over commodities, and to maintain security. He affirmed 
that the government has the right to seek to bring about the 
enactment of supplementary legislation by the Constituent 
Assembly. He said that there is no justification to proclaim a 
state of emergency just to cover up the government's failure to 
effectively enforce the law. 


MENA: Coup Attempt Following Emergency Act Denied 
NC262200 Cairo MENA in English 2144 GMT 26 Jul 87 


[Text] Cairo, July 26 (MENA) — Speculation on a coup in 
Sudan, reportedly attempted immediately after declaring a one- 
year state of emergency there, is denied by a Cairo daily. 


Tomorrow’s Al-Ahram, a Cairo morning Arabic-language daily, 
is running on page five a dispatch from Khartoum filed by its 
staffer, Salah al-Din Hafiz, who is quoting a high-placed Suda- 
nese source as discrediting these speculations and dismissing 
them as having no foundation. 


The dispatch is further asserting that the Sudanese Armed 
Forces are giving their support and backing to the legitimate 
democratic regime in Sudan.
