SPOKESMAN DENIES INVOLVEMENT IN EVENTS IN CHAv 


LD300012Y Tripoli Voice of the Arab Homeland in Arabic 231° MT 2° fun 78 ID 


[text ] An official spokesman of the Jamahiriyah Foreign Secretariat has stated that the 
French press last night published a statement attributed to Chad President Felix Malloum 
about what it called the Jamahiriyah's connection with the events inside Chad. 


We regret these statements attributed to President Malloum, who knows better than anyone 
the extent of tne Jamahiriyah's efforts in the persistent work to bring peace to Chad and 
achieve a national conciliation. If they are true, these statements indicate that there 
are certain circles with in.ercst in keeping the internal situation tense in Chad in order 
to foil the negotiations between the Chad National Liberation Front [FROLINAT] and the 
Chad Government, which are scheduled to take place in the Jamahiriyah in accordance with 
the statement signed at Benghazi between the Chad Government and FROLINAT. The responsi- 
bility for the failure of these negotiations will rest witi those who fail to abide by 

the provision, of the Sabha statement. 


Out of its policy of good neighboriiness, its African policy, and its adherence to the 
OAU Charter and goals, the Jamahiriyan has worked and continues to work to solve the 
African problems within an African framework, particularly when the matter affects a 
friendly neighboring state. 


Though these statements attributed to the president of Chad, if true, will render the 
future negotiations meaningless, the Jamahiriyah, pursuing the same policy, will do all 
it can to bring peace and security to Chad. 


FRENCH JOURNAL INTERVIEWS JALLUD 
( Tripoli JANA in English 1424 amT 28 Jun 78 LD 


[Text] Tripoli, June 26, JANA--Staff Major 'Abd as-Salam Jallud, member of the General 
Secretariat of the General Peoples Congress, has affirmed: "The struggle of humanity for 
democracy will be crowned by the realization of people's power and the destruction of the 
traditional instruments of power as a final svlution to the problem of democracy." He 
also affirmed: "There is no solution more democratic than the establishment of people's 
power, the removal of all authoritarian set ups like presidents, kings and parties and 
their replacement py the power of the whole people. I believe that this will lead to a 
noble civilization." 


He said, in an interview with the French magazine LE NOUVEL OBSERVATEUR, "The soctalist 
People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah has solved the problem of democracy and has started to 
implement direct democracy... For the Libyan Arab peopie are ruling themselves by themselves. 
The people are organised into 187 people's congresses through which they discuss domestic 

and foreign politics and take the necessary decisions regarding these policies. They also 
supervise the implementation of these decisions through the other basic institution of 
popular administration, namely the people's committees which are answerable to the congresses 
as the latter choose and depose the committees."

He added: "The world mass media claim that it is not possible to practice direct 
jemocracy, as represented by people's power, in countries with large populations like 
China, but this is incorrect because this system can be applied all over the world. 
France, for example, could be divided into 400 congresses, etc." 


He went on to say: "The era of republics which started in France won victory despite 
the massing of troops along French borders by empires and monarchies. ‘Tne era of the 
masses and people's vower will likewise be victorious. 


Staff Major Jallud, answering a question about the claims that the Revolutionary Command 
Council is still in power through the General Secretariat of the General People's Congress, 
affirmed: "Those who spread rumours in the worli said this because they were flabber- 
gasted when they witnessed those who led the revolution have handed power to the people." 


He added: "People's power is legal and constitutional and it is our task to transform 
this constitutionality into a reality confirmed by people's congresses and committees, 
and to traisform theory from mere ink on paper into actual practice." 


He went on to say: "The theory demands that three things must be transferred to the 
people, for whoever possesses these three things is the ruler. These things are: 

firstly power, because it is an authoritarian instrument that must be transferred from 
individuals and groups to the people. §econdly the wealth, for whoever owns the wealth 
possesses power, social and political hegemony and controls public opinion. Therefore 
wealth must be transferred from individuals and groups to the people. Thirdly the arms, 
for if arms were in the hands of individuals cor a group it constitutes an authoritarian 
instrument. Therefore they must be transferred to the people." 


Staff Major Jallud added: "The whole people in the Jamahiriyah are receiving military 
training. The entire population own the arms. Thus there is no problem when the arms 
are possessed by the people, ending the traditional army, Evei'y city will have its 

own stockpile of weapons and the people will choose those who administer these stockpiles 
provide) it is done in an organised and proper manner." 


Answering a question cn the recent law regarding conscription, he said: "The people's 
congresses decreed these laws as a temporary measure to meet a transitional period and 
specific circumstances until the realization of the people possessing the arms." 


On the new socialism in the Jamahiriyah he said: "The socialism we are building is 
based on the dicta of the second chapter of the green book." He added: "There is no 
Libyan socialism but there is one socialism which exists wherever people exist. When 
the first 'primitive' society was formed, people were equal and there was no rich and 
poor," 


He affirmed that returning to the natural bases which determine the relations between 
the elements of production was the basis for achieving the new socialism. He said: 
"There is no partial freedom, freedom is one and indivisible."

Staff Major Jallud pointed out that socialist society was one of two things: Either 

one would work for oneself and one's family and by one's own efforts meet one's material 
needs, or one would work in a socialist institution in which one is a partner in production 
and where one is guaranteed sufficient income to meet one's material needs. The new 
socialism affirms "basic human needs are sacred, a principle which is affirmed by one of the 
green book's dicta under the title "Freedom Lies in Needs". 


The basic human needs arise as soon as tne human being is born, such as accommodation, 
clothing and transport. These are sacred needs which cannot be questioned or provided 
for by a law ana there is no ruler or any other person who donates them to others. 


In a socialist society people give more because they know that their rights cannot be 
taken away by the state or any individual. Staff Major Jallud added: "The new theory 
tackles the small and great needs, it is a humanitarian theory, it forbids the hiring 
of domestic servants and it raises the slogans "the land belongs to all generations", 
"the house is a sacred possession" and "wealth belongs to all generations". 


n French policy in Africa, he said; "This is a return to the old way which proved a 
failure. The Jamahiriyah's stand towards the question of interventions is clear. 
However, France still has a chance %o reconsider her stand towards this question". 


n international terrorism, he said: "We are against terrorism and we do not believe 

in it. Our way of thinking and attitude do not go along with terrorism and our laws are 
against it. The Jamahiriyah is strongly against hijacking and believes that even 
revolutionary and progressive movements harm themselves and their cause if they used this 
method,"
