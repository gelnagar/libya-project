Summary (AI): A Washington Post article from late August 1986 reports that the Reagan administration ran a "scare Gadhafi" campaign, including talk of U.S. naval maneuvers near Libya, a Forrestal port visit cancellation, and European economic pressure after the April 1986 U.S. bombing. It concerns U.S. policy toward Libya and does not describe Tripoli sites or Libyan internal repression.
• Plan devised in the White House in July 1986 to scare Gadhafi into an irrational reaction, per informed sources.
• Administration discussed and discarded creating sonic booms over Libya by U.S. warplanes.
• Forrestal planned port visit to Haifa, Israel was canceled; carrier ordered to remain in central Mediterranean near Libya.
• Soviet-supplied Scud B missiles were fired at a barge in the Gulf of Sidra in July 1986.
• Libya's dollar-denominated oil proceeds reportedly were cut about in half since last year.
• 1986-04-15: U.S. air raid (bombing) on Tripoli and Benghazi, referenced as background to the later pressure campaign.
