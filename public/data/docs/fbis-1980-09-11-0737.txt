LIBYA

IMAM AS-SADR REPORTEDLY HELD PRISONER IN LIBYA 
NCO91214 Paris AFP in English 1207 GMT 9 Sep 80 


[Text] Paris, 9 Sep, (AFP)--Imam Musa as-Sadr, missing leader of the Shi'ite Muslim 
community in Lebanon, is alive and imprisoned in a Libyan army camp near the frontier 
with Algeria, Radio Luxembourg said today. Imam as-Sadr disappeared after visiting Libya 
in August 1978 for celebrations to mark the Libyan revolution. Libya has consistently 
maintained that he left the country for Rome. 


Radio Luxembourg's French service said a former French Army officer and veteran of the 
independence war in Algeria had coordinated the kidnapping of the imam with the Libyan 
secret services. The officer, named as Major Jean-Jacques Delaporte, had taken the pseu- 
donym "Captain Karima" for the operation, the radio added. 


He was now being held at Boufarik, 60 km (40 miles) from Algiers by the Algerien authori- 
ties who were using him to "blackmail" both France and Libya, it said. 


Major Delaporte, according to Radio Luxembourg, had gone to Algeria in August 1979, 
where he had been arrested and accused of being a French spy. 


The Algerian authorities also accuse the major, a former member of France's "shock 
battalions" in Algeria, of being behind arms shipments to Algerian dissidents in the 
Kabylie region some 18 months ago, the radio said. 


Algeria, according to the radio, had offered to free him on condition that France agree 
to stop supporting Morocco in its disputed Western Sahara region. Algeria had offered to 
hand the major over to Libya on condition that Libyan leader Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi 


agree to stop "manipulating the population," it said. 


(In Tehran, sources close to religious leader Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeyni said that Imam 
as-Sadr's son had shown him documentary proof two weeks ago that his father was being 
held in Libya). 


People's Bureau Denial 
NCO91615 Paris AFP in English 1556 GMT 9 Sep 80 


[Text] Paris, 9 Sep (AFP)--A spokesman for the Libyan People's Bureau here today cate- 
gorically denied reportsthat the missing leader of the Lebanese Shi'ite Muslim community, 
Imam Musa as-Sadr, was imprisoned in a Libyan army camp near the Algerian border. 


The spokesman, who called the reports broadcast by Radio Luxembourg today "a biased 
invention devoid of any foundation," said that the religious leader was nowhere to be 
found in Libya. 


"Since his departure from Libya for Rome on August 28, 1978, Libya has had no further 
relation with Imam Musa as-Sadr," the spokesman said. 


Imam as-Sadr disappeared after visiting Libya in August 1978 for celebrations marking the 
anniversary of the Libyan revolution. Libya has consistently maintained that he left the 
country for Rome.

LD072004 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1820 GMT 7 Sen 80 


[Text] Tripoli, 7 Sep (JANA)--Within the celebrations of our Libyan Arab people of the 
llth anniversary of the great 1 September revolution, the general commander of the armed 
forces, brother Brigadier Abu Bakr Yunis Jabir, this mo-ning opened a plant for repairing 
tanks and light and medium troop carriers. The ceremony was attended by brother Lt Col 
Mustafa al-Kharrubi. 


This achievement is regarded as one of the industrial pillrs in the country which provide 
services for the armored crops in the Libyan Arab Armed Forces. The project contains units
