Summary (AI): A March 1982 State Department cable to NATO capitals and many embassies, drafted as talking points, announces U.S. measures against Libya: an import ban on Libyan crude, tighter export controls on U.S. goods and technology, and a request that U.S. citizens leave Libya. It describes Qadhafi as a dangerous threat to other states.
• The U.S. planned to prohibit imports of Libyan crude oil and to require validated licenses for exports of U.S.-origin goods and technology to Libya, with licences generally denied for national-security items and oil and gas technology.
• The cable says the U.S. imports about 500,000 barrels per day of Libyan crude (OCR text reads '...SS BARRELS PER DAY'; figure uncertain).
• The U.S. asked allies to close Libyan People's Bureaus and to press for U.S. citizens to leave Libya; it also mentions Italian exports of Sepalemarche/Spaghetti-type equipment (OCR uncertain) and about 20,000 Italians in Libya.
• The cable states the U.S. was protecting its nationals and requesting withdrawal of Libyan diplomats; it describes Libya's support for subversion and terrorism.
• 1982-03: U.S. plans during the week of March 8 to prohibit imports of Libyan crude oil under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act and to tighten export controls on U.S. goods and technology to Libya
