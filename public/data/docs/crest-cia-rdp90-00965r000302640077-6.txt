Summary (AI): A December 1981 New York Times article reports that President Reagan and his advisers were weighing punitive economic and political actions against Libya, while the Senate backed off a nonbinding resolution supporting an oil boycott. It notes concern that Americans in Libya could be taken hostage and that about 1,000 of 2,500 Americans had left since May.
• Reagan conferred with the National Security Council on Libya on Dec. 7 and Dec. 8, 1981.
• Senate Democrats sought a nonbinding resolution backing a boycott of Libyan oil but called it off after White House intervention.
• About 1,000 of the 2,500 Americans in Libya had left since May 1981, after an Administration request to return home voluntarily.
• US officials said the administration had shared intelligence on Libyan assassination plots with some allies.
