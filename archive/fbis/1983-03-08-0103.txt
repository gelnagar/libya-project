LIBYA

AL-QADHDHAFI UN MESSAGE RELEASED IN INDIA 


BK080510 Hong Kong AFP in English 0454 GMT 8 Mar 83 


[Text] New Delhi, March 8 (AFP) -- Libyan leader Colonel Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi 
threatened to pull his country out of the United Nations and create a new body of 
smaller nations in a message to U.N. chief Javier Perez de Cuellar published today. 


The flamboyant Libyan leader, known for his dramatic political gestures, scored the 
United Nations as a "failure" and its Security Council as an agent for "guarding U.S. 
and Israeli national security." 


"I shall call on my country to withdraw its membership from this organisation and I 
shall direct all my efforts to this end and incite all the smaller nations to do the 
same," said the message, published by the Libyan Embassy here in the NATIONAL HERALD. 


Col. al-Qadhdhafi said le will "spare no effort" to set up an alternative organization 
of smaller nations "unless the right of veto is cancelled or modified to include all 
U.S. members and until the U.N. acquires an efficient executive authority to control a 
Security Council based on a majority vote and not on the veto right." 


The message was released amid discussions at the Seventh Nonaligned Summit that opened 
yesterday on the role and operations of the United Nations. Mr Perez de Cuellar is 
present here but Col. al-Qadhdhafi is not. 


Blasting the United Nations for its failure to provide the Arab world protection against 
Israel, Col. al-Qadhdhafi said: "We the Arabs are more aware than any other nation of 
the failure of the U.N. to preserve peace and suppress flagrant aggression.

"Thus, as these reasons testify, there is every reason for finding an alternative to the 
existing U.N." 


But a U.N. spokesman here said that the message was the same as Mr. Perez de Cuellar 
received from tie Libyan last autumn. Since then, the spokesman said, Mr. Perez 

de Cuellar has met with Colonel al-Qadhdhafi in Tripoli and the threat of the withdrawal 
was "never followed by action," the spokesman said. He was unable to explain why the 
Libyan Embassy would release the letter here today. 


MANY NONALIGNED STATES NOT QUALIFIED AS MEMBERS 


LDO71756 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1330 GMT 7 Mar 83 
{Abu Bakir Harun commentary } 


[Excerpts] The Seventh Nonaligned Summit Conference -- regarded as the largest inter- 
national organization after the United Nations -- opened in New Delhi today. It will 
end on Friday. There seems to be a clear persistence by the peoples of the world to 
rally around this organization. They see it as the clearest (?way) to get them out of 
the world polarization whirlpool. 


Political affairs editor Abu Bakir Harun wrote this report on the subject: 


The Seventh Nonaligned Summit Conference was opened in New Delhi at 0400 today with the 
participation of more than 90 countries. Despite this enormous number of those describ- 
ing themselves as nonaligned, many of them have not been able to face up to the world's 
polarization, which has left no opportunities for them to assert their effective inde- 
pendence. However, peoples’ attachment to this movement and its objectives is indica- 
tive that there is definite desire by the nations and peoples to get rid of political, 
economic, and military guardianship. This movement has strived since an early stage to 
stress the independence of nonaligned countries, and laid down conditions describing 
neutral and nonaligned countries at the movement's June 1961 preparatory meeting. It 
stressed that a nonaligned country must follow an independent policy; support indepen- 
dence and national liberation movements in the world; must not be a member of a military 
pact or a party to a bilateral agreement with a great power; and must not allow a 
foreign country to establish military bases in its territories. This does not conform 
to reality; many members of the Nonaligned Movement do not quality as such on the basis 
of these conditions, and some of the founding member countries openly state that they 
have military bases of the great powers on their territories; we need not mention Egypt 
as an example of this, because it is too obvious to be mentioned. 


Observers expect that the periphery of the conference will witness the first chapter of 
the attempt to return Egypt to the Arab family, despite its commitment to the Camp 
David accords; this assumption is made on the basis of the latest statements by Presi- 
dent Mubarak before leaving Cairo for Delhi. The British DAILY TELEGRAPH reported 
recently that meetings in Delhi between Mubarak and a number of Arab leaders have been 
organized.
