Yugoslav Foreign Secretary Meets With Counterpart 
LD232014 Belgrade TANJUG in English 1616 GMT 23 Sep 83 


[Text] Tripoli, Sept 23 (TANJUG) — The all-round development 
of Yugoslav-Libyan relations, particularly in economic and 
scientific-technical cooperation, was the focus of the talks 
resumed here in Tripoli today, Friday, between Yugoslav Foreign 
Secretary Lazar Mojsov and Libyan Secretary of the Bureau for 
External Relations “Abd al-Ati al-'Ubaydi. An exhaustive 
exchange of views was made on the occasion on the situation in 
the Middle East and in Africa. The need was noted for investing 
additional efforts on the part of the nonaligned towards secking 
peaceful solutions and eliminating crisis spots in these world 
regions. Lazar Mojsov is on a brief working visit to Libya. 


Makes Statement, Departs 
LD231708 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1535 GMT 23 Sep 83 


[Text] Tripoli, 23 Sep (JANA) — SFRY Federal Secretary for 
Foreign Affairs Lazar Mojsov left Tripoli this afternoon after a 
visit to the Jamahiriyah, during which he conveyed to the brother 
leader of the revolution Yugoslavia’s point of view regarding U.S. 
imperialist intervention and the deterioration of the situation in 
the Middle East and Mediterranean regions. 


In a statement to JANA Lazar Mojsov expressed his deep 
satisfaction with the results of his visit to the Socialist People’s 
Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah. He alss lauded the prominent and 
efficient role played by the Jamahiriyah at various levels, and 
stressed the identity of views between the Jamahiriyah and 
Yugoslavia on a number of international issues. The Yugoslav 


federal secretary for foreign affairs expressed his deep concern, 
however, over the current situation in Lebanon, underlining the 
need for a solution without any foreign interference

Al-’Ubaydi Rejects Libyan Dialogue With Habre 
AB231306 Paris AFP in French 1154 GMT 23 Sep 83 


[By Marie Joannidis] 


[Text] Tripoli, 23 Sep (AFP) — Libya is ready to participate in 
all peace initiatives in Chad including the creation of a pan- 
African force, provided it is accepted by the Transitional 
National Union Government [GUNT] presided over by Gou- 
kouni Oueddei, Libyan Foreign Minister ‘Abd al-’ Ati al-'Ubaydi 
has declared to AFP. 


In an interview held in Tripoli on Thursday, Mr al-’Ubaydi 
reaffirmed the Libyan position, which is opposed to all direct 
dialogue with Ndjamena as Chadian President Hissein Habre 
has been demanding. Mr Habre does not recognize the represen- 
tatives of the GUNT, which is fighting against his force with the 
support of Libya. “The Chadian conflict is a civil war,” stressed 
Mr al-'Ubaydi. “Hissein Habre must have discussions with 
Goukouni and the GUNT. We have nothing to discuss with him.” 


At a press conference held in Tripoli on Wednesday, Goukouni 
Oueddei announced that he was ready to have direct negotiations 
with Mr Habre under the auspicies of the OAU; he did not make 
the withdrawal of French troops from Chad a precondition for 
the opening of negotiations. Mr al-’Ubaydi indicated that Libya's 
contacts with France “have never ceased at any time including 
when they nearly clashed.” 


“We have seen the two faces of France,” continued Mr al- 
"Ubaydi: “that of a country in search of peace in Chad and 
which respects the Africans and the OAU, and that of the France 
of the past which considers Africa and Chad as a former colony 
and a sphere of influence.” “We have chosen to believe in the 
peaceful intentions of France,” he added, “because we believe 
that it is in its interest to keep the continent outside the reach of 


- the superpowers and the Americans outside this African prob- 


lem.” The minister however affirmed that “Libya is on its guard 


against the strengthening of the French military position in Chad 
and in the neighboring countries,” and wondered whether the 
French are not trying to gain time to enable Hissein Habre to 
become familiar with the sophisticated equipment he has 
received and to thus threaten Libya’s security. 


It was learned from a reliable source that Paris also is asking the 
same question concerning the intentions of Libya, which still 
officially denies its presence in Chad. To this, Mr al-'Ubaydi 
without explicitly admitting the participation of Libyan troops in 
the fighting — replied: “We are not going to remain idle,since 
the conflict has been internationalized with the presence of 
Americans, Zairians, French, and even Israelis in Chad.” 


“We gave our support to Goukouni and the GUNT,” said Mr 
al-'Ubaydi, who added that any peace formula from a Lagos-type 
conference will be respected by Libya even if it calls for the 
withdrawal of “all foreign troops.” The Lagos conference 
resulted in the formation of the GUNT in 1979, bringing 
together 11 political and military factions in Chad including 
Hissein Habre’s FAN. “As such,” Mr al-'Ubaydi added, “we 
consider Hissein Habre as an opportunist who does not keep his 
word and today is blackmailing France. We feel he is an enemy 
of Libya and a danger for Africa who serves America’s interest.” 


Concerning the OAU initiative, Mr al-’Ubaydi explained that it 
first of all provides for the sending to Chad of observers of the 
demarcation line and the eventual deployment of a pan-African 
peacekeeping force during the negotiations. “France's participa- 
tion in the peace negotiations is explained by its military presence 
and by the fact that it could put political pressure on Hissein 
Habre for the negotiations to be initiated,’ he added. Concerning 
the forthcoming French-African summit in Vittel, Mr al- 
"Ubaydi only stated: “We are against this kind of meetings, 
which have nothing to do with the OAU.” 


Jabir Leaves for YAR With Message 25 Sep 
LD251521 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 
25 Sep 83 


[Text] Brigadier General Abu Bakr Yunis Jabir, commander in 
chief of the Libyan Arab Armed Forces, left Tripoli this morning 
for San‘a’ on a visit to the Yemen Arab Republic within the 
framework of bolstering and strengthening relations of cooper- 
ation between the brothers. Brigadier General Abu Bakr Yunis 
Jabir carries a message from the leader of the revolution to his 
Brother Colonel ‘Ali ‘Abdallah Salih, president of YAR. During 
this visit, he is to take part in celebrations to be held tomorrow 
on the occasion of the 21st anniversary of the revolution in North 
Yemen.
