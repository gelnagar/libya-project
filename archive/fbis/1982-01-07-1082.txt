LIBYA

AL-QADHDHAFI ADDRESSES GENERAL PEOPLE'S CONGRESS 


LD051920 Tripoli Voice of Arab Homeland in Arabic 1731 GMT 5 Jan 82 


[Speech by Libyan leader Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi at Green Hope Hall in Tripoli on the 
occasion of the final session of the General People's Congress -- live] 


[Excerpts] In the name of God. Brothers, I would like to express at this time strong hope 
in the fact that it has become possible for the people's authority to be established and for 
a people to rule itself without a government. Evidence of this fact is this meeting of 

the secretaries of the basic popular congresses from all parts of the Jamahiriyah, the 
secretaries of the people's committees which are everywhere and the secretaries of the 

[word indistinct] people's congresses. These people are meeting, not in the way 

parliaments meet, since there can be no deputization for the people, the parliament is 

but a sentence in absentia. But the resolutions of the popular basic congresses are 
formulated at this meeting; these congresses represent all the popular masses. 


There is strong hope that it is possible to establish a jamahiri system on earth. It 
seems that the beginning of the era of the masses has become a fact. It is rather 
strange that those who have laid down the cornerstone for the great era of the masses 
are but a small people who until recently were unknown. 


At this moment, while we are standing here at the General People's Congress, we want to 
strengthen our self-reassurance more than ever before because we are capable of establi- 
shing a people's authority and of founding a jamahiriyah. We feel that the dictatorship 
of government is over and that it should be finished for good. We feel that the 
restrictions which used to fetter the citizen psychologically and materially are being 
smashed as a result of the effectiveness of the people's authority. 


What I want to stress in this connection is the fact that the people are reassured 
psychologically and materially about their ability to run their own affairs without any 
ruler, without any imam, without any caliph because these examples I mentioned are on the 
list of the enemies of the masses. There is no democratic ruler and there is no democratic 
government at all. Government and democracy do not meet; a ruler and democracy do not 
meet. 


The slogan 'no democracy without popular congresses’ is not yet clear everywhere. Simi- 
larly, the slogans say that representation is charlatanism, that democracy is popular rule 
and not popular expression, and also attempts to undermine democracy and its popular alter- 
native, are not yet clear. [sentence as heard] But the masses do sense that they face 
enemies, enemies of democracy, and they are fighting them every day. 


This confirms the correctness of the historical strategic orientation followed by the 
Libyan people. Hence, the Libyan people have become the vanguard of peoples. I would also 
like in this respect to stress that the people must rely on themselves and they should not 
always expect somebody to come and deliver a speech in this place at the conclusion of 

the sessions of the General People's Congress. They also should not always expect 

someone to present draft laws and to submit them to the popular congresses or to work 

out an agenda for the popular congresses. These things must stop gradually, year after 
year. 


Actually, this year, you have made a big step forward. For the first time, the popular 
congresses have prepared the agenda.

We are striving, and I personally strive, to absent ourselves gradually so that the people 
can replace us. 


Two weeks ago, I went to a region inside the country. [ particularly inspected a strategic 
road now being built, linking southern and central Libya, and also the areas where opera- 
tions to produce artificial rain from clouds with the use of aircraft were conducted. 

We saw the results of producing artificial rain from clouds by using aircraft. 


During these 2 weeks, there were many rumors. I heard about them and we laughed about 
them a lot. I was in that region and it was reported that I was in Moscow. Well, Moscow. 
First, I cannot go there this winter because of its subzero temperatures [word indistinct]. 
[applause] 


Such rumors circulate when one doesn't speak on the radio. I deliberately avoided speaking 
on the radio. And all of us, those who lead the revolution, deliberately avoid speaking on 
the radio or in the papers. Let the popular masses speak on the radio. We want to elimi- 
nate individuals bent on substituting themselves for the will of the masses. 


Your resolutions so far still do not go deep enough, I mean the serious problems are still 
being tackled superficially. As for the coastal strip, its issue is still pending and this 
is one of the grave resolutions. The question of oil is also a grave question. If we are 
to decide to do away with oil and do away with the United States, this is a grave resolution. 
Or, if we declare that we are not prepared for this, this is also a grave resolution and 
the United States and anyone can understand this. The transformation of production 

means that even government employees work productively, but this is also a problem. 

Is the government employee working in agriculture or in a workshop or any other productive 
work to earn an income, is he producing in the administrative job or not? Do we consider 
him to be someone doing a public service, thus, the community ensures his living so that 

he does not have to think about something else to bring him an income because the community 
will provide the income heneeds? This you must define; it is not so easy. The trans- 
formation of production means that the armed forces -- until they disappear in the future 
when the armed people are established -- must also produce. Now the armed forces with 
their large ranks are consuming but not producing; they consume millions of dollars a 

month in salaries; tens of millions just to be prepared for war. War may not come. 
Preparing for war is a must, whether through the armed forces or the armed people. This 

is a big expenditure without a return. If there is a transformation of production, 

you must think about this. How can you be prepared for war and how can you make those 

who fight also work? Make everything available to them and bear this loss. These 

matters must be thoroughly discussed at the popular congresses. 


You discussed the question of rural militarization when allocating its budget. If you 
succeed in militarization, then you must comprehend it fully. If you succeed in 
militarization, you would solve the problem of compulsory military service, the problem 

of the regular army, the problem of production. Militarization simply means that 

everyone is trained in the use of arms continuously and is aware of the combat unit 

to which he belongs. And during the war, he would be a fighting soldier. If we attain 
this, then there is no need for compulsory service. There is a handful of cowardly Libyans 
abroad who are afraid of compulsory service. Libyan women are being trained in the use 

of arms; there are now large fighting formations of women.

Circumstances show that wars are not over or peace established in the world. On the 
contrary, the world considers that force alone decides everything. However, force grows 
and becomes greater with the establishment of the armed people. Today you fight with 
100,000 but tomorrow you fight with 1 million. Now you fight with 250,000 soldiers, 
tomorrow you will fight with 2 million members of the armed people. It is the armed people 
who cannot be defeated; there are no armed people who were defeated; but regular armies are 
defeated. We do not go back; we advance to confront all eventualities but in a manner that 
does not affect production. 


The other point about education which you have been discussing is that the students, 
particularly the revolutionary forces, the revolutionary committees in colleges, are strict 
about establishing higher studies at home and they are not sending students abroad. From 

the revolutionary point of view, students abroad, especially those who have been sent from 
Libya, are a target for all hostile forces -- imperialism, Zionism, reaction -- who want 

to recruit them. In actual fact, 30 out of 4,000 students -- there are 4,000 Libyan students 
studying in the United States -- 30 of these students were recruited by U.S. intelligence 

and now they receive salaries from and work for the United States. These students have 

been dropped from the 4,000. Actually, this is an unimportant figure. But 4,000 is a 

large number. 


What I am referring to is the point which (?had been discussed) at the popular conferences 
and the revolutionary committees about the question of education. If you are a country 
that does not possess large economic potentials with the exception of oil, which must not 
be relied on always, nothing can prevent you from receiving an education. We are prevented 
from producing sugar because we do not have water. We also cannot grow rice. We must import 
it because we have no water. We have conditions preventing you from doing this or that 
because you do not have the natural resources or that certain thing in your country. But 
the only thing that you can have and no one can prevent you from having or in which you 

can excel or receive the lion's share of is education. No one can stop us from becoming 

an educated nation because education has nothing to do with the things that are lacking 

in Libya. It has no connection with water, oil, location or climate. 


We must concentrate on education. In this connection, I am not at all afraid of higher or 
lower studies whether abroad or at home. At this point, we need to spread ourselves among 
all the states in the world and learn from them and benefit from them. I believe the 
opposite is true of the United States. The United States will impose a ban on you in the 
future, a ban against Libyan students studying in the United States [applause] because 

the presence of Libyan students in the United States is considered a danger and the 

United States fears them because it believes they [words indistinct]. And the other point 
is that Libyans learn and the United States does not want them to learn. 


It is possible that among the things it will impose on you in the future is that it will 
not accept students from our country. And those who go over and are recruited by U.S. 
intelligence are better exposed for what they really are [words indistinct]. Why are 
you afraid that they will recruit some people. There are some Libyans who, like stray 
dogs, stayed abroad and were recruited by U.S. intelligence. What effect can this have 
on Libya? The United States, the leading power in the world, is against Libya. If 1, 2, 
100 or even 1,000 Libyans joined the United States, what difference can this make? The 
NATO alliance which the United States is trying to align against Libya is against us. 

The United States is against us. [sentence indistinct] A Libyan enlisted by the United 
States -- so what? What can he do? [applause]

Therefore, we are not at all afraid if somebody studies in the United States or Europe 
or anywhere. On the contrary, we are the ones to influence the world. We have a very 
convincing theory, the theory of tomorrow, and we will spread it all over the world, if 
we scatter ourselves all over the world. Those from us who go, they do so as [word indistinct]. 


But this does not mean that we should not pay attention to education at home. No. If we 
rely only on education abroad, then education at home would be worthless. We must act at 
home as if there were no possibility of studying abroad. And at the same time, we must 
send [students] abroad as if there were no possibility of study at home. As for the 
emotional approach to the issue, saying that we should fear those who go abroad, well 

this is nonsense. Those who leave for good, well, the devil take them. A believer is one 
who is a nationalist whether at home or abroad. [applause] 


Another problem: Chad. You have decided to help Chad; some popular congresses have 
decided to help Chad economically; others decided against supporting Chad except under 
certain conditions. How can such a resolution be issued? We say either we have decided 
not to support Chad or have decided to support Chad unconditionally. Naturally, if Chad's 
policy is hostile to the Libyan people, we will not be able to support it. Naturally. 


The revolutionary committees will find themselves compelled to bring the people's 
committees or the secretariats of the popular congresses to trial before the masses if they 
find that these groups are harming the authority of the people. One of the basic tasks of 
the revolutionary committees is to exercise a revolutionary watch to underline the authority 
of the people. If a people's committee goes over the line and becomes a government which 
makes its own resolutions and does not keep in line with the resolutions of the people's 
congresses, if it neglects to implement the resolutions of the prople's congresses, if it 
ignores guidance and if it is established that the committee harbors ill intentions toward 
the authority of the people, then this committee will stand trial just as exploiters and 
[word indistinct] did in the past. This applies to anyone who turns against the people's 
authority, soldier or civilian alike. If anyone makes any attempt, through words or 


anything else, to harm the authority of the people, the revolutionary committees will 
immediately stand up to him. The revolutionary committees will tell the popular masses 
that their authority is threatened by this or that side. This must be clear and always 
practiced. This point is an internal one. Now for the external point. 


First, we greet the delegates of the Arab popular congresses abroad, who are present here 
and some of whose speeches I heard over the radio. [applause] At the same time, the 

General Secretariat of the General People's Congress must follow the activities of the 

Arab popular congresses abroad in order to become sure of their effectiveness. As from 

now, no popular congress will be relied upon unless it has a revolutionary committee. 
Revolutionary committees must be set up in the Arab popular congresses abroad -- in 
Australia, in Latin America, in Europe, in the Arab homeland and everywhere. The 
revolutionary committee is to be relied upon in this stage in reaching the complete 

jamahiri system. The revolutionary committee must incite the masses to exercise authority, 
to take over authority and to exercise authority after taking it. Therefore, we must make 
sure that true revolutionary committees have been set up, which comprise true revolutionaries. 
You have 10,000 citizens in Australia, for instance, who form a popular congress; if 5 or 10 
of these citizens form a revolutionary committee -- not necessarily 100 or 1,000 -- 5 or 10 
true revolutionaries are enough. 


We must make sure that there are revolutionary committees in the popular committees. The 
General Secretariat of the General People's Congress must make sure that the existence of 
the popular congress abroad is true and effective, that there is membership, that the 
masses are the members in the congresses and that there are resolutions.

Therefore, we call for the formation of Arab people's congresses through revolutionary 
committees, covertly and overtly, in the Arab world. We must accept their membership 
in the General People's Congress. [applause] 


As from today, the invitation is open to all the masses of the Arab nation anywhere, 
inside and outside the Arab homeland, to form people's congresses covertly and overtly 
according to their circumstances and to join the General People's Congress for the Arab 
people. The Arab people's congresses, covertly or overtly, inside and outside the 

Arab homeland will then have the right to have a membership in this secretariat -- the 
secretariat of the Arab people's congress in Libya. This secretariat will then become 
a secretariat of pan-Arab congress. This would mobilize the Arab masses on the 

road to unity. Perhaps the domination of the rulers over the masses would end peace- 
fully. This could be attained at places where it would be possible to form congresses 
overtly. Where the masses cannot form overt congresses, they would form covert con- 
gresses; but one must preserve the existence of the revolutionary committee as the driving 
force for every people's congress abroad. 


This is a revolutionary call we are now making. Let us strive for the realization of 
Arab unity of the masses. In this connection, I refer to the resolution adopted by the 
Libyan people's congresses and the recommendation for merging the Syrian Council of 
Ministers and the General People's Committee in Libya into a single executive body; 

as well as the General People's Congress and the people's assembly, revolutionary leader- 
ship and military leadership. In fact, this is a very large step on the road of Arab 
unity, particularly between Libya and Syria. 


I sometime ago discussed this matter with Brother President al-Asad. There are no 
objections or problems in implementing this step. It seems that this is the correct 
beginning for implementing the jamahiriyah unity. 


The Syrian Council of Ministers and the Libyan General Popular Committee should meet 
together a number of times and (?form a single body) as if they are one council of 
ministers or one general popular committee and take decisions, And then we will see 

how these decisions are applied in Libya and Syria. The important thing is that the 
decision should be a united one. Afterward, each side will go back to his own country 

and implement the decision. It does not make a lot of difference that in Libya there 

is a popular committee and there is a Council of Ministers in Syria but what is important 
is that the decision which the popular committee implements in Libya and the decision 
which the Council of Ministers implements in Syria is the same decision, unity of decision 
making is the important thing. 


If the General People's Congress and the Syrian People's Assembly -- the Syrian People's 
Assembly is a parliament and its power is like the power of the popular congresses in 
Libya. They issue laws and approve policy and they supervise the Council of Ministers. 
This system concerns domestic Syria. It does not affect the move toward unity at all. 

In Libya power is in the hands of the popular congresses. If the Syrian People's 
Assembly and the Libyan General People's Congress meet together at one session and form 

a national congress, there is no contradiction if the Libyan side brings forth prepared 
decisions already made by the popular committees and the Syrian side has approval to 
make decisions at the same time. The important thing is that common decisions are issued 
by this national congress to be implemented by the executive. There is no contradiction. 
In this way we can begin to implement unity. 


Two Arab countries, geographically far apart but close to each other in other aspects -- 
how would they undertake the experience of uniting. Experiences, unity experiences, 
training on unity. For the moment, Syria is Syria, and Libya is Libya, but the regions 
to which they belong can get together and form a unified state.

It is not necessary for Syria to form a general people's congress that will bring together 
the resolutions of popular congresses. Unity between Syria and Libya is possible without 
the need for the National Assembly in Syria being abrogated to become a people's congress, 
without legislative power. The point is when we meet, we make the same decision. Can we 
or can't we make the same decision? If we can make the same decision, regardless of the 
structure of the Syrian and Libyan sides, then unity is realized. If we can make one 
common decision, names do not matter. 


Unity of Arab ranks, yes, [words indistinct] but what use is there if we stand together on 
the same line and an American comes to [words indistinct]. There must be unity of Arab 
action against the common enemy or nothing. 


With regard to the summit meetings, there is an invitation on the part of the Arab League 
for an emergency summit meeting. This is really a deception, a summit meeting is not 

the solution. This summit meeting is not rejected but it does not constitute the solution. 
A solution means bringing a real change in the positions of pro-American Arab states because 
the friends of the United States are the friends of Israel. So long as this does not 

happen our position on the summit must remain as it is. [Word indistinct] the summit. 

Well, if I [word indistinct] with you, an American agent, recognizing Israel, while I am 
against the enemy; and would the problem be solved if I meet you at a summit. The issue

positions so that they will undertake joint action at the summit. 


Every pro-American Arab government must make real changes in its pro-American stance so 
that Arab policies are no longer contradictory. Then when the Arabs meet at the summit, 
they can carry out joint action. We are looking for joint Arab action. 


I was very stunned on hearing that the Saudis recognized [sentence unfinished] Really it

As-Sadat said (?nothing more) but As-Sadat was a traitor and he was executed because he

(?There) can be nothing hostile or alien to the Arab nation. You have seen the result when

We Arabs feel that the presence of the Israeli nuclear reactor in Dimona conflicts with

We are prepared to shoulder our responsibility in this, [applause] because the Israeli

The Israeli entity has now proven its true nature to the world; we do not need any other

This is an armed entity, armed in a dangerous manner; Israel is now arming itself with atomic

We and the Israelis do not exchange diplomatic representation because we do not recognize
