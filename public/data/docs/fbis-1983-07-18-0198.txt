LIBYA

Al-Qadhdhafi Greets Mitterrand on Bastille Day 
LD160743 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 0700 GMT 16 Jul 83 


[Text] Tripoli, 16 Jul (JANA) The brother leader of the 
revolution has sent a cable to the president of the Republic of 
France. Following is the text of the cable: 


To the president of the Republic of France 


As one of the lovers of freedom for the masses, among the most 
hostile to the forces of injustice and exploitation, and one of those 
who are leading the popular revolution to destroy the remaining 
Bastille forts of the world, | have the pleasure to congratulate 
you on the 194th anniversary of the French revolution and the 
fall of the mighty Bastille Prison. On this memorable day | do 
hope that France recalls the revolution and the cause of freedom 
‘o realize that there are peoples in Palestine and Africa whose 
natural right is revolution for freedom. 


May grace be upon he who follows the right path. 


[Signed] Colonel Mu’ammar al-Qadhdhafi 


Al-Qadhdhafi Criticizes France in Interview 
PA1l70405 Paris AFP in English 0346 GMT 17 Jul 83 


[Text] Paris, July 17 (AFP) — France has no right to intervene 
or to send arms to Chad, “nor even to speak of its problems,” 
Libyan leader Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi said in an interview in 
the latest issue of Le Point magazine here. 


Responding to a warning from President Francois Mitterrand 
about any Libyan involvement in the latest fighting, Colonel 
al-Qadhdhafi retorted that Mr Mitterrand had no right to speak 
that way because France “has the worst colonial past in Africa. 
It is the cause of all the problems on our continent.” 


He said the French Government's interpretation of its military 
cooperation agreements with Chad “is hoodwinking public opin- 
ion because any accord between France and an African country 
can only concern an external threat and not an internal conflict.” 


In that context, Col. al-Qadhdhafi argued, current Chadian 
leader Hissein Habre “is only a rebel, a defence minister fighting 
against his head of state.” (Mr Habre was for a period defence 
minister in the transitional government of Goukouni Oueddei, 
the president he ousted last year and who is now attempting to 
regain power) 


Col. al-Qadhdhafi denied supplying the Goukouni side with 
advanced Soviet weaponry for the latest fighting, though he 
conceded that arms had been provided when Mr Goukouni was 
in power in Ndjamena. When Libyan troops withdrew from 
Chad in November 1981, they had left some arms at the Chad 
Government's disposal, the colonel added.
