Summary (AI): A 1981 New York Times article reports that the CIA acknowledged some former employees, without its knowledge, supported Edwin P. Wilson's dealings with Libyan leader Muammar el-Qaddafi, including explosive timers, a terrorist training school in Libya, and an alleged assassination plot. No Tripoli-specific sites are named.
• CIA said two employees were forced to resign and that there was no official CIA encouragement or involvement.
• Former Special Forces men were reportedly recruited to train terrorists in Libya about four years earlier, with pay of about $4,500 a month plus a bonus.
• Wilson was reportedly actually in the operation, according to the New York Times.
• 1980-04-25: Federal grand jury in New York indicted Edwin P. Wilson and Frank Terpil on charges of supplying Qaddafi with explosive timers, setting up a terrorist training school in Libya, and conspiring to assassinate the Libyan leader
