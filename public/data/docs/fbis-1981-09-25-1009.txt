LIBYA

USSR MISSILE SITES DISCLOSED IN RECENT UK STUDY 


PM241427 London THE DAILY TELEGRAPH in English 24 Sep 81 p 4 
[Air Comdr G.S. Cooper report: "Soviet Missile Sites Reported Inside Libya"] 


[Excerpt] Libya has a dozen Russian Scaleboard SS-12 surface-to-surface missiles capable 
of reaching Athens, Cairo and targets in southern Turkey, it was disclosed in a report 
published yesterday. 


The Libyan Air Force, which was shown last year as being similar in strength to Egypt's 
force of 290 combat aircraft, is now credited with 408 Russian and French planes. The 
nuclear warheads of Scaleboard SS-12 missiles deployed in Russia are reported to be 

in the megaton range, suggesting greater destructive power than NATO's American Pershings. 


Disclosure of the Russian missiles on Libyan soil is made by the International Institute 
for Strategic Studies in its report "The Military Balance 1981-82." 


An independent source puts the number of Libyan combat aircraft much higher and reports 
seeing the newly-delivered Russian missiles installed in bunkers cut into the hills 
outside Tobruk. Russians, estimated to number at least 10,000 are said to be "thick 

on the ground," in and around Tobruk and Benghazi. 


Many other missile sites have been built on the Egyptian and Chad borders. These 

are equipped with 118 Soviet Frog and Scud battlefield support missiles capable of 
firing nonnuclear warheads at a range of up to 150 miles. Recently the Libyan leader 
Col al-Qadhdhafi threatened to attack nuclear bases in Western Europe and Mediterranean 
islands if Libya was attacked. 


Italy is planning to base 121 cruise missiles at Comiso in Sicily and the Italian 
foreign Ministry was reported as saying it was "greatly surprised" by Col al-Qadhdhafi's 
threat to retaliate against the "nuclear arsenals" in Italy, Greece and Turkey if inci- 
dents similar to the shooting down of two Libyan fighter aircraft by the United States 
Navy occured again. 

Sicily and the southern tip of Italy would fall well within the range of Scaleboard 
SS-12 missiles if they were to be deployed in the Tripoli area. Attacks could also 


be mounted by Libya's ground attack aircraft.
