[Text] Maj ‘Abd-al-Salam Jallud, accompanied by a Libyan delegation, 
spent 2 weeks in Iran to disciss with Iranian officials the nature of 
Iranian-Libyan relations which still appear to be troubled. It seems that 
this visit has produced satisfactory results for both parties. A Libyan 
official has informed AL-NAHAR AL-‘ARABI WA AL-DUWALI that diplomatic 
relations between the two countries will be resumed soon an? that only a 
few formal arrangements remained to be made. 


Informed sources have revealed that Col Mu'ammar 2i-Qadhdhafi was the first 
Arab official who took action to support Khomeyni's movement at its inception. 
He thus anticipated most Arab rulers in recognizing the seriousness of 

the movement when Khomeyni was in exile in Saghdad and later when he 
settled in Paris to lead his revolv’ion. At that time Arab circles were 
still casting doubts about Khomeyni's movement and about his character. 
Al-Qadhdhafi was misled by communications from Arab circles calling upon 
him to bide his time and to be cautious, and it seems that the colonel 
complied with t ese appeals. He waited for a long time until what happened 
did happen. These sources go on to add that when everything became -lear, 
the colonel realized that the ultimate purpose behind these communications 
was to keep him away from the Iranian Revolution. 


In another area, during the celebrations last September of the ninth 
anniversary of the revolution of the Libyan al-Jamahiriyah Col al-Qadhdhafi 
announced that he would not assume any executive or administrative function, 
but that he would return to his natural and true place, namely, the 
revolution and not government. The colonel gave up his position as president 
of the al-Jamahirifyah. In spite of the fact that he has stepped aside, 

Col al-Qadhdhafi remains Libya's chief figure because he himself represents 
the source of power. This situation might lead us to ask what is the nature 
of power in Libya today? 











Libyan democracy rejects [the principle of] representation and does not 

depend on a parliament. Instead, it depends on revolutionary committees 
whose representatives are elected by the people in stages. It also depends 
on popular congresses and on state secretariats--ministries. As far as 

the economic aspect is concerned, Libya's economic policy depends on the 
socialist philosophy that Col al-Qadhdhafi outlined in his book, "The Economic 
Problem" and in his speeches. This socialism does not call for an end of 
individual ownership, but ic rather calls for making all individuals owners. 
It does, however, “disapprove of discrepancies in individuals’ wealth in 

the new socialist society.” 


The Free Officers 


A few days ago the historic first Free Officers Congress was convened. It 

was attended by groups of Free Officers from different parts of al-Jamahiriyah. 
The congress continued for two consecutive nights during which internal 
conditions were reviewed. Conditions in the Arab region which is going 
through a crucial stage under current circumstances were also reviewed. 

In addition to al-Qadhdhafi, members of the Revolutionary Council, Abu Bakr 
Yunis, ‘Abd-al-Salam Jallud, al-Khuwaylidi and Kharrubi attended the congress. 


The congress resulted in the removal of five members of the Libyan Revolu- 
tionary Council. They are ‘Abd-al-Mun'im al-Huni, Muhammad Najan, Mjkhtar 
al-Qarawi and Bashir Hawadi. Those four were removed for different reasons. 
In addition, ‘Umar al-muhayshi was also removed. He had attempted a coup 
against al-Qadhdhafi in 1976 and after his attempt failed had escaped first 
to Tunisia and then to Egypt where he still resides. 


Elsewhere, the congress adopted secret political resolutions as well as 
other resolutions that were referred to as “congress recommendations.” 

The most significant of these resolutions is the fact that Free Officers 
were to be considered part of the Arab Revolutionary Movement and that they 
were to become members of the revolutionary committees whose formation in 
Libya has been recently determined. 


The congress called upon the Free Officers to assume their “historic 
responsibilities.” The congress asked them to place their military experience 
at the service of students in schools and universities and to train those 
students to fight. Al-Qadhdhafi also told the Council of Free Officers that 
some “opportunists" had infiltrated the revolutionary committees and that 

the Libyan Revolution had to remove them and to call them to account. 


With regard to the economic problem, some observers are saying that al- 
Qadhdhafi will have to face the problem of those who have enriched themselves 
during the administration of the Libyan Revolution sooner or later. Those 
people are condemned in the second part of The Green Book where al-Qadhdhafi 
states, "No one has the right to engage in economic activity for the purpose 
of possessing wealth in excess of what will satisfy his needs. Such 

conduct is the essence of exploitation."

These two problems are interconnected; opportunists in politics are 
opportunists in economics. It is for this reason that al-Qadhdhafi finds 
himself compelled to adopt a series of administritive changes and 
modifications that now constitute a broad experiment in government. It 

is an experiment with a system [of government] in the full sense of the 
word. In other words, al-Qadhdhafi is trying to find a better system 

that would achieve his views by involving all the people in a manner that 
would protect his philosophy and the Libyan people from those that Col 
al-Qadhdhafi call opportunists. During the Libyan-Egyptian confruntation 
those opportunists donned a political garb and tried to overthrow the 
regime in Libya. Hence, al-Qadhdhafi has a permanent need for power to 
protect the government and to protect the regime. In other words, he reeds 
power to protect the government from its enemies abroad. The Free Officers 
Council might just be the best body to ensure this protection.

[Article by Qasi Salih al-Darwish: "The Libyan Revolution Between Reality 
and Media Distortion: Two Dangers Threaten the Revolution: Reactionaries 
Inside the Country and Hostile Forces Abroad; Al-Qadhdhafi Affirms the 
Necessity of Arming All the People and of Continuing To Establish a Strong 
and Modern Military Arsenal"] 


[Text] As soon as a visitor arrives in Tripoli, he finds himself puzzled 
by the obvious differences between the picture of al-Jamahiriyah that is 
shown abroad by the western media and that which is actually taking place 
on the Libyan scene. No matter what the position and the relationship of 
this observer might be to al-Jamahiriyah, if he is seeking objectivity, he 
can only acknowledge that what is taking place in Libya is a new experiment 
that is unique in the history of political action. It cannot be compared 
with other experiments in the world, and especially in the Third World. 

New terms and slogans are being coined in the political literature by 

tying the rules of etymology to the particular nature of the experiment and 
to the substance of the steps that Libya is going through. These new terms 
begin with the expression, “Al-Jamahiriyah" and include "The age of the 
masses;" "Committees in every locality;" "Representation is deception;" 

"Up with people's committees!" "The revolution is to be separated from 
authority;" "The [workers'] takeover;" "The masses are to take over authority.” 


At first it may seem difficult for the visitor or for the passing observer 

to conceive of the practical and the special significance of these terms 

and slogans. But when one approaches the Libyan situation and the new 
factors that enter into it daily, one does begin to see clearly the total 
significances and implications of the particular nature of the al-Jamahiriyah 
experiment in Libya. 


In a recent visit that Col al-Qadhdhafi had made to Algeria while the late 
President Houari Boumedienne was still alive, che Libyan president met with 
the members of the Algerian National Assembly and the members of the Central 
Organization of the National Liberation Front Party. He engaged them in a 
dialogue which he initiated by criticizing the notion of parliamentary or

party representation. He then explained the first part of “The Green Book” 
which deals with the question of solving the problem of democracy by means 
of congresses and popular committees. Some Algerian repcesentatives 
responded sharply to al-Qadhdhafi, telling him that nothing had changed 

in the structure of government in Libya except the names. The minister 

had turned into a secretary; the president had turned into a secretary 
general; and the director had turned into a chairman of a popular committee. 
They told him that those who were in power were in fact the same people. 

The colonel responded to these charges with a calm smile, and he explained 
that the notion of the masses seizing power was not only related to the will 
of the political leadership, but that it was also related to the initiatives 
of the populace and to the degree of growth and development their conscience 
had achieved in the direction of steering themselve: towards power. 
Al-Qadhdhafi added that he would welcome the masses’ march to the presidency 
and their direct participation in government. 





Exclusive Preoccupation with the Revolutionary Command 


About 3 months ago Col al-Qadhdhafi gave up the position of secretary genera. 
of the Central People’s Congress so he can devote himself to the leadership 
of the revolution. This action was taken under the slogan of “the revolution 
is to be separated from government.” Throughout this period political 
activity in Libya whose purpose was to put more than one slogan into 

practice was taking place in more than one sphere. At first, the popular 
committees were to be strengthened so they can avoid the errors that had 
taken place when the means of production were seized and managed autonomously 
by these popular committees. Reliance on bureaucratic action by these 
popular committees was to be avoided because such actions would create 
interests and privileges for new social groups and segments [of the popula- 
tion]. Al-Qadhchafi had taken special interest in the p~-cess of strengthening 
these committees, and he had interfered personally on mo.e than one occasion 
and in more than one location to straighten out matters after they had gcue 
out of control by the zeal of the masses. He did explain one more time 

the objective behind the popular committees. For example, a dairy plant 

had been seized and its workers had formed a popular committee that dis- 
tributed [the proceeds] from the plant's sales directly and equaliy to the 
workers without taking into consideration the need to allocate a portion 

of these funds for maintenance and for emergencies, another portion for 
primary materials, and a third for plant development and fr opening new 
sections in the plant. As soon as al-Qadhdhafi heard about his, he went 

to the plant to conduct a lengthy dialogue with those workers. And in the 
same context [i.e., through the popular committee] some of the goods that 

had been seized were returned without becoming the means for the direct 
exploitation of the people. Last week the colonel sharply criticized the 
popular committees in Libyan airports, and especially in Tripoli International 
Airport. These committees had failed to manage these vital facilities 
because of personal seusitivities. Col al-Qadhdhafi threatened that if 

these committees were not to rise to the level of responsibility and become 
aware of the responsibility they are required to assume to fulfill this 
function, he would dissolve these committees and would appoint in their 

place a temporary director.

The second sphere of political activity in Libya is related to assembling, 
organizing and unifying the revolutionary forces amongst the soldiers, the 
workers and the students by means of revolutionary committees which protect 
the revolution and the power of the masses. One of the most prominent 
events that took place in this sphere is the convening of the first Free 
Officers Congress and the revival of this old relationship that dates back 
to 1969 when the roval regime was overthrown and a republic was proclaimed 
on the first of September. This congress was characterized by an address 
delivered by the colonel in which he affirmed that organizing the Free 
Officers into revolutionary cells would move the army forward and would 
ultimately turn the army into a traditional aruy which would then become 

a popular army shat would in turn participate in the process of arming the 
people. Al-Qadhdhafi identified three groups of Free Officers: the first 
group is that which got together and contributed to the outbreak of the 
revolution of the first of September. The second is that which did not take 
part in the outbreak of the revolution, but did join its ranks later. The 
third group is that which joined the armed forces after the revolution. 
There is no doubt that reorganizing the cells of the Free Officers is tantamount 
to extending the revolutionary committees outside the armed forces. Alto- 
gether these revolutionary committees constitute a revolutionary structure 
that parallels that of the government. It is thus that the process of 
separating the revolution from the government is completed. It is on this 
basis that Col al-Qadhdhafi became the commander of the revolution 

instead of the secretary general of the General People's Congress. And it 
is on this basis that his colleagues whom he mentioned in his speech, and 
especially Lt Col Abu Bakr Yunis and Maj ‘Abd-al-Salam Jallud became members 
of the revolutionary council for which a specific name has not yet been 
announced. 


At present, the third sphere of political activity in Libya is that of 
arming the people. It is directly tied to the second sphere and constitutes 
with it one of Col al-Qadhdhafi's principal concerns. This activity may 

be exemplified and identified by protecting the revolution from local 
reactionaries who seem to be waiting for more opportunities to harm the 
revolution. If uniting and organizing the revolutionary forces would 
constitute a front that would oppose these reactionary forces, arming 

the people is tantamount to the tool for such opposition. In this area, 
one must call attention to the recent development of situations and the 
concurrent accomplishments which had adverse affects on the interests of 
whole classes of society and expanded the front of the revolution's enemies 
inside the country. It is known that these classes [which are adversely 
affected] do not have deep roots in society and have not lived in it for 
over 10 years. In other words, these classes are as old as the revolution. 
They grew and developed under the wing of national control of the means 

of production, the expulsion of Italian colonists and the encouragement of 
national enterprise. Many workers and employees assumed full ownership 

of plants and establishments after they were nationalized or seized from 
the colonists. They thus became wealthy in a very short period of time. 
There are other groups that benefited from the easy terms that were offered

wealthy class. [his fact can be confirmed when we learn that there were 
only five millionaires in Libya before the revolution, but that there _re

when the means of production were seized, and when the slogan, “The house 
belongs to its tenant” was implemented. Some used to own 126 homes. 
Othe.s owned 200 apartments, etc. In spite of the compensations that were

To these two groups of society another segment that was hurt by the [revolu- 
tion's) accomplishments may be added. Although this group has not yet 
proclaimed its opposition to the revolution, it has, accordingly, not taken 
a positive position on the revolution’s recent accomplishments. This group 
may be found in the bureaucratic administrative organization whose influence 
has come to an end--or so it is assumed--with the establishvent of the 
popular committees. 


In the opposite direction, just as the front of the revolution's enemies 

has grown, so did that of its forces grow as the revolution's accomplishments 
grew and deepened. These forces may be found in the wo king class who 
prospered after “the [people's] encroachment" when they began to experience 
the autonomous management of organization. These forces may «lso be found 
among the students who in turn seized the university and in April 1976 

rid it of rightist elements. It is known that there were numerous at.eapts 
to force young people and students to oppose the regime, especially after 
the decision for military training and mandatory military service was 
proclaimed. The forces of the revolution may also be fcund in the armed 
forces which have experienced a new dynamism in the attempt to demolish 
their classical system and build a new one in its place that would be 
supplied with the most modern weapons. In addition, there are ihe Bedouins 
who enjoyed the revolution's generosity. They are loyal to Col al-Qadhdhafi 
himself who, they believe, represents many of their interests and their 
values. Most of those Bedouins are armed. 


The truth is that protecting the revolution is a two-sided operation. The 
internal aspect involves fighting local reactionaries. This is being 
achieved now by the revolutionary committees and by the threat of executing 
anyone who contemplates betraying the revolution and the homeland. This 
threat--which was later endorsed by the Congress of the free Unionist 
Officers--was stated by the colonel in the speech he made at the congress. 
He spoke firmly and sharply, "I pardoned some of those who had betrayed us 
and received a death sentence from the courts. I think this was an error 
on my part because treason is uniorgivable. [fhere must be a price for

The other aspect of protecting the revolution [takes place outside the 
country] and may be found in the permanent threat to the revolution from 
al Sadat's regime. This threat may come as a direct attack, and it may 
also come as an indirect attack through Sudan, Chad or Tunisia. Some clamor 
has been noticed in recent days around Libya and its neighbors. The first 
such outcry was raised by the Tunisian media after the football game that 
took place between [the teams] of the two countries in Tripoli. The game 
was one of the playoffs for the Moscow Olympics. Libya won the game and 
the score was Libya, 3 and Tunisia, 0. Tunisian newspapers accused Libyan 
authorities of mistreating the Tunisian team while its members were going 
through customs and while they were practicing. The Libyan government did 
not wish to prolong the argument about this case so the colonel ordered an 
investigation into this matter. He affirmed that if these actions alleged 
by the Tunisian papers were true, their perpetrators would be considered 
enemies of the revolution and of unity. 


Foreign observers have noticed that the sharp tone of the Tunisian news- 
papers, including the official ones, could not be attributed only to the 
football game during which nothing negative had been obsersed. Those observers 
were repeating the Lebanese proverb [which signifies] that it was not that 
incident, but rather an accumulation of small irritants [that had unleashed 
this hostility]. The fact is that the campaign by the Tunisian newspapers 
began a few days before the game. It started with criticism of al-Jamahiriyah's 
position on the question of Uganda. The Tunisian press as well as the 

Egyptian and the Sudanese press were the only ones that were critical of 
al-Jamahiriyah. Diplomatic circles in Tripoli do not find it unlikely 

that Tunisia would reopen the continental shelf issve and that this could 
become the means for an Egyptian intervention in al-Jamahiriyah. 


The Other Motive 


After a few days of this affected tumult between Tunisia and Tripoli, European 
newspapers raised the question of Chad in an inflammatory and provocative 
fashion, accusing Libya of a military intervention in Chad. The fact of 
the matter is that a dispute had arisen among the ranks of the Frolinat 
Front between Gou Kouni Oueddei chairman of the front and Acye, one of 

its leaders. This question foiled the Chad al-Salhah Conference in Canu 
although Libya had backed the Frolinat Front and provided it with financial 
support during the past years. It seems that France and Sudan wanted to 
cut Gou Kouni short so that he could not be proclaimed president of Chad 
unless he severed his ties with Libya. Regarding this question the Foreign 
Affairs Secretariat denied that the Libyan army had intervened in Chad. 


These indicators affirm the magnitude of the danger which al-Jamahirtyah is 
exposed to from enemies who are waiting for opportunities to undermine its 
revolution. This is perhaps the reason why, on the one hand, Col al-Qadhdhafi 
is putting emphasis on arming the people and continuing to create a strong 

and a modern arsenal of weapons on the other hand.
