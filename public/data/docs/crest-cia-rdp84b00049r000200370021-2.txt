FROM : National Intelligence Officer for Near East-South Asia 

SUBJECT : February 4 NSC Meeting on Libya 

1. Attached is the discussion paper on Libya for the NSC meeting of 
4 February. As indicated in previous memorandum, it includes the following: 

a. An essentially accurate description of the current state 
of play of Libyan subversion and support for terrorism, 
concluding that it has not diminished since December. 

b. A recommendation for the revalidation of contingency 
military planning, focusing on the assassination threat. 

c. A recommendation to proceed with economic measures without 
awaiting further provocation. Specifically: 

- An embargo of imports of Libyan oil. 

- An embargo of exports to Libya, whether total or 
selective. 

2. The discussion accurately states that the economic measures will 
not have a serious impact in and of themselves, and would be symbolic in 
impact only. The proposal is framed in a manner which would minimize the 
need for participation by our allies, although problems remain with whether 
or not extraterritorial application of the embargo should be attempted.

3. The question remains open on whether or not a legal basis for 
implementation of the International Economic Emergency Powers Act (IEEPA) 
exists. A case is made in the paper that use of IEEPA is feasible, and 
states that a draft Executive Order to that effect has been prepared. We 
have supplied Treasury with a set of documents on Libyan activity which they 
will utilize to make a determination if invocation of these powers does have 
a legal basis or not. Presumably, their viewpoint will be aired at the NSC 
meeting. 

4. A check sheet is included in the paper on pages 20 and 21, which 
lists the options and requests an approval or disapproval. 

5. I believe the pluses and minuses of the imposition of these 
economic sanctions without further clear Libyan provocation have been fully 
outlined in memoranda during the past several days. My personal recommenda- 
tion remains that the imposition of measures without serious impact, especially 
at a time when we are engaged in similar actions regarding Poland and the 
Soviet Union, gains us little. As you are aware, the DDI, C/NE/DDO, and 
D/NESA/DDI feel a petroleum boycott is warranted as stated in the RepertNSC pape. 

Za, 
Charles E. Waterman
