LIBYA

JANA: U.S. TO ASK AS-SADAT TO FULFILL PROMISES TO ISRAEL 
LD021512Y Tripoli JANA in English 1426 GMT 2 Feb 78 LD 


[Text] Tripoli, 2 Feb, JANA--President as-Sadat has been called to Washington by 
President Carter on Friday to be told to fulfill his secret promises to Begin and 
Israel, Arab affairs editor of the JAMAHIRIYAH NEWS AGENCY stated yesterday in a 
commentary. 


He said: "As-Sadat's reluctance to fulfill them was due to the pressure being exerted 
upon him by his armed forces officers and resentment among the people. 


"As-Sadat will undoubtedly be asked to fulfill his promises and make more concessions 
to Israel during his stay in Washington, »but whatever hopes he has would never see the 
light of day and he will soon find out that his hopes clashed with America's firm 
stand and persistent support to Israel." 


The JANA editor referred to the latest drama by As-Sadat when he stopped the Jerusalem 
taiks and summoned a meeting of the Egyptian parliament, suggesting that he would t ike 
a firm stand by resigning if necessary. He stated this when mock rallies were already 
being staged not to let him resign. 


"Is this an attempt on the part of As-Sadat to prove to the United States and Isra:l 
that he was still powerful and enjoyed the support of his people and that he was ready 
to announce his resignation before Americans abandoned him like many of their age its 
in the past?" The editor asked. 


The editor continued: The United States mist be knowing more about him (As-Sadat) 
than he knows himself. His continuous proposals to become the security agent of the 
U.S. in the Middle East and Africa would not have gone unnoticed hy the Americans. 

The United States would not risk giving him arms for fear of tilting the balance of 
power which is presently in favor of Israel, especially after recent Israeli suspicion 
about As-Sadat's intentions and its reaction to As-Sadat's plan to start a new war. 


Therefore As-Sadat would return from his U.S. visit emptyhanded exactly like his 
Jerusalem sojourn. He would only have received more orders to carry out. And no 
matter how far he goes to grant more concessions at the expense of rights of his 
people and those of the Arab nation, his efforts will remain in vain. 


The editor stressed that whatever concessions As-Sadat had made or was likely to make 
in future would only increase Israeli intransigence and desire to humiliate As-Sadat 
and his people. "Had it not been for his capitulation and his shaking hands with the 
Israelis at Ben Gurion Airport, the Israeli radio would not have dared to call him 
stupid and ill-mannered," the editor concluded.
