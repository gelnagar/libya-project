Summary (AI): A declassified memo (catalogued 2011, apparently from the early-1980s sanctions period) sets out US options for enforcing a January 7 decision to sever economic ties with Libya, including how to handle US oil concessionaires with a February 1 deadline. It concerns US economic and foreign policy toward Libya, not Tripoli sites or repression.
• Options presented: (1) order companies to sever ties immediately; (2) give companies an extension to arrange termination with Libya; (3) allow transfer of operations to foreign subsidiaries.
• Libya's payments due to be made in January total about $178 million owed by oil companies, per the document's OCR.
• Estimated windfall to Libya from transfer of concessions ranged from about $700 million to roughly $1-2 billion, the document says, with speculative estimates.
• Libya's national airline needed Boeing spare parts, which France and other West European states had been willing to supply; the memo suggests threatening Boeing parts sales or US landings.
• Libyan government assets frozen in the US could be used as leverage in negotiations.
