[Article by Nabil Khuri: "It Is True--Why Shouldn't We Withdraw?"] 


[Text] These days Brig Gen Raymond Iddih is demanding that the Arabs 
"collectively" withdraw from the UN. 


As is usual whenever the brigadier general brings up an idea, those who 
heard the idea are divided between those who enthusiastically support the 
idea and those who are so opposed to the idea that they are boycotting him-- 
that is, they are boycotting the brigadier general's social gatherings!! 





The brigadier general is defending this latest idea of his as follows: 
"Belonging to the UN has not only meant losses for the Arabs, it has also 
meant humiliation for them. Not once has this body sided with the Arabs 
(especially the Security Council) in support of any of their just causes. 
Quite the contrary. Arak causes have been brought to the Security Council 

and it has been demanded that justice be done with regard to them, but these 
Arab causes emerge from the Security Council looking as if they are the cause 
of the injustice!!" 


Brig Gen Iddih enumerates a thousand and one cases to prove the truth of what 
he is saying.

INFORMATION MINISTER'S REMARKS ON AL-QADHDHAFI 
Quote by Saudi Radio 
GF140705 Riyadh Domestic Service in Arabic 0600 GMT 14 Mar 82 


[Text] Bahraini Information Minister Tariq 'Abd al—Rahman al-Mu'ayyad has ex- 
pressed Bahrain's strong condemnation of the Libyan colonel's attack against 

the kingdom and its wise policy. He said it is not strange to see such behavior 
from Colonel Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi, who is known for his demagogy and anarchy. 


In a statement to the newspaper AL-MADINAH published today, Mr al-Mu'ayyad said: 
Al-Qadhdhafi is being led by communist powers which dictate his stupid policies. 
He called on the Arab nation to put an end to this idiot and to rescue the 
Libyan people from him since he has only accomplished disorder and devastation, 
and he has turned Libya into camps for purely communist purposes. He added: 
the GCC has condemmed al-Qadhdhafi's attack against the elder brother, the 
Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, and we, in the Bahraini Information Ministry, strongly 
denounce these falsifications against a country which is known for its favor- 
able stands on Arab issues. 


Minister's Denial 
GF141332 Manama WAKH in Arabic 1050 GMT 14 Mar 82 


[Text] Manama, 14 Mar (WAKH)--Bahraini Information Minister Tariq ‘Abd al- 
Rahman al-Mu'ayyad has categorically denied what the Saudi newspaper AL- 
MADINAH attributed to him today. He said he has not received or addressed 
any correspondent from the above-mentioned newspaper during the past few days. 


He said: “Bahrain supports Saudi Arabia's media policy, which avoids personal 
insults and slander." 


The Bahraini information minister has lauded Saudi Arabia's wise policy and 
its credible stands on the Arab nation's fateful causes, the progress and 
stability it has achieved for its citizens and the admiration that King Khalid 
ibn ‘Abd al-'Aziz's government has won from the Arabs and the entire world.

UNITED ARAB EMIRATES 


U.S. BOYCOTT OF LIBYAN OIL CALLED NEW AGGRESSION 
GF131035 Abu Dhabi WAM in Arabic 0710 GMT 13 Mar 82 


[Text] Abu Dhabi, 13 Mar (WAM)--The newspaper AL-ITTIHAD today warns that 

the recent U.S. decision to boycott Libyan oil aims at intimidating and pres- 
Suring others. The paper describes this decision as a "new U.S. aggression 
against Arabs." 


In its editorial, the newspaper Says that the United States has protested any 
Arab initiative to use oil as a political weapon and has threatened to use 
force if its economy "suffocates" because of an Arab oil boycott. Under the 
Slogan of threatening to use force, "the rapid deployment force for the Middle 
East was established." 


The newspaper adds: "Thankfully, Washington violated the principle of using 
oil as a political weapon in its recent decision to boycott Libyan oil and in 
its call to its consumer allies to join in the boycott and to turn it into a 
collective action of economic war against an Arab country." 


AL-ITTIHAD continues to say that the United States views an economic blockade 
in two ways: an economic blockade is acceptable to the United States when the 
decision to boycott is a U.S. one; it is unacceptable to the United States 
when the decision is an Arab one. The U.S. decision to boycott Libyan oil is 
an aggression, which is rejected and must be fought by force. 


In conclusion, AL-ITTIHAD says that a lesson should be learned from the U.S. 
decision, which is that (?the security of oil for us and for our coming gen- 
erations lies underground) and that we must realize that "in the absence of 
Arab solidarity, the United States will deal with Libya separately and we will 
not know which country comes after Libya on the new U.S. oil policy list."
