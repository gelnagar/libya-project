Summary (AI): A Jack Anderson column (Washington Post, 21 October 1980) says former CIA operative Frank Terpil trained and equipped Qaddafi's assassination and sabotage teams, and that American operatives were based in a former Libyan royal palace in 1976-77. Its specifics are mostly from Justice Department files as the column describes them.
• Column says Terpil trained Libyan agents in espionage, sabotage, psychological warfare and explosive devices, disguised as mine-clearing.
• Terpil reportedly closed the deal in London with Qaddafi's brother.
• Palace basement arsenal reportedly held thousands of timers; one order of 100,000 timers bought at $4.50 to $6 per unit was sold to Libya for up to $250 apiece.
• Terpil reportedly offered to smuggle a U.S. Redeye missile into Libya.
• A death squad was reportedly discovered in the United States and expelled.
• 1976-08: American operatives recruited by Frank Terpil settled into the former Idris winter palace, code-named 'Swanee'.
• 1977-07: Explosives on a truck detonated after a Libyan military officer picked up a booby-trapped device, killing three Libyan soldiers and wounding three American instructors. (3 killed)
