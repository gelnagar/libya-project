LIBYA AIR FORCE PILOTS 'RECRUITED IN LONDON OFFICE'

Summary (AI): A 1981 press clipping (London Daily Telegraph, reprinted in a CIA reading-room file) reports that Western pilots and mechanics were recruited through a London office, Brilhurst, to fly and maintain Libya's air force aircraft. It also names Edwin P. Wilson, a former CIA figure then based in Tripoli near Gaddafi.
• Brilhurst was a London service company that paid travel expenses and salaries for pilots working in Libya and obtained Libyan visas for them.
• Two companies linked to Wilson were identified: a Swiss corporation and a company with a Swiss post office box.
• Libya had acquired American C-130 Hercules transports before relations with the US broke down, and these were reportedly flown and maintained by Western pilots.
• Wilson was wanted in the US on a 1980 federal indictment for illegally shipping explosives to Libya to help train terrorists.
• 1981: Western pilots reportedly flew CH-47 Chinook helicopters in support of the Libyan invasion of Chad earlier in the year.
• 1981: Libyan air force reportedly lost two Russian-supplied fighters in a dogfight against two American fighters from the carrier Nimitz the previous summer.
