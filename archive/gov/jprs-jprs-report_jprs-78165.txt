OTRAG OFFERS ROCKETS TESTED IN LIBYA 
Tel Aviv YEDIL'OT AHARONOT in Hebrew 22 Mar 81 pp 3, 6 


[Cable from Avi'ezer Golan, New York correspondent: “German Company Offers 
Saudi Arabia Rockets Tested Thies Month in Libyan Desert") 


[Text] The West German scientific-industrial company, OTRAG, with which are 
connected a number of the larger manufacturing enterprises of G ay, is 
secretly engaged at a remote desert site on the Sahara border, within Libyan 
boundaries, in the development of a ground-to-ground missile which will be 
capable of carrying a particularly heavy payload of several tons--apparently a 
nuclear bomb. 


This was revealed by an investigative program which was broadcast on the American 
television network NBC. 


The person conducting the study, the newsman Tony Van Witson, related that the 
German company was established in 1975. Van Witson reported that he was unable 
to determine whether the company was established from the start in order to 
engage in development of military equipment far from the overseeing eye of the 
government, or whether at its inception the income tax of the investors in the 
company was designed only to solve tax shelter problems for the rich. In any 
event, ite managers, Lutz Kaiser and Frank Wakasch “related to the assignment 
with a great deal of seriousness." 


They concentrated a number of scientists, among them those who had worked with 
Prof Werner von Braun for Hitler (von Braun, who died a year ago, was the 
inventor of the V-1 and V-2 rockets with which the Nazis pounded London, and 
after the war moved to the United States, where he was considered to be "the 
father of the spacecraft" of America). 


Afterwards the German company obtained a permit from Mobutu, president of Zaire, 
over an area of about 40,000 square kilometers, an area which constituted a kind 


of “autonomous state." Only President Mobutu himself was permitted to enter 


there without a special permit from the OTRAG Company. 


According to the allegation of newsman Van Witson--the promised quid pro quo was: 
an annual payment of $50 million beginning in 1980. 




















In 1977, the first experimental dispatch of a rocket manufactured by OTRAG took 
place. Rumors about the mysterious experiments in the jungle began to spread, 
and those particularly worried by them were the Russians. They had not for- 
gotten that after World War I, when Germans were forbidden to develop armaments, 
they had engaged in this kind of development in secret in other countries, in 
Spain os well as in the Soviet Union. 


When Premier Leonid Brezhnev visited Bonn in 1978, he raised the question of 
OTRAG, and in order to convince him that the government of Bonn was not involved 
in the matter, Chancellor Helmut Schmidt exerted pressure on Mobutu, who then 
cancelled the company's permit. However, owners of the German company did not 
say die, and within a short time found an alternative solution, they entered 
into discussions with the leader of Libya, Mu'ammar Qadhdhafi, who granted them 
the desert base in his country. And on 1 March this year, the first rocket 
experiment was carried out. Qadhdhafi does not meed the millions of OTRAG. 
Despite this, it is no secret that he is prepared to invest a great deal in the 
development of “the Muslim nuclear bomb," in Pakistan, and it is decidedly to be 
assumed that he is also prepared to invest in a vehicle to carry the bomb. 


As proof of the military character of the development work of OTRAG, two documents 
were displayed on the American television program. One, a copy of the contract 
between OTRAG and the Government of Syria, according to which the German company 
will develop and build for Syria rockets of a range of 100 miles (160 kilometers). 
For some reason the transaction was never signed. 


The second document tells about the person who was the commander of land forces 
of West Germany, General West, who after resigning from his duties--traveled to 
Saudi Arabia, and in the name of a company called AMIEPLAN proposed to the Saudis 
tc uild for them a rocket system which will be manufactured on the basis of 
kno. ‘dge in possession of the OTRAG company. 


When Tony Van Witson appeared in the offices of OTRAG, the general manager, Frank 
Wakasch, refuse@ to examine the documents--but declared them forgeries, and that 
the meeting between General West and the chief of Saudi Intelligence never took 
place. 


Despite this, a spokesman for the large Telefunken Company admitted that the 
meeting between West and the head of Saudi Intelligence did in fact take place. 
and he added that a representative of Telefunken participated in it. Aside from 
television receivers, Telefunken also manufactures electronic equipment for 
rocket guidance. The spokesman stressed, however, that his company withdrew 
from the project immediately after that meeting between West and the Saudis. 


Another large German company which manufactures components for sophisticated 
armament., whose representative participated in that meeting--is the well known 
Doehl Company. 


Van Vitson interviewed Mu'ammar Qadhdhafi also, who denied categorically that 
OTRAG is engaged in development of equipment of a military nature. He insisted 
that the activity of this German company is “within the area of pure science." 











Van Witeon closed his report with the statement that all of these details have 
been known to the German Government which prefers to look the other way. How 
ever, after particularly sharp protests from the governments of Egypt and 
Morocco~~following a rocket experiment early this month, it is now anticipated 
that steps will be taken by the West German Government against OTRAG,
