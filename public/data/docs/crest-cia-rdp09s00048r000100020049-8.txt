Summary (AI): A 1982 New York Times article reports on congressional closed hearings into former CIA agents Edwin P. Wilson and Frank E. Terpil and their dealings with the Libyan government. It covers CIA internal inquiries, a new code of conduct for former employees, and the limits on enforcing it. No Tripoli places are named.
• Two internal CIA inquiries concluded that CIA aid to Wilson's recruitment project was not official.
• The CIA adopted a new code of conduct extending agency regulations to former employees, but a spokesman conceded it was not a legally binding agreement.
• Two senior officials suspected of involvement were exonerated; Wilson later set one up in a business that hired the other.
• 1981: House Select Committee on Intelligence closed hearings on former CIA agents Wilson and Terpil and their dealings with Libya (week before article)
• 1980: Wilson and Terpil indicted for illegally shipping explosives to Libya
• 1977: Two middle-level CIA employees dismissed for their part in the Libyan affair
