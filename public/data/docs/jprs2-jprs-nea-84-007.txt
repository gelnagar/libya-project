Riyadh AL-YAMAMAH in Arabic 5 Oct 83 pp 42-43 
[Article: "Interest Has Prevailed Over Reconciliation"] 

[Text] London--The tense truce between Sudanese President Ja'far Numayri and 
al-Sadiq al-Mahdi has ended with the Sudanese authorities’ arrest of the 
former Sudanese prime minister after he had delivered a political speech in 
Omdurman criticizing the present government's policies, including the recent 
decrees issued by the Sudanese president on application of the restrictions 
of the Islamic Shari'ah. 

The relationship between President Numayri's government and the faction of 
al-Sadiq, who heads a wing of the former National Party and who is one of 

the prominent leaders of al-Ansar sect whose members are concentrated in 
western and central Sudan, had undergone various phases ranging from complete 
estrangement to temporary relaxation and concealed tension. This relation- 
ship reached its worst phase in the mid-1970's when al-Mahdi formed what is 
known as the National Front in alliance with the leaders of the former 
political parties, especially with the Democratic Unionist Party, led by 
al-Sharif Husayn al-Hindi, the ex-minister of finance in the 1968 cabinet 

of al-Mahdi, and the Muslim Brotherhood, led by Hasan al-Turabi, the Sudanese 
president's adviser for foreign affairs at present. This front used Libya 

as its base of opposition and staged the most serious attempt to overthrow 
President Numayri's regime in 1976 when it sent a large force of militia 

men, most of them members of al-Ansar, that infiltrated the Sudanese capital 
at various periods of time and staged a quasi-military coup when it attacked 
military barracks and vital utilities in the capital. The attempt would have 
succeeded had not units of forces loyal to Numayri, some of which came from 
outside Khartoum, moved quickly. 

That attempt left a deep feeling of bitterness among many Sudanese whose 
history had not known armed political violence. Moreover, the attempt 
demonstrated to the government the danger of the National Front to the stabil- 
ity of the country's political system. Those events were followed by a series 
of arrests and trials. The death sentence issued against al-Sadiq al-Mahdi 

in absentia was one of the most significant results of those trials. But 

the sentence was not carried out and contrary to the expectations of numerous 
observers who had predicted the development of a major crack in Sudan's 
national unity in the wake of those events, the signs of an imminent political 

relaxation began to appear with secret contacts took place in London and a 
number of West European capitals and were ultimately crowned with the arrange- 
ment of a secret meeting between the Sudanese president and al-Sadiq al-Mahdi.

numerous security laws and restoration of the liberties. 

Meanwhile, the Sudanese Government insisted that the first priority should be 
the reconciliation and repatriation and that political reform can come gradu- 
ally and in freedom from radical demands and prior conditions.
