Three Results 
What are the results of this smuggling on the national economy? 


Undoubtedly, smuggling has a negative effect on the state's economy and on the 
production sectors. This can be shown as follows: 


l. The state budget: 


If it were not for the customs duties paid through the port of Beirut, there would 
be no duties worth mentioning. During the first 8 months of this year, the state 
received 313.5 million Lebanese pounds, compared to 175 million last year. The 
increase amounted to 79 percent. Customs duties paid through the port represent 
65 percent of the total customs duties paid. 


As for the other ports of entry, the airport, Juniyah, Tripoli, al-Masna’ and Sidon, 
customs duties paid through them registered a decline of 33.4 percent, or 166.8 
million Lebanese pounds, compared to 250.5 million paid last year for the same period. 


The decline in receipts was most severe at the port of Juniyah, amounting to 46 
percent, followed by Tripoli and the northern region, 58 percent; Sidon, 50 percent; 
al-Masna’', 2.4 percent; and the airport, 1.2 percent 


If the port of Beirut, in resuming its natural role, has raised customs receipts, 
while they have declined at the port of Juniyah, in view of its being a wartime 

port used as a result of Beirut'’s closure, then the other ports of entry, especially 
the airport, should have had increased customs revenue. The disparity in customs 
duties paid demonstrates to some degree the amount of smuggling within them, 
considering the fact that the illegal ports were active in 1979, and their effect 

on receipts for this year should not be of any consequence.
