Civilians, soldiers, fascists, dictators, traditionalists, negativists,

Tripoli AL~ZAHAF AL~AKHDAR in Arabic 9 Jun 80 pp 16-17 


[Article: "Four Million Palm Trees Have Been Destroyed; the Revolutionary 
Information System Has Missed a Chance To Guide Consumption") 


[Text] "Eat and drink, but not to excess; God does not like wastrels” 
[Koranic verse]. A nation which must get ite food from abroad cannot 
be independent. 


Most of the consumer goods--day to day necessities and major appliances-- 
found in our markets are imported and take a large part of our nation's 
treasury. While we import most of our goods from abroad, we have locally 
produced alternatives which can be substituted for certain imported 
goods. This could save huge sums of money. Certain studies indicate 
that local production is still insufficient, despite the high food value 
of our produce, which is attested to by nutritionists. Imported goods 
have not been properly steered towards sound consumer practices inasmuch 
as we find that the cost to our national treasury has been rising 
continuously as a result of the ever-increasing world prices, despite 
the stability of prices in our markets. 


We can never become independent because "a nation which must get its 

food from abroad cannot be independent." Nor can we be independent as 

long as we continue to waste without limits. Statistics attest to our 
wastefulness. fFamiles are wasteful, and buy unnecessarily large quantities 
of various goods for private consumption. Under no circumstances does 
consumer guidance mean belt-tightening or lowering our standard of 

living. It means guiding society towards the goal of self-sufficiency 

in the basic commodities without encroaching on necessities or eliminating 
consumption. 


Some might say that we cannot modify our buying habits. But the purpose 
of guidance is not to encroach on necessities so much as it is to regulate 
consumption properly on a purely economic level. The purpose of consumer 
steering or guidance is also to replace imports with substitutes produced 
within the jamahiriyah. Why don't we promote date production as an

Scientific studies have proved that dates make a nutritionally balanced

at the expense of our principal food resource, the food that nourished 
our forefathers and enabled them to continue their holy war against 

all forms of imperialism, when their sole source of sustenance was 

dried dates and milk. 


Sixty Percent of Our Food Arrives By Ship 


Private consumption is ordinarily considered that part of the gross 
national product which families and individuals use to fill their needs 

for goods and services. Imports play an important part in supplying the 
local market in the jamahiriyah with agricultural and manufactured consumer 
goods. Approximately 60 percent of our food is imported. An even larger 
share, perhaps 90 percent, of our manufactured goods are imported. 


In view of the large part imports play in supplying the local market 
with consumer goods, there is a strong relationship between the amount 
and type of consumption and the amount and type of goods that are 
imported. Changes in the quantities and types of imported consumer 
goods--particularly food and major appliances--lead to corresponding 
changes in the amounts and types of consumption. Simiiarly, changes 
in consumption have an effect on the amounts and types of imported 
consumer goods. During the period of the five-year plan 63.5 percent 
of our food and major appliances are expected to be imported. This means 
that for every 1,000 dinar increase in private consumption 650 dinars 
will be spent on imports. 


During the last 14 years the rate of consumption has increased. The 
possibility of decreasing the amount of imported food and other consumer 
goods to a level below the 65 percent determined by demand for imports 
is not foreseen, at least in the near future, unless domestic prices 
rise or an insufficient supply of foreign goods puts a damper on imports.

A Steady Rise ln Calorie Consumption 


In the last decade the jamahiriyah has seen a huge increase in food 
consumption, Per capita daily calorie consumption rose from 1,890 

in 1964 to approximately 3,000 in 1975. The number of calories from 
animal products rose from 164 to 385 in the same period. In 1975 imports 
comprised 64 percent of all foodstuffs consumed. The ratio in 1976 was 
55 percent. From 1964 to 1974 consumption continued to rise at a high 
rate, eyproximately 10 percent a year, with prices remaining stable. 
However, there has been a trend towards a gradual decrease in real 
private consumption, which dropped 12.4 percent from 1964 to 1970, 

7.8 percent from 1975 to 1978, and is expected to drop 2.9 percent from 
1978 to 1980, 


The sums used for domestic price supports vary. World food prices in 
September 1978 were four times higher than they were in 1970. During 
the same period prices of essential foods in the jamahiriyah remained 
stable. They did not change because of the support given by the nation's 
treasury. The total amount of this support varied from year to year 
according to the changes in world food prices. In 1975 the amount paid 
by the national treasury in food price supports amounted to 75 million 
Libyan dinars. This amount dropped by 40 million dinars to 41.6 million 
[sic] in 1977, Despite a steady decrease in the last 2 years, some 
increase in price supports for consumer goods seems inevitable in the 
future because the object is to avoid or prevent higher food prices, and 
a big increase in world food prices is a strong possibility in the 

long run. 


Per Capita Purchases of Major Appliances Have Risen 


The demand for major appliances has been one important sign of the 
changed pattern of individual consumption during the period of economic 
development and higher median incomes. This has been a conspicuous trend 
in the jamahiriyah. Per capita purchases of major appliances rose at 

an annual rate of 20 percent from 1970 to 1975. The rise was 11.5 
percent [sic]. In 1976 sales of the following major items per 1,000 
citizens rose as follows: 


Television sets: from 19 to 60; 
Automobiles: from 5 to 12; 

Washing machines: from 2 to 15; 
Refrigerators: from 15 to 26; 

Furniture: from 2,300 to 9,400 kilograms. 


Two main factors account for these increases in major appliance sales 
in the jamahiriyah: 


l. Per capita income has increased steadily.

2. the citizens have a strong tendency to furnish their homes with major

A key element in our future consumption strategy is to improve the 
quality and variety of goods rather than increase the quantity. 


Demand for certain goods will apparently be greater than supply for a

Stable prices in the local market despite higher prices in the world

Customs laws and taxes, as well as price supports on the following

Controlling supply and increasing the life span of major appliances so 
as to make a contribution toward less frequent reliance on imported

which provide post-sale services such as maintenance, spare parts and

There is a clear need to decrease imported consumer goods, but certain 
foods and major appliances are evidently being bought in greater quantities 
than is necessary. Imports of these items must be cut down. These 

items are sugar, tea, grain, radios, private automobiles and so forth. 


The following measures can be taken to reduce factors encouraging the 
import of the following goods: 


Sugar: raise the price, strengthen customs regulations and combat 
smuggling. 


Grain: raise the price of bread and baked goods. 
Radios: improve repair services and modify customs taxes. 
Private cars: raise the price of luxury cars and improve repair services. 


Wristwatches: raise customs taxes and redouble efforts at combatting 
smuggling. 


Many countries have attempted to reduce their imports of some of these 


goods by using quotas, but their experiences have proven that such a 
system has many drawbacks. 


A better way of achieving the same goal is to raise customs taxes on 
imports and raise prices. There are also other methods.

dates As an Alternative To Certain Imported Goods 


With regard to date production, the jamahiriyah is famous for its culti- 
vation of the date palm, There used to be about 9 million date palms 

in the country, but as a result of lack of care and actual neglect today 
the number is only about 5 million, 


‘he palm trees had been well cared for in the past, and were used only 
as a source of edible dates and as a source of fuel for providing heat. 


But now, unfortunately, during a time of great technological progress 
which is affecting all forms of life, we find a lack of concern for the 
palm trees which could be used to help alleviate the world food shortage 
which is menacing mankind. With the fruit of the palm tree we can 
increase our agricultural production, since the palm tree takes up an 
inconsiderable amount of land area, thereby allowing the cultivation of 
other crops. As a matter of fact, the palm tree is actually beneficial 
to other crops planted with it. It shelters other plants, protecting 
them from the biting cold and the scorching sun, It also serves as a 
barrier against the wind. 


The date has a high food value, and it protects the body from diseases. 
it is also used in making date preserves which resemble the better known 
kinds. 


Spoiled dates can be used to produce pure alcohol which is used in many 
industries and has many other uses. Furthermore, a higher percentage 

of sugar can be extracted from dates than from sugar cane. Technological 
progress has made it possible to use the pits, by extracting 8.5 percent 
oll of excellent quality, better than cottonseed oil. The remainder 

of the pit is used to make concentrated fodder. 


Even the leaves of the palm tree can be used to make rayon, a synthetic 
silk. 


The date consists of two parts: the fruit and the pit. 


fhe pit consists of several important substances, particularly oils and 
proteins, which are ready to be used in making animal fodder and other 
products. 


rhe truit is the part that is eaten. It consists of several food 
substances: oils; fiber; minerals, such as calcium and phosphorus; 
sugars--glucose, fructose and sucrose; and amino acids. Recent scientific 
experiments have proven that the bedouins’ meal consisting of dates and 
milk provides the body with all its nutritional requirements and calories, 
in addition, Laboratory experiments have proven that microorganisms do 

not infest dates.
