LIBYA

OFFICIAL EXPLAINS PRINCIPLES OF PEOPLE'S BUREAUS 
LD152106 Tripoli JANA in English 1847 GMT 15 May 80 


{Text] Tripoli, May 15, JAMAHIRIYAH NEWS AGENCY--Mr Ash-Shahhati, the head of the Foreign 
Liaison Bureau at the G,P.C, said that the seizure of the Libyan embassies and their change 
into people's bureaus represents a radical transformation which is line with the people's 
power in force in Jamahiriyah. 


During a meeting with the ambassadors of Pakistan, Austria, Turkey, Bangladesh, Czechoslovakia, 
East Germany, Yugoslavia, Belgium and Switzerland as well as with the Romanian charge 
dtaffaires, he said that the Jamahiriyah has gone beyond traditional diplomacy and that deal- 
ing with the people has become the basis of its relations with the world. He continued that 
in Libya the people govern themselves through their revolutionary and popular committees and 
that accordingly there is no president or king to appoint ambassadors. 


He added that in conformity with the third international theory and the rise of the 
Jamahiriyah and the people's power, which put an end to all the instruments of the traditional 
government system, the Libyan nationals seized their embassies, turned them into people®s 
bureaus and formed people's committees to run them. This action was carried out to dispel 

the contradictions of the embassy system, 


Mr Ash-Shahhati pointed out that the people's bureaus will have the task to promote relations 
between the Libyan Arab people and the peoples of the world.

LIBYAN BANK TO INTRODUCE NEW 5, 10 DINAR BILLS 
LD151418 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 15 May 80 


[Summary] ‘%1e Central Bank of Libya announced today that on 16 May 1980 it will put 
into circulation new 5 and 10 dinar currency bills. The bank also announced that as of 
1430 on 15 May 1930 the 5-and 10-dinar denominations of the first issue and all the 
previous issues in pounds will be invalidated and they will be withdrawn from circula- 
tion. All nationals and foreigners who possess currency in these two denominations 
should proceed with their identification papers to any branch of the commerical banks to 
exchange them, or to any of the other special centers opened at police stations in areas 
where there are no banks and which have been authorized to open their doors for this 
purpose from 0800 GMT to 2000 from Friday, 16 May 1980, to Thursday, 22 May 1980. The 
old notes will not be accepted for exchange after the latter date. 


TUNIS IA 
PRIME MINISTER PRAISES CONDUCT, OUTCOME OF OAU SUMMIT 


LD151117 Tunis L!ACTION in French 1 May 80 p 4 


{Unattributed report: “Lagos Conference Has Turned Out a Success"] 


[Text] Yesterday afternoon Prime Minister Mohamed Mzali returned to Tunis after 
representing President Habib Bourguiba at the OAU economic summit in Lagos, which was 
concluded on Tuesday. On his arrival at Tunis-Carthage International Airport, Mohamed 
Mzali was greeted by government and Political Bureau members and many other national 
cadres. 


On this occasion the prime minister delivered a press statement in which he said that 

he would inform the head of state of the results of the conference. Mohamed Mzali 
emphasized that the conference turned out a success. In fact, he stated, it was the 
first African summit to be devoted to studying the problem of Africa's economic develop- 
ment within a framework of complementarity and cooperation and later within a framework 
of both regional and general integration in the 2lst century.

As some proofs of the conference's success, the prime minister mentioned the participation 
of all African states in the deliberations. In fact, 49 of the 50 OAU member-states par- 
ticipated in the conference, the only exception being Liberia, which was refused admis- 
Sion by the conference in view of the recent events in that country. 


Furthermore, Mr Mohamed Mzali stated in this context, African states were represented at 
the conference at a highest level since 30 African heads of state, 2 vice presidents and 

8 prime ministers participated in this "summit," while most other countries! delegations 
were led by their foreign ministers. The prime minister also stated that the conference 
concluded its deliberations as planned, that is, the day before yesterday. As a result, 
pointless discussions were avoided. All concerned dealt with problems listed on the aggen- 
da objectively and wisely. 


And Mr Mohamed Mzali continued: "Furthermore, the conference issued a warning against the 
dangers and economic calamities which will threaten the African Continent if nothing is 
done to remedy the present situation. In fact, Africa produces what it does not consume 
and consumes what it does not produce. This is a sign of dependence and inefficiency and 
augurs ill for the future unless African leaders do samething to face up to these econo- 
mic dangers and extricate Africa from the present situation. 


"T am pleased," Mohamed Mzali stated further, "that I was able to participate in this 
conference only 5 days after being appointed prime minister. As a result, I was able to 
meet many African heads of state, prime ministers and ministers and become acquainted with 
all those African leaders. That in itself is a positive fact. 


"T had talks useful and beneficial to Tunisia. At the same time, I was able to observe 
the esteem enjoyed by Tunisia and the unparalleled respect and consideration which Afri- 
cans have for President Bourguiba. All the heads of state, prime ministers amd ministers 
whom I met inquired after President Bourguiba and asked me to convey their greetings to 
him. They believe that President Habib Bourguiba is the wisest man in Africa and the 
leader who sheds light on Africa's present and future paths." 


PRIME MINISTER SEEKS MORE AID FROM FRG 


ID151436 Hamburg DPA in German 1234 GMT 15 May 80 


[Excerpt] Tunis--Following the bloodily quashed attempted coup at the beginning of the 
year in Qafsah, Tunisia intends to equip its armed forces with the "necessary minimum" 
of equipment in order to secure its borders and guarantee order in the country. That 

is why it is seeking increased aid also from the Federal Republic. This was stated in a 
DPA interview with the new Tunisian prime minister, Mohamed Mzali. 


He was appointed by President Habib Bourguiba to succeed "Crown Prince" Hedi Nouira who 
was ill. Mzali praised German-Tunisian cooperation to date and particularly mentioned 
in this respect Bonn's development aid (DM 1.3 billion to date) and German investments 
which have created about 8,000 jobs.
