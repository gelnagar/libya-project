FRENCH DENY ISSUING INVITATION TO AL-QADHDHAFI 
LD260930 Paris Domestic Service in French 0600 GMT 26 Sep 79 LD 


{Excerpt] The Bokassa affair has created a slight impediment in relations between France 
and Libya.

The Libyan head of state, Colonel al-Qadhohafi has decided to cancel the visit which 
he was due to make to France to protest, he says, the finishing touch given by France 
to Bokassa's downfall. The trouble is that France has replied: No official trip by 
Al-Qadhdhafi was planned. 


SECRETARIAT PROTESTS FRENCH ‘INVASION! OF CENTRAL AFRICA 
1D251538 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 25 Sep 79 LD 


[Text] The foreign secretary this morning summoned the French ambassador to the 
Jamahiriyah and conveyed to him the protest, condemnation and denunciation by the 
Foreign Secretariat of the French forces! invasion of Central Africa and all the con- 
sequences of this colonial policy. The Foreign Secretariat has issued the following 
statement about the French invasion of Central Africa: 


The French Government on 20 September, using its armed forces present in Gabon and 
Chad, carried out a brutal and barbaric invasion of Central Africa, thus violating the 
sovereignty and the independence of a sister African country, a member of the Organi- 
zation of African Unity and of the UN organization and the nonalined movement. 


The French Government's invasion of a sister African country can only be considered as 
a violation of the UN Charter and the OAU Charter, and as a direct hostile action 
directed against all the countries of the African Continent. Such an invasion by 
France, a permanent member of the Security Council, and its occupation of a small 
defenseless country, is a matter of extreme gravity which is bound to endanger inter- 
national security and peace, and the safety and security of the countries of the 
African Continent in particular. The action of the French Government is but a new 
manifestation of its colonial policy in the African Continent. 


This government for years has been pursuing a brutal colonial policy in the African 
Continent, and has resorted to using mercenaries to assassinate the leaders of the 
continent and to alter their national regimes. It has used its regular armed forces 
to strike at the African people in the Western Sahara and Chad, and has now used its 
armed forces to occupy and directly invade Central Africa. 


The French Government, by invading Central Africa, has violated all international 
norms and laws and has violated the sovereignty of an independent state by pursuing 
the same French colonial policy as in the 19th Century. The Jamahiriyah drew the at- 
tention of its African sisters some years ago to the seriousness of this colonial 
French policy, and warned against its dire consequences. The events of Central Africa 
have come to confirm this. In the face of the gravity of this situation, the Foreign 
Secretariat, which most strongly denounces this barbaric brutal aggression against a 
sister country, sees fit for the African countries to adopt a collective posture 
against French colonialism and for their commitment to the OAU Charter and to the 
honor and dignivy of the continent. 


It is time for the African countries to liquidate the French military presence on the 
continent, because this presence, facts have proved, not only represents a danger to 
the country in which French forces are to be found but also to the other African coun- 
tries, whose right it has become, in order to maintain their security and independeme, 
to resist this French colonial military presence by every means they possess. 


The French Government, which bears full responsibility for invading Central Africa-- 
which it has officially admitted through the French minister of cooperation--should 
realize that this aggression against the continent cannot be accepted lightly.

This aggression will be met by practical reaction from the countries and peoples of the 
continent, for Africa cannot allow France to reoccupy and recolonize the countries of the 
African Continent. On the contrary, it is time to liberate parts of the African Con- 
tinent which France still colonizes, such as Reunion Island, 


By invading Central Africa and by threatening the security and the safety of the African 
Continent, the French Government bears full responsibility for all that is bound to 
destroy the existing cooperative relations between the French people and the African 
peoples, 


After the Great First of September Revolution the Jamahiriyah, in appreciation for the 
independent policy pursued by the late President de Gaulle and his policy of liquidating 
colonialism in Africa, established balanced and fruitful cooperation with France. However, 
the French Government's reversal from this policy and its pursuit of an aggressive 
colonial policy against the African countries is not advantageous for the continuation 
and development of this cooperation, 


Brother Colonel Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi, leader of the Great First of September Revolution, 
was to pay an official visit to France, but as a result of France's aggression against 
and occupation of Central Africa, it has been decided to cancel this visit. 


The Foreign Secretariat, in stressing this position which stems from the revolutionary 
principles of the Great First of September Revolution and from its commitment to the 
Charter and aims of the organisation of African Unity, is not defending persons or regimes 
but is defending principles by which it has committed itself to the peoples of the African 
Continent. This it has continuously stressed since the outbreak of the Great First of 
September Revolution. 


The Foreign Secretariat has contacted African, Islamic and other countries regarding the 
French military invasion of Central Africa. Yesterday the African ambassadors to the 
Jamahiriyah were summoned, and the assistant general secretary for political affairs at 
the Foreign Secretariat had meetings with the African ambassadors and explained to them 
the Foreign Secretariat's view of this invasion, its gravity and the real reasons behind 
it. The assistant general secretary for political affairs exchanged opinions with the 
African ambassadors on this subject and asked them to inform their governments of this 
exchange. 


At midday yesterday the ambassadors of the Islamic countries accredited to the Jamahiriyah 
were also summoned, and they had a meeting with the assistant general secretary for 
political affairs at the Foreign Secretariat for the same purpose. During these two 
meetings the assistant generalsecretary stressed to the ambassadors that the real gravity 
of the situation lies in the fact that a foreign country from outside the African 
Continent carried out a direct military invasion of one of the countries of the continent 
to change its regime and to impose another pro-French regime. The assistant general secre- 
tary for political affairs at the Foreign Secretariat warmed that any African or non- 
African country could be a target of similar intervention in the future; in the event 

of adopting an attitude of forebearance and of not standing firmly against such direct 
interventions. 


Message to Islamic Congress 
LD251837 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1400 GMT 25 Sep 79 LD 
[Text] Jiddah--The Libyan charge d'affairs in Jiddah has delivered to the secretary 
general of the Islamic Congress a message from the foreign secretary conceming the 


barbaric invasion which France planned and executed against Central Africa, the African 
member of the OAU, the United Nations and the nonalined movement. 


V. 26 Sep 79 T4 NORTH AFRICA 


The brother charge d'affaires explained to Karim Gay that the invasion renders Muslims 
in that African country subject to danger, especially since Islam has clearly begun to 
spread among the citizens of Central Africa. Brother Karim Gay, secretary general of the 
Islamic Congress, informed the brother charge d'affaires of the sympathy of the Secre- 
tariat with the Muslims of Central Africa. He also told him that he would contact all 
Muslim countries to inform them of the dangers that threaten the Muslims in that African 
country as a result of the French invasion.
