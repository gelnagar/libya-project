Al-Qadhdhafi on Arab Ties, Support for IRA 
LD131403 London ITV Television Network in English 
1800 GMT I1 Apr 88 

[Report by Trevor MacDonald on the “Channel 4 News” 
program—intercepted in progress; all Al-Qadhdhafi 
statements in English and as heard] 

[Excerpt] [MacDonald, over video of ceremonial demo- 
lition of Libyan border post] With one simple gesture 
Colonel al-Qadhdhafi had struck what he believes to be 
a decisive blow for the cause of Arab unity. The two 
Tunisian ministers who attended the demolition cere- 
mony found its significance much more prosaic. Tunisia 
needs to be able to send more workers into Libya, a 
richer country with a less skilled work force. But it has 
also told the Tunisians that their own idiosyncratic 
neighbor has embarked on a policy of reconciliation. 

The day after he went to the Tunisian border, I met Col 
al-Qadhdhafi at a tent on the grounds of his house in 
Tripoli. He told me that the borders between Arab 
countries had been drawn by Africa’s colonial masters, 
but that in independence those divisions should cease; 
an Arab land should be one again. 

[Al-Qadhdhafi] The Arab land is one for all Arabs. 
Colonization made these borders to divide our one land. 
Now we are independent; we have to get rid of this fix of 
the colonization. What I...It is what I did yesterday. 

[MacDonald] What about breaking down other borders? 
Are you hoping that you will break down borders with 
other Arab countries? 

{Al-Qadhdhafi] We will try....[laughing] We will try. 

[MacDonald] It’s on the subject of the Middle East that 
Col al-Qadhdhafi is most forthright. He said the: Pales- 
tinians will be freed of Israeli occupation, not by diplo- 
macy, but by force because, he argues, Israel is supported 
by the powers of imperialism. 

{Al-Qadhdhafi] Imperialism are behind them. It is is not 
easy for all these aggressive parties to leave Palestine for 
some people. It is not easy, unless by force. 

[MacDonald] But what about the possibility of a diplo- 
matic solution? The Americans, for example. The Amer- 
ican secretary of state, George Shultz, is going round 
trying to get a diplomatic solution. Don’t you have any 
regard for that kind of possibility at all?

ARAB AFRICA 

[Al-Qadhdhafi] No, no. We don’t recognize any Ameri- 
can role because America is not a neutral country. 
America is in the side...pro-Israelis completely and 
America in this case is our enemy. 

[MacDonald] What about your support for the IRA—the 
Irish Republican Army? 

[Al-Qadhdhafi] We believe the cause of Ireland is a just 
cause, and we support this just cause because we believe 
Ireland is Ireland and Britain is Britain, and the exist- 
ence of. Britain in the north of Ireland is sense of 
colonization. And this...we will fight to get rid of this 
colonization, which is just fight and we support it. 

[MacDonald] But does that mean, leader Al-Qadhdhafi, 
does that mean supporting people who place bombs and 
who kill and maim innocent people? 

[Al-Qadhdhafi} We are not responsible about thi- 
s...means. We support the cause. 

{MacDonald] You don’t give them weapons and money? 

[Al-Qadhdhafi] We support the cause because it is just 
one, but we are not responsible and involved in any act 
that is particularly that they do. It is their responsibility. 

[MacDonald] Col al-Qadhdhafi believes that better rela- 
tions with the United States are possible, but not under 
President Reagan, who he describes as foolish. I asked 
him when he expected Libya’s relations with America to 
improve. 

[Al-Qadhdhafi} Not now. I expect it after Reagan. Any 
new American president will not follow this foolish man, 
Reagan. He will be reasonable I think. 

[MacDonald] What about relations with Britain? Do you 
think that there’s a chance of improving Libya’s relations 
with Britain? 

[Al-Qadhdhafi] No, because Britain is like America, and 
Britain is following America. I don’t think that Britain is 
an independent country during Thatcher’s regime. We 
consider that Britain is a colony for America. 

[MacDonald] This week Libyans mark the second anni- 
versary of the American attempt to destroy Col al- 
Qadhdhafi’s command center in Tripoli. The abortive 
bombing raid has made the Colonel’s house Libya’s most 
powerful propaganda weapon. All the foreigners visiting 
the country are taken to see what Col al-Qadhdhafi calls 
real evidence of the language of terrorism. Two years on, 
Col al-Qadhdhafi is no longer talking of one single Arab 
empire, economically powerful and militarily invincible, 
but he’s beginning at least to repair his relations with his 
neighbors. Trevor MacDonald, ITN, Libya. [passage 
omitted]
