LIBYA

REAGAN IN FRG JOURNAL CRITICIZES SAUDI ARABIA 


LD081420 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1330 GMT 8 Feb 82 


[Text] The head of the U.S. administration has attacked Saudi Arabia and strongly 
criticized the regime there. The U.S. President said in an interview yesterday with WELT 
AM SONNTAG of West Germany that his country considers Israel, because of its technology 
and military potential, to be a strategic asset for America and its most faithful ally. 

He added: Israel differs from the other pro-Western countries such as Saudi Arabia, whose 
existence depends on autocracy. 


LE MONDE DESCRIBES AL-QADHDHAFI VISIT TO TUNISIA 


PM081425 Paris LE MONDE in French 26 Jan 82 p 6 
[Michel Deure and Paul Balta dispatch: "An Embarrassing Guest in Tunisia"] 


[Text] Gabes -- Colonel al-Qadhdhafi returned to Libya by road Sunday 24 January, after 

a surprise visit which ended calmly though it started amid unbelievable confusion. Indeed, 
during the 36 hours he spent in Tunisia, the leader of the Libyan revolution indulged in 
an outright orgy of rude offhandedness. 


On Friday evening, Colonel al-Qadhdhafi announced from Algiers that he had decided to return 
home by crossing Tunisia by road. At the same time, two Land-Rovers and trucks carrying 
some 50 bodyguards turned up at the Tunisian-Libyan border "to go to meet the brother 
colonel," who was expected at the other end of the country. 


Tunisian authorities turned back that commando group recalling it is Tunisia's job, as a 
sovereign country, to guarantee its guests’ security. Moreover, Interior Minister Driss 
Guiga and Defense Minister Slaheddine Baly planned to welcome the visitor in person 
Saturday morning as he entered the country, with a view to escorting him on his trip. But 
Guiga and Baly did not find anybody: The expected guest had changed his mind and decided 
to land at Monastir, President Bourguiba's birthplace in the Sahel, where nobody was 
expecting him. Shortly afterward, the bodyguards, turned away the previous day, also 
landed on board a Fokker aircraft, still carrying their weapons which were confiscated 
before being returned on their departure. Another aircraft then arrived with four armored 
Mercedes on board. Authorities also learned that, back on Friday, the Libyans had 
reserved more than 100 hotel rooms "for a group of tourists" in Monastir, Sfax and Gabes, 
where Prime Minister Mohamed Mzali was on a tour of inspection. 


The attitude of the 100-strong Libyan delegation was bound to intrigue, if not worry, the 
Tunisian leaders. When Colonel al-Qadhdhafi suddenly arrived in Sfax, his economy minister 
[title as published] Abu Zayd ‘Umar Durdah telephoned Mzali to ask for a meeting. He was 
invited to dinner, accepted and then omitted to send his apologies. During the telephone 
conversation, the prime minister, who did not intend to alter his program to visit Sfax, 
had nonetheless offered to receive the colonel in Gabes if he wished: He was given only 

3 hours' notice of his arrival on Sunday. That forced him to alter his program. 


Mzali, disregarding this strange behavior and a lively quarrel between the Libyan body- 
guards and the Tunisian security officers they intended to replace, showed unshakable 
serenity and received Colonel al-Qadhdhafi as if nothing had happened. The meeting which 
lasted 2 hours, including lunch, related essentially to the development of economic and 
commercial relations. The two men tacitly avoided raising political problems on which 
Tunis and Tripoli often have differing views. They probably postponed those questions 
until the visit to Tunis, which the Jamahiriyah leader has been invited to pay, when 
President Bourguiba returns from the United States.

Paradoxically, according to the Libyans themselves, their baffling visit to Tunisia ended 
better than it had begun. They seemed to be disappointed with their visit to Algeria and, 
although they certainly did not succeed in rekindling the unitary flame of Jerba [refe- 
rence to abortive attempt at Tunisian-Libyan merger], they were able to revive dialogue. 
This is not negligible either for Tunisia, anxious to maintain good relations with its 
powerful neighbor, or for Libya, which wants to win over the moderate country where the 
Arab League has its seat a few months before the OAU summit which it [Libya] is preparing 
to host despite U.S. hostility.
