AL-QADHDHAFI RULES OUT RECONCILIATION WITH U.S. 


AU070836 Rome ANSA in English 0812 GMT 7 Dec 81 


[Text] Rome, 7 Dec (ANSA) -- Libyan leader Mu'ammar al-Oadhdhafi cateforically excluded 
any prospect of dealings with the United States and said he believed that an American 
Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) plot against his life was going forward in an interview 
given to the big Milan newspaper, "CORRIERE DELLA SERA." 


In his statement, carried on the front page of the Sunday edition and taking up over 
half of page two, the Libyan colonel declared, "We have no confidence in America and 
none especially in President Ronald Reagan's government. It's a theatrical government 
based on illogical policies." In reference to what Al-Qadhdhafi feels to beaCIA threat 
to his life, he said, "we have taken all possible precautions to come to terms with 

the danger driving from an excess of irresponsibility." On the same issue, the Libyan 
leader said, "there is no reason to think the Americans have given up their project. 

The first phase of this plot was the launch of a campaign of disinformation. This is 
what has happened."

In response to the question on his country's ties with revolutionary organizations and 

some groups which could be labeled "terrorist," Al-Qadhdhafi strongly declared that Libya 

"has never helped any terrorist and there is no evidence which denies this. We have always 
made a distinction between liberation movements and terrorist organizations like (Italy's) 

Red Brigades or the West German RAF [Red Army Faction]. Our backing is only for those 
movements struggling for a just cause, like the Palestine Liberation Organization in Palestine," 
he argued. Speaking about the situation in the Middle East, Colonel Al-OQadhdhafi described 

the eight-point Mideast peace plan put together by Saudi Crown Prince Fahd as “an extention 

of Camp David and, as such, it was born dead." He added that Libya, if faced with danger 

from abroad, would reassess its position as a non-aligned country. 


On the bilateral front, Al-Qadhdhafi said that he was ready and willing to come to Italy on 
visit. "There is no opposition to my visit except from the Americans and their servants. 
My visit would represent a real trial to see whether Italy is an independent country or an 
American protectorate," Al-Qadhdhafi challenged.

Text of Joint Communique 
LD240005 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1700 GMT 23 Nov 81 


{[“Text™ of joint Libyan-Yugoslav communique released on 23 November in Tripoli and 
Belgrade on the visit of Sergej Kraigher, president of the SFRY Presidency, to Libya on 
21-23 November] 


[Text] At the invitation of the leader of the Great 1 September Revolution of the Socialist 
People’s Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah, Col Mu‘ammar al-Qadhdhafi, Sergej Kraigher, president 

of the Presidency of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia, paid an official 
friendly visit to the Socialist People’s Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah between 21 and 23 
November 1981. 


President Sergej Kraigher laid a wreath on the martyrs of Al-Hani battle monument. He 
also visited a number of factories and projects implemented by the Great 1 September 
Revolution. 


The commander of the Great 1 September Revolution, Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi, and President 
of the SFRY Presidency Sergej Kraigher and members of their delegations carried out 

a thorough exchange of views on the development of relations and cooperation between the 
two countries and on the most important international issues. 


The talks were held in an atmosphere of frankness, understanding and mutual respect and 
in the light of the traditional friendly relations which characterize relations between 
the two nonaligned countries. 


Col Mu‘ammar al-—OQedhdhafi acquainted the Yugoslav president with the accomplishments of 
the Creat 1 September Revolution in the political, economic and social fields and 
particularly in the building a socialist society, and the realization of direct democracy 
and the people's authority and control of their destinies in authority, wealth and arms. 
The Yugoslav president expressed his utmost admiration for these accumplishments. 


The Yugoslav president acquainted Col Mu‘ammar al-Qadhdhafi, the commander of the Great 1 
September Revolution, with Yugoslav accomplishments in internal development and with the 
progress made in the policy of economic stability. 


The two sides reiterated their strong adherence to the principles of respect for terri- 
torial security, noninterference and freedom of choice by all countries. 


The two sides stand against imperialist and other threats.

The Libyan side acquainted the Yugoslav side anew with its claims for World War II 
reparations from Italy, the FRG and Britain for the harm suffered by the country during 
and after the war. This Libyan claim has already been supported by UN and nonaligned 
resolutions. The Yugoslav side supported this claim for damages. 


During the talks, the two sides paid special attention to the question of developing their 
bilateral relations and expressed their satisfaction at the fact that cooperation between 
the two countries has been developing successfully in all fields on a basis of complete 
equality and in a spirit of the traditional friendship -- the foundation of which was 

laid down by Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi and the late President Tito. 


The two sides expressed their satisfaction at the positive development of political rela- 
tions between the two nonaligned countries and their readiness to bolster their bilateral 
and multilateral cooperation with the Nonaligned Movement member countries and with the 
United Nations through constant consultations and the exchange of views on some of the 
important international issues of interest to the Nonaligned Movement and the inter- 
national community. 


The two sides expressed their deep concern over the deterioration of international condi- 
tions which can be seen in nearly all aspects of international life and which is reflected 
most in the growing number of international problems. They believe that the factors which 
affect and cause the deterioration of international conditions are coexistence and inter- 
national trust, confrontation between alliances, the escalating arms race and particularly 
mounting nuclear armament, the desire for the widening of influences into nonaligned areas, 
the proclamation of some areas as areas of vital interests, mounting interference in the 
internal affairs of other countries, the various forms of domination and hegemony, the 
growing resort to pressures, force and armed intervention. 


All these pose a threat to independence, sovereignty and security of the countries and 
their territories and in particular the nonaligned countries as well as to world peace. 
They also make the task of ending all areas of tension more difficult and create a 
favorable atmosphere for the emergence of new crises in other parts of the world. 


Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi and the Yugoslav president paid special attention to develop- 
ments in the Arab area, Southwest Asia, Africa and the Mediterranean. 


The two sides strongly denounced the continued Zionist occupation of the Arab territ~ ies 
and the Zionist policy of aggression against the Arab countries and the Palestinian _-usple. 


They affirmed that a stable and lasting peace in the Arab area can only be achieved through 
a just and durable solution of the question, which would include unconditional and 

complete Israeli withdrawal from all the occupied Arab territories including Jerusalem, 

the recognition of the national established rights of the Palestinian Arab people to 
self-determination, return to their homeland and the establishment of their independent 
state on their land. They affirmed particularly that the Palestinian question is the 

crux of the Middle East problem and that no solution can be considered complete, just and 
acceptable without the participation of the PLO, the sole legitimate representative of 

the Palestinian Arab people as an independent and equal partner in all talks. 


The two sides denounced the expansionist policy of the Israeli aggressors in estab- 
lishing settlements on the occupied Arab territories and the changing of the historic 
and cultural features of Jerusalem with a view to annexing these lands.

The two sides reiterated the attitude adopted by the Nonaligned Movement at Havana and New 
Delhi -- an attitude which denounces unilateral and partial solutions for the Middle East 
question and the Camp David agreements. 


The two sides express their belief that the rebuilding of solidarity and unity among the 
Arab states is an essential condition for finding a just solution to the Middle East 
problem and for successfully checking the Israeli aggression, the foreign pressures and the 
attempts to transform this sensitive area of the world into an area of conflict of interests 
of the various alliances. 


The two sides reiterated their conviction that Arab unity constitutes an important factor 
toward the realization of the Arab nation's aspirations for liberation, social progress and 
the destruction of the vestiges of imperialism in all its forms. In this regard, they 
reiterated the positive role played by the Steadfastness and Confrontation Front. 


The sides strongly denounced the escalation of the Zionist Israeli aggression against 
Lebanon, which is directly aimed against Lebanon's territorial integrity and sovereignty 
and at the Palestine resistance. The two sides strongly supported the Arab countries and 
the PLO in their struggle against the Zionist aggression and those behind it. 


They expressed their readiness to bolster any international action taken by the Arab 
countries concerned, including any action taken at the United Nations. They reiterated 

the need to maintain the status quo in Lebanon on the basis of guaranteeing the country's 
independence and sovereignty and the affirmation of the legitimate authority of the Lebanese 
Government on all the Arab lands and the safeguarding of the legitimacy of the Palestine 
resistance in Lebanon. 


The two sides expressed their deep concern over the attempts being made to interfere in 

the internal affairs of the Gulf countries, including the deployment of the Rapid Deployment 
Forces. They consider these attempts as a threat to the security and independence of the 
nonaligned countries. They also urgently demand the removal of all the military bases from 
the area. 


The two sides underlined the importance of a political solution for the Afghan problem in 
accordance with the stands and principles of the nonaligned countries -- principles which 
should guarantee Afghanistan's nonalignment and the right of its people to determine their 
future and mode of development in complete freedom. 


The two sides attached yet greater importance to cooperation among the countries of the 
Mediterranean. They reiterated their attitude that Eurcpean security is closely linked 
with that of the Mediterranean and that the Mediterranean should be transformed into a 
zone of peace. 


The two sides expressed their concern over the growing presence of and rivalry between the 
imperialist forces in Africa -- a matter which poses serious threats to the independence 
and sovereignty of the African countries. They affirmed that strategic, military, economic 
and political objectives could be used as pretexts for prolonging the role of imperialism 
and imposing new forms of imperialism, hegemony and domination. 


They pointed out that the Namibian liberation movement and the establishment of majority 
rule in South Africa are basic issues affecting international peace and security and the 
speeding of the liquidation of colonialism and racial discrimination. In this regard, the 
two sides expressed their absolute support for the results achieved at the bureau of the 
coordination committee of the nonaligned countries which convened in Algeria.

They also expressed their commitment to the implementation of UN Security Council Resolution 
No 435. They reiterated their absolute support for the struggle of the Namibian people 

for independence under the leadership of SWAPO, their legitimate representative. They 

also expressed their solidarity with the frontline states which are exposed to threats 

from the racist regime in South Africa and also with the oppressed people of South Africa. 
They also expressed their solidarity with the South African people's liberation movements 
and their struggle against racial discrimination and for freedom and human dignity. 


The two sides expressed their support for the struggle of the Western Saharan people under 
the leadership of the Polisario, and for their right to self-determination and independence 
in accordance with the UN Charter and the attitudes of the OAU and the Nonaligned Movement. 


The Libyan side acquainted the Yugoslav side with recent developments in Chad. The Yugoslav 
side expressed its appreciation for the Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah's 
decision to withdraw its forces from Chad at the request of the legitimate Chadian 
Government. The Yugoslav side expressed its admiration for the role played by the Socialist 
People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah and the OAU in bringing peace and stability to this 

African country. 


The two sides denounced the imperialist attempts to strike at the national liberation move- 
ments by mixing up international terrorism and the struggle waged by the liberation organi- 
zations. 


Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi and the Yugoslav president praised the role and policy of nonalign- 
ment with regard to solving urgent international problems. They expressed the conviction 
that there can be no alternative to active peaceful existence. They affirmed the historic 
role of the Nonaligned Movement's member countries in favor of establishing a new system 

of international relations in which independence, freedom, social progress and economic 
progress in peace, security and conditions of equality become acceptable to all peoples 

and countries. They pointed out that this would strengthen further the role and policy 

of nonalignment as a driving force opposed to the division of the world into blocs and the 
use of force, particularly military intervention, as a system of international relations. 


The two sides committed themselves to bolstering the basic principles of the policy of 
nonalignment and reiterated their two countries’ complete commitment to these principles. 


They expressed their belief that it is essential for the nonaligned countries to cooperate 
more actively among themselves on the international level and particularly within the 
framework of the United Nations with a view to developing the principles and objectives 

of the Nonaligned Movement's policy. 


Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi and the president of Yugoslavia attached special attention to 

the bolstering of solidarity among the nonaligned member countries because this will 
guarantee and affirm the role of the Nonaligned Movement as an independent factor opposed 

to imperialism and hegemony in international relations. In this regard, they called on 

all the countries to fully adhere to the principles of nonalignment. The two sides share 
the view that it is essential to exert efforts to prevent conflicts among some members 

of the Nonaligned Movement and to ensure that these conflicts will not constitute a threat 
to the solidarity and coexistence of the movement as a whole and to secure the settlement 

of conflicts by peaceful means in a manner compatible with the UN Charter and the principles 
of nonalignment. 


They also affirmed the need for bolstering bilateral cooperation among the nonaligned 
countries on the basis of equality and mutual benefit as well as on solidarity and recipro- 
cal cooperation with a view to speeding the development of the movement and bolstering its 
unified action.
