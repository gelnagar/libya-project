two-way trade between Fast Germany and Iraq had soared in the past five

(about $440 million). 


About two thirds of the total was made up of Iraqi oil supplies, which 
average about 20,000 barrels a day; the Soviet Union has pegged supplies 
of crude to East Germany at 19 million tonnes a year (380,000 b/d) for the 
coming five years, the report said. 


East Germany, is however, short of hard currency with which to pay for 
crude, and, according to the report, is offering exports of industrial ma-

Impressive as the two-way trade figures may be for East Germany, they are 
very small by comparison with those for Iraqi trade with the West (AN-

plus in Iraq's favour; in the first quarter of this year, Iraqi exports 
to the EEC were running at an annual average of $11.2 billion and imports 
at a rate of $4.5 billion.

WESTERN SUPPORT MAY ENABLE NIMETRI TO SAVE ECONOMY 
Kuwait AL-RA'Y AL='AMM in Arabic 3 Jun 80 p 17 


[Article: ‘Economic Crisis and Anxiety Prevail in North and South, But 
New Development Projects Could Save Situation") 


[Text] The Sudan, the largest African nation, has become one of the most 
important nations to the West. It is now the second largest nation in 
Africa which receives British and West German aid. Western commercial 
interests are deeply involved in the Sudan's economic development. Stra- 
tegically, the Sudan falls between two countries hostile to the West, 
Ethiopia and Libya, who are both bristling with Soviet arms. Western 
nations favor President Gaafar Nimeiri, the popular military leader who 
has been ruling this country for 1l years. They like him because he seems 
to be a pragmatic, realistic man, who steered away from the socialist 
method followed by Moscow and leaned toward free enterprise; because his 
feelings are strongly pro-West; because his moderation has its influence 
in African and Arab politics; and because the Sudan has a good history 

of human rights and applies the principle of individual ri,hts more than 
any other country in Africa or the Middle East. 


Economic Deterioration 


Despite all the above, Nimeiri's country has a deteriorating economic 
situation, where major services are chaotic and production is stagnant 
and declining. The Sudan was forced to conduct economic reform, which 
angered its people, in order to satisfy the international monetary fund 
which recommended such reform, Furthermore, there are more than 490,000 
refugees from Ethiopia, Uganda and other places who impose a burden on 
the deteriorating economy. Thus, the country now suffers from a deep 
crisis which leads many Sudanese seeking higher wages to travel to Libya 
and the Arabian peninsula. 


The Heat Wave 


The summer temperature is now rising in Khartoum. This is usually asso- 
ciated with political unrest and attempts to overthrow the government.
