LIBYA

PAPER COMMENTS ON EGYPT'S ARMS ACQUISITIONS 


PM201527 London AL-ZAHAF AL-AKHDAR in English 14 Jan 83 p 4 
[Unattributed article: "Egypt: Arming for What?"] 


[Excerpts] Egypt's quiet arms build-up continues, yet with the country wedded to a 
humiliating relationship with both the United States and Israel, it is hard to see what 
the arms are for, at least in the context of Egypt's present policies. At the same 
time, Egyptian arms sales have grown to become the country's second largest export. 


Last November, the first locally-assembled model of the Franco-German Alpha jet strike 
aircraft and jet trainer was delivered to the Egyptian Air Force. Egypt has 45 of the 
planes on order, all but eight of which are to be assembled from components supplied by 
the makers France's Dassault-Breguet and West Germany's Dornier. The planes will replace 
existing Czech-built jet trainers and will, thanks to their advanced equipment, prove to 
be a small, but valuable addition to Egypt's air power. 


Egypt concluded a similar deal recently with China for the local assembly and part- 
manufacture of obsolete Chinese F-7 fighters, based on the Soviet MIG-21 already in 
Egyptian service. The above-mentioned deals, together with last year's billion dollar 
deal for the supply of twenty Dassault Mirage 2000 fighters, show that Mubarak's regime 
is continuing to pursue a certain diversification of arms supplies, although the bulk 
of these still of course come from the United States. 


The U.S. arms connection cannot be under-valued, for the major proportion of Egypt's 
re-equipment programme consists of the purchase, either directly or on credit, of large 
quantities of U.S. arms, ranging from F-16 fighters, 40 of which are on order, to M-60 
main battle tanks which are now replacing earlier Soviet models. At the same time, 
Egypt herself is building up her own arms industry, which now produces a range of 

items from training planes to anti-tank missiles, mines and tank and artillery 
ammunition. 


A Major Exporter 


The result of this, as was revealed recently by Defence Minister ‘Abd al-Halim Abu 
Ghazalah, isthat Egypt itself has become a major arms exporter, selling a billion 
dollars’ worth of arms in the last financial year. But it is clear from Abu Ghazalah's 
statement that these exports have been used to finance Egypt's own arms purchases. 
Thus, Egypt's arms sales serve to permit a degree of self-financing within the Defence 
Ministry, which of course increases the military's political weight in the country. 


Deputy Defence Minister Mustafa Sadiq recently affirmed that Egypt does not want to 
assemble weapons from kits, we want to produce them here’. But what are these weapons 
for and to whom has Egypt been selling arms? To answer the last question first, the 
major recipient of Egyptian weaponry has been Saddam Husayn's regime in Iraq. Surplus 
T-55 tanks and spare parts and ammunition for Soviet-supplied equipment which are now 
made in Egypt have been dispatched to Saddam's armies fighting the Iranians in the Gulf 
war. This is certainly not just in the interests of the Egyptian regime, but also those 
of the United States, whose support for Iraq has thus far been low key. 


The question which naturally arises when considering Egypt's arms acquisitions and 
exports is how they relate to Egyptian foreign policy.
