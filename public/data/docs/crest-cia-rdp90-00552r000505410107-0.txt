Summary (AI): A Washington Post article reports U.S. concern in December 1981 over an unconfirmed intelligence report that a five-person Libyan assassination squad had entered the United States to kill President Reagan or his top advisers. Secret Service protection was ordered for senior White House aides, and the Libyan news agency JANA denounced the claims as lies.
• An informant who is not an American gave U.S. authorities the names of five people he claimed were members of a Libyan assassination squad.
• The informant's credibility had not been ascertained and his story had not been verified, according to intelligence sources.
• Federal investigators were reviewing case files on Edwin P. Wilson and Francis E. Terpil but not because of the assassination team; they sought information on sources about terrorist or assassin training in Libya.
• The JANA news agency called the U.S. claims a series of lies, monitored in Beirut by Reuters.
• ABC News reported U.S. intelligence had partially identified some alleged Libyan agents by name and picture.
• 1981-12-03: Reported entry of a five-person Libyan assassination squad into the United States over the weekend, based on an unconfirmed informant; Secret Service protection ordered for James Baker, Edwin Meese and Michael Deaver
