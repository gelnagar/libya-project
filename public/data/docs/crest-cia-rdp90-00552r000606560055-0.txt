Summary (AI): A 1981 Washington Post article reports on a Fort Collins, Colorado trial of Eugene Tafoya, a former Green Beret accused of shooting Libyan dissident Faisal Zagallai on a 1980 contract allegedly tied to Edwin P. Wilson and Qaddafi's death decree. It concerns Libyan regime-directed killings abroad, not places in Tripoli.
• Qaddafi allegedly issued a 'death decree' in February 1980 against Libyan opponents abroad.
• Tafoya admitted shooting Zagallai twice in the head, claiming self-defense and that he worked for the CIA (the CIA denied this).
• Judge Miller barred prosecution from presenting a purported hit list and chemical delay pencils seized from Tafoya's home.
• Tafoya traveled to Libya three times before the shooting and once after, per passports allowed as evidence.
• 1980-10-13: Zagallai says a woman called his apartment offering a job interview with a recruiter for U.S. companies doing business in the Near East; Tafoya later allegedly shot Zagallai at his apartment in the Fort Collins area.
