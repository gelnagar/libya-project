Summary (AI): A 1981 New York Times report on the trial in Fort Collins, Colorado, of Eugene Tafoya, a former Green Beret charged with the 1980 shooting of Libyan dissident Faisal Zagallai, a Colorado State graduate student. The defense said Tafoya believed he was carrying a CIA message, and a source suggested Libya may have hired him. Nothing here places the events in Tripoli.
• Trial held at the Larimer County Courthouse in Fort Collins, Colorado; jury of nine women and three men.
• Prosecutor said he would call as many as 50 witnesses to show Tafoya plotted to kill Zagallai.
• Tafoya applied for a CIA job when discharged from the military in 1976; in 1979 a man who appeared to know his application contacted him.
• Before trial, a source said Tafoya might have been hired by the Libyan government to kill Zagallai.
• 1980-10-14: Eugene Tafoya allegedly shot Faisal Zagallai, a Libyan dissident graduate student at Colorado State University, at his apartment; Zagallai was shot twice in the head, survived, and lost sight in one eye.
