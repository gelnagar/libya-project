Summary (AI): A 1987 Washington Times article reports the White House denying that the April 1986 US raids were planned to assassinate Qaddafi, while a New York Times magazine piece (Seymour Hersh) alleged a hit plan. It describes the raids on Tripoli and Sidi Bilal, the death of Qaddafi's adopted daughter, and the targeting of his compound and family.
• The raids were carried out by F-111s; laser guidance failed on four of nine F-111s according to Hersh's sources.
• White House said there was no plan to kill Qaddafi per se, and that bombs on his compound were aimed at a military barracks 200 meters away.
• Hersh reported North proposed Stealth bomber, submarine-launched Tomahawk, and a Navy SEAL laser-guided team; all were rejected.
• Hersh reported that State Department lawyers prepared a legal paper arguing that in military action murder is not murder.
• Qaddafi's adopted daughter was reported 15 months old and killed in the 1986 raids.
• 1986-04-14: US bombing raids on Tripoli and Sidi Bilal; Qaddafi's 15-month-old adopted daughter killed, his wife and eight other children hospitalized, Qaddafi not reported injured. (1 killed)
• 1984-11-12: Former Libyan Prime Minister Abdul Hamid Bakkoush photographed in a pool of blood in Egypt; Egypt had faked the photo and he was not hurt, revealed after Libya claimed to have executed him.
