[Excerpts] Good evening. In the name of God. At the sixth 
anniversary of the revolution we said that the people lead the 
transitional stage. The stage of revolutionary transformation or 
transitional stage cannot be exclusively run by any individual or 
party. It cannot be a justification for governing on behalf of the 
Libyan people, nor can it be an excuse for the emergence of a 
dictatorship. It also cannot be a suitable vessel for military and 
fascist domination of the (?whole) of the country. 


The transitional! period should be the suitable medium for involv- 
ing the Libyan people as individuals in the building the popular 
and democratic state. The popular and democratic society cannot 
be attained unless the Libyan people exercise popular democratic 
experiences from now on. 


As of the sixth anniversary of the revolution, the historical 
turning point toward building popular democracy and establish- 
ing the people's state, the state of the masses had begun. Conse- 
quently, any discussions or efforts on the part of myself or anyone 
else are designed to implement the revolutionary program, which 
is in fact the establishment of the popular power, the 
strengthening of the popular will, and completing the construc- 
tion of the Jamahiriah system. 


Since the sixth anniversary the burden has been placed on the 
shoulders of the Libyan people, that is, they are the ones leading 
the stage of transformation. Evidence of this is the emergence of 
the people’s power, the popular congresses, the emergence of the 
popular committees. Now the socialist companies are beginning 
to be established.

In front of me is the agenda which has been drawn up by the 
popular congresses and popular committees. If | or any of my 
colleagues in the revolution command have an idea, we present 
it to the people, and we explain our thoughts to the people so that 
they can adopt or reject them. | had previously put forward ideas 
that were revolutionary and basic, but the people did not adopt 
them, such as primary education [words indistinct] which is not

issues | had put forward but which had not been adopted by the 
people. Today the people are free. They can reject or accept what 
they like, even the ideas which | personally put forward. 


I will not deal with the whole agenda but | would like only to 
stress some of the points on the agenda, to make them more easily 
understood by the people who may have trouble understanding 
them without explanations points which | think are important 
and are connected with the revolution that | am leading with 
revolutionary transformation. 


Before | start on the agenda there is an issue written down on the

| assured this man that if it were up to me, because of his appeals 
and the message from the archbishop, and because of the inter-

understand that | am not a head of state or a prime minister, 
similar to Thatcher. Authority rests in the hands of the Libyan 
people. Therefore, | promised him that | would present the matter 
to the people’s congresses for them to discuss as the first item on 
the occasion of the beginning of the New Year 1985. 


There are Libyans detained in Britain, some of whom have been 
sentenced and some still under interrogation. In fact, the British 
Government is not in a position to interfere in a legal or criminal 
issue. But | assured the envoy of the Church of England that the 
issuc is basically political. The detention of the Libyans in Britain 
is for political reasons, and constitutes a political detention. | 
stress here that the British Government is to a great extent in 
collusion with the government of the United States which is 
extremely hostile to the Arab nation, to the Socialist People’s 
Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah, and to me personally as the leader. 
Therefore, the matter as a whole is a political issue. We refuse, 
and will continuc to refuse, to look at the detained and sentenced 
Libyans from a purely judiciary and legal view.
