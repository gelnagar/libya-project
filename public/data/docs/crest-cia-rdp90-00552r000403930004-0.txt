Summary (AI): A 1980 Chicago Tribune article, reproduced in a CIA reading-room file, reports that a CIA document sent to the Justice Department outlined a multimillion-dollar Libyan effort to influence U.S. policy and opinion by cultivating American figures, including Billy Carter, farm leaders and Idaho and Georgia politicians. It concerns Libyan influence-buying in the United States rather than conditions inside Libya.
• Shadyac said he spent more than $225,000 on air fare to bring 125 Americans to Libya after the President's brother's first visit in late 1978.
• A CIA document described by Director Turner said Libya worked to win favor in three areas: lucrative broker offers, commissions on peanut, soybean and wheat deals, and paid trips to Libya.
• Senator Bayh's nine-member panel would decide after Oct. 4 whether to widen its examination of Libyan influence-buying.
• Trip participants included former Senator William Fulbright, Rep. Nick Joe Rahal, former FAA administrator Najeeb Halaby, and Wyoming state senator Bill Edwards.
• Senators James McClure and Rep. Steven Symms, both Idaho Republicans, reportedly travelled to Libya.
• 1978-10: Richard Shadyac's group, described as an Arab-American dialogue seminar, travelled to Libya days after Billy Carter's visit; some Americans were offered business deals by Libyan hosts.
