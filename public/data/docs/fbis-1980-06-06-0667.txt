LIBYA

JALLUD COMMENTS ON 'LIQUIDATING! LIBYAN EXPATRIATES 
AU041959 Rome ANSA in English 1930 GMT 4 Jun 80 


[Text] Rome, June 4 (ANSA)--'Abd as-Salam Jallud, the number two man in Libyan 
Jamahiriyah!s Revolutionary Command Council, put the killing of Libyan nationals abroad 
in the context of a battle between good and evil and appealed to Italy, where four such 
Slayings have taken place, for comprehension of the requirements of the Libyan revolu- 
tionary struggle in an interview published in today's edition of the Rome daily 

IL MESSAGGERO, 


Referring to the link between the killing of Libyan expatriates in Europe and the Libyan 
leader Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi's statement on "liquidating" the enemies of the Tripoli 
regime, Jallud said that "Al-Qadhdhafi has never said to kill that one or that one. 

He has only indicated the danger posed by these people. "The Revolutionary Council makes 
note of the cases and then the revolutionaries, as individuals, act," he explained. 

Jallud went on to disclose that no orders are given. "These are all personal initiatives, 
Not even I am able to know in advance the decisions of the Revolutionary Council, The 
people decide. The people act," 


Broadening the topic the Libyan official declared that his country!s revolution is 
"profound" and compared the developments in the North African state to the "French 
revolution." “Europe must not judge all this as terrorism. it would be a naive 
interpretation". 


Jallud connected the displeasure of the Revolutionary Command Council with the fact 

that "many of the people who fled abroad took with them the wealth of the Libyan people. 
They illegally exported currency. They are thieves like your (Sindona)," he said, 
comparing these Libyan expatriates to Italian financier (Michele Sindona), found guilty 

by a United States Federal Court on most of the 99 counts connected to the bankruptcy of 
New York's Franklin National Bank. The Libyan revolutionary council official then declared 
that, "We hope that the Italian people become aware of the requirements of our struggle, of 
the pathway of the revolution, This is a fight between Libyans, between good and evil", 


He went on to note that, "The presence in Italy of enemies of the revolution, of people 
who hate what we are doing, harms relations between our countries. We have conducted many 
efforts to overcome the past and we will not (?bring) about new tensions. We must work 
together to maintain our good relations", Jallud urged and added, "Italy has an enormous 
stake in this area". 


In reply to a question of possible retaliatory measures against Italy if Libyan nationals 
are not expelled from the country on the request of the Tripoli authorities, Jallud 
said, "I cannot exclude this. The Revolutionary Council will decide. They are the ones 
who have thus far indicated the road to follow, autonomously". 


In his statement to members of the military in Banghazi, Al-Qadhdhafi fixed the deadline 
for the reentry of Libyan expatriates as June 10.

Another Italian daily, Turin's LA STAMPA, took up today the issue of the threat to 
Libyans living abroad and cited measures taken by the Italian police for the surveillance 
of suspected Libyan gunmen carrying out the desire of the Revolutionary Command Council. 
According to the STAMPA account, the likely targets in Italy are some 800 Libyan expat- 
riates out of a total of 2,000 Libyan nationals in the country.
