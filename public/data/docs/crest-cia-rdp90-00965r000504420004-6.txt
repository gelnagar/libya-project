Summary (AI): A 1985 New York Times article (datelined Paris) reports that Libyan opposition groups are weak and that neighbouring states are unlikely to help a US-backed plan to topple Colonel Qaddafi. It covers the National Front, Egypt, Tunisia, Morocco, Algeria and Sudan.
• The National Front has representatives in London, Rome, Paris, Baghdad and the United States, and runs an anti-Libya radio station from Cairo for four hours a day.
• Algeria denied the week before that it would countenance an American-backed plan against Qaddafi.
• Diplomats said Qaddafi was heavily surrounded by East German security guards.
• An American executive order prohibits direct or indirect US participation in assassination attempts.
• Sadat reportedly wanted to invade Libya in the 1970s but was held back by US opposition.
• 1984-07: Egypt humiliated Qaddafi by permitting an assassination attempt (summer 1984 per OCR, possibly misprinted as 1964) and denounced Libya for its assassination attempt; the article also mentions a Libyan-sponsored hit squad that shot exiles in Egypt.
