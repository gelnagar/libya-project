Summary (AI): A 1981 New York Times article reports the CIA denied official involvement in the Libyan activities of former agents Edwin Wilson and Frank Terpil, who were charged with shipping explosives to Libya. It says Wilson was living in Tripoli at the time, though the piece is mainly about U.S. intelligence, not Tripoli itself.
• CIA issued a roughly 200-word unsigned statement denying official involvement, prompted by news reports.
• Wilson left the CIA in 1971 but worked for the Office of Naval Intelligence until April 1976.
• Wilson and Terpil were indicted in 1980; a superseding indictment was unsealed in October 1981.
• The House Select Committee on Intelligence was investigating the case.
• 1976: Meeting at which Wilson planned the sale of explosive timing devices to Libya, which Thomas Clines acknowledged attending.
• 1977-04: Stansfield Turner, then CIA director, conducted a more thorough investigation after a news account of Wilson's Libyan activities, leading to dismissal of two employees.
• 1980: Federal grand jury indicted Wilson and Terpil for activities on behalf of Libya in 1976 and 1977.
