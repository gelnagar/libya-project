Summary (AI): A New York Times article (1 November 1981, datelined Washington, Oct. 31) reports that Libya's intervention in Chad relied on American pilots and technicians working under former CIA agent Edwin P. Wilson, who was a fugitive living in Tripoli. It describes Libyan Air Force Chinook and Hercules flights, a "clean room" facility, and recruitment of pilots and oil technicians.
• Libyan Air Force had a fleet of 20 CH-47 Chinook helicopters.
• Wilson's Swiss company recruited and paid the American pilots, which the Justice Department said may not violate federal law.
• A sterile, soundproof 'clean room' in Libya was reportedly used for assembly of conventional and possibly nuclear weapons; US intelligence said Libya did not then possess nuclear weapons.
• Wilson was indicted by a federal grand jury for illegally shipping explosives to Libya and reindicted in October 1981.
• Wilson left US government employ in 1976 after 15 years with the CIA.
• 1980: Libyan military intervention in Chad, which the article says began late last year (1980), was resupplied by Western-piloted aircraft.
• 1976: Wilson and former agent Frank E. Terpil signed a contract to sell their services to Libya.
