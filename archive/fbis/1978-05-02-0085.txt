to the peoples of the African Continent.

BEGIN INTERVIEWED ON U.S. VISIT, DAYAN'S TALKS 
TAOQ12025¥Y Jerusalem Domestic Television Service in Hebrew 1900 GM? 1 May 78 TA 


{interview with Prime Minister Menahem Begin by Dan Shilon on the plane from New York 
to Washington on 1 May--videotaped ] 


{Text ] [Question] Mr Prime Minister, a few weeks ago you left Washington with what 
appeared a heavy heart. How do you feel today on your return to Washington? 


[Answer] I did not leave with a heavy heart then. I said that the talks were hard, 

and I think I fulfilled my duty as the representative of Israel. However, these visits 
are totally different. This time I have come to take part in the celebrations of the 
30th anniversary of our independence, This is the objective of the visit. The president 
as well as the secretary of state invited me for a talk. I accepted the invitation 
willingly, and the talks will be held today. Therefore, no comparison should be drawn 
between these two visits. Then the goal was meeting with the administration, while now 
the real objective is to meet with the Jewish community that is celebrating the 30th 
jubilee, 


[Question] Do you, in view of the report you received from Foreign Minister Moshe Dayan 
yesterday, have some answers to the questions Dayan was asked here last week? 


[Answer] Yes, I received a detailed report. I think that regarding certain questions, 
discussion should be held and government decision should be made, This is what I think, 
This will be clarified in the talks. If we can say something substantive on the spot, 

I will willingly do so. However, the authority of the government should be kept. This 


is how the foreign minister acted and this is how I will act. 
s 


[Question] In your opinion, do these questions mean that there is a possibility that 
different decisions may be taken by the government with regard to its basic policy? 


[Answer] No. The basis is our peace plan. The intention is a positive and desired 
intention, I think, to talk this time about the essence, about the substance and contents. 
This is not simple--it is being clarified. Therefore, it does not constitute a substantive 
change but a change in procedures. These procedures are very important though, We will 
continue the clarification of this question today. 


[Question] Are there, as Dayan reported, signs of a thaw in Israeli-U.S. relations? 
[Answer] The atmosphere during his talks was good. There were very serious and thorough 
clarifications on both sides, Mr Dayan indeed emphasized that the government will be 
called on to make decisions on certain subjects,

[From the Morning Newsreel ] 

(Text ] Let us first go to Los Angeles, where the prime minister is now making the 
central speech at a massive rally to mark Israel's 30th anniversary. Approximately 


12,000 Jews are taking part in the rally. Our correspondent Eli Nissan accompanied 
the prime minister on his journey to the West. [begin recording] 


V. 2 May 78 N 2 ISRAEL 


The prime minister flew from Washington to Los Angeles aboard a special Israel air force 
aircraft. He was accompanied by a large entourage of both Israeli and U.S. correspondents 
who have been covering the entire tour in the United States. During the flight, Mr RBerzin 
talked with representatives of the Israeli press, and pointed out the following issues: 
fhere has been a marked improvement in the atmosphere of the relations between Israel 

ani the United States. The atmosphere is this time totally different from that which 
prevailed during the prime minister's visit in March. Sources in the prime minister's 
entourage say that the change in the atmosphere is not incidental, and that it seems to 
have resulted from a high-level decision. Mr Begin stated that there were no traps during 
his talk with the president. The pleasant atmosphere was genuine and sincere, and found 
its expression in President Carter's speech on the White House lawn. The prime minister 
stated that it was a morally significant speech. 


On the plane, Mr Begin sounded very optimistic. He later said that a breakthrough in 
the political negotiations is to be expected in the near future. In his opinion, the talks 
held by Foreign Minister Moshe Dayan last week have contributed to this change in atmosphere. 


Israel does not intend to change its peace plan, but it comes with new ideas. Within a 
few weeks, the government will convene to deal with matters that, in Mr Begin's opinion, 
may be termed significant. 


At the end of the political meeting with President Carter, Mr Begin met with him again for 
a tete-a-tete that lasted approximately a quarter of an hour. The president said some 
important things during that talk, Mr Begin said, however, I regret that I cannot quote 
them. 


The prime minister said that his preliminary talk with Secretary of State Cyrus Vance was 
also sincere and straightforward. "We have clarified things, and promised each other 
answers," the prime minister said. For the time being the prime minister refused to 
elaborate on the nature of the questions and of the requested answers. However, it was 
gathered from his words that the political clarifications with the United States have now 
taken a new direction the essence of which is a discussion of the contents of peace, rather 
than mere formulas. [end recording] 


Eli Nissan reported from Los Angeles. 
Activities in U.S. Reported 
TAO20456Y Jerusalem Domestic Service in English 0500 GMT 2 May 78 TA 


[Excerpt] In Washington, Prime Minister Begin met with President Carter and Secretary 

of State Vance. Our reporter says that the arms package was not brought up in the discussion. 
However, our reporter adds that presidential adviser Zbignew Brzezinski attempted to 
convince Jewish leaders to stop their campaign against the arms package. 


Following the prime minister's meeting, a reception was held at the White House in honor 

of Israel's 30th anniversary. President Carter promised that the United States would continue 
to be committed to Israel's security, not just for 30 years, but forever. He added that 

in spite of the differences, the United States would never waiverin its absolute commitment 

to Israel. Carter also announced that he would appoint a presidential commission to make 
recommendations for a federal memorial to honor the 6 million Jews killed in World War II. 


1 response, Prime Minister Begin called Carter's pledge "one of the greatest ever made
