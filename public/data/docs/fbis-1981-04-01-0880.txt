ARAB CONGRESS ADVOCATES ACTION AGAINST HAIG TOUR 


LD312150 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1910 GMT 31 Mar 81 


[Text] Tripoli, 31 Mar (JANA)--The Permanent Secretariat of the Arab People's Congress this 
evening issued the following appeal to the Arab masses, regarding the intended visit by the 
U.S. secretary of state: 


Everyone knows the role which American imperialism plays in the Arab world and the Middle 
East, particularly since the Camp David agreements, which humiliated Egypt and made its great 
people, history and struggle subject to American imperialism and international Zionism. These 
forces use Egypt's potential in the service of conspiracies opposed to the Arabs and to peace- 
loving and justice-loving people throughout the world. 


The dangers of American policies in the Arab world have increased since Reagan took power, 
because of the hostile policies he declared against the Arab nation and its national and 
progressive forces. 


Thus, the visit by the U.S. secretary of state to Egypt and the Arab region, which begins 

14 April, marks the beginning of implementing anti-Arab policies. The Permanent Secretariat 
of the Arab “2ople's Congress therefore draws the attention of the Arab masses and their 
national, democratic and progressive forces to the importance of expressing their anger and 
rejection of American policy--in any way they see fit. The Secretariat hopes these forces 
will act swiftly against this visit, both on the policial level and on the level of informa- 
tion and the media, and that they will condemn imperialist U.S. policy and its aggressive 
plots. 


The honor and the freedom of the Arabs, bought at a high price by generations of our people-- 
indeed our independence and our very existence--are in danger of being destroyed. Let us 
therefore arise to defend ourselves. 


[Signed] The Permanent Secretariat of the Arab People's Congress 


COMMENTARY EXAMINES U.S. ARMS SUPPLY TO MOROCCO 


LD311954 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 31 Mar 81 
{Unattributed commentary] 


[Text] The U.S. declaration made through the U.S. deputy assistant secretary of state 
for Middle East and South Asian affairs, Morris Draper, with regard to the unconditional 
resumption of the supply of U.S. arms and ammunition to the Moroccan regime, came after 
a period of U.S. hesitation.

I wonder what the reasons are which made the U.S. administration resume the supply of arms 
to the Moroccan regime after a period of partial, if not total, suspension? 


The reply to this question demonstrates the role of the Moroccan regime as the first and 
most important factor in the U.S. administration's decision to resume supplies of war 
equipment, within the framework of the position which this regime occupies in U.S. policy 
in the Arab region in particular and in Africa in general. 


In 1960, a treaty was signed between Morocco and the United States on arms supplies. The 

United States made it a condition that the Moroccan regime should not use those arms 

outside its internationally recognized borders. But this last phase was amended to read 
“outside its borders." This implies agreement between the United States and the Rabat regime to use 
the U.S. arms in military tasks outside Morocco. This demonstrates in the first place 

the threat not only to Western Sahara but also to neighboring countries. 


A study of the quantity and kind of the developed arms which the United States is to give 
the Moroccan regime--bombers, missile launchers, guided missiles, reconnaissance aircraft 
and modern tanks--reveals the fact that the volume of the Moroccan action is not confined 
only to neighboring countries but it goes beyond this to link up with racist South Africa, 
which in its turn is getting the same arms from the United States within the framework of - 
South Africa's colonialist role in Africa in implementing the colonialist plans. 


The fact is that the U.S. colonialists look toward Africa, and its reliance on racist 

or reactionary support falls within the framework of developments in recent years, in the 
wake of the severe blow that U.S. interests received in Iran following the overthrow of 
the shah and the triumph of the revolution. 


The U.S. decision to resume arms supplies to Morocco, in response to the king's request, 

is in line with the motives of the Moroccan regime, which has failed in the Saharan war 

and suffered frequent defeat= at the hands of Polisario. Thus, the U.S. decision, according 
to these facts, underlines that U.S. policy seeks to spread its domination over the world. 


JALLUD, NICARAGUA'S BORGE EXCHANGE DINNER SPEECHES 


LD271522 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 27 Mar 81 


[Excerpts] Staff Major ‘Abd as-Salam Jallud has stressed that the victory of the people 
of Nicaragua under the leadership of the Sandinista Front was no ordinary victory against 
American colonialism and the reactionary and bourgeois forces. 


Staff Major Jallud, speaking at a dinner banquet held yesterday in honor of Tomas Borge, 
member of the National Command of the Sandinista Front and Nicaraguan interior minister, 
said that this victory is a victory for the people's revolution and an end to the revolution 
of the few and the elite and a start of new phase, that is, the phase of the people's 
revolution, which has become the only path for the salvation of the peoples. He added 

that the victory of the people's revolution in Libya and the victory of the people's 
revolution in Iran and in Nicaragua confirm that the peoples have turned to the path of 
people's revolution. 


Staff Major ‘Abd as-Salam Jallud stressed that the Great Fatih Revolution is allied with 
the people's revolution in Nicaragua against colonial plots. He said: fhe people 

of Nicaragua continued along th‘s path and were able to snatch victory after a lengthy 
struggle.

Staff Major Jallud stressed that the talks confirmed the coincidence of the viewpoints of 
both revolutions for their faith in people's authority, the armed people, partners and 

not hired people, and that home is for the person who lives in it, and he said that he 
believes that the happiness of man can be achieved only by lifting all pressures off him. 
He said: We are certain that the people in Nicaragua are capable of defeating the enemies, 
of achieving victory and of continuing with the people's revolution. 


Mr Tomas Borge, member of the Sandinista National Command and Nicaraguan interior minister, 
for his part praised the extraordinary efforts of the Socialist People's Libyan Arab 
Jamariyah in supporting liberation movements. He stressed that these efforts are a model 
of revolutionary work. He confirmed that there is coincidence in the viewpoints of the 
two revolutions. He stressed the solidarity of the people of Nicaragua with all peoples 
struggling for their freedom and independence and the readiness of the people of Nicaragua 
to fight against all enemies. He also stressed the solidarity of the people of Nicaragua 
with the Libyan Arab people against all colonial challenges. He also confirmed the read- 
iness of the people of Nicaragua to fight against American colonialism in Latin America. 

He explained that the people of Nicaragua will never surrender to the American threat. 


JOINT STATEMENT ISSUED AT END OF BORGE VISIT 


LDO010330 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 31 Mar 81 


[Joint statement issued at the conclusion of the 25-31 March visit to Libya of Nicaraguan 
interior minister Tomas Borge] 


[Text] At the invitation of the revolutionary command of the Socialist People's Libyan 
Arab Jamahiriyah [SPLAJ], Tomas Borge, member of the National Command of the Sandinist 
Front and minister of interior of Nicaragua, paid an official visit to the SPLAJ from 25 
to 31 March 1981, heading a delegation comprised of the minister of mining and energy, 
the governor of the Bank of Nicaragua, the deputy minister of planning and investments, 
the deputy minister of agricultural planning, the deputy minister of the interior, and 
representatives of Nicaraguan republican and cultural organizations. 


The guest and those accompanying him were warmly welcomed in the Jamahiriyah by Maj 
al-Khuwaylidi al-Humaydi, commander of the militarization of the towns and member of the 
General Secretariat of the General People's Congress, and members of the General People's 
Committee. The guest was received by brother Colonel Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi, leader of 
the Great Fatih Revolution. He had meetings with Staff Major ‘Abd as-Salam Ahmad Jallud 
and Lt Col Mustafa al-Kharrubi. The guest conveyed to the brother leader the apprecia- 
tion of the people of Nicaragua and of the leadership of the Sandinist National Front to 
the people of the SPLAJ for the assistance they gave to the people of Nicaragua in their 
struggle to overthrow the fascist ruling regime and for their stand with them in their 
efforts to build their country. Tomas Borge stressed the similarities of the two popular 
revolutions and of their joint concepts and principles in confronting colonialism and 
imperialism. 


He stressed that the solutions included in the Third World theory, as given in the Green 
Book, are valuable guidelines for the solution of the chronic fundamental problems of 
humanity. The Third World theory, which heralds the final freedom of humanity and the 
emergence of the people's authority, is in agreement with the aims of the Nicaraguan 
popular revolution, which believes in the precept “Authority, resources and weapons must 
be in the hands of the people." He also stressed that the Green Book includes funda- 
mental principles, the understanding of which is considered important to the future of 
the march of progress of the Nicaraguan popular revolution. He also supported the pre- 
cept "freedom is one and is indivisible" and noted that the popular revolutions in the 
Jamahiriyah and Nicaragua recognize that the freedom of their peoples is incomplete as 
long as there are other peoples suffering from colonialism, domination and control over 
their resources.

The guest expressed his pleasure at visiting the SPLAJ while its people were celebrating 
the anniversary of the expulsion of the British colonial forces from the homeland. 


Talks took place between the Arab Libyan and Nicaraguan sides in a spirit of fraternity 
and friendship. They exchanged views on the situations in the Arab homeland, Central 
America, the Caribbean and Africa and on the international situation in general. The 
stands of both countries are the same regarding the present problems and issues and are in 
accordance with the previous joint statements issued in Managua and Tripoli. 


The Libyan Arab side for its part once again stressed its appreciation of and admiration 

for the active role played by the people of Nicaragua in laying the foundations, principles 
and bases of the revolution under the command of the Sandinist National Front, in con- 
fronting the American imperialist plot and its agents and in overcoming the economic 
pressures and armed aggression of reactionary regimes. It stressed that the Great Fatih 
Revolution will always firmly stand along side the Nicaraguan popular revolution. The two 
sides agreed to maintain the solidarity of the two revolutions and continuously develop 

it until the will of their two friendly peoples triumphs against the enemies and imperialist 
plots and until the aims of the oppressed and struggling peoples for liberation and progress 
and for building a human society free from injustice and exploitation are realized. 


On this occasion and within the framework of strengthening the relations of cooperation, 
solidarity and joint struggle, the two sides signed bilateral agreements in the spheres of 
economic, commercial, agricultural, scientific and cultural cooperation and on the continued 
exchange of visits at all levels. 


Tomas Borge, member of the National Command of the Sandinist Front and minister of interior 
of Nicaragua, in the name of the command and the people of Nicaragua, extended an invitation 
to brother Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi, leader of the Great Fatih Revolution, to visit 
Nicaragua. The brother leader has accepted the invitation, the date of which is to be fixed 
later.
