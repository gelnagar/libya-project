Summary (AI): A 1980 Washington Post article reports that the White House acknowledged President Carter discussed low-classification State Department cables about Billy Carter's September 1978 Libya trip with his brother, and that Billy Carter claimed Jimmy gave him the cables. It also covers the C-130 export dispute and the Justice Department inquiry into Billy Carter's Libyan ties.
• Billy Carter reportedly said he was paid $20,000 by the Libyans via a check Randy Coleman picked up in Washington on Dec. 27 (per sources).
• The U.S. had refused an export license for eight C-130 aircraft that Libya paid for in the early 1970s.
• Justice Department documents said Billy Carter was invited to Libya with an invitation addressed to the brother of the US president, and was told he could bring anyone except Jews.
• The House Judiciary Committee approved a resolution of inquiry; a House floor vote could not occur before Aug. 19, after the Democratic National Convention.
• 1978-09: Billy Carter's first trip to Libya, reported in embassy cables that the White House said indicated the trip had gone well.
• 1980-01-16: Justice Department interview of Billy Carter, in which he said he had received State Department cables from the president and had been paid by the Libyans.
