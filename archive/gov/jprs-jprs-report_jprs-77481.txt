In brief, the objectives of the plan can be summarized as follows, he said: 


1. Achieve an annual growth rate of 9.4 percent in the economic activity iusuring 
that the industrial sector has a growth rate of 21.6 percent; agriculture, 7.4 per- 
cent; electricity, 15.1 percent; transport and communications, 7.4 percent; education, 
8 percent; health, 10 percent; public services, 5 percent; construction, 1.5 percent; 
and housing, 7.1 percent. 


2. Increase the contribution of nonpetroleum activities to the national income from 
35.7 percent in 1980 to 53 percent in 1985, and lower the petroleum contribution in 
the same way. 


3. Decrease imports for local requirements--from (6.5 percent to 38 percent for 
foodstuffs ([matieres alimentaires] and from 73 percent to 65 percent for food products 
[produits alimentaires]. 


The secretary of the General Peoples Committee explained that during the next 5-year 
plan 1980-85 [as printed], the productive sectors will be emphasized; in fact, the 
industrial sector will be allocated approximately 4 billions or 22 percent of credits 
allocated to the 5-,/ear conversion plan. Likewise, the agricultural s« tor is 
allocated approximately 3 billions or 18 percent. 


The secretary of the General Peoples Congress explained that the basic peoples con- 
gresses have affirmed the conversion of Libyan Arab society from a consumer society 
into a socialist productive society, in conformity with the theses of the third 
international theory [as printed], as well as the increase in productive ability and 
the different means of production and the fact of deriving benefit from all abilities. 


"These congresses have also affirmed the absolute priority given education and tech- 
nical training serving the objectives of the 5-year potential in order to achieve 
self-sufficiency,” the secretary of the General Peoples Congress explained, adding 
that there is a harmony between what the basic peoples congresses decided and what 
the 5-year plan 1981-85 proposes, namely 18,000,500,000 dinars. 


The secretary of the basic peoples congress from Sydney, Australia, addressed the 
congress, first of all bringing greetings from the congress members of Sydney to the 
children of the Arab Libyan people, aud to the chief of the revolution, declaring 
that the masses of the Sydney congress have faith in the thoughts and principles of 
the chief of the revolution. He mentioned the determination of these masses to pro- 
claim still further the Green Book's theses, to exercise the power of the people and 
to recist ali campaigns hostile to the Arab nation

After a recess the congress resumed ite work, during which the decisions of the 
peoples congresses relative to the fifth point, concerning the general housing policy, 
were made known. At this time certain secretaries of peoples congresses presented 

the decisions of their congresses. Then the secretary of the General Peoples Congress 
indicated that there would be a meeting to reexamine housing policy and begin a 
savings bank for housing. The congress then passed on to the study of the sixth 

point of the agenda relating to the reexamination of the consumer products subsidy 
policy. The deputy secretary reviewed the results of the work of the basic peoples 
congresses on this subject. He stated that 131 congresses are for the revision of 

the consumer products subsidy policy. Twenty-two congresses disapproved of the note 
on this matter while 20 congresses disapproved of the note by placing certain reser- 
vations and posing certain conditions. The meeting ended at 1330 and should resume 
at 1700.
