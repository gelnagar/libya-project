Summary (AI): A 1981 newspaper article (Philadelphia Inquirer, via CIA reading room) reports that former CIA agent Edwin P. Wilson is living in Tripoli, Libya, running a business empire and allegedly supplying arms and explosives to Libya's Qaddafi government. It also covers U.S.-Libyan tensions and the expulsion order for U.S. citizens in late 1981.
• Wilson and Terpil were indicted in 1980 on charges of shipping 40,000 pounds of illegal explosives and timing devices to Libya in 1976 and 1977.
• Wilson reportedly received $1 million from Khadafy for the munitions sale.
• The FBI said the Libyan embassy in Washington was a source of murder plots against more than 100 Libyans living in the U.S.; the U.S. closed it.
• Two U.S. jets downed two Soviet-built Libyan fighters off the North African coast the previous summer.
• 1976: Wilson began a highly profitable business deal with Khadafy; he and Frank Terpil were later indicted for shipping 40,000 pounds of illegal explosives and timing devices to Libya in 1976-1977.
• 1981-07: Wilson denied links to Khadafy in an interview with an American journalist.
• 1981-12: Reagan ordered all U.S. citizens, many of them oil company workers, to leave Libya.
