Summary (AI): A 1981 Boston Globe Magazine article describes a CIA-linked plot in which former CIA employees and American ex-soldiers allegedly ran explosives and terrorist training for Gaddafi's Libya from 1976 to 1979. It reports a hidden bomb-camouflaging workshop in a palace basement about twenty miles south of the seaside hotel where the Americans stayed in Tripoli.
• Behind locked doors in the basement of the servants' quarters of the former king's palace, Americans camouflaged small bombs as lamps, candles, briefcases and ashtrays.
• Alleged support included more than twenty Americans, five former CIA employees, a Green Beret on leave, five ex-Green Berets, five former army explosives experts and two civilian naval engineers.
• Millions of dollars of hardware was allegedly shipped to Libya, including 25,000 pounds of explosives and a ground-to-air Redeye missile, without required US approval.
• Americans allegedly trained Libyan commandos in bomb detonation and parachute jumping.
• 1976-09: Americans recruited for the Libyan affair informed the CIA of the operation; the agency notified the FBI.
• 1979-12: According to a federal investigators' report, the operation continued at least until late December 1979.
