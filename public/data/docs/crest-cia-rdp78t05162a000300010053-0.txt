ABSTRACT 

1. ‘This report provides detailed information on the 84 air warning radar sites identified in 
Egypt and the three identified in Libya on KEYHOLE photography KEYHOLE 

photography of Sudan was searched but no air warning radar sites were identified. 

63 of the Egyptian sites were occupied and 62 contained hardened facilities. Two of the Libyan 

sites were under construction and contained hardened facilities. 

2. This report updates portions of NPIC Air Warning Radar Facilities in the 

Middle East. November 1971, and contains two large-scale location maps, a tabulation of data on 

the identified sites, and three photographs. 
INFRODUCTION 

3. Fable 1 presents the bulk of the detailed information on the air warning (AW) radar sites 
treated in this report. It indicates site hardness, lists permanent facilities, and provides electronics 
orders of battle (OBs) during two periods of photographic coverage. 

4. Hard sites designated in Table 1 are those which contain concrete control bunkers, radar 
bunkers, and other permanent facilities. Soft sites are those which are nonpermanent and/or not 
of reinforced construction. These sites usually contain an indistinct control area comprised of vehicle 

revetinents. 

5. The table provides only a general description of the permanent facilities at the radar sites 

and should be used as an indication of the types of facilities present rather than as a precise accounting. 

6. Dummy radar equipment has been observed at AW radar facilities in the Middle East but 
cannot always be identified on photography. Therefore, notations in Table 1 of the presence of 
a radar or an occupied mound at a given. site do not necessarily mean that the radar or site is 

operational. 
7. Differences between the electronics OBs given in Table 1 for the two periods of photographic 
coverage may reflect differences between camera systems or varying image quality: however, in many 

instances these differences represent actual changes in equipment. 

BASIC DESCRIPTION 

8. 84 AW radar sites had been identified in Egypt and three had been 

identified in Libya (Figures 1 and 2). Although Sudan was searched on KEYHOLE photography 
through this date, no AW radar sites were identified. 

Egyptian Sites 

9. The total of 84 AW radar sites identified in Egypt by June 1973 has increased from only 
11 sites identified at the time of the Arab-Isracli War in June 1967. Of the 84 sites, 63 were occupied 

and 62 were hardened. 

10, Deceptive measures were observed at nine sites. These measures consisted for the most 
part of dummy bunkers, with or without mounds, which simulated the configuration of the actual 
radar site. Dummy radar equipment was also observed, mostly on recent photography: prior to January 
1973 it was identified at only one site but by June 1973 it had been observed at seven sites. An 
Egyptian hardened radar site with dummy bunkers and equipment is shown in Figure 3.

Libyan Sites 

11. Two of the three AW radar sites identified in Libya, Tarabulus AW Radar Facility TALL 
KING and Tubruq AW Radar Facility, were under construction in June 1973. Both sites contained 
TALL KING radar facilities identical to the TALL KING facilities in Egypt (Figures 4 and 5). The 
other radar mounds and bunkers under construction at these two sites were also similar to those 
at hardened sites in Egypt.
