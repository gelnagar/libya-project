Summary (AI): A 1985 CIA assessment of the Moroccan-Libyan union agreed in August 1984, covering its political aims, economic ties, Moroccan worker migration to Libya, and implications for US-Moroccan relations. It concerns Libya's foreign policy and does not describe any place or event in Tripoli itself.
• The annual union budget of $60 million was to be provided primarily by Libya.
• Moroccan exports to Libya totaled $34 million in 1984.
• About 20,000 Moroccans were reported in Libya, with the agreement calling for about 80,000.
• Union headquarters were initially to be in Tripoli, alternating with Rabat.
• Hassan's relationship with Qadhafi began in late June 1983 after Saudi pressure.
• 1984-08-13: Arab-African Federation (Moroccan-Libyan union) treaty signed by Hassan and Qadhafi at Oujda, Morocco
• 1984-08-31: Union approved by referenda in Morocco and Libya

Hassan almost certainly calculated that a union agreement 
would cause some cooling in his relationship with the 
United States. We believe, however, that Hassan is sincere in 
telling US officials that he wants to maintain close relations 
with the United States and that he has no illusions about 
transforming Qadhafi's errant behavior. Nevertheless, Hassan 
believes that he: can control the direction of the union. 

At the same time, Hassan will be watching closely for 
indications that Washinyton wants to redefine its relationship 
with Morocco. Hassan's wish to limit the damage to US-Moroccan 
relations gives the US some leverage with him on such issues as 
technology transfers and intelligence matters. On the other 
hand, Hassan's personal prestige is so heavily engaged in the 
union that--as he has already amply demonstrated--he will 
strongly resist any external pressures to cancel it.
