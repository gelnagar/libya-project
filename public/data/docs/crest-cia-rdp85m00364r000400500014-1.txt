Summary (AI): A November 1983 State Department memo to the White House proposes extending U.S. export-license denial to Libya for sectors that could aid its strategic infrastructure, as part of pressure on Qadhafi's support for terrorism and subversion. It concerns trade policy, with no Tripoli-specific sites or events.
• Proposal would presume denial of export licenses worth $1 million or more in specified categories destined for Libya's strategic infrastructure.
• In the period covered, 36 export license applications worth $611.4 million were pending, of which applications worth $545.5 million would be subject to presumption of denial.
• U.S. exports to Libya dropped from $416 million (January-June 1982) to $170 million (same period 1983) and $105 million for a later period.
• Approximately 600 U.S. nationals could remain in Libya despite passport restrictions.
• The memo was dated November 30, 1983 and responded to a September 9 NSC request.
