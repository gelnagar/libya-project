Summary (AI): A 1981 Time magazine article (reprinted in CIA reading room) reports that former CIA agents and U.S. firms helped train and arm Gaddafi's terrorists, including a claimed RDX shipment, a training program in espionage and sabotage, and a lab near Tripoli making assassination bombs. It is mainly about American contractors, with little on Tripoli sites themselves.
• Mulcahy's firm Interarms-type company was 'Technology, Inc.' (OCR-garbled); partners Wilson and Terpil.
• Reported shipment of about 40,000 lbs of RDX explosive flown to Libya in 55-gallon drums marked 'industrial solvent'.
• Gaddafi allegedly sought thousands of timers to set off explosives, ostensibly to clear Israeli mines from the 1973 October War, though no such mines were known in Libyan waters.
• American Electronic Laboratories, Inc. of Falls Church, Va. reportedly agreed to build prototypes for Gaddafi's order.
• Terpil reportedly had a $3.2 million contract to supply arms, explosives and torture devices to Idi Amin's Uganda.
• 1981-06: Reported laboratory near Tripoli making assassination bombs disguised as ashtrays, lamps or teakettles, part of a Libyan training program in espionage, sabotage and psychological warfare run by Wilson and Terpil (as alleged in Hersh's account).
• 1981-05: Reagan Administration ordered Libya's diplomatic mission in Washington to close and leave the US (stated as 'last month').
