TEXT OF DER SPIEGEL INTERVIEW WITH AL-QADHDHAFI 


DW210929 Hamburg DER SPIEGEL in German 21 Sep 81 pp 148-156 


{Editors Adel S. Elias, Johannes K. Engel, and Wolf Dieter Steinbauer "2-hour" interview 
with Libya's Mu‘ammar al-Qadhdhafi in his Bedouin tent in the desert town of Sabha; date 
not given] 


[Text] SPIEGEL: Colonel al-Qadhdhafi, in the West in general and in the United States in 
particular you are considered to be the world's most dangerous man. Do you feel this is 
justified? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: It depends on what is meant by dangerous. 


SPIEGEL: The Americans interpret this to mean really dangerous because they are convinced 
that Colonel al-Qadhdhafi--barring the Russians--is the biggest troublemaker who backs any 
revolt, especially here in Africa. 


Moreover, Colonel al-Qadhdhafi has now threatened to attack the U.S. nuclear bases in the 
Mediterranean--even at the risk of creating an international catastrophe and triggering 
World War III--if the U.S. 6th Fleet shows up in the Gulf of Sidra off the Libyan coast 
again. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: All I can do is explain to you why the United States has used this descrip- 
tion of the most dangerous man: What is involved is merely political and psychological 
preparation for Libya's conquest. The United States intends to attack Libya and subject it 
to its domination again. 


SPIEGEL: So you don't simply believe that according to Reagan's script you are the cowboy 
wearing a black hat, the villain. You mean to contend in all earnestness that the Americans 
intend to attack and conquer Libya? 


Al-ONadhdhafi: This is what I believe. 


SPIEGEL: How is one to visualize the conquest of Libya nowadays? What you are voicing is 
a monstrous suspicion. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: There are various methods, yet all of them would be aimed at subjugating 
Libya and including it again in the U.S. sphere of influence. This does not require the 
direct occupation of the country by troops. It would be sufficient to overthrow the 
revolutionary regime here and to replace it with another, with a pro-American regime. 
This is one method used by U.S. imperialism. This is what I meantwhen I referred to the 
plans of conquest by the Americans. 


SPIEGEL: Did you have this in mind when you said in your 1 September speech that the 
Americans had tried to poison you? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Of course, the Americans did that. They have to get me out of the way to 
attain their objectives. This insidious plan hatched by the U.S. president with his CIA 
to eliminate me was another step on the road toward getting control of Libya. 


SPIEGEL: It is a fact that in early August the U.S. press reported on a CIA operation plan 
aimed at driving you from office. This plan was submitted to the Select Committee on 
Intelligence, and intelligence service committee of the House of Representatives, and it 
had been approved by CIA boss Casey and the crisis management team of the White House. 


Members of the committee then sent an outraged letter of protest directly to Ronald Reagan 
since the plans seemed to reveal that an assassination attempt was planned against Colonel 
al-Qadhdhafi. Nothing was said about poisoning, however. What do you know about all this?

Al-Qadhdhafi: I became aware of the clues only afterwards. Just prior to the revelation 
of these plans, many U.S. visitors called on me as journalists, businessmen and politicians. 
All of them suddenly showed interest in talking with me. I found out only afterwards that 
this had been done on the instructions of the CIA to determine my living habits--where I 
sleep, where I live and what I do. 


Hints also arrived from the United States--from friends of Arab origin who meanwhile have 
become U.S. citizens and who more or less perhaps worked for the CIA or were supposed to 
be recruited by the CIA. 


These people had already noticed that the CIA was developing more activities toward Libya. 
They let me know that information about my eating habits was being gathered. They warned 
me: Watch your food and whomever serves it. You should not eat directly; have your food 
and beverages tested first. 


At the time I did not take these reports seriously because I did not think that the United 
States would pursue such insidious plans with the participation of its president. 


The campaign against me and my policy then intensified. I was held responsible for any 
evil deed that took place in the world; I was seen behind any terrorist act. When they 
realized that they were not making any headway with their plan to poison me, they suddenly 
decided to close our people's bureau in Washington... 


SPIEGEL: ...that is, your embassy... 


Al-Qadhdhafi: ...to deport the personnel and to sever relations with us totally. Then 
they tried to put one over on me in the Gulf of Sidra. 


SPIEGEL: To us this naturally sounds like a wild tale. But unfortunately we cannot but 
say that the CIA plans to eliminate Fidel Castro, details of which become known in 
Washington a few years ago, likewise sounded like a wild tale. They wanted to get at him 
with the help of poisoned” shoestrings or poisoned cigars. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: These facts cannot be denied. It is also a fact that they tried to kill me. 


Martin Luther King, Allende and Lumumba are on the account of the CIA--so why should it 
hesitate to add me to this record? 


SPIEGEL: We cannot vouch for that list. But a lot is possible where the CIA is concerned, 
and this even excited the American public. On the other hand, what also shocked us in 
Europe, is that Colonel al-Qadhdhafi has thre»tened to attack the U.S. 6th Fleet if and 
when it shows up again in the Gulf of Sidra, and to create an international catastrophe, 

if necessary. Have you lost all sense of perspective? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Nonsense. What I meant to say by that is the following: Some units of 
the U.S. 6th Fleet are carrying nuclear bombs. If the Americans attack us in the Culf of 
Sidra, I must consider that they will attack us with nuclear bombs as well. 


In this case I have the right to defend my people and my country by attacking the NATO 
bases in southeastern Europe where these nuclear bomb carriers originate before they even 
reach us. If we had the option of destroying these bases or ships, we would do it. 


SPIEGEL: You would really be prepared to attack U.S. or NATO bases in Greece, Italy or 
Spain? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Understand me correctly. I did not say I wanted to attack Greece, Italy 
or Spain. I said: If I found that an American attack originated from these bases, and 
if I had the opportunity to eliminate them, I would not hesitate to destroy them.

SPIEGEL: There is probably no room here for realism. Border quarrels, such as that in 
connection with your territorial waters, exist everywhere inthe world--for example, 
between Japan and Russia, China and India and so forth. So far nobody has threatened 
an international catastrophe in this connection. You are the first head of state to 

do this. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: We are not neighbors of America. We have no border quarrels. 
SPIEGEL: Territorial waters are involved here. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: We have no quarrels about territoral waters either. We consider the action 
of the Americans in the Gulf of Sidra to be pure aggression. The Americans have lost 
nothing in our country. We are not their neighbors. Therefore, we do not consider the 
term border conflict to be valid. 


SPIEGEL: What is involved is adherance to a 3-mile zone or a 12-mile zone which was 
always accepted or respected by the Americans. The Americans were outside this zone in 
international waters. 


Al-Oadhdhafi: That is not true. We have not extended the territorial waters to 12 miles. 
Sidra is a security zone for us. I do not want to determine how f from the coast the 
territorial waters exist for me. But I say: The Gulf of Sidra #s an indispensable 

part of Libya. Whoever penetrates it, threatens us. This area indispensable for 

our security. 


Our factories and industrial installations are there, and our oil ports--from which 
we supply oil to the whole world--are there. Whoever enters the Gulf of Sidra must 
know that he has entered Libyan territory and is illegally on Libyan territory. 


Whoever enters the Gulf of Sidra has declared war on us. That pertains not only to the 
Americans but to everybody, regardless of who he is. That matter has been finally decided 
and I will not deviate from the decision. 


SPIEGEL: You said that you would even accept a world war. Colonel al-Qadhdhafi, a 
serious question to you: Could you imagine that you could be guilty for World War III? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Since when is the one defending his country considered to be someone unleashing 
wars? If we accepted that, nobody would defend his country any more. I do not unleash 

wars. I do not want war; I want to defend my country. America claims to be civilized 

and polite. If the Americans have a conflict with me and believe that I am wrong, 

why didn't they go tothe United Nations, the Security Council, the International Court 

or the UN Law of the Sea conference? 


SPIEGEL: Why didn't you go to the United Nations or appeal to the Security Council 
when the Americans entered the Gulf of Sidra? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: America does not need the Gulf of Sidra at all. America can live without 
the Gulf of Sidra. 1 cannot understand why America decided to play world policeman 

and set an precedence in the Gulf of Sidra. That is no reason formeto go to the United 
Nations. 


SPIEGEL: Did you realize that the Americans had planned their Gulf of Sidra action 
from the very beginning as a showdown--that can be seen from the plans? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: I can tell you one thing: After this attack by the Americans, I feel 
much stronger in my country and my regime is stronger than before. All of Arabia 
supported me. Some 80 states officially confirmed and supported my demands. 


I can also tell you something that is still unknown: I received official letters from 
Greece, Italy, Spain, France and many West European countries, telling me that I was 
right and that they do not see why the Americans started playing with fire. 


Many European countries even told me officially that they recognize the Gulf of Sidra 
as a Libyan security zone.

It seems to me that the Americans and some of their allies are so dumb and stupid that 
they have not learned anything from the lessons of history. Jamal ‘Abd an-Nasir became 
a hero only when the West Europeans waged war against him in 1956 at the Suez Canal. If 
they want to make me the hero of the Arabs here, the Americans should just continue what 
they are doing. I have nothing against this. On the contrary I even welcome it. 


SPIEGEL: Then the next conflict seems to be already programmed because America, as a 
maritime nation, will insist on its interpretations of territorial waters. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: That is a problem for the Americans, not us. 


SPIEGEL: What political lessons have you learned from this confrontation? Must you make 
new alliance policy orientations? Little Libya cannot hope to have a chance alone against 
the 6th Fleet, the most powerful naval combat unit in the Mediterranean. Do you want 
conduct some rapprochement toward the Soviets? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: It is not necessary for us to run to the Soviet Union and say: Protect 
us from the Americans. 


We figure that America, as a big power, will not show much concern for us because we are 
a small country. But you in the West should know one thing most exactly. We will not 
shrink back one bit from our demands in the Gulf of Sidra. 


SPIEGEL: Colonel al-Qadhdhafi, do you have che means to implement these demands? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: It is not a question whether I can defeat America. Every one knows about 
each other's strength. I just want to make the following clear: One must not shrink 
back if one is right. 


What kind of logic would it be otherwise? Everybody who is weaker would have to shut up, 
if the Americans wanted it. If I don't have the strength to implement my rights, it 
does not mean that I don't have any. 


At any rate we have military strength. Even if we cannot defeat America, the Americans 
must not believe that they can finish us off just like that. 


SPIEGEL: Initially people in the United States were inclined not to take your threats 

seriously. The word in Washington is that, peculiarly enough, after the Gulf of Sidra 

incident you did not apply the only effective weapon you have, namely, the oil weapon. 

After all, you could have cut off the 10 percent of the imported oil that the Americans 
obtain from Libya. Why did you not do this? 


Al-Qadhdhafi (laughing): It makes no difference to me whether they take this seriously 
or not. If need be we will apply the oil weapon. The United States is playing a very 
stupid game when it plays with fire in the middle of a crude oil region. 


SPIEGEL: When Ronald Reagan was elected president you said that you were glad that 
Carter, who is "naive," "ignorant," and "particularly loathsome," was out of the way. 
Reagan, in contrast, is "more serious," you said, and the American Republicans are 
closer to you than the Democrats. You have probably changed this view in the meantime, 
have you not? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: After all, I turned out to be right on what I said about Jimmy Carter--that 
he is stupid and naive. Otherwise he would have been reelected. 


I did not, of course, give preference to Reagan as a person but to the Republican Party 
and its program, because several points seemed to us to be better than what the Demo- 


crats had. 


SPIEGEL: This amazes us because Reagan also stood for an antisocial, stepped-up arms 
program. 


V. 22 Sep 81 NORTH AFRICA 


Al-Qadhdhafi: You are right on that score. But I cannot imagine Reagan being able to 
sustain his arms buildup for long. He had demanded weapons at the expense of the social 
sector to gain more votes. 


This was a political chess game. It was not wise. But if you ask me specifically, then 
I say that what we see in the so-called democratic world is comedies. At their core 
most democracies are nothing but demagogy. Everybody tries to seduce everyone else by 
saying: "I am the better man." Yet, one is worse than the other. What the two parties 
staged during the election campaign was nothing but scorn for the commonsense and 
intelligence of the people. 


Many promises are made, which later turn out to be empty. It is as with a drum in the 
bush: When you first hear the sound you think it is a huge drum but once you come 
closer, it is just a little fart. 


SPIEGEL: It is generally known that you ridicule the Western democracies, and in your 
latest speech you also said of President Reagan that he is an unsuccessful, third-rate 
actor. Did you ever see him in the movies? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Naturally, and also on Libyan television. 
SPIEGEL: Were Reagan pictures shown there? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Just imagine, we showed these films on television because we refused to 
believe that such an actor could become the President of the United States. We were 
convulsed with laughter. (laughing) We have these films run again now for the merriment 
of the people. 


SPIEGEL: Objection: In our country Reagan had been classified one grade higher as an 
actor, But this does not necessarily say snything about the political quality of a man 
because Harry Truman, for instance, was definitely a second-rate haberdasher but later 
became a first-rate president. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: I have no objection to anybody engaging in the profession of his choice. 
A profession is something honest and honorable. Nothing can be said against anybody 
earning money through his profession and raising his family. What I find fault with, 
however, is this: As an actor, Reagan is unsuccessful and third-rate... 


SPIEGEL: ...but he did earn his living that way over many years. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: ...so that he has a inferiority complex and is inclined to take 
unpredictable actions for the sake of compensating for this complex. This is even true 
for the Sidra action by which he intended to compensate for his lack of success as an 
actor. 


SPIEGEL: It is characteristic of the situation that in the United States, the actions 
of Colonel al-Qadhdhafi are likewise attributed to inferiority complexes. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Why, was I an actor? I have no need to compensate for complexes. 


I would like to remind you of something else, by the way, and maybe your Western world 
will learn from this: American imperialism and Zionism sought to overthrow ‘Abd 
an-Nasir. He died when the Lord so willed. The more plans the Americans hatched, the 
more solid its adversaries became. Castro outlived three U.S. presidents, and if they 
continue as they do have been I will outlive several presidents as well. 


SPIEGEL: So you expect to die in bed or in a tent some day? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: This is for the Lord to decide, not me.

SPIEGEL: Did you actually expect Reagan to set his sights on you as the main villain so 
soon? From visits to your country, Ameiicans brought home the impression that you 
obviously were very perplexed and confused by the hostility of the Reagan government. 

You even told an American visitor in private: "I love the Americans, and would very much 
like to visit the country, not just the deserts but all of America." 


Al-Qadhdhafi: The course of action of the U.S. Government, and above all of the president, 
naturally took me by surprise because I harbor no hatred for the Americans. No people 
hates another. I like the Americans very much. 


I would also like the President if he were not against freedom, if he didn't oppress the 
people and if he didn't pursue any imperialist goals. In that case I would not have any 
conflict at all with him. 


May I return to the personality of Reagan? 
SPIEGEL: Yes, because it seems to occupy your mind. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: I am seriously afraid that the inferiority complex Reagan suffers from might 
cause a global disaster. I am afraid that Reagan has not gotten rid of this dual role 
because he probably still thinks he is on stage, on the world stage, to play some role. 


He believes that everything will turn out well, as in his films, because he is just an actor. 
He has not yet become fully care that he is president now and no longer an actor. This is 
why I am afraid that he might cause a global catastrophe because he still plays his role as 
an actor and considers the world a mere stage. 


SPIEGEL: That is how the sides see each other. The Americans fear that Colonel al-QOadhdhafi 
could touch off a catastrophe, and Colonel al-Qadhdhafi fears that Reagan could touch off a 
catastrophe. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: On the contrary. I am not a third-rate actor. In contrast to him, I have 
tradition, I have roots and I have self-confidence. I have brought about a revolutionary 
movement; I have made a revolution. I was able to achieve the position I have now only 
through long and hard work. Reagan was an actor. He just dreamed of becoming president 
some day, and now his dream has come true. 


SPIEGEL: But he perservered doggedly and in a tough way. That is some sort of quality 
for a politician. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Well, maybe, but he remains what he was, an unsuccessful third-rate actor. 

I am a re “%lutionary, however, and have made a revolution. Reagan just represents the peak 

of a rotten capitalist society where everybody is prepared to make promises just to be elected. 
SPIEGEL: This rotten society, however, still has a lot of vitality. 

Al-Qadhdhafi: That may be correct today, but the system cannot be maintained forever. 

SPIEGEL: Capitalism has proven to be very tough so far. May we get back to your recent 


speech? You said that America hates Islam, that America defends the religion of alcohol, 
palaces, prostitution and female slaves. 


One could counter this by saying America has pursued a policy of interests, just as you 
have when you supported and tried to save the murderous regime of Idi Amin for political 
interests. Would you recognize this parallel? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Could be, you are right. Everybody looks after his own interests. Why not? 
We have nothing against the Americans defending their interests, but not at the expense 

of ours. They should please take into consideration the fact that we are not prepared to 
sacrifice our interests so that the Americans can follow theirs.

SPIEGEL: Let us discuss Libya's action in Chad. 
Al-Qadhdhafi: OK. 


SPIEGEL: You have always said that nobody has the right to interfere in the internal 
affairs of other countries. Why did you interfere in Chad? In doing this, you have provoked 
half of Africa and are paying a high political price for it. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: It seems to me that you are ignoring one aspect and that you intentionally 
do not want to say what really happened in Chad. If you have already taken a certain 
position from the very beginning, I do not want to try to influence your position. But if 
you were prepared to really understand wha. is going on in Chad, I would like to discuss it 
with you. 


SPIEGEL: Please. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: I consider your question--saying that we interfered in Chad--to be not 
objective. I think it is subjective and unjust. The Chad conflict had been going on for 
11 years and we did not interfere at all. 


We marched in when President Goukouni came to us. We did not bring him here by force. He 
came to Libya voluntarily, and concluded a defense pact with us. He asked us to help him 
militarily to reestablish law and order in his country. We agreed to his oral, written, 
as well as official, request. 


SPIEGEL: Is that your story? 
Al-Qadhdhafi: Yes. 


SPIEGEL: But before that you supported the man who is now the opponent of your current 
favorite, Goukouni, namely Hissein Habre. How come this change of sides took place? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: What we have done--first we supported the Frolinat liberation movement with 
arms, money and all other possible means. One they had come to power, we said: Well now, 
there is no need to support them any more; now they must act on their own. 


When Frolinat split between Habre and Goukouni, it was their business. We maintained 
restraint. We invaded only when we were asked officially. 


SPIEGEL: Is that in accordance with reports saying that you want to extend your influence 
further in Africa? This is indicated by the fact that you are offering Chad a merger with 
Libya, planning to bring about state unity of both countries. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: What is wrong with unity between Chad and Libya? The OAU Charter says that 
African unity should be implemented. If the union really came about some day, it would be 
a stabilizing factor. Would you rather prefer daily unrest in some corner of Africa? The 
union would provide for good economic and political cooperation. 


SPIEGLL: There is uranium in Chad, is not that so? 

Al-Qadhdhafi: There is no uranium in Chad. But if you are so piqued about our being in 
Chad, why were you not to piqued when the Tanzanians invaded Uganda, or when Senegalese 
troops invaded Gambia? You were not piqued either about the action of the French and the 


Moroccans in Zaire. 


SPIEGEL: One should not imitate bad examples. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Naturally these are bad examples. But why didn't you protest as strongly 
as you do to me?

SPIEGEL: Maybe because Colonel Al-Qadhdhafi is such a puzzling figure. If he does 
sometHing like that, one takes a closer look at it. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Why am I such a puzzling person to you? I am no villain. All I do is

SPIEGEL: We would like to once again return to an issue about which you have been 
questioned very often: ithe Islamic nuclear bomb, the production of which you are 
allegedly involved in. You appealed to the Arabs to destroy the Israeli nuclear research 
center in Dimona in retaliation for the Israeli attack on the Iraqi reactor, but also 

to prevent the construction of an Israeli nuclear bomb. Would it not be more logical 
along the lines of power policy for the Arabs to work on a nuclear weapon themselves, 
since you believe that the Israelis are working on one? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: I am against the production of nuclear arms throughout the world. 
People have never becn more criminal than at the time when they invented the atom 
bomb and used it. 


For that reason I say: The Dimona installation must be destroyed for the sake of 
mankind. Possession of nuclear bombs means destruction of the people. It must be 
destroyed before matters have developed that far. I am firmly convinced that Israel 
has a nuclear bomb. 


SPIEGEL: Has it already? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Yes, because the Israelis do not permit anybody--not the International 
Atomic Energy Agency or any one--else to make inspections there. They have something 
to hide. 


SPIEGEL: Let us discuss your Moscow visit. You supported German unity there. That 
passage was not published in the Soviet statement. Was there any difference of opinion 
in this respect? 


Al-Qadhdhafi (laughs): I demanded German unity not just there; I do that on every 
international occasion. I demand German unity wherever I am. The German nation is 
in an unnatural situation now and the Germans have the right to live alone in their 
country. 


SPIEGEL: You said in your speech on the 12th anniversary of the revolution that you are 
getting the German people who have now become awake; bombs are exploding every day at 
American bases. Do you realize that in saying this, you are greeting just a handful 

of people who we consider terrorists? The majority of German. who reject Reagan's 
counterarmament want to fight it with political means. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Naturally, I welcome it very much if one can achieve more with political 
means than with bombs. Germany is in a rather difficult situation at the moment. 
Reagan's policy means in the end that the Germans must sacrifice themselves for 
American interests. 


SPIEGEL: The situation in central Europe actually has always been dangerous if you 
bear in mind the number of nuclear weapons deployed there on a very small territory. 
But the public has realized it just now--some 4,000 nuclear arms in the Federal Republic 


alone. 


Al-Qadhadhfi: Not just the Germans, but all of West Europe is in danger. If there 

were no American bases in West Europe, the superpowers would have to fight their war. 

if there ever is one, with navies and air forces in their own territory. But since there 
are bases in West Europe, the West Europeans are in even more danger then before of 
becoming victims of the superpowers. 


SPIEGEL: On the other hand, however, the balance of power in Europe has brought us a 
very long period of peace, longer than most periods of peace in European history.

Al-Qadhdhafi: Yes, that is true, you have lived in peace but on a powder keg that 
could explode at any minute. 


SPIEGEL: Well, this is alright if the fuse on the powder keg is not burning. 
Al-Qadhdhafi: Who can guarantee that the fuse will not be lit some day? 
SPIEGEL: That's the question. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: If a third world war occurs, which I wholeheartedly hope does not happen, 
the Europeans definitely will suffer most among all peoples. 


SPIEGEL: If any great war occurs again at all, it would emerge in the area of the oil 
states rather than in central Europe, and it will rather break out as a result of errors 
and erroneous assessments, and as a result of an escalation nobody wants. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Many thanks for your efforts to export your wars to us. 


SPIEGEL: Our last question pertains to the Americans. In highly colorful language you 
said in your speech on the 12th anniversary of the revolution that you have farted on 
the United States a thousand times. Could you imagine yourself seeking rapprochement 
with the United States some day for reasons of political commonsense? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Of course. We have always said that if there is a reasonable policy which 
makes allowances for the interests of both sides, we would be prepared to do that, but 
not at our expense. We have really tried to have good relations with the United States. 
But the United States is not willing to have them at this time. Well, if the United 
States does not want them, then I fart on the United States once again. 


Through DER SPIEGEL I would like to address my words to the Americans: They should 
ponder Reagan's policy for a change. What he is doing is not in the interests of the 


American people. I am not interested in living in hostility with the American people. 


I repeat once again: I do not wish to live in hostility with the United States, but 
neither do I wish to crawl on all fours before the United States. 


SPIEGEL: Colonel Al-Qadhdhafi, thank you for this interview. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: The plans to poison me, after all, were a scandal. The fact that the 
President is involved in them is a scandal. 


SPIEGEL: Do you know that people in the United States take very seriously the assassi- 
nation threats directed against Reagan by your league of free officers? 


Al-Qadhdahfi: I would like to set something straight here once and for all, so that 
people will quit talking about it. Our officers made that threat only after Reagan's 
assassination plans against me became known. Then they said: If you do that, your 
number will be up, too. I make no secret of the fact that revolutionary committees 
all over the world might take some action if something happened to me. 


I address my words to the American people. The playing with fire by the Americans 
here, and the shifting fleets back and forth, is no sandtable game. It might become 
very dangerous indeed. The Americans should prevent their president from playing 
with fire.

[Text] Cairo, 22 Sep (JAMAHIRIAYAH NEWS AGENCY)--The ruling regime in Egypt yesterday 
openly contradicted itself and implicitly admitted that it was planning to launch an 
aggression against the Jamahiriyah and Chad. 


An official spokesman for the regime's Foreign Ministry yesterday afternoon issued a 
statement distributed by the official Egyptian news agency in which he claimed that 

Egypt did not take part in the aggressive attacks made against the Chadian people. 

He also indicated that he had received the accusations the Chadian President Goukouni 
Oueddei made against the Egyptian regime. The spokesman also claimed that the Egyptian
