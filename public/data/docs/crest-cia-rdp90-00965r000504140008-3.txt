Summary (AI): An ABC News broadcast from late November 1985 reports U.S. intelligence concern that Libya has received Soviet SA-5 long-range anti-aircraft missiles at the port of Misurata, possibly to challenge U.S. planes over the Gulf of Sidra. It also notes tensions on the Libya-Egypt border. Nothing here is specific to Tripoli.
• U.S. intelligence sources said two Soviet ships were unloading SA-5 missiles, launchers, radar and transporters at Misurata, enough for two anti-aircraft sites with six launchers each.
• The SA-5 was described as having a range of nearly 200 miles and a closing speed of three times the speed of sound.
• Libya and Egypt had moved additional men and tanks near their border, both keeping forces about 15 miles back from the line.
• Egypt claimed Libya masterminded a hijacking in Malta.
• 1981: Four years before the broadcast, U.S. F-14s from the carrier Nimitz shot down two Libyan interceptors that attacked them over disputed waters.
