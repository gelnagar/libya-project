PEOPLE'S BUREAU DENOUNCES REAGAN ON POLAND 


LD191002 Tripoli JANA in English 0915 GMT 19 Dec 81 


[Text] Tripoli, 19 Dec (JANA) -- During his press conference last Thursday the American 
President Reagan discussed with severity the latest developments in the Polish crisis 
following the military crackdown in Poland which aims at stabilizing helpful conditions 
to the Polish people to solve their crisis and defend their country. 


The American President said that according to him the present situation in Poland is of 
extreme danger and that his administration will not be able to forward economic aid to 
the Polish people in order to help them solve their economic crisis as long as there is 
a military rule in the country. 


The People's Committee of the Foreign Liaison Bureau issued a statement yesterday after- 
noon to comment on these statements which denounce U.S. administration's intervention in 
the internal affairs of Poland. Following is the full text: 


For the second time, Reagan discloses the terrorist and aggressive nature of his personality, 
since he gives himself the right to intervene boldly and flagrantly in the internal affairs 
of others. 


When Reagan announces before international public opinion his interference in the affairs 
of Poland, and falsely alleges his concern over the freedom of its people, he is proving 
his ignorance of the nature and circumstances of matters and in particular his obvious 
ignorance of the great history of the Polish people. 


The freedom of the Polish people was the product of a bitter struggle, as Poland was a 
scene of fierce battles fought by the Polish people courageously against nazism and 
fascism to reinforce their freedom and national independence. Reagan's announcement to 
stop U.S. economic aid to Poland using the pretext of the new military rule is evidence 
of the U.S. policy of terrorizing and submitting the people by using the weapon and 
economic aids. 


The Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah, which has close links and strong friendship 
with Poland, strongly condemns any intervention into the internal affairs of this friendly 
people by the imperialists and calls upon all the peoples of the world to joint efforts 
towards putting an end to the madness for power, force and arrogance that outline Reagan's 
policy. 

Let Regard realize that the world is not a U.S. property but for all the peoples, great 

and small, and that he cannot control the destiny of the peoples and bring them to sub- 


mission because man kneels only to God and not to Reagan's administration.

OIL MINISTER SUGGESTS EUROPEANS REPLACE U.S. 


NC202229 Paris AFP in English 2215 GMT 20 Dec 81 


[Text] Tripoli, 20 Dec (AFP) -- Libya suggested today that European petroleum companies 
and experts would be welcome to replace the Americans being pulled out of Libya. 


Libyan Oil Minister ‘Abd as-Salam az-Zaq'ar told AGENCE FRANCE-PRESSE: 


"The Americans have dominated our petroleum industry since its creation, in prospecting, 
drilling and supplying equipment. That will no longer be the case. The door is now wide 
open to Europe." 


U.S. companies handle 60 percent of Libya's oil output at present. 


Mr az-Zaq‘ar predicted that all American oil technicians will leave Libya, as urged by 
White House officials 10 days ago amid concern over an alleged Libyan assassination plot 
against President Ronald Reagan. 


The Libyan minister estimated there were 600 U.S. petroleum technicians among a total of 
2,500 Americans living in Libya. (Other reports have said there are 1,500 Americans in 
Libya.) 


"A hundred oilmen already have left and have been mostly replaced by other nationalities," 
the oil minister said. 


The U.S. pullout and need for spare parts would only perturboil output for a while, Mr 
az-Zaq'ar forecast. He said the status of the U.S. oil companies in Libya would be 
unchanged: "Aside from Esso Libya (Exxon), none of the them has asked to stop its operations 
here." Libya holds a majority of stock in all U.S. oil affiliates in this country. 


The minister said Iran and a number of Arab countries have promised technical help and 
spare parts to compensate for the departure of the Americans. 


Mr az-Zaq'ar said Libyan oil output rose above one million barrels a day this month because 
Libya's crude oil price was lowered to 36.50 dollars a barrel last October in agreement 
with the Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC). 


Output had slumped from 1,400,000 barrels a day at the start of the year to 600,000 barrels 
in September. Mr az-Zaq'ar said that was due to the world oil glut, the Western recession, 
and Libya's high price of 40 dollars. 


He said reports that Libya opposed letting neighboring Tunisia join the Organization of 
Arab Petroleum Exporting Countries (OAPEC) were "exaggerated and twisted." Libya had 
merely asked for a delay to study the case, he said.

REAGAN FAILS TO SUPPORT HIT SQUAD CLAIMS 


LD191028 Tripoli JANA in English 0825 GMT 19 Dec 81 


[Text] Washington, 19 Dec (JANA) -- The American President, Reagan, showed himself 

entirely incapable of providing any evidence to prove the validity of his allegation 
regarding Libya's dispatching an alleged assassination squad to America to kill him and some 
of his allies. 


This failure was apparent in all the answers given to pressmen with whom he met last 
Thursday during the press conference in Washington. 


Despite his insistence to raise the questions about these allegations to pressmen, he 
still evaded answering the flood of questions requesting from him to give to the American 
public opinion any evidence to save him and his aides from disgrace. Journalists 
considered that Reagan, by avoiding to answer their questions, was trying to protect 
himself from the eventual risk of having his lies discovered and his allegations 
immediately discarded. 


What was clearly observed during the press conference was Reagan's failure to present 
any evidence that proves his allegations against Libya when he alleged that he gave 
instructions to the top journalists, referring to his close reporters who receive his 
rumours to circulate, to stop completely from reporting on his stories and allegations. 


President Reagan fumed and said: "I personally told the top journalists to stop publishing 
this story," and very rudely admitted that several members of the Congress have reiterated 
the same allegations against Libya, but those were unauthorized and had no sources of 
information. Reagan acknowledged his ignorance of the reality of the security situation 
surrounding the American citizens in the Jamahiriyah and siad: "I feel very sorry for 

the difficulties my decision has created for them. They, in Libya, have many friends 

and it is very difficult for them to leave Libya." 


Thus Reagan presents himself to the American and world public opinion as a person who 
takes offhand decisions based on the lies of informers and rumours of the agents.

FOREIGN MINISTER DESCRIBES "BAD SITUATION' 


AB201157 Paris AFP in English 1151 GMT 20 Dec 81 


[By Jean-Paul Rodrigue] 


[Text] Ati, central Chad, 20 Dec (AFP) -- As Nigerian troops of the Chadian peacekeep- 
ing force sit outside Ati waiting for orders, Chadian Foreign Minister Acyl Ahmat 
prepares to fight the rebel troops of former Cabinet colleague Hissein Harbre now 
threatening his heartland in a drive west. 


Mr Acyl, head of the Democratic Revolutionary Council (CDR), one of Chad's many factions 
represented in the Transitional Government of National Unity, told AFP that his forces 
badly needed arms, ammunition, food and medical supplies. 


In fighting further east against Mr Havre's FAN, notably round Abeche in mid-November 
and at Kotoro-Falata at the beginning of this month, the CDR suffered heavy losses. But 
if I had not been there Hissein would have been in Ati by now, Mr Acyl said. We want 
people in Ndjamena (the Chadian capital) to know how bad the situation is and take it 
seriously. 


The foreign minister expressed strong dissent with President Goukouni Oueddei's state- 
ment in a recent press conference that he would be prepared to negotiate with the FAN. 
Such negotiations, even without Hissein Habre, are unforseeable, Mr Acyl saia. 


But he agreed with President Goukouni that the inter-African peacekeeping force was not 
playing the role it ought to in Chad, of ensuring national security. 


Some 600 to 700 Nigerians, the advance guard of their national contingent, are encamped 
beside tre airfield here, with armoured reconnaissance vehicles and white-painted land- 
rovers marked with letters OAU (Organisation of African Unity) in black. The OAU 
contingent arrived after the Libyan garrison called in by President Goukouni a year ago 
to help him throw the FAN out of Ndjamena pulled out again two months ago, once more at 
the president's request. 


Mr Acyl denied charges frequently made that he was pro-Libyan, saying that his opinions 
had been falsified. All I said was that once the Libyan withdrawal had been completed 
a replacement force had to be found to support the government, he declared. 


But the foreign minister backed up Libya's regular allegations that Sudanese troops are 
fighting alongside the FAN, saying they were present at Abeche which the FAN took a 
month ago. 


Mr Acyl said that both Egypt and Sudan would continue supplying aid to the FAN, despite 
assurances to the contrary secured by Chadian Justice Minister Kassire Koumakoye on a 
recent visit to Cairo and Khartoum. I have never been against a dialogue with either 
country, but I have doubts about their sincerity, Mr Acyl said.

AFP CORRESPONDENT ON MILITARY SITUATION IN EAST 


AB191357 Paris AFP in French 1214 GMT 19 Dec 81 
[By AFP special envoy Jean-Paul Rodrigue] 


[Text] Oum Hadjer (eastern Chad), 19 Dec (AFP)--A month after the fall of Abeche into 
the hands of Hissein Habre's FAN, government forces are still on the defensive in the 
east of the country, meeting with logistic as well as organizational difficulties. 


An AFP special envoy who has just spent 1 week in the east observed that the counter- 
offensive announced some 15 days ago by Ndjamena is still under preparation and one 
cannot rightly say, in view of on-the-spot observations, that the conditions for its 
success are all set. 


At Oum Hadjer, a small straggling village of 5,000 inhabitants situated 150 km east of 
Abeche, officials of the headquarters of the National Integrated Army [ANI] accept the 
manner in which Hissein Habre's forces--which have accelerated their activities since 
the withdrawal of Libyan forces in November--have been progressing. 


Apart from several neighboring areas along the Sudanese border, FAN controls the capital 
of two prefectures—Abeche and Biltine--and directly threatens Le Batha, of which Oum 
Hadjer is the eastern stronghold. 


Stationary, but not for long--this is how Rakhis Manani, first deputy to the ANI chief 
of defense staff, describes the situation in the east. Surrounded by his officers 
squatting on a shadowy terrace, he examines a list showing the details of their needs 
in weapons, ammunitions and foodstuffs. 


With his face banded in a long black turban and with an elegant posture, Rakhis Manani 

looks caim and determined. He knows Hissein Habre very well; he is the one, exactly a 

year ago, chased him out when the FAN had evacuated Ndjamena to seek refuge in the mas- 
sive eastern mountains. 


Time is working against him, he said, referring to Hissein Habre. He has to advance 
rapidly—-before we consclidate our position. But our great problem is, he adds, that 
we have to drive back the enemy and at the same time form an army, some of our soldiers 
have neither weapons nor uniforms. 


Two batallions of the ANI, flanked by many hundreds of supporters of Ahmat Acyl, leader 
of the Democratic Revolutionary Council [CDR] this week took up positions situated about 
30 km east of Oum Hadjer. On Wednesday, the first group of the 1,300 soldiers of the 
Chadian Armed Forces [FAT] sent to the east as reinforcements arrived at Oum Hadjer from 
Ati, 170 km to the west. This disparate force--professional soldiers, young recruits not 
used to fighting, soldiers of the CDR entirely devoted to their leader--henceforth has a 
common mission: to stop the advancement of the FAN and to retake Abeche. 


V. 21 Dec 81 CENTKA. AFRICA 


This semidesert region, covered by thorns where sandy roads lengthen the duration of 
journeys, is propitious to guerrilla activities. Government forces had the experience at 
the beginning of the month, when Hissein Habre's supporters carried out within 24 hours 
two deadly raids around Kotoro-Falata, a water belt situated 70 km east of Oum Hadjer. 
Since then, the young soldiers of the ANI and the CDR know that it is better not to be hit 
in the chest or in the abdomen -- the length of evacuation to hospitals at Ati and 
Ndjamena rules out any chance of survival. 


We lack ammunition, means of transport, armored vehicles, any form of logistics. I went 

to Ndjamena to make known some of our needs. I hope I was heard. This is how Rakhis 
Manani summarizes the state of mind of the officials and members of the forces. The latter 
hear on the radio that dozens of arms and ammunition recently arrived in Ndjamena from 
France and that Algeria is giving important logistic assistance to the ANI; they do not 
feel they will be the sole beneficiaries of these. 


The vice president of the Transitional National Union Government, Col Abdel Kader Kamougue, 
this week gave a warning on his return to the capital after a brief visit to the east. 


It appears the success of the military efforts in preparation for the eastern front will 
depend on the government's capability to respond to the needs and expectations of its 


soldiers. 


FAO COMMUNIQUE CALLS FOR ROUNDTABLE NEGOTIATION 


AB181247 Paris AFP in French 1028 GMT 18 Dec 81 


[Text] Yaounde, 18 Dec (AFP) -- The 3rd Army of the Western Armed Forces [FAO], one of 
the politicomilitary factions in Chad, whose members are mostly stationed at the banks of 
Lake Chad, demands the organization of a roundtable negotiation grouping the 11 factions 
which signed the Lagos accord, including Hissein Habre's FAN. 


In a communique sent to AFP in Yaounde today, FAO states that this discussion should take 
place under the auspices of the OAU. The FAO feels only the Lagos accord on the Chadian 
problem, applied in its totality, can restore true peace in the country. 


In its communique, the FAO, concerned with the achievement of national unity, calls on the 
OAU and all the countries participating in the neutral force to support the application of 
the Lagos accords. The departure of the Libyan forces of occupation and the stationing 
of the neutral force only constitute one stage on the way to peace, the communique adds.
