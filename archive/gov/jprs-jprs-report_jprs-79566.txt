MASSIVE NEW GULF INSURANCE, REINSURANCE VENTURE FORMED 
Dubai AL-BAYAN in Arabic 21, 24 Oct 81 


/Article by ‘Ali Ibrahim: "The Story of the Birth of the First Arab Insurance 
Leviathan"/ 


/21 Oct 81 p 3/ 


/Text/ Any company that wants to engage in economic activity can enter the market 
for the first time in two ways: either by infiltrating quietly and without con- 
motion and implanting its presence step by step, or by invading the market in the 
midst of a commotion that stirs up great clamor, meaning, at the same time, that 
the entry will be strong from the beginning. 


The Arab Insurance Group chose the latter way to begin its activity, in the midst 
of a massive Arab and international insurance display in Bahrain last Saturday and 
Sunday, on the occasion of the official inauguration of its activity, in the pres- 
ence of more than 700 heads of insurance companies, major investors and journalists 
from various areas of the world. The emirates were represented there by a delega- 
cion headed by Shaykh Hamdan ibn Rashid, minister of finance and industry, the con- 
tained Ahmad al-Tayir, assistant deputy minister of finance and the vice chairman 
of the group, and Ahmad Lutfi, manager of public relations in the ni:-istry. 


The story of the group began, specifically, in April 1980, when circles in inter- 
national insurance organizations greeted the news of the signing by Libya, the 
emirates and Kuwait of a contract to establish a massive insurance and reinsurance 
organization under the name of the Arab Insurance Group with great amazement. 


The source of the amazement was the immensity of the group's authorized capital, 
which was $3 billion, representing 20 times the amount of insurance shares in the 
New York insurance exchange and more than six times the shares of the international 
Lloyd's organization which is more than 300 years old. 


At that time, circles in international insurance organizations raised questions 
on the degree of seriousness of the people who were planning to establish the 
group, especially since they were doing so at a time when the market was suffer- 
ing from recession and a price war. 














However, when the group started its forward progress, international insurance 
organizations came to sense the seriousness of the project, and the group, as 
western sources said at the inauguration celebration, proved that it was more 
advanced than the international bodies had expected and that it could challenge the 
most massive international insurance groups because of its immense financial re- 
sources. 


AL-BAYAN's partic. -ation in the inaugural ceremony displays gave it an opportunity 
to get close to the manner of thinking of the people who established the group and 
its officials in order to observe its procedures, projects and goals.

A Show of Force 


The Western reactions were characterized by receptivity mixed with a certain show 
of force. Peter Green, head of the British Lloyd's group, said that Lloyd's wel- 
comed the Arab Insurance Group as a new force in the world of insurance and wel- 
comed cooperation with it in the field of training its staffs. He added that he 
welcomed the statement by the group's head that it would not outstrip its own ex- 
pertise and go into international markets without sufficient experience. 


Lionel G Jones, chairman of the Panatlantic Insurance Group and chairman of the 
federation of members of the New York exchange, said thet the group had great op- 
portunities, with its massive capital, but that capital was not everything. 


This all shows the extent of the competition and the challenges the group is ex-

We must not expect enormous activittes from it at the beginning, since it is not 
expected immediately to dominate a major share of market transactions; rather, the 
group intends to let its transactions evolve as it acquires the necessary 
expertise.

Yemen on the mediation the two countries had been carrying out between South Yemen
