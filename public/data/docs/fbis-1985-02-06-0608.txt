LIBYA

Libyan Radio on Release of UK Detainees 
LDO51444 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 
1330 GMT 5 Feb 85 


[Text] The four Britons, held in Libya on charges relating to 
security and undermining the economy, were released this morn- 
ing impiementing decisions by the Basic Popular Congresses in 
their third ordinary session of 1984. The release of the Britons 
was made public in a news conference held at the headquarters 
of the General Secretariat of the General People’s Congress in 
Tripoli this morning. It was attended by the secretary of the 
General People’s Committee of the General People’s Congress 
Secretariat, Anglican Church envoy Terry Waite, and a number 
of international radios, news agencies and newspapers correspon- 
dents. 


The secretary of the People’s Committee addressed the news 
conference. He announced the results of the decisions by the 
2,107 basic popular congresses regarding these Britons. The 
results are as follows: 


|. Congresses that decided to unconditionally release the British 
detainees, a decision made out of courtesy to the Anglican 
Church — 272 popular congresses or | 2.9 percent; 


2. Congresses that decided conditionally to release the British 
detainees — 1,752 popular congresses, $3.16 percent or the vast 
majority. The following are the most important conditions put 
forward by this vast majority of popular congresses: 


a. That the British make a commitment not to protect the 
misguided dogs, work to hand them over, and prevent anti- 
Libyan people activities; 

b. That the British Government endeavors to release the 
Libyans detained in Britain; 

c. That the church pledges to care for Libyan students in 
Britain and seeks to have the Libyans detained in Britain 
released; 

d. That information campaigns hostile to the Jamahiriyah 
through news media halt; and 

e. That Britain be committed to treat Libyans according 
to international laws. 


3. Congresses that decided the detainees should not be released 
6 popular congresses; and congresses that did not make clear 
their view about the subject — 77 congresses. 


The secretary of the People’s Committee made it clear at the 
news conference that the basic popular congresses responded to 
the humanitarian appeal by the brother leader of the revolution 
to have these Britons released. He made the decision after 
endeavors by the envoy of the Anglican Church; King Hassan II, 
whom the British prime minister asked to intervene on behalf of 
the detainees; President of Tanzania Julius Nyerere; and also the 
mediation of many Greek and Italian friends.
