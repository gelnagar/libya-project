Libya Working Group Meeting, 24 February 1982 

The Libya Working Group met on 24 February to set in train 

preparations to implement the decisions expected to be made at the ASC meeting 
on Libya on 25 February. It is clear that any new steps against Libya wil] 

involve

a major effort by numerous departments and agencies and the more

ional relations, and relations with allies and other Arab countries

2. 

The following tasks have been laid on, for completion by 26 February 

unless otherwise specified: 

State is to prepare two sets of cables for use in consultations with 
other governments. One will cover the specifics of USG plans and the 
reasons therefore in general terms. The other will be an intelligence 
"bill of particulars." I offered to provide the material we have 
recently pulled together as the basis for this. We will be in touch 
with the DO to obtain the necessary clearances. 

Commerce and Treasury will give State some inputs on what is not being 
done -- financial sanctions, third country measures, etc. -- for use 
in consultations. 

State will do a strategy paper for congressional consultations, laying 
out the committees and individuals to be contacted and by whom. 

State will develop questions that may be asked on the Hill and other 
Agencies will be tasked to prepare answers. é 

State will begin work on press guidance to be used once the decisions 
are formally announced or become known through leaks. In the short 
run, however, the guidance is, "We do not discuss the details of NSC 
deliberations." 

Commerce will begin developing procedures for implementing any 
boycotts or embargoes that are decided upon (completion in two weeks).
