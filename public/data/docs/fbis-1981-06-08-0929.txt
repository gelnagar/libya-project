LIBYA

AL-QADHDHAFI INTERVIEW ON FRANCE, U.S. REPORTED 


LD061946 Tripoli JANA in English 1800 GMT 6 Jun 81 


[Text] Tripoli, 6 Jun (JANA)--The revolution's leader Colonel Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi, 
said Valery Giscard d'Estaing's defeat represents an end to France's colonialist military 
policy and exploitation of Africa, and perhaps an end to U.S. hegemony over France. 


In an interview carried out by the French newspaper LIBERATION yesterday, he said the 
Giscard regime was basically rightist leaning and similar in outlook to the Reagan 
administration. 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi continued: "Most Arab countries were against Mitterrand's election 
because they believed this would signify the victory of socialism in France. But Libya, 
socialist in outlook as it is, we (?welcomed) Mitterrand's victory along with other 
progressive Arab countries." 


Answering a question on the Jamahiriyah's role in resisting d'Estaing's colonialist 
policies, and the effects of this role on the outcome of France's general elections, 
Colonel al-Qadhdhafi said: 


"The Jamahiriyah helped uncover d'Estaing's policy in Africa, his occupation of central 
Africa and the rigging of elections there. (?We) further revealed his connections with 
the Sudanese regime, the selling of arms to the Numayri regime in a bid to threaten 

peace and stability in Chad, plus the sending of French forces to Niger. D'Estaing wanted 
to achieve phony glory for his country at the expense of ordinary French citizens." 


He continued: "D'Estaing never hesitated in his Chad policy, but never found the oppor- 
tunity to intervene militarily. France has no special relationship with Chad because Chad 
is an independent country, a neighbour of ours. Libyan and Chadian territories are one 
and their peoples are one. If there are any problems between the French and Chadian 
governments, Libya feels it is not part of this. But as friends of both parties, Libya 

is capable of offering help to solve these problems...." 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi said: "We respect other people's religions, languages and cultures 
and we do not impose our culture and traditions on anybody." 


The real dialogue between the Arab nation and Europe in general, and France in particular, 
Colonel al-Qadhdhafi said, should be based on the joint interest of the parties con- 
cerned. "In the event of France's partiality for Israel, cooperation will be unlikely, 
whether bilateral or on a more embracing level", he added. Here the leader commented 
that he did not believe France would sacrifice its interest. 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi continued that he did not find a logical reason which should lead 
President Mitterrand to become partial towards Israel. He reasoned: "The president is 

a socialist and the Israelis represent a U.S. base and American rightist policies and 

are leading a policy inspired by war and expansion. Israel is a threat to world peace, 
especially in the Middle East, and it is impossible for a socialist to support the hostile 
rightist camp." 


On the question of American imperialist propaganda regarding the Jamahiriyah's support 
for liberation movements the leader said: "The American Government is not qualified to 
speak on terrorism because it itself practices it on the highest levels in the world. 
It, therefore, has no right to assume the role of policeman."

Colonel al-Qadhdhafi elaborated on this point, saying "There is a great deal of difference 
between terrorism and supporting liberation movements and just causes of freedom-fighting 
people. Thus, it is impossible to place the Palestine Liberation Organisation on the terrorism 
list. The U.S. is notorious for practising all forms of terrorism against Libya and accusing 
us of terrorism is itself one form of its own terrorism."

and Syria, who are also members of a progressive front, that of steadfastness. The AFP report

in the desert of southeastern Libya, was asked about reports from Sudan, which is anti-Lit--n, 
that Tripoli was reinforcing rather than drawing down its troop presence in Chad. Libyan

The report goes on to note that Al-Qadhdhafi said he was confident that there could be a 
Franco~Arab dialog. a 

[A dialogue between the Arab world and Europe in general--France in particular--must be based 
on guarantees of mutual interests of the parties. There must also be political guarantees. 

If France aligns itself with the Israelis, all cooperation will be ruled out, either bilateral 
cooperation or with the whole of the Arab world. But I cannot think that France will sacrifice 
its interests,’ Col. Al-Qadhdhafi told LIBERATION. He also warned against party-to-party
