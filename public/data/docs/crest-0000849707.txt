Summary (AI): A January 1986 CIA talking-points memo for the DDI assesses Qadhafi's domestic standing after the US confrontation, saying he has not significantly strengthened his position and is probably not in immediate danger of being ousted. It also considers how a US strike could affect nationalist sentiment and coup prospects.
• Qadhafi's rallies suggest he has been unable to expand domestic support beyond a hardcore of young radicals.
• Many Libyans reportedly believed the US was not prepared to attack Libya despite Qadhafi's warnings.
• Middle-class Libyans feared US economic sanctions would collapse the Libyan economy within two years.
• Qadhafi's crisis responses included tightening travel restrictions for Libyans, militarizing schools and society, and maintaining a constant military alert.
• Qadhafi reportedly was willing to accept Algerian preconditions, including discussing a Libyan withdrawal from Chad and the Aozou Strip, to meet with President Chadli Benjedid.
• Potential coup plotters were expected to lay low until the crisis subsided to avoid being branded US puppets.
