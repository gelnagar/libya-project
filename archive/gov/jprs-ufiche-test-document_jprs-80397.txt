from outside OPEC, and is this part of a campaign against the organization?

[Answer] Of course this campaign is led by the consumer states, their aim 

is to weaken the collective bargeining power of the OPEC states in order to

in obtaining a suitable price for their resources--which are running dry--or 
their attempt to prolong the age of these resources in order to preserve part 
of them for upcoming generations. 


There is another side to this issue. The exporting states outside OPEC do 
not abide by a fixed or clear and defined price. Their prices are subject to 
supply and demand and to the market situation. This encourages buyers 
because they can obtain immediate prices that are less than official OPEC 
prices.

UNITED ARAB EMIRATES 


PRESS COMMENTARY ON U.S. BOYCOTT OF LIBYAN OIL 
GF271259 Abu Dhabi WAM in Arabic 0602 GMT 27 Feb 82 


[Excerpts] Abu Dhabi, 27 Feb (WAM)--Two local papers comment today on the 
United States' decision to boycott Libyan oil. The papers request Arabs to 
review the decision and ask them not to let Libya face the U.S. threat alone. 
The papers say that this U.S. decision is an extension of hostile American 
stands against the Arabs. 


In its editorial, AL-ITTIHAD calls on OPEC states in general and its Arab 
members in particular to confront the U.S. threat to Libya and to put an end 
to this new conspiracy from the very start. The paper says: ‘What is 
happening to Libya is an example of what will be done with other OPEC states 
in general and Arab members in particular. Through this attitude, Washington 
means to say: I mean you and your neighbor must listen too. 


The paper stresses that the threat Libya faces today--because of the oil 
boycott and the military maneuvers--must draw our attention to what the 
United States has in store for whoever says "no" to it from among the Arabs 
or whoever objects to U.S. hegemony and support for the enemies of the Arabs. 


AL-KHALIJ, which is published in al-Shariaah, says in its editorial today 
that--in confrontation with these threats and in view of the Arab stand, 
which usually remains silent when an aggression occurs--Libya may actually 
resort to signing a friendship and cooperation treaty with the Soviet Union, 
at least in order to protect its economy and secure the equipment and instru- 
ments required for its oil. The paper expects that the United States would 
then propagate its known statement that Libya had been circling in the Soviet 
orbit. The United States would also seek to convince the Arabs to believe 
this. Some Arabs will actually believe it and forget that they did not support 
Libya when it was besieged by the U.S. aggression and that they did not 

stand against the United States when it bit part of our nation. 


The paper expresses wonder at the Arab silence toward the U.S. threats to 
Libya and expresses fear that the United States might interpret this silence 
to mean that the Arabs are afriad of it and that "some Arab states submit to 
it due to military and economic blackmail." The paper adds: ''The United 
States can see the gap in the Arab ranks and has discovered that scores of 
gaps can be created through aggression at one time, threats at another or 
blackmail at another."
