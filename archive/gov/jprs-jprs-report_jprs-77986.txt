[Text] Dr Khalifah al-'Azbi, director of the Foreign Information Office of the 
Libyan Jamahiriyah, said that the Libyan media was directed toward pan-Arabiem and 
unionism, and that media coordination between Libya and Syria was a fait accompli, 
which came within the perspective of full unity between two nations active in the 
Steadfastness and Resistance Front. 


Dr al-'Azbi referred to the Jamahiriyah being exposed to a visious, concentrated 
and organized media attack, directed by the Egyptian regime, all of which is aimed 
at accusing Libya of terrorism. The world must understand that there is a funda- 
mental difference between terrorism and revolution, because the twisted inter- 
national logic sees the daily Zionist bombardment of Lebanese cities and villages, 
and Palestinian camps, as legitimate. We should keep in mind that true terrorism 
is terrifying people, massing aircraft and fleets, and threatening to starve out 
commun it ies. 


Dr al-‘Azbi stressed that right-wing, fascist elements within the Italian parlia- 
ment are the ones who are leading the distortion campaign against Libya. This is 
not in the best interests of the two peoples’ the Italian people bear responsibility 
for this. 


Regarding the crisis with France, Col al-Qadhafi emphasized the friendly relations 
between France and Libya. "We shall not accept conspiracies on our borders or a 
threat to our security. We also reject haggling over our rights.” 


Dr al-'Azbi called for coordination between the Arab media organizations, in view of 
the human, technical and material capabilities, which would qualify us, in the event 
of unity, to fight colonialism, imperialism, and reactionism, for the benefit of 

se Arab Community.

ideological, economic and social aspects, because this revolution has intellectual,

are misunderstood or deliberately distorted. Por our part, we are concerned with 
pucting matters into their natural perspective, with informing world and Arab public 
Opinion about our revolutionary achievements, and with emphasizing its human aspect, 
which has been, and still is, exposed to conspiracies and distortion every day. 
Furthermore, one of the office's basic missions is to present the justice of the 
Palestinian case, to concentrate on the fact that our Arab people in Palestine are 
embarked on a just war of liberation, and that it is their right to bear arme in 
defense of their land and sons. There are several programe allocated to the issues 
of the Palestinian revolution and people, and their legitimate rights. Since the 
inception of the First September revolution, the Jamahiriyah has placed its material,

a burden on the Libyan media in general, and the Foreign Information Office speci- 
fically. We are aware of all these burdens, and undertake them with firmness and 
boldness. They are burdens for which the price must be paid. We ourselves have 
become content with this position, because we now support all liberation causes, 

and all revolutionary liberation movements of all the world's peoples." 


The Media and Libyan-Syrian Unity

Dr al-'Azbi emphasized that, for sometime past, the direction of Libyan media has

tion, we have known the impossibilities of achieving unity. Media coordination be- 
tween the Jamahirivah and Syria is a fait accompli, and is within the perspective 
of full unity. The important thing is that we demand that there be coordination 
among all the Arab media organizations, in order to confront the Zionist, imperial- 
ist challenge against the Arab Nation. We must realize that all Zionist and im- 
perialist media campaigns do not differentiate between a Libyan, an Egyptian, a 
Palestinian, Syrian or any other. On the contrary, they are directed against the 
Arab. They say Arabs, without making a distinction. Therefore, I can state that 
with all the means of Arab media, with its vast material, human and technical capa- 
bilities, we can fight, or rather thwart all the Zionist media campaigns. Our 
numbers, as an Arab community, are several times their number; our capabilities are 
greater than theirs. The difference is that we are divided, when they are joined in 
a definite goal, which is to counteract the Arabs and distort their reputation."

AL-QADHDHAFI EXPLAINS POLICIES TO ITALIAN PAPER 
Rome IL TEMPO in Italian 1 Apr 81 p 22 


[Interview with Al-Qadhdhafi by Livio Colasanti, special correspondent of IL TEMPO: 
"Al-Oadhdhaf ft: I Am a Revolutionary") 


[Text] The Tripoli leader in a frank interview with a group of Italian journalists: 
The visit to Rome "is not definite yet." 


With respect to war damages, the same as was proposed recently with respect to 
Ltaly: 


"The most important thing is the recognition of the full right of Libya to indem- 
nities"; but this matter represents an obstacle to good relations between the two 
countries. 


On the thorny question of Chad: "We have no expansionist aims." "We do not support 
any separatist movement." "But we will give aid to Latin American revolutionaries." 


Regarding the hunt for refugee dissidents in Europe: "Did Italian public opinion 
perhaps show concern over the slaughter of a million Libyans during the occupation 
period?" 


Bengasi,3l March--'\ot only the date, but the visit, itself, to Italy is not yet 
“definite.” As for the war damages that were once again demanded last Saturday in 
Tobruk of Germany, |ltaly, and Great Britain, Libya perhaps might even be satisfied 
with a decluration of principle. A resolute rejection of nuclear weapons and no 
Libyan support of "separatist" movements, such as the IRA and the ETA | Basque 
Fatherland and Liberty Group]. These are the important points of the long interview 
with brother Qadhdhafi (who does not want to be called either president or colonel) 
that he granted to a limited group of Italian journalists, invited to Libya within 
the framework of what the Libyans have characterized as a “friendly exchange" or a 
“goodwill exchange" with our country. 


The meeting took place in a barracks in the center of Bengasi. It was already hot; 
the air lay heavy with the presage of the “simun," which in fact was to blow in a 
few hours. As always, the location of the meeting was made known only at the last

moment and as always security was tight, implacable. We were in a prefabricated 
atructure, It could have been the small waiting room of a secondary air station, 
with reddish armchairs along the walle and a desk behind which towered a wooden 
shield depicting an eagle, the emblem of the Libyan "Jamahirivah."” But brother 
Qadhdhafi, almost as if to emphasize hie lack of connection with power ("I am not 
a politician, but a revolutionary,” he said), did not take a seat. He answered 
questions while standing. He entered with his usual light tread, put on a beige 
“bush-facket," and looked even vouneer and more vigorous than when he was wearing 
a khaki uniform in Tobruk as he addressed a crowd of soldiers, The first remark 
was almost humorous: "Do you just want to say hello to me, or do you also want to 
ask something of me?" No answer was expected. 


The opening remark obviously concerned Chad. At the assembly in Tobruk, called to 
celebrate the expulsion of the British from that base in 1971, Qadhdhafi was quite 
explicit. "We do not have expansionist aims," he had said. But the Chad delega- 
tion had immediately shouted, "We are one people, not two peoples.” But now he 
avoided any binding declaration. He preferred to justify the intervention. "Chad's 
infection,” he said, "threatened to contaminate all of the surrounding African 
countries. We helped the lawful government of Chad to avoid this danger, and we 
thus avoided the return of colonialism, in one form or another. But it must be 
clear that any threat to the peace of Central Africa will have to be imputed to 

the Sudan and Egypt.” 


On the eventuality that Libya might aspire to nuclear weapons, there was a flat 
answer: "We are against any nuclear program, anywhere in the world. We consider 
the manufacture of nuclear weapons to be a crime and we also hope that existing 
weapons will be destroyed. We would like nuclear programs to be converted into 
peaceful programs. Above all, we would hope that there would be no terrorism 
against the small countries that aspire to use this energy for peaceful purposes." 


In Tobruk, too, where many members of the diplomatic corps were present, Qadhdhafi 
had spoken long anc resolutely on war damages. But how is the demand to be under- 
stood? Might Libya, which after all does not have financial problems, be satisfied 
with just a declaration of principle’? The answer, even if circumspect, did not 
atall exclude this hypothesis. "The most important thing is the acknowledgment of 
Libya's full right to the indemnities. As for the material aspects of the question, 
1 believe that there can be negotiation and consideration." Nevertheless, the 
topic immediately came up again when he was asked for an evaluation of relations 
with Italy: "At present these relations are good and for our part we want to keep 
them that way. But the matter of compensation for war damages represents an 
obstacle." 


An undoubtedly new element emerged when we asked brother Qadhdhafi if Libya's 
support of all revolutionary movements, such as those of the IRA or the independent 
Basques, would not end by becoming an objective support of international terrorism. 
The reply was sharp: "We do not support any separatist movement." But immediately 
afterward there was a statement of Libya's revolutionary choice when it was con- 
firmed that Libya would furnish aid to the revolutionary movements of Latin-America. 
Furthermore, at the assembly in Tobruk, Sandinist Colonel Thomas had spoken at 

















length and had explicitly expressed Nicaragua's appreciation to the Libyan people 
for the aid provided to the revolutionary movement. 


What will you do when you retire from political life? “I am not involved in any 
political activities, I am a revolutionary. In our country, the people, through 
the people's committees, are the ones who are in charge of political matters." 
When you come to Italy, will you visit the Fiat company? And when you go to 
Moscow, will you talk with Breahnev about the Afghan matter? The answers were 
partially evasive, partially completely lacking. At the word, Fiat, brother 
Qadhdhafi only smiled. Of the visit to Italy, he said that it was not yet 
“definite.” Of the visit to Russia, he said nothing at al) 


Very severe, even resentful, was the reply to the question a leading leftist 
journalist who asked him what he thought of the killing of  -:byan citizens in 
Italy. Oadhdhafi answered with a question: "Did Italian public opinion perhaps 
show concern over the slaughter of a million Libyans during the occupation period 
and is it worried now about the mass killing of the Palestinian and Lebanese 
people?" The answer was that we were concerned. "But how long must the European 
left have to pay for the errors of colonialism? We are concerned over the killing 
of Palestinians, but also over that of dissident Libyans." But not even this 
restatement of the matter by our leftist colleague lessened Qadhdhafi's outburst 
of temper. He coldly replied: "This happens because you have not understood the 
Situation. I mean that the right in Europe besieges the left through propaganda. 
If you had fully understood the reality of the various situations, you would surely 
have allied yourselves with us. However, we attach a great deal of importance to 
the European left." It is easy to see that there was no repudiation of the recent 
series of outrages against expatriated Libyan citizens. 


Then we came to the concluding remarks. To the journalist who asked Qadhdhafi why 

he, who declares himself to be the only true interpreter of Islamic orthodoxy, had 
prevented Libyan citizens from visiting Mecca, sacred to every Moslem, he explained: 
"It is not true. I simply condemned the American occupation of Mecca. This is 

what made the pilgrimage impossible, since Mecca is now under a political power. 

We do not have relations with Saudi Arabia. How could Libyan pilgrims go into the 
territory of a country with which we do not have relations? This is the error-- 
putting holy places under a political power. They must be internationalized."” 


Again there were questions on relations between Italy and Libya. "The factors that 
unite us are much more numerous than those that divide us. But one must also take 
into account the influence that has been exerted by Italian colonialism and also 

by the Americans--which is contrary to the interests of the Italian people." Again: 
"We want to mobilize the Italian people to rebel against the continuance of American 
bases on their territory. Because in this case it is the very destiny of the Italian 
people that is in danger. In any nuclear attack, Italy today will be a central tar- 
get. italy is now a shield for the United States, a shield that avoids possible 
attacks against America itself."
