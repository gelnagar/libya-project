JANA Editor Denies Connection With Karachi Hijack 
LDO51329 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1247 GMT 
5 Sep 86 


[Text] News agencies reported that gunmen today hijacked a Pan 
American jumbo aircraft while it was stopping over at Karachi 
airport en route to Frankfurt and New York. The news agencies 
reported that the identity of the gunmen who seized the aircraft 
is not yet known, and that three of the aircraft's crew, including 
the pilot, were able to escape. The BBC reported that the 
hijackers who boarded the plane disguised as Pakistani security 
men asked to be taken aboard the plane to Cyprus, and that 
Pakistani airport security men have encircled the plane and 
prevented anyone from approaching the airport. 


The JANA political editor today commented on repo .s by news 
agencies on a statement by an anonymous person which says that 
the group that hijacked the U.S. civilian airliner this morning at 
Karachi airport belongs to what this person called the Libyan 
Revolutionary Cells. He said: We were surprised by the news 
agencies’ report that an anonymous person has stated that the 
group which hijacked the U.S. civilian airliner this morning at 
Karachi airport belongs to what he called the Libyan Rev- 
olutionary Cells. We in Libya affirm that we have no connection 
whatsoever with what happened and express our surpris. at such 
methods being used to involve Libya in this matter. 


The JANA editor adds: Noonecan believe such obvious tactics 
because the Great Jamahiriyah is the primary victim of terror-

cent old men, women, or children anywhere, be it on board a 
plane or anywhere else. It is for this reason that the Jamahiriyah 
has enacted the Harabah Law, which is one of the harshest laws 
for combatting terrorism.

UN Delegate Al-Turayki Views ‘U.S. Threats’ 
PM041911 London AL-MAJALLAH in Arabic 3-9 Sep 86 p 14 


[Interview with head of Libyan UN delegation Dr ‘Abd al-Salam 
al-Turayki by Salih Qallab by telephone from New York; date 
not given] 


[Text] Qallab: How serious are the “U.S. threats”? Do you 
really expect a new raid? 

Al-Turayki: U.S. officials have not concealed their intentions 
toward Libya. Events have proved that what we have been telling 
the world community about Washington’s intentions against us 
is true. We now confirm that there are elements in the U.S. 
Administration that are seeking to create tension in the area and 
to launch a new attack against Libya. 


Qallab: Do you expect Washington to carry out a military 
action or will it be content with diplomatic and economic mea- 
sures? 

Al-Turayki: Washington is now in a difficult situation. The 
U.S. Administration is in a very conflicting situation; it talks 
about having evidence of Libya’s involvement in what it calls 
terrorist operations, but in fact it has found out that its organs 
have misled it and that there is no such evidence. We believe that 
one of the objectives of the U.S. alert against Libya is to cover 
up certain U.S. attitudes toward what is happening in South 
Africa and to cover up George Bush’s recent visit to the Middle 
East as well as to circumvent the economic situation in Egypt. 
[as published] 


Qallab: But Washington claims that the “revolutionary com- 
mittees” recently adopted a decision to resume acts of violence, 
a decision that has been taken as being an official Libyan line. 
Al-Turayki: This is unacceptable logic, and if we were to adopt 
it, then we would consider any decision or action by a group of 
students in the United States as reflecting an official stand by 
the White House and the U.S. Administration. What Washing- 
ton says in this regard is simply an attempt to justify its campaign 
against Libya and against the Arab nation. In any case, we call 
for the setting up of an international committee consisting of 
neutral states to search in the Libyan territory for what Washing- 
ton calls “terrorist” bases. Furthermore, if Washington claims 
that it has evidence supporting its allegations, then we are ready 
to seek adjudication by the International Court of Justice. 


Qallab: It is understood from your statement that Libya is not 
officially committed to the revolutionary committees’ resolution 
and that these committees’ resolution on escalating violence will 
not be carried out by the Libyan state. 

Al-Turayki: The Libyan state commits itself to resolutions 
only by the “popular congresses.”” These congresses are the 
highest authority in the country; they draw up the country’s 
foreign and domestic policies on all levels. 


Qallab: On the eve of the previous U.S. raid on Tripoli and 
Banghazi you were expecting a direct Soviet intervention. Did 
you receive assurances that the Soviet Union’s attitude this time 
will be different if your country becomes the target of a U.S 
attack? 

Al-Turayki: Neither in the past nor in the future will we wager 
on anything but our people and the Arab nation. In the past the 
Libyan people confronted Mussolini’s fascism and it is now 
confonting Reagan’s fascism. 


Qallab: President al-Asad visited Libya when the recent esca- 
lation was at its height. A statement was issued saying that any 
attack or Libya will be regarded as an attack on Syria. Do you 
believe that the Syrian stand will be put into effect if there should 
be a repetition of what happened last April? 

Al-Turayki: I have no doubt about this. We are confident 
about our alliance with Syria. We fully believe that this alliance 
is natural, firm, and strategic. We have confidence in our Arab 
nation of which Syria is one of the principal countries. But we 
realize that division and rifts encourage our enemies to launch 
adventures against us. If there were a unified and firm Arab 
stand the United States would not have dared to do what it did 
and what it intends to do. 


Qallab: It has been rumored that the plan Washington is 
talking about calls for supporting Libyan opposition. Are you 
anticipating any specific action in this sphere? 

ésl-Turayki: It has become clear during the recent events that 
the United States, which is classified as one of the world’s two 
superpowers, has no true information and no real evaluation of 
the internal situation in Libya.
