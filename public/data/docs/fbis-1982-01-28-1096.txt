LIAISON BUREAU SOURCE SAYS U.S. FORCES IN CHAD 


LD271644 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1615 GMT 27 Jan 82 


[Text] Tripoli, 27 Jan (JANA) -—- A source at the People's Foreign Liaison Bureau in 
Tripoli today issued the following statement. 


It has been established that there are elemnts of U.S. forces among some of the African 
peacekeeping units in Chad. This is regarded as a grave development and one which the 
Socialist People’s Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah and other African states that care for security 
and stability in Chad cannot ignore. 


A spokesman added that the interference by and the presence of the forces of a great power 
in Chad will place Chad within the sphere of international conflict and will shake stability 
and security in this fraternal African country and consequently expose to danger the peace 
and security of Africa. 


The Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah, which decided not to send military forces 
into Chad even if asked to do so, canmmot remain indifferent to the presence of the U.S. 
forces in Chad. While exposing this U.S. interference and military presence in Chad, 
which are a violation of the OAU resolutions on Chad, the Socialist People's Libyan Arab 
Jamahiriyah calls on the fraternal African countries and the OAU to shoulder their 
responsibilities in this respect. 


GOVERNMENT DENIES REPORTED CONTACTS WITH ECYPT 


LD271454 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1430 GMT 27 Jan 82 


[Text] Kuwait, 27 Jan (JANA) -- The Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah today 
categorically denied recent Kuwaiti press reports about alleged contacts between Cairo 
and Tripoli. 


A statement issued by the Jamahiriyah political mission in Kuwait, published in the 

Kuwaiti press today, said relations with the Egyptian regime are not a question of bilateral 
disputes or personal attitudes and the Socialist People’s Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah's position 
stems from its pan-Arab commitment as dictated by the supreme national interests of the 
Arab nation. 


The statement referred to the resolutions adopted by the Arab countries with regard to 
boycotting the Egyptian regime because it had thrown itself in the lap of Zionism and 
U.S. imperialism and pointed out that this attitude should not prevent the return of 
Egypt to the Arab nation’s fold if the Egyptian regime retracts from its policy and 
abrogates all its ties with the Zionist enemy and U.S. policy, which is hostile to the 
Arabs. 


The statement, as carried by Kuwaiti papers, says all the reports published in this regard 
are completely fabricated and aim at harming the Jamahiriyah people and their established 
pan-Arab stands and also at harming the interests of the Arab nation, thereby serving 

the Zionist enemy and U.S. imperialist policy in the area. Furthermore, the statement 
said, it is an attempt to distort Arab steadfastness in the face of capitulatist designs. 


COMMENTARY SCORES REAGAN ADDRESS TO NATION 


LD271518 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1330 GMT 27 Jan 82 
{"Abd al-Hamid ibn Barkah commentary] 


[Text] Last night, U.S. President Ronald Reagan made a speech to the U.S. people, in which 
he made a general review of his domestic and foreign policies. 


Vv. 28 Jan 82 Q2 NORTH AFRICA 


He admitted his policy's failure to make any economic progress. He spoke of the financial 
deficit in the general state budget, which he said, will not exceed $100 billion this year. 


On the occasion of 1 year since Reagan's assumption of office, observers pwint out that 
Reagan's failure seems real as shown by the statistics which speak of the high inflation 
rate, high bank interests and the escalation of unemployment to levels unprecedented in 
the United States. Unemployment now stands at 8.9 percent of the total manpower, that is, 
about 9.5 million umemployed. This constitutes the worst economic crisis in the United 
States since World War II. 


U.S. official figures show that national income dropped in the last 4 months of 1981 by 
5.3 percent, which represents the highest drop since the 1980 crisis. 


The deterioration of economic conditions has intensified the growing rate of inflation, 
which has reached 10 percent. This constant deterioration of the U.S. economy is the 
result of Reagan"s umsound policy in rumning matters and a bias in favor of the interests 
of the capitalist forces and monopolist companies at the expense of the overwhelming 
majority of the U.S. people. Reagan has cut taxes on major capitals and increased taxes 
on other sectors particularly education and health services. 


To find a way out of this impasse, President Reagan began to rely increasingly on the 
export of arms to specific areas of the world. U.S. arms sales during the current fiscal 
year set a record $24 billion, that is more than $65 million per day. 


This concentration on arms sales is in line with the terrorist course adopted by the 

U.S. administration and its persistent efforts to impose its will over the world and 

check the will of the peoples struggling for freedom. Foremost among the countries 
attached to the bandwagon of U.S. arms are the racist regimes in South Africa and occupied 
Palestine. 


Perhaps the biggest negative element in the U.S. budget is its move toward the militerization 
of the foreign policy of the United States -—- a move adopted by Reagan with such vehemence 
that it led the U.S. administration to commit acts it should not have committed. Recently, 
U.S. relations with other world countries have witnessed some straining particularly in 

view of the United States’ oper interference in the internal affairs of world countries 

and peoples. 


Consequently, this has had its effect on the general state budget of the United States. 
Military expenditure in the budget has been put at $327 billion in the coming year — an 
increase, which undoubtedly, is being made at the expense of the interests of U.S. society 
particularly umder the slogan of cutting taxes on major capitalist and monopolist companies. 


SUDAN RECIME FALTERINC: PRICE INCREASES PENDING 


LD280908 Tripoli JANA in English 0810 GMT 28 Jam 82 


[Text] Khartoum, 28 Jan (JANA) -—- The Sudanese ruler has begun to falter in his internal 
policies as his last moments are getting nearer andthereins of pcwer is slipping away 
from his hand following the uprisings of the courageous Sudanese people. 


During this week, Numayri issued a series of decisions to dissolve his political apparatus, 
dismiss his vice president and replace him by others. Omce more, he is desperately 

trying to save his regime from collapse. He issued ome more statement for the formation of 
a committee which includes 73 of his aides whose responsibility is to draft a new policy 
for the so-called Sudanese Socialist Union so as to make it easier to rule. 


Vv. 28 Jan 82 Q 3 NORTH AFRICA 


Concerning the acute internal economic crisis, which has reached its worst stage since 
British colonialism, Numayri ordered yesterday a price increase of 25 percent on imported 
cigarettes and 10 percent om home produced ones. The regime's minister of economy found 
a pretext for the increase by saying that it was part of Numayri's decision to revive the 
economy and back the bankrupt budget of Sudan. 


It is worth noting that Numayri had ordered a price increase on sugar and petrol and is 
likely to announce soon further increases in food prices.
