Trade  Structure 
* 

In  1989,  West  Germany’s  need  for  oil  was  2.3  million 
barrels  a  day  [pbd]  compared  to  2.4  million  a  day  in 
1988.  Local  production  covers  less  than  five  percent  of 
the  total  local  demand  and  the  rest  is  imported.

West  Germany  buys  60  percent  of  its  oil  needs  via 
bilateral  deals  and  the  other  40  percent  is  obtained  from 
oil  markets. 


Oil-exporting  Arab  counties  had  a  35  percent  share  of 
West  Germany’s  known-origin  oil  imports  for  1989,  the 
same  rate  as  in  1988.  This  share,  however,  does  not 
include  oil  exports  from  some  Arab  countries  such  as 
Egypt  and  Yemen  which  are  listed  under  the  “other  Arab 
countries”  category.  Furthermore,  spot  market  deals 
comprise  an  important  factor  for  Arab  oil.  Because  of 
special  arrangements  between  Libya  and  the  Hamburg 
oil  refinery,  Libya  is  considered  the  largest  crude  oil 
exporter  to  West  Germany  (230,000  bpd).  In  1989, 
Saudi  Arabia  was  in  second  place  (120,000  bpd),  fol¬ 
lowed  by  Algeria  (85,000  bpd).  Algeria  also  exports  to 
West  Germany  liquified  natural  gas  which  accounted  for 
about  12  percent  of  Algeria’s  total  exports  to  West 
Germany  in  the  first  six  months  of  1989.  As  for  Kuwait, 
its  oil-product  exports  to  West  Germany  were  estimated 
at  about  48  percent,  which  is  more  than  its  crude  oil 
exports  to  this  country. 


In  terms  of  value,  hydrocarbons  are  considered  the  main 
Arab  export  to  West  Germany.  However,  many  other 
Arab  countries  export  to  West  Germany  other  products 
that  are  vital  to  their  economies.  Cotton  and  cotton 
textiles  accounted  for  2 1  percent  of  Egypt’s  total  exports 
to  West  Germany  in  1989  (January-June);  aluminum 
accounted  for  50  percent  of  Bahrain’s  total  exports;  and 
foodstuffs  accounted  for  23  percent  and  apparel  for  40 
percent  of  Morocco’s  total  exports.  As  for  Tunisia, 
apparel  accounts  for  about  67  percent  of  its  total  exports 
to  West  Germany.  The  important  standing  of  Tunisian 


and  Moroccan  apparel  is  attributed  to  the  preferred 
status  these  two  countries  enjoy  in  exporting  to  EEC 
markets.

Industrial  Sector 

The  report  summed  up  last  year’s  industrial  develop¬ 
ment  accomplishments  and  indicators,  as  follows: 

•  New  industrial  plant  expansions:  Industrial  permits 
have  been  granted  to  four  large  industrial  plants  in  the 
food,  metal,  and  chemical  industries. 

•  New  industrial  vocation  expansions:  Permits  have 
been  granted  to  76  new  industrial  vocations  in  var¬ 
ious  industrial  fields. 

•  Existing  industrial  plant  expansions:  13  industrial 
plants  have  expanded  their  industrial  production. 

•  Start  of  actual  production:  Actual  production  at  15 
new  industrial  plants  has  started.
