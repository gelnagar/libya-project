LIBYA

IRANIAN PRIME MINISTER DEPARTS 21 JULY 
LD211344 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 21 Jul 82 


[Text] Brother Mir-Hoseyn Musavi, prime minister of the Islamic Republic of Iran, and 
the delegation accompanying him left Tripoli this morning. Staff Maj Gen ‘Abd as-Salam 
Ahmad Jallud headed the delegation that saw him off at Tripoli International Airport. 
Brother members of the general people's committee, a number of officers of the armed 
forces and Iran's ambassador to the Jamahiriyah also saw him off. 


Brother Musavi declared that the talks during his brief visit to the Jamahiriyah dealt 
with current conditions in the Arab region, especially the barbaric Zionist aggression 
against the Lebanese and Palestinian peoples, and that the views of the Jamahiriyah and 
Iran on the aggression are the same, especially as regards repelling the attack and 
foiling all imperialist and reactionary plots against the Arab and Islamic nations. 


22 Jul 82 Q 2 NORTH AFRICA 


Brother Musavi, in a statement to a JANA correspondent on his departure from Tripoli 
International Airport, affirmed the stand of the Great al-Fatih Revolution and the 
Islamic revolution in Iran in the face of all imperialist and Zionist plots aimed at 

the freedom of the Arab and Islamic nations and of all the helpless peoples struggling 
for their freedom and salvation from imperialist and Zionist hegemony. In his statement, 
he emphasized the need for the unity of the Islamic nation in the face of the plans and 
plots hatched by imperialism, Zionism and reaction and for a unified action plan to 
confront these plots. 


At the end of his statement, he praised the results of his talks in the Jamahiriyah. 
He also extended greetings to the Libyan Arab people and the leadership of their historic 


revolution on the occasion of the blessed ‘Id al-Fitr. 


OPPOSITIONIST ON STRUGGLE AGAINST AL-QADHDHAFI 


PM220721 Casablanca LE MATIN in French 7 Jul 82 pp 1, 4 


[Interview with ‘Abd al-Hamid Bakkush, secretary general of the Libyan Liberation Organi- 
zation, by Mustapha Zehimi ] 


[Text] Opposition to Colonel al-Qadhdhafi's regime is being organized. ‘Abd al-Hamid 
Bakkush, secretary general of the Libyan Liberation Organization, recently showed us the 
political manifesto that governs its activities. 


Bakkush is a former Libyan prime minister, ambassador to Paris and high official in 
Libya, which he left in 1977. His organization's chairman is Bashir ar-Rabiti and its 
deputy chairman is Yusuf Abu Ruwavis. 


Many people oppose the Al-Qadhdhafi regime but there has been no organized opposition. 
Referring to this, ‘Abd al-Hamid Bakkush first stated that the platform of the new organ- 
ization is: "Revolution for liberty, justice and fraternity." He believes the objectives 
defined in that platform are likely to interest everyone being organized whether in Libya 
or abroad with a view to overthrowing Colonel al-Qadhdhafi's present regime which is based 
on repression, murder, terror and personal power. 


He further emphasized that the Libyan people are now subjected to a most bloody oppression. 


Bakkush says he believes the regime represents a real plot against the Libyan people 

and calls on all patriots to rise, organize themselves and join the ranks of the Libyan 
Liberation Organization to end these dark years in his country's history. He also believes 
the present Tripoli regime has remained in power only because of the active support received 
from certain non-African powers that have infiltrated the state apparatus and the country's 
security services. 


The appeal addressed to Libyan patriots is not only consistent with patriotic requirements 
but also shows a desire to restore the Libyan citizen's dignity. ‘Abd al-Hamid Bakkush 
explained in this connection that even though his organization is primarily political, 

it will have no future option but to wage a military struggle against the Tripoli regime. 


Figures have been given with respect to waste in the Libyan economy, a lack of a real 
national budget, foreign debts and squandering of public resources. They pinpoint the 
regime's failure. 


However, there is worse than that. ‘Abd al-Hamid Bakkush emphasizes the fact that the 
regime has been transformed into an agency of powers alien to Africa, that have established 
throughout our continent a network of relays and "training" centers that are, in fact, 
bridgeheads for penetration and subversion. The ramifications of these networks are 

known in some places. They serve hegemonist ideologies.
