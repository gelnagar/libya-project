Summary (AI): A December 1981 New York Times article reports President Reagan insisting that the threat from Libyan assassination teams was real and based on several sources, after the White House had banned travel to Libya and told Americans there to leave. It also describes leaks, confusion among officials, and an informer who took lie detector tests.
• Last week the White House banned travel by Americans to Libya and advised Americans living there to leave.
• Federal agencies circulated composite sketches of five terrorists to senior government officials.
• The State Department cabled a detailed account of the threat to embassies.
• The first indication of a Libyan plot followed American pilots shooting down Libyan jets off Libya in August, per officials.
• An American diplomat in Paris barely escaped an attack in November that officials thought involved Libya.
• The FBI said many tips received were dead ends.
• 1981-12-17: President Reagan news conference denying the administration overreacted to reports of Libyan assassination teams; said the threat was real and based on several sources
• 1981-11: Informer reportedly flown to the United States and given polygraph tests by the FBI, Secret Service and CIA, whose results persuaded officials the threat was real
