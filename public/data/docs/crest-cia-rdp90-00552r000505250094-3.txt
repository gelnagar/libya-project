Summary (AI): A New York Times article reports Libyan leader Muammar el-Qaddafi denying a plot to assassinate President Reagan, while the State Department says it has evidence of such plans. It also covers U.S. alert measures and congressional reactions. Nothing here is tied to a specific Tripoli site.
• Qaddafi denied on the day of the article that he had plotted to assassinate President Reagan or anyone else.
• The State Department said it had evidence of such plans; FBI and Secret Service agents were reportedly put on alert after reports that five terrorists trained in Libya had come to the United States.
• Qaddafi was interviewed on television and in the Italian daily Corriere della Sera, where he denied Libya had helped groups such as the Red Brigades.
• Senator Moynihan said the United States had firm evidence that Qaddafi had been planning terrorist actions for years.
