Summary (AI): A Wall Street Journal article (6 October 1986) reports that the Reagan administration's psychological campaign against Libya's Gadhafi damaged U.S. credibility, with senators criticizing the deception plan. It describes U.S. concerns about Libyan-backed terrorism and options including a possible repeat of the April 1986 U.S. bombing. No Tripoli-specific sites or events are described.
• The article is a reprint of a Wall Street Journal piece dated 6 October 1986, marked declassified in part.
• Evidence cited included a Libyan airliner making an unscheduled stop in Cyprus after an attack on a British base, and a terrorist caught in Togo with a bomb similar to those used in Libyan training camps.
• Officials said the FBI was asked to find the source of the leaks to the Post and the Journal.
• The article mentions a proposal to encourage the French to move against Libyan forces in Chad, which Paris rejected.
• 1986-08-06: State Department memo, reported by the Washington Post, allegedly called for spreading false information overseas, partly to encourage a coup or assassination attempt; the White House said it had no standing as administration policy.
• 1986-08-25: Wall Street Journal story on U.S. belief that Libya was stepping up terrorism; the White House strongly confirmed it, and officials later said some falsehoods were passed to U.S. newspapers.
• 1986-08: U.S. issued three alerts to embassies and military posts warning of possible resumed Libya-, Syria- and Iran-backed terrorism; UN Ambassador Vernon Walters was sent to Europe with the same message.
