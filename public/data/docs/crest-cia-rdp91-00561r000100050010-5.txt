Summary (AI): A December 1981 Washington Post column by Joseph Kraft criticizes the leaking of intelligence about alleged Libyan hit squads targeting President Reagan, and describes the administration's tense response to Qaddafi. It covers Libyan foreign policy in general terms, with no places in or near Tripoli.
• Reports said Libyan hit squads were after President Reagan; the stories first appeared on ABC News, then Newsweek and The New York Times.
• Qaddafi denied the hit squad charge in an interview on the David Brinkley show and called Reagan 'silly', 'ignorant' and 'unfit' to be president.
• Libyan planes were shot down in the summer of 1981 after attacking American jets on naval maneuvers in the Mediterranean.
• Qaddafi made Libya available to the Soviet Union as a storage site for weapons.
• The US relied mainly on France and a regional African force in Chad, which was replacing Libyan troops there.
