Summary (AI): A 1983 Washington Times article reports former Reagan adviser Joseph Churba urging covert and overt U.S. support for overthrowing Qaddafi, including possibly his assassination. He also claims that in 1971 Libyan officers who sought U.S. support were handed over to Qaddafi and killed.
• Churba said Qaddafi's oil wealth provided heavy financing for a new airstrip on Grenada.
• Churba said Qaddafi had become a major destabilizing force in Latin America, Africa and the Mideast.
• Churba said he was not calling for direct military action but for covert and overt means, and that assassination should be considered among alternatives.
• Churba said the administration did not adopt the original defense advisory team's thinking on dismantling the Soviet empire, including Libya.
• 1971: According to Churba, a group of Libyan officers told the American Embassy they planned to overthrow Qaddafi and sought support; the information was passed to Qaddafi and the officers were eliminated.
