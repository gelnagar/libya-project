AMERICAN PILOTS REPORTED FLYING IN LIBYAN FORCES

Summary (AI): A 1981 New York Times article (reprinted in a CIA reading-room file) reports that Americans, Canadians and Britons recruited by former CIA agent Edwin Wilson, then a fugitive living in Libya, were flying and maintaining Libyan Air Force aircraft, including helicopters used in the Chad campaign. It also gives the size of the Libyan air fleet as reported by U.S. aircraft company spokesmen.
• One Western pilot recruited by Wilson said American pilots flew helicopters in support of the Libyan invasion of Chad that year; he was paid about $3,000 a month.
• Recruiting program started about two years earlier and included dozens of pilots, flight engineers and mechanics from the US, Britain and Canada.
• Libyan Air Force reportedly had eight C-130s, 20 CH-47 Chinook helicopters, 10 727s, nine C-47s and one 707.
• Wilson was under a federal indictment (1980) for illegally shipping explosives to Libya to train terrorists.
• Several Wilson-controlled companies used the London office of Brilhurst Ltd.
• The US State Department said the involvement did not appear to violate American law; neutrality laws apparently did not apply.
• 1981-10-22: New York Times reports dozens of Western pilots and mechanics recruited by Wilson flying and maintaining Libyan military aircraft.
