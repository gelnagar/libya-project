Summary (AI): Broadcasts from Tripoli carry Qadhdhafi's April 1985 address urging Sudanese people to continue the revolt that toppled Numayri, plus Libyan reactions to the Sudanese coup. The material concerns Libyan foreign policy toward Sudan, with no specific places in or near Tripoli beyond a Tripoli rally venue.
• Qadhdhafi's 5 April 1985 address was broadcast by Tripoli Voice of the Greater Arab Homeland; the place of recording was not given.
• Qadhdhafi urged Sudanese to seize the airport, radio, electricity and hospitals and to send a delegation to Libya.
• Libya declared recognition of the new Sudanese regime on 6 April 1985, while affirming neutrality on internal developments.
• Libyan radio reported demonstrations toward the Egyptian Embassy demanding that Egypt hand over Numayri.
• 1985-04-05: Qadhdhafi broadcast an address from Tripoli urging Sudanese workers, peasants and soldiers to seize factories, the airport, radio and hospitals and declare popular authority after the uprising against Numayri.
• 1985-04-06: Libya's People's Bureau announced recognition of the new Sudanese regime; Qadhdhafi sent a message urging SPLA commander John Garang to join the revolution.
• 1985-04-07: Libyan foreign minister Ali Abd al-Salam al-Turayki denied Libya had been behind the Sudanese coup.

: Al-Turayki Denies Involvement 
LDO70921 Kuwait KUNA in Arabic 0900 GMT 7 Apr 85 

[Text] Kuwait, 7 Apr (KUNA) — The Libyan foreign minister, 
‘Ali ‘Abd al-Salam al-Turayki, has denied that his country was 
behind the coup which took place in Sudan yesterday saying that 
“the recent events in Sudan were internal and the Sudanese 
people alone were the ones that staged them.” 

The denial was made during a telephone interview with the 
Kuwaiti paper AL-WATAN. In this interview, published today, 
Al-Turayki said “what has taken place in Sudan was an upsurge 
which put an end to many long years of suffering by the Sudanese 
people. It was a natural result of the practices of the defunct 
regime.” 

With regard to his government’s position toward any foreign 
intervention in Sudan, Al-Turayki said that his country will not 
keep quiet in the case of any foreign intervention in Sudan 
because “of our responsibility as a fraternal Arab country adjoin- 
ing Sudan.”“With the ending of the anti-Sudanese people and 
anti-Arab nation regime in Sudan,” he added, a new chapter 
has been opened in Libyan-Sudanese relations.” During the 
defunct regime, it is recalled, Libyan-Sudanese relations wit- 
nessed a great deal of tension and disharmony. The defunct 
regime had close relations with the Egyptian regime. 

Al-Turayki said that the Arab nation is called upon to exploit 
Sudan’s enormous wealth for the good of the Sudanese people 
and the Arab nation. “Sudan,” he added, “is one of the richest 
Arab countries and can feed Africa and the Arab homeland.” 
Al-Turayki expressed his country’s readiness to extend its hand 
to Sudan and to its people and help them.
