Summary (AI): A 1981 Boston Globe Magazine article describes how former CIA employees Edwin P. Wilson and Francis E. Terpil and American mercenaries ran a program training terrorists and camouflaging bombs for Qaddafi's Libya from 1976 through at least 1979. The operation was based in a basement of servants' quarters at a former royal palace about twenty miles south of Tripoli.
• More than twenty Americans, including five former CIA employees, were flown to Libya to support Khadafy's military.
• Millions of dollars of military hardware, including 25,000 pounds of explosives and a ground-to-air Redeye missile, were contracted for shipment to Libya without required US approval.
• Some Americans camouflaged terrorist bombs as lamps, candles, briefcases and ashtrays; others trained Libyan commandos in bomb detonation and parachuting.
• The article says the Libyan project was run by mercenaries, with some participants believing the US government knew of it.
• 1976: Start of an alleged American-run program supplying explosives and training for terrorists in Libya, reportedly running through at least 1979.
• 1976-09: Americans involved in the Libyan affair informed the CIA of the operation; the agency then notified the FBI.
• 1979-12: Operation reportedly continued until at least late December 1979, per a federal prosecutors' report.
