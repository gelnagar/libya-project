C.I.A. DENIES ANY OFFICIAL ROLE IN LIBYA ACTIVITIES OF 2 EX-AGENTS
https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp09s00048r000100020098-4
https://archive.org/details/cia-readingroom-document-cia-rdp09s00048r000100020098-4

Summary (AI): A 1981 New York Times article reprinted in a CIA reading-room file reports the agency denied any official role in the Libyan activities of former agents Edwin P. Wilson and Frank Terpil, who were accused of shipping explosives to Libya to train terrorists. The statement says the agency investigated and referred allegations to the FBI and U.S. Attorney's Office.
• The CIA statement said it referred all allegations of criminal complicity to the FBI and the U.S. Attorney's Office under executive order 12036.
• Wilson and Terpil left the CIA in 1971 according to the article.
• Terpil was reported living in Tripoli, Libya, and Wilson was reported in Beirut, Lebanon.
• The House Select Committee on Intelligence was examining the CIA's handling of the case.
• 1976: Mr. Wilson planned the sale of explosive timing devices to Libya at a meeting attended by Thomas G. Clines, per the article
• 1977-04: After a news account of Wilson's Libya activities, DCI Stansfield Turner's inquiry led to dismissal of two CIA employees
