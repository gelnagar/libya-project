Summary (AI): A 1958 CIA Office of National Estimates memo assesses the Western position in Libya as deteriorating, with Egyptian and Soviet subversion and risks to King Idris's government after his death. It discusses Western bases, British troops at Tobruk, and possible Western intervention, with no specific Tripoli sites named.
• The memo is dated 26 November 1958 and addressed to the Director of Central Intelligence.
• Libya's people were described as divided on tribal and geographical lines, with the King the only symbol of national and religious unity.
• British forces in Tobruk could maintain order, but more Western troops would be needed to control the coastal areas and main centres.
• The Soviets would likely respond to Western intervention with propaganda rather than direct action, as they did in Lebanon and Jordan.
• Continued US aid would stabilize the government only temporarily.
