London AL-~MAJALLAH in Arabic No 111, 27 Mar-2 Apr 82 p 6 
[Article: "Behind the Syria-Iran ‘Alliance'"] 


[Text] The visit of the Syrian foreign minister, Mr 'Abd-al-Halim Khaddam, 
to Iran at the head of a large official delegation consisting of 40 members, 
signifies an important change in Syrian policy not only with regard to Iran, 
but particularly with regard to Syrian-Iraqi relations and Syria's relations 
with the countries of the Arab Gulf. 


Despite the fact that Syrian-Iranian relations have been close and warm, and 
Syria's position on the Iraq-Iran war has pleased the Iranian ayatollahs, 
observers of political developments inthe Middle East note that Syria has 
until recently managed to maintain relations with the Arab regimes which have 
taken a position in support of Iraq in the war. 


However, the current shift in Syria's political position changes the former 
policy. The Syrians have now become adversaries not only of Iraq and Jordan, 
but the Syrian-Iranian alliance has caused the Gulf Cooperation Council [GCC] 
to study the question of cutting off aid and allocation to Syria "becau

the framework of a "progressive Islamic front." Khaddam said that Damascus 
and Tehran would adopt joint positions on most political issues of concern 

to both nations, in particular with the Iraq-Iran war, opposition to "secular 
heresy" and confronting Zionism. Khaddam publicly attacked the Iraqi regime 
for "getting embroiled" in the war with Iran. He also accused it of providing 
the "Muslim Brotherhood" in Syria with arms. In a recent speech Syrian 
President Hafiz al-Asad said that Iraq had supplied the "brotherhood" with 
the weapons which had been used in the recent events in Hamah. 


Military Cooperation 


In addition to the joint political positions there are military agreements 
between Syria and Iran which have not been made public in official statements 
or in the joint communique. AL-MAJALLAH has learned that "military 
cooperation" between Syria and Iran now includes providing Iran with Soviet- 
made Syrian weapons. Iranian sources report that these weapons have been 
stockpiled in Syria for a long time, and are not particularly modern. Syria 
wants to replace them with new weapons. Furthermore, security and intelligence 
experts brought by Iran from North Korea will be replaced by Syrian experts 
who have begun to arrive in Tehran. From now on Syria will be an assured 
source of weapons for Iran from the Eastern bloc countries, particularly 

the Soviet Union, East Germany and North Korea. [ran currently receives 
quantities of these weapons through Libya, and is supplied with Libyan 
weapons. It is common knowledge that Israel recently sold Iran Israeli-made 
weapons, among them surface-to-surface and air-to-air missiles. Iran has also 
obtained from Israel spare parts for the American-made weapons still in the 
possession of the Iranian army from the time of the shah. 


Economic and Oil Cooperation 


On the subject of economic and oil agreements the joint communique released 
at the end of the visit said that an agreement was reached whereby Iran 

would supply Syria with 8.8 million tons of oil annually to operate the Homs 
and Baniyas refineries. This quantity of oil will enable Syria to make up 
for the Iraqi oil that used to arrive through pipelines through Lebanon. 
Syria will begin importing Iranian oil next April. The agreement is for l 
year, subject to renewal for 10 years. Informed sources in Tehran have told 
AL-MAJALLAH that the agreement calls for Iran to supply Syria with oil at the 
world market price, as well as pistachios, carpets and minerals in exchange 
for foodstuffs, agricultural goods, phosphate and textiles. According to 
these sources, “the agreement will have important oil ramifications for 

Syria and will affect Syria's relations with the Arab countries, particularly 
with Iraq."’ The Iranian sources also said that an agreement was reached on 
restructuring Syrian debts to Iran, which amount to 150 million dollars.

COOPERATION AGREEMENT SIGNED--Tunisia and Saudi Arabia have signed an indus- 
trial and trade cooperation agreement, the OPEC news agency reported from 
TUNIS recently. Under the terms of the accord, Saudi Arabia will supply Tuni- 
sia with sulphur and other raw materials it needs for its industries, while a 
joint Saudi Arabian-Tunisian investment company will carry out feasibility 
studies on the production of chemicals in Saudi Arabia. The agreement, signed 
by Abdul Aziz al-Asram, Tunisian National Economy Minister, and Ghazi Abdul 
Rahman al-Gosaiby, Saudi Minister of Industry and Electricity, also calls for 
the secondment of Tunisian experts and technicians to Saudi Arabia. It recom- 
mends the creation of a services company to develop industrial and commercial 
exchanges between the two countries. In addition, cooperation between the 
Tunisian Electricity and Gas Company and the Saudi Public Electricity 
Authority is to be developed. [Text] [Paris AN-NAHAR ARAB REPORT & MEMO in
