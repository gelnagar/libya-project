AL-QADHDHAFI CALLS FOR MOBILIZATION AGAINST U.S. 
LD130012 Tripoli Voice of Arab Homeland in Arabic 2315 GMT 12 Nov 80 


[Text] Within the context of the leader's meetings with the student masses to clarify 
the picture before them and alert them to the dangers to which the Arab nation is 
being subjected at the hands of the imperialist, Zionist and Arab reactionary forces, 
the brother leader met this morning with students and pupils of institutions and 
schools in Tripoli and students of Al-Fatah University. The leader confirmed the need 
to quickly transform the institutions, colJ).eges and factories into military barracks 
and the need for the students to become rvgular soldiers. carrying military ranks and 
weapons, who will wake up to the sound c¢ the trumpet and sleep to its sound, in order 
to face the challenges facing the Arab nation at the hands of the imperialism, Zionism 
and reaction. The leader explained chat the transformation of the schools, institutions 
and colleges into military barracks will hasten the creation of an armed people as 
soon as possible, confirming tha’. this was the real popular mobilization of the masses 
to confront the aggression to which the Arab nation is being subjected these days and 
to face the siege which imperialism is trying to lay around Libya. The leader pointed 
out that the forces of U.S. and Zionist imperialism make daily advances in the Arab 
homeland, occupy parts of it and establish their aggressive military bases on its land, 
confirming that Egypt is completely occupied by U.S. military forces and that the 
Zionist enemy is now trying to annex the Golan so as to transform it into Zionist land, 
after having annexed Arab Jerusalem and making its capital. The leader confirmed that 
the Arab nation was facing a real direct military danger and facing a colonialist 
crusade led by America, directed against the Arab nation and that should this situation 
continue without confrontation the Arab existence would end, explaining that there 

was no other way before us than the comprehensive mobilization of all the fighting 
forces and the declaration of a jihad to confront the challenge of the colonialist 
crusade against the Arab and Islamic nation. The leader stressed the need to prepare 
a sufficient military force and that we should be ready to face the dangers when they 
come, because fighting might be imposed on us in any of the hostile fronts which are 
mobilized by America. He explained that we should not stop at replying to aggression; 
rather we should destroythe aggressive positions and prepare for an attack when the 
need arises. 


The leader explained that there were attempts to silence the voice of the free Libyan 
Arab people and prevent it from alerting the Arab nation to the colonialist danger 
closing in on it and that in various parts of the Arab world an operation of deceiving 
and lulling of the Arab person is being waged so as to make him unaware of the intrigues 
being hatched against his existence. The leader explained that with the creation of 

the armed people there would be no danger to freedom, while freedom would be in danger 
without the creation of the armed people and that should we fight we would be doing so 
in self-defense and in defense of our freedom.

PAPER ATTACKS ‘FIFTH COLUMN’ IN TRADING SECTOR 
LD101514 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 10 Nov 80 


[Excerpts] Under the heading "Women Lead the Fifth Column and It Is the Duty 
Revolutionary Committees To Check this Phenomenon," AZ-ZAHF AL~AKHDAR writes: 


V. 13 Nov 80 14 NORTH AFRICA 


When the idea of public markets emerged to lay the foundation of a new socialist principle 
through which the masses can obtain their needs without exploitation, the masses welcomed 
this accomplishment and within a few days, felt the wide gap betweenthe principle of un- 
restricted trading and the method of public markets. 


However, those who missed the boat and who were brought up and nurtured on social rancor 
and the love of domination and egoism preferred to stand in the way of the march of the 
masses and to plot against this socialist way. Thus they drew up an ugly plan which mani- 
fested itself in the form of long lines outside markets and stores during official working 
hours in search of supplies which dc not in fact represent any vital necessity. Thousands 
of letters poured in from many popular quarters asking in a manner beyond all imagination 
for thousands of Arab suits, bags of tea and sacks of semolina, as though the task of these 
quarters since the formation of revolutionary committees has become the marketing and 
distribution of goods. 


The matter might not have looked like a plot against the new socialist idea had it taken 

the form of an organized demand. But these quarters went beyond all limits in their demands 
while thousands of women stormed the markets and stores in a desperate bid to empty them and 
trade with their contents in the streets and lanes, bartering in everything from eggs to 
gold bullion. 


The sudden emergence of thousanis of traders in a society which is undergoing a socialist 
change is a matter which demands that the revolutionary committees make added efforts to 
put an end to it. The question is not one of the disappearance of commodities from the 
markets within a few hours because of excess demand for commodities which are sold cheaply 
by socialist stores and establishments, nor is it one of exploiting the chance given to 
the masses to compensate for the sufferings they had when the merchants were dominent. 

It is much more than that. To ensure that some popular quarters do not depart from their 
original tasks for which society set them up and do not turn into centers for marketing 
and distribution of goods, that we do not have to abort the attempts to lay down a new 
economic regime which can guarantee the liberation of man's needs as a basic step for the 
realization of complete freedom for the masses, that we can affirm the idea that he who 
uses his skill to amass greater wealth than needed for his requirements is actually en- 
croaching on a public right with his society's wealth, the revolutionary committees must 
act firmly to check this phenomenon and check also all those who are tempted to obstruct 
the course of the new socialist society. 


V. 13 Nov 80 ISRAEL 


SHAMIR SAYS REAGAN MAY CHANGE APPROACH TO ACCORDS 


TA101217 Tel Aviv HA'ARETZ in Hebrew 10 Nov 80 pp 1, 2 


[By political correspondent ‘Uzi Benziman] 


[Excerpts] President Ronald Reagan's administration will initially adhere to the Camp 
David accords; however, it may later change its approach according to developments. 
Foreign Minister Yitzhaq Shamir made this evaluation at yesterday's Cabinet meeting and 
also pointed to the difficulties Israel would face in the U.S. Senate. 


Based on analyses drawn by the Foreign Ministry, Shamir expressed the belief that the 
election reflected disappointment with President Carter and expressed a conservative 
mood which has been increasing in the United States during the last 10 years. According 
to Shamir, the election results indicate the creation of a new coalition in U.S. society 
which is attempting to find solutions for the problems troubling the United States. This 
new coalition is formed by neoconservatives, intellectuals and Christian organizations. 
The foreign minister recalled that for the first time since 1932 Jewish support for the 
Democratic Party dropped to a rate of less than 50 percent. 


Minister Shamir noted that Israel should now expect problems in the Senate because most 
U.S. Jewish organizations were active within the Democratic Party. Nevertheless, he 
noted that some Jewish leaders have also had certain contacts with the Republican Party. 


The foreign minister also analyzed the positions proclaimed by the future president in 
his public appearances: support for a U.S. presence in the Middle East; support for 
U.S. installations in Israel; recognition of Israel's sovereignty over all of Jerusalem; 
refusal to define the settlements as illegal; against a radical Palestinian state; his 
declaration that Israel was not responsible for the creation of the Arab refugees' 
problem; his declaration that the future of Judaea and Samaria will be determined 
through negotiations with Jordan without demanding an absolute Israeli withdrawal; his 
objection to recognition of the PLO so long as it does not change its positions and 

its charter; his support for making U.S. arms supplies to Egypt conditional on its 
willingness to implement the peace treaty with Israel. 


The foreign minister said that Ronald Reagan did not think that the oil price paid by 
the U.S. consumer should depend on the Israeli-Arab conflict; he aspires to abolish U.S. 
dependence on oil imports; he expressed his opposition to abandoning Taiwan; he de- 
plored the U.S. vote at the Security Council which was unpleasant for Israel. 


Mr Shamir added that, in his assumption, Reagan would adopt the belief that Israel was 
important to the United States from the strategic standpoint although he also mentioned 
that Reagan was surrounded by a second school of thought which claims that the fact that 
the Israeli-Arab conflict has not been solved makes it difficult for the United States 
to increase its influence over the countries in the region. 


Mr Reagan will want to restrain Soviet expansion, to ensure oil supplies to the West, 

to build a U.S. infrastructure for an independent fuel supply and to ensure Israel's 
security. Reagan may adopt a different approach than Carter for solving the U.S. 
hostage problem in Tehran and for strengthening the U.S. position as leader of the free. 
world. A change is also likely in the U.S. attitude toward the United Nations and the 
Third World. 


Shamir went on to say that Reagan's advisers and aides will become known in December and 
Israel will have to redeploy accordingly. The foreign minister also said that the 
Israeli Embassy in Washington has expressed its concern over the future attitude of the 
chairmen of the Senate Appropriations and Foreign Relations Committees toward Israel's 
requests and positions. Shamir said: "Our people should get organized and explain our 
positions to the new administration."

TZIPORI HOPES REAGAN WILL WRITE OFF ISRAEL'S DEBTS 


TA111239 Tel Aviv YEDI'OT AHARONOT in Hebrew 11 Nov 80 pp 1, 7 


[By Ya'ir ‘Amiqam] 


[Text] Deputy Defense Minister Mordekhay Tzipori has expressed the hope that, following 
his election as U.S. president, Ronald Reagan will agree to write off all of Israel's 
debts, or at least its arms procurement debts, which total billions of dollars. 


"The state of Israel's defense expenditures," Tzipori said, "has also contributed a great 
deal to the development of the sophisticated arms system of the Western world in general 
and of the United States in particular and Israel is today a strategic asset for the 
United States." 


In a talk with our reporter, Tzipori revealed that the Defense Ministry has drawn up a 
special document containing all the stands taken by the president-elect in the course 

of his entire political career, along with his declarations during the election campaign. 
"An analysis of the document," Tzipori said, "reveals that the common denominator be- 
tween Reagan's stands and Israel's security needs could well be very high. Reagan said 
Israel is a strategic asset and this is the first time anyone in the United States has 
formally made this kind of unequivocal declaration. His position regarding the terrorists 
also brings about more understanding between him and his administration on this issue." 


Tzipori added that he has learned that Reagan intends to invest large sums in strategic 
assets which are vital to the United States and, of course, Israel.

centers in Haifa and in Beersheba, with the declared purpose of tightening the relations

EGYPTIAN FOREIGN MINISTER INTERVIEWED ON U.S. VISIT 

TA111312 Tel Aviv MA'ARIV in Hebrew 11 Nov 80 pp 1, 15 

[By Cairo correspondent ‘Oded Granot] 

[Text] Egypt's deputy prime minister and foreign minister, Kamal Hasan ‘Ali, is to go 
to the United States in order to tighten relations with "Reagan's people," "to bid fare- 


well" to the Carter administration and to submit a “working paper" to the staff of the 
new administration about the autonomy discussions to date. 


In a special interview with this reporter, Gen Hasan ‘Ali stated yesterday that he will 
visit Washington next month in order to hold discussions with members of the new admin- 
istration on, among other subjects, bilateral relations and on the scope of military 
economic aid which, he believes, will increase considerably. 


He revealed that he intends, among other things, to meet with several of the figures who 
are likely to occupy a central place in the Reagan administration, including Dr Henry 
Kissinger and Gen Alexander Haig, as well as with former President Gerald Ford. 


In the course of the interview he revealed that he will submit to the new U.S. admin- 
istration a “working paper," which will contain a summation of the course of the autonomy 
negotiations during the year and a half to date, and suggestions for continuing the 
negotiations on this subject. } 


He said that Egypt continues to favor the/idea of a tripartite summit meeting, though a 
final decision should be made only after’a meeting is held between President Carter and 
President-elect Reagan. 


Referring to the maneuvers scheduled to begin next week between the Egyptian and U.S. 
armies, he revealed that the Americans have already completed the general planning for 
the preparation of the Ras Banas air and naval base, which is situated on the Red Sea, 
for the use of the U.S. Army in the time of need. 


Involved is a plan to deepen the port and add runways to the air base which, he says, 
will become operational "immediately after the U.S. Congress approves the money budgeted 
fer it.” 


Gen Hasan ‘Ali agrees with the assessment that several months will be required before 
the new president has the time to examine U.S. policy in the Middle East, but he is 
convinced that no change will take place in the U.S. concept regarding a resolution of 
the Middle East issue, as well as regarding bilateral relations with Egypt which, as 
noted, could well be broadened even further during the Reagan administration. 


"It is in the interest of Egypt, which is surrounded now by Soviet influence, and it is 
in the interest of the United States that it should have a country with a similar 
strategy (of combating Soviet influence) which can play a military role in the region," 
he said. 


V. 13 Nov 80 N 4 ISRAEL 


With respect to the opening of the land border between Egypt and Israel for the transit 
of goods, he estimated that 3 months would be needed until the Egyptians can repave 
several of the "bottlenecks" on the road to Al-Qantarah. He confirmed that in line with 
the agreement attained between President as-Sadat and President Navon, during his 

visit to Egypt, foreign tourism also would continue from Israel to St Catherine's. 


General ‘Ali rejects the supposition that the visit to Egypt by the [Israeli] Labor 
Party delegation was intended, among other purposes, to enable Egypt to ready itself 

for a possible change of government in Israel next year. He said: "We do not see it 
like that. We are interested in getting to know 4ll the political viewpoints in Israel, 
not just one viewpoint, and this will also be the goal of the delegation of Egypt's 
ruling party which will soon be visiting Israel." 


Concerning the "Jordanian option," he said: "We cannot now talk about King Husayn's 
joining [the negotiations], not until we finish setting up the self-governing authority. 
Then the Palestinians will decide whether they want to choose the Jordanian option." 


The Egyptian foreign minister holds that Israel is being too hasty in its striving to 
accelerate normalization: "Everything must be implemented gradually; thus, for example, 
we did not begin with telephone cables for 1,000 outlets, but with 12, then 24, then 

36, and so on. The same applies to the flights. W> began with two on each side, and now 
El Al has four flights [per week] and we shall also request this. Trade has also grown 
significantly." 


Mr ‘Ali stresses--in the spirit of what he told the Egyptian press yesterday--that the 
Jerusalem issue "must come up for negotiations," and that the new U.S. administration 
must be a partner in the resolution of this problem, in the spirit of President as-Sadat's 
proposal to establish a "joint roof municipality" [‘iriyat gag meshutefet]. He noted that 
the visit to Jerusalem of the minister of state for foreign affairs, Butrus Ghali, was 
canceled because of the Jerusalem law and said the visit would not take place if Foreign 
Minister Shamir insists that the meeting between him and Ghali take place in Jerusalem. 

At the same time, he added that Ghali is ready to go to Israel and meet with the foreign 
ministers and other figures in any city but Jerusalem. 


POLL SHOWS MAJORITY SUPPORTS GOLAN HEIGHTS ANNEXATION 


TA121137 Tel Aviv HA'ARETZ in Hebrew 12 Nov 80 p 1 


[Text ] A majority of 68 percent supports the law for the annexation of the Golan Heights, 
with 16.3 precent opposed, according to a Pori Institute countrywide public opinion poll 
commissioned by HA'ARETZ. To the question, "Are you for or against the law for the annex- 
ation of the Golan Heights, 68 percent replied "for" and 16.3 replied "against." A 

further 3.7 percent said it depends on "timing," or "not now," or "to annex just part 

of the heights," "to annex it but without publicity," or if the annexation “will not prove 


an obstacle to peace." The remaining 12 percent had no opinion either way on this subject. 


An analysis of the results shows that among those opposing the annexation law there are 
more persons with a high educational level (25.8 percent), persons in the academic or 
administrative professions (25.9 percent), high income earners (23.7 percent), persons who 
have resided in the country since prestate days (26.5 percent) and persons of European 

or American origin (19.8 percent). 


The poll covered a representative sample of 1,200 men and women who were interviewed 
personally in their homes throughout the country.
