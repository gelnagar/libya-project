Summary (AI): A 1980 Los Angeles Times clipping reports that Billy Carter told federal investigators that President Carter gave him State Department cables about his 1978 Libya trip, and that Billy had deposited a $20,000 Libyan check in Columbus, Georgia. The cables were low-classification reports from the U.S. chargé d'affaires in Tripoli.
• Cables were classified only as low-secrecy State Department reports from the U.S. chargé d'affaires in Tripoli.
• A $220,000 payment from the Libyan government was described by the Justice Department as compensation for lobbying services; Billy Carter called it a loan.
• Billy Carter was interviewed January 16 in Americus, Georgia.
• The Justice Department said a $40,000 reimbursement was claimed for expenses; the Libyan government had paid for a delegation he headed.
• Billy Carter initially said he had not received money from the Libyans for the American School in Tripoli for a Koran purchase, then the matter was disputed.
• 1978: Billy Carter's controversial trip to Libya; U.S. chargé d'affaires in Tripoli sent State Department cables reporting on it
• 1979-12-31: Billy Carter deposited a $20,000 check from the Libyan government in his joint account at Columbus Bank & Trust Co.
