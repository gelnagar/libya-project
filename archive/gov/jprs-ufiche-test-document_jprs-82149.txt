WESTERN DESERT 


The Western Desert area covers about 270,200 
square miles or more than two-thirds of the whole area 
of Egypt. Until 1977, exploration activities in this area 
were restricted to the northern one-third, north of lati- 
tude 28°N, where Alamein, Yidma, Razzak, Umbarka 
and Abu Gharadig oilfields were discovered in the late 
sixties and early seventies. The Abu Gharadig field 
produces gas condensate only. Oil and gas production 
in the Western Desert is from reservoirs of early and lat 
Cretaceous age. In the last few years geological am 
geophysical activities have been extended to cover most 
of the southern part of the area and the first explora- 
tory well located south of latitude 28°N was spudded 
early in 1979. — 

The stratigraphic column in the northern Western 
Desert is composed of thick carbonate and clastic inter- 
calations and includes most of the sedimzntary suc- 
cession from Recent to Pre-Cambrian basement. Total 
thickness generally increases progressively from about 
600 feet in the south to more than 25,000 feet estimated 
along the northern coastal area. Many depocenters of 
various ages can be seen from data from 150 explora- 
tory wells drilled in the area north of latitude 28°N. A 
Palepzoic depocenter in the far west parallels the Libyan 
border, and early Cretaceous Aptian embayments have 
northerly trends. The most well-developed and signifi- 
cant depocenter is the late Cretaceous Abu Gharadig 
basin (Map 3).
