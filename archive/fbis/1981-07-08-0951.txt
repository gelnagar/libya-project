LIBYA

ASH-SHAHATI CITED ON OAU SUMMIT, U.S. TIES 


NCO72106 Paris AFP in English 2051 GMT 7 Jul 81 


[Text] Paris, 7 Jul (AFP)--The Organisation of African Unity (OAU) showed "support for 
Libyan activities in Africa" with the resolutions passed at its recent sumuit in Nairobi, 
a Libyan official said here today. 


Libya's African policy is aimed at unifying the continent and liberating it from “racist 
regimes like South Africa, and Zionist and colonial influence," added Ahmed ash-Shahati, 
Libyan leader Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi's special envoy to the United States. Libya also 
wanted to free Africa economically from the influen 2 of multinationals and from "cultural 
colonialism," Mr ash-Shahati said. 


He was passing through Paris after a trip to Washington during which he renewed contact 
with U.S. Government officials after the expulsion of all Libyan diplomats in May. 


"We are also working towards a peaceful and honourable settlement of the Saharan people's 
problem with Morocco. We consider that conditions are favourable at the moment for a 
beginning of direct negotiations between the two sides," Mr ash-Shahati added. 


Libya, which looked like being heavily criticised at the OAU conference over the presence 
of its troops in Chad, escaped lightly and is hosting next year's conference. 


Mr ash-Shahati also said that in its efforts to reunite Arabs Libya had reestablished 
diplomatic links with Morocco and Iraq, and was on the way to doing the same with Saudi 
Arabia. "The only beneficiaries of Arab splits are the United States and Israel," he 
added. 


Mr ash-Shahati said Tripoli would like to talk with the U.S. "on an equal footing" but 
rejected any American interference in its internal affairs. 


During his stay in the U.S. he had met officials of the State Department and members of 
the House of Representatives and Senate, Mr ash-Shahati said. "TI told them that Libya was 
a non-aligned country, independent, strategically linked with the Palestinian cause, 

and that it intended to have balanced relations with all states and all peoples in the 
world." 


The Libyan added, "We hope that Washington will reconsider the situation and change its 
policy towards Libya so that it can maintain its interests in Libva and the Arab world in 
general." If the U.S. persists in attacking Libya, Tripoli will reexamine its position with 
regard to Washington, Mr ash-Shahati warned. 


PAPER ATTACKS U.S. CAMPAIGN AGAINST TERRORISM 


PM071420 London AL-ZAHF AL-AKHDAR in English 26 Jun 81 p 2 
{Untitled editorial] 


[Excerpts] It is becoming increasingly obvious that the Americans are determined to force 
us to view events and ideas through the jaundiced prisms of their world perspective. This 
perspective sees the world divided into two parts: America and Russia with Europe seen 

as marginal to this dichotomy and the rest of the world as merely appendages of either 
side. With such a division, it is easy to see how moral and ethical values become ascribed 
to each side. Thus America would like to be viewed as the representative of good struggl- 
ing against the evil influences of Russia and her allies.

The principal exponent of this american view is Alexander Haig, the U.S. secretary of 

state. Little does he realise that such a view is not grounded on a knowledge of U.S. 
history or an understanding of present American policies in the world. Or perhaps, he 
does but refuses to acknowledge the truth. 


Not since the end of the Second World War has the USA stood on the side of a just cause. 
This is because America has not sought to make friends but to enslave the world. The 
recent history of the nation includes frequent attempts to decimate native populations, 
in an effort to institute political and economic domination. 


It is the only power in the world to support the barbaric Zionist aggression against the 
Palestinian and Lebanese peoples. Under Ronald Reagan, the U.S. nation has adopted a 
Janus-like approach to other nations. Soon after the Zionist bombing Iraqi territory, 
the Americans suspended sales of four F-16 fighters. This was presented as a snub to 

the Zionists for their unwarranted attack but after the furor had died down the Americans 
revealed their true colours. In his first press conference after being shot, President 
Reagan justified the Zionist action by citing "reasons for concern" by the Zionist entity 
and the notion that the Zionist aggressors "might have sincerely believed was a defensive 


move." ’ 


America is the only country which defines people's liberation movements in a manner which 
contraticts historical logic and is incompatible with their definition by other nations. 
This insolence has prompted Washington to describe the struggle of the Palestinians as a 
terror campaign. The same logic is used towards the Namibian and Saharan peoples' struggle 
to achieve independence and freedom. 


It is clear that the politicians and planners in Washington have failed to develop even an 
inkling of the reasons why people are prepared to die in defence of their soil, freedom and 
way of life. Little do they realise that self-sacrifice is a supreme act towards a noble 
goal. Ever since people's awareness started to take shape and crystallise in the world, 
man's task has been to wrest freedom from those who control destiny. The struggle for 
freedom all over the world is rooted in this basic desire. 


Thus when the U.S. administration accuses Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi of being the spiritual 
leader of terrorism, it is merely trying to find a convenient scapegoat to divert world 
attention away from its own foul deeds. Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi is, in fact, the spiritual 
leader of the people's struggle to find and defend their final emancipation. Terrorism 
according to the thoughts of Haig is the people's struggle and so anyone engaged in de- 
veloping that struggle is aiding and abetting terrorism. 


The USA does not see the establishment of its military bases in the Arab homeland as an 
act of terrorism nor does it think that Zionist aggression against the Palestinian and 
Lebanese people is a terrorist act. This is not due to shortcomings on the part of those 
struggling against domination and aggression, but to the fallacies and erroneous premises 
on which American policies are based. 


It should be evident to all peace-loving peoples that it is the USA and not Mu‘ ammar 
al-Qadhdhafi that is striving to put out the flame of freedom. The Americans apparently 
have not learnt their lessons from the debacles in Vietnam, Iran or Nicaragua. Why else 
would they want to repeat the same action in Guatemala, El Salvador and Grenada? Why 
else would they want to dominate Egypt, Sudan and Saudi Arabia? 


Defeat in those areas are inevitable if the lessons of America's bloody history are not 
learnt.
