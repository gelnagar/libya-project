Summary (AI): A New York Times report from Rome (January 1974) says a satirical column in La Stampa about Qaddafi angered him and strained Libya-Italy relations, with Libya reportedly threatening to break diplomatic ties unless an apology came. The story also covers the Arab Boycott Committee's demand that Fiat dismiss the authors and the managing editor. Nothing here is set in Tripoli itself.
• La Stampa column headlined 'It Seems That' was written by Carlo Fruttero and Franco Lucentini
• The Beirut Arab Boycott Committee reportedly warned Fiat would be blacklisted in the Arab world unless it dismissed the authors and managing editor Arrigo Levi
• Libya's press counselor in Rome protested the article as a defamatory attack
• Colonel Qaddafi ordered Italian settlers to leave after seizing power in 1969
• Libya was an Italian colony from 1911 to the end of World War II
• 1974-01: Libya reportedly threatened to break off diplomatic relations with Italy unless an apology was offered over La Stampa humor article about Qaddafi
