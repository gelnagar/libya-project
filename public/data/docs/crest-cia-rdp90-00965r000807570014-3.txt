Summary (AI): A CIA memo (22 April 1986) and a Washington Post article by Bob Woodward describe U.S. intercepts of Libyan People's Bureau messages from East Berlin linking Tripoli to the 5 April 1986 La Belle discotheque bombing in West Berlin, which killed one American and injured over 200. The memo concerns leak accusations against the CIA over the intercept material.
• Libya fired missiles at U.S. carrier-based planes over the Gulf of Sidra the day before March 25 cable; US fired at a Libyan missile site and sank at least two patrol boats.
• The March 25 cable was sent to eight People's Bureaus, including East Berlin, Paris, Rome, Madrid and four other European capitals, but not London, which had closed its bureau after Britain broke relations in 1984.
• NSA officials were alarmed by public revelations and said they were severely hampering the ability to intercept Libyan cables.
• Gen. Rogers said the US was about 15 minutes too late to warn GIs to vacate La Belle.
• 1986-04-04: US intercepted a message from the Libyan People's Bureau in East Berlin to Tripoli saying Libyan authorities 'will be very happy when you see the headlines tomorrow.'
• 1986-04-05: Bomb exploded at 1:49 a.m. Berlin time, killing one American and injuring over 200; a second intercept from East Berlin to Tripoli reporting an operation 'happening now' was sent within 10 minutes. (1 killed)
• 1986-03-25: Libyan cable to eight People's Bureaus, including East Berlin, directed readiness for operations and attacks against American targets, intercepted by NSA.
