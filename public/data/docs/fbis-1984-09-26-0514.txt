LIBYA

Restored Jordan-Egypt Ties Viewed as Betrayal 
LD260826 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 0700 GMT 26 Sep 84 


[Text] Jordan, 26 Sep (JANA) — The ruling regime in Jordan 
decided to restore diplomatic relations with Egypt. This was 
announced today by the Jordanian Foreign Ministry. This deci- 
sion adopted by the Jordanian regime is a betrayal of the Arab 
nation and confirms the capitulatory course being implemented 
by the ruling regime in Jordan, because it is also considered a 
departure from Arab unanimity of boycotting the Egyptian 
regime that recognized the Zionist enemy and exchanged ambas- 
sadors with it. 


Al-Qadhdhafi Swears in African Arab Union Secretary 
LD252017 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1815 GMT 25 Sep 84 


[Text] This evening the brother leader of the revolution received 
Brother Abdelouahed Radi, secretary general of the African 
Arab Union, together with a Moroccan delegation with includes 
Brothers Ahmed Reda Guedira, private adviser of King Hassan 
Il; Mustapha Belarbi Alaoui, minister of justice; Driss Basri, 
minister of the interior; Shaykh Makki Naseri, head of the 
Scientific Council for the city of Rabat and Sala and member of 
the Regency Council. Brother Abdelouahed Radi was sworn in 
by the brother leader during the meeting. 


Tripoli Cites BBC Analysis of Libyan-Chadian Relations 
NC250630 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 0500 GMT 
25 Sep 84 


{Text} In a political analysis of the nature of relations between 
the Arab Libyan and Chadian peoples and the historical back- 
ground to these firm relations, the BBC’s Arabic service has said 
that the Jamahiriyah has a basic justification [mubarrirat] for 
being interested in what is taking place in Chad and that it is only 
right to say that the Jamahiriyah’s interest in Chad is the same 
as that of any two neighborly countries’ interest in each other’s 
affairs. 


It said that Libyan-Chadian relations are historically deep- 
rooted. There are racial links between the inhabitants of the 
Jamahiriyah and the Chadians in addition to religious, tribal, 
familial, and kinship relations. The trade relations between them 
are centuries old. This is also true of [word indistinct], and 
linguistic and other relations that have been going on for hun- 
dreds of years. Hence, it is not surprising, and it is very natural, 
for the Libyans to be interested in events taking place in Chad 
and for the Chadians to be interested in events taking place in 
the Jamahiriyah.
