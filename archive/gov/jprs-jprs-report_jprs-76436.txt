SABAH REVIEWS RESULTS OF ISLAMIC FOREIGN MINISTERS’ CONFERENCE 
Amman AL-RA'Y in Arabic 14 Jul 80 p 18 


[interview with Kuwait's Foreign Minister, Shaykh Sabah al~Ahmad, by 
Ahmad Salamah, in Amman; date not given)
[…]











In response to a question about selling oil to non-oil producing Arab 
nations at special prices differing from world oil prices, the Kuwaiti 
Foreign Minister said: "We are not even considering selling oil at dif- 
ferent prices to non-oil producing Arab nations, and we will not discuss 
it, because the selling price of oil is set as a world-wide decision 
through OPEC, and it is not an Arab decision." 


Shaykh Sabah continued: “However, we are in favor of the principle of 
offering assistance to the Arab nations which do not possess oil, but 
through methods other than lowering the price. There are various methods 
and many channels through which to compensate for the rising international 
price of oil. We have this trend in mind, and this subject will be dis- 
cussed at the next summit conference, which will be held this year in 
Amman." 


His Highness Shaykh Sabah denounced foreign intervention in the Gulf 
region, saying: "We denounce the offer of the concessions granted to 
the American forces by any party in the Gulf." He said that in the con- 
ference the foreign minister of Oman had announced that there are no 
concessions to the American forces in his country's laws. 


His Highness Shaykh Sabah asked the Arab community to be self-reliant in 
its struggle with Israel. He said that all of the African nations have 
promised not to renew their relations with Israel.

/Article by Sulayman al-Farzali: ‘Taking an Important Step, American Com 
panies Return to Libya, and Syria Differentiates between Oil and Politice"/ 


/Text/ One characteristic of American oil companies is that they do not 
quickly wash their bands of any area where political winds change, even 
after they leave it. It is not important to them if a regime changes from 
capitalist to socialist or even to communist; what is important, rather, 
is that it remain in the market. Whoever remains in the market buys and 
sells, and that is the secret. 


This policy was asserted and drawn up by Armand Hammer, president of 
Occidental Petroleum Company, who had the distinction of meking the first 
great oil discovery in Libya in the early sixties and became internation- 
ally famous after that. Mr. Hammer is an American Jew who is famous for 
his liberalism toward the Scviet Union; his relations with the Soviets 
have remained warm and firm since the days of Lenin. Hammer received oil 
concessions in the Soviet Union from Lenin personally in 1919. Stalin 
lost no time in nationalizing these some years later; however, Hammer did 
not despair and when the opportunity arose again in Nixon's era, at the 
beginning of detente, he signed broad-scale economic agreements with 
Brezhnev. Indeed, he presented valuable gifts of art to Soviet museums, 
including an original painting by the artist Goya worth more than $1 mil- 
lion, in consideration of the fact that he is one of the greatest enthus 
iasts for collecting antique treasures in the world. The painting was 
placed in the Hermitage Museum in Leningrad. 


Nationalization does not concern American oil companies either, as long 
as they have an entree into purchases or participation. It is well known 
that Mexico was the first non-Communist country in the world to national- 
ize oil; that occurred in 1936. Nonetheless, American oil interests found 
a way into Mexico after a long absence.

LIBYANS AT CROATIA UNIVERSITY--A group of 83 students from the Social- 
ist People's Libyan Arab Jamahirya who finished secondary school in Zagreb, 
will enroll this autumn at the universities of Zagreb, Rijeka and Split, 
in the Republic of Croatia. The republican institute for Scientific- 
Technical Cooperation has been the organizer of the education of students 
and personnel from nonaligned and under developed countries for a long time. 
The cooperation with the Libyan Jamahiriya began in 1978 with the training 
primarily of medical personnel. In 1979 institute started the training of 
more than 200 Libyan secondary school and university students for the re- 
quirements of the country's maritime economy and fishing industry. At the 
moment the institute is organizing the schooling of over 350 students 

from the Libyan Jamahiriya. It has organized the education of personnel 
from Iraq and Nigeria, and is also considering the possibilities for the 
secondary-school and university-level education of personnel from Algeria. 
At present, a total of about 12,000 young people from all parts of the 
world, mostly from nonaligned countries, are attending secondary schools 
and universities throughout Yugoslavia. [Text] [Valletta JAMAHIRIYA MAIL 
in English 9 Aug 80 p 7]

LRAQL PRESIDENT INVITED TO VISIT MALTA 
Valletta JAMAHIRIYA MAIL in English 9 Aug 80 p 11 


[Text] The Malta Government has invited the President of Iraq Saddam Hus- 
sein to make an official visit to Malta. The invitation is to be conveyed 
to Baghdad by the Iraqi high-powered delegation which yesterday concluded 
its talks with representatives of the Maltese Health Ministry. 


The invitation to Saddam Hussein has been made on behalf of the Malta go- 
vernment by Health Minister Dr V. Moran who visited Baghdad last month. 


The Iraqi delegation follows Dr Moran's visit. It came here to investigate 
how Iraq could help develop its national health service and its system of 
medical education. 


The minutes of the talks that the delegation had in Malta during the 
past six days were signed this morning in the presence of Dr Moran. 


In a short address, Dr Moran said the Malta Government extends to Iraq all 
its medical facilities, just as it had done to friendly European and Arab 
nations. 


Dr Moran said that the Iraqi Government would now investigate how it could 
help Malta both technically and financially. He said that after its visit 
to Malta, the Iraqi delegation would now be in a position to advise its 
Gavernment how to finalise a reciprocal health cooperation. 


This agreement is expected to be signed later this year in Malta during a 
projected visit by Health Minister Riyad Hussien. 


The head of the Iraq delegation, Dr Kamal Mustafa, who is the chairman of 
the Baghdad Medical Centre, replied that this delegation appreciated the 
invitation that had been extended to the Iraqi President. He praised exist- 
ing medical services in Malta and hoped that the projected Maltese-Iraqi 
agreement would become effective as soon as possible. 











The Maltese Minister also thanked the delegation on its suggestion on ways 
and means to consolidate bilateral cooperation between Malta and Iraq. 


The minutes of the talks were signed by Dr Mustafa and chief Government, 
Medical Officer Dr Alfred Grech.
