LIBYA

NOTE TO UN SECURITY COUNCIL PROTESTS U.S. POLICIES 


LD042032 Tripoli Voice of Arab Homeland in Arabic 1713 GMT 4 Aug 81 


[Text] The Popular Committee of the People's Bureau for Foreign Liaison has sent a note 
to the Security Council concerning the hostile attitudes of the United States toward the 
Great 1 September Revolution and its leadership and concerning blatant threats against 
the Libyan Arab people and treacherous attempts to strike at their achievements. The 
Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah's delegate to the United Nations delivered 


the text of the note to the president of Security Council. Here is the text of the note: 


Mr President of the Security Council: Greetings. I have the honor to inform you of the 
following: 


The U.S. Government has been escalating its campaign against the Socialist People's 
Libyan Arab amahiriyah at all levels with the aim of preparing the atmosphere to embark 
directly or ia its agents upon a hostile action against the Jamahiriyah. This escala- 
tion is manifested in U.S. moves that clearly predicate premeditated, hostile intentions, 
specifically as follows: 


1. The announcement of the readiness of the U.S. administration to give military aid to 
African states which the U.S. administration claims are threatened by the Jamahiriyan, 
as stated by Crocker, the U.S. assistant secretary of state for African affairs, before 
the Senate Foreign Relations Committee. 


2. The announcement by the U.S. administration that it will raise the value of its 
so-called military aid to the states neighboring the Jamahiriyah, particularly the 
Egyptian and Sudanese regimes. 


3. The escalation of a U.S. propaganda campaign against the Jamahiriyah and its revolu- 
tionary leadership for the purpose of preparing U.S. and world opinion to accept any 
aggression by the United States or any of its agents in the area against the Jamahiriyah 
and its revolutionary leadership. 

4. The spread of reports about a plan prepared by the U.S. administration for the pur- 
pose of committing aggression against the Jamahiriyah. These plans reached the stage of 
the announcement of a plot to carry out the physical liquidation of the leader of the 
Great 1 September Revolution. This was made clear in a report by the U.S. Congress’ 
Intelligence Oversight Committee, which said that plans were prepared by the U.S. CIA 

to carry out this physical liquidation, as well as to carry out subversive acts against 
civilian targets in the Jamahiriyah and commit acts of destruction and the murder of 
innocent people. 


5. The increase of military supplies and cooperation in the area surrounding the 
Jamahirivah.

This began with moves by the U.S. Sixth Fleet near the Libyan shores and ended with the 
exchange of expertise and visits by U.S. war and political officials to hireling regimes 
in the area to prepare plans aimed at the Jamahiriyah and its people, as well as plans to 
station rapid deployment forces in the area to carry out U.S. plans. The joint Egyptian- 
U.S. maneuvers which took place in 1961 near the Jamahiriyah borders are but one example 
of threats to use force against the Jamahirivah. 


6. On the basis of what has been cited above, the acts of murder, subversion and direct 
interference and the overthrow of regimes in the Third World countries bv successive 

U.S. administrations, the Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah draws attention 

to the serious and deteriorating situation caused by the policy and measures taken by 

the U.S. administration in the area, measures which threaten international peace and 
security. The Socialist People’s Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah considers this policy and these 
measures a threat to use force and an attempt to interfere in the domestic affairs of the 
Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah and as international terror practiced by a 
state who is a member of the United Nations and a permanent member of the Security Council 
against another member state. In so doing it defies international laws and practice, 

and violates the principles and aims of the UN Charter and resolutions and, also, the 
fundamental principles of international ethics and behavior.
