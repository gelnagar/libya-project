Summary (AI): A 1981 New York Times Magazine article by Seymour Hersh describes former CIA men Edwin Wilson and Frank Terpil selling expertise and weapons-related services to Qaddafi's Libya, including a planned assassination-weapons lab near Tripoli and a timer-device order. It covers Libyan terrorism support and a reported $1 million plot against a Libyan defector.
• Libya was reported to have a 60,000-man army with more than 5,000 Warsaw Pact advisers.
• Wilson established a trading company in Tripoli known as Meprico to supply Qaddafi's army.
• Wilson and Terpil reportedly bought a US Army vehicle with night-surveillance equipment for $60,000 and sold it to Libya for $990,000.
• Qaddafi reportedly ordered hundreds of thousands of timers, with a final contract of $35 million for 500,000 timers, according to the article.
• 1976-07: Demonstration in Libya of timing devices by John Henry Harper; six of ten timers had worked, yet Libya still ordered 100,000, later increased to 300,000 timers.
• 1981-05: Administration ordered the Libyan Government to shut its offices in Washington as part of anti-terrorism campaign.
