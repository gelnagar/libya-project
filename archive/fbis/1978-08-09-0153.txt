[Text] The Chinese now believe that there are three worlds: the capitalist bloc, the 
socialist bloc,and the Third World. They advocate the three as though each could be 
substituted for the others, or stand in opposition to them. There may be those who listen 
to and respond to such a theory, in one way or another; however, there is disagreement over 
the manner of confrontation. Are the three worlds totally at loggerheads or are there 
opportunities for coming together, to a great or lesser degree? The recent Chinese- 
Albanian dispute was over this point and some of the Arab states might disagree with 

China over this point. However, the Arab states need a world power, or world powers, 
sufficiently advanced in the scientific, technological and industrial fields to supply them 
with the equipment, factories and technical know-how, particularly since some or all of the 
Arab states are in the process of developing and therefore require a good deal of positive 
international cooperation. 


Thus, the majority of developing countries, the Arab states in their forefront, have found 
in the socialist bloc a friendly ally, in spite of ideological or social differences. 

Such differences are in the nature of the prevailing ideologies on either side. Thus, the 
Soviet Union became amain source of arms for numerous developing Third World countries. 
Other socialist states took part in development projects in developing countries, espec- 
ilally in providing the expertise of their companies in construction and research. This 
has been particularly true of the Jamahiriyah. Such cooperation was reinforced by the 
recent visit of the leader of the Glorious 1 September Revolution to countries of the 
socialist bloc. 


The countries of the Third World cannot pretend that the socialist bloc is a remote or 
hostile party. Far from it. They act on the assumption that the socialist bloc is a 
friendly ally in the fight against two deadly enemies underdevelopment on the one hand, 
and imperialism, racialism and Zionism on the other. Inevitably, there have been frequent 
meetings within regional and international gatherings between Africans, Asians and Latin 
Americans and between members of the socialist bloc. Among themselves, these two blocs 
discussed contentious problems in a reasonably harmonious manner, but it is always a 
manner very different from what they get from the United States and Western Europe.

ignored, and must be reckoned with. The mistake the Arabs have made vis-a-vis China is 
that they have relied on the fact that China would be consistently on their side, [t 

is true that China has a.ways supported the Arab cause; but there is no such thing 
immutable situation, Cir:umstances change and necessitate a change of attitude ace 
ingly. New facts have obliged China to reemerge from its isolation, reappear on the w 
stage, and to become particularly active in the Third World and in Europe. If in its 
activities China is impelled by its own circumstances, then it is up to us Arabs to go to 
China, talk to China and listen to China. We must reassure it that our views are close to 
its views, bring its views closer to ours; we must bring China to a closer understanding 
of our problems and national needs. 


The Arabs may accept or reject the new Chinese orientation, but they do not disagree with 
China one bit, nor can China disagree with them, for national disagreements and economic, 
political or cultural rivalries hardly exist between the Arabs and the Chinese people. 
China is an energetic world power with a great future: It follows that forward-looking 
Arab thinking is the kind which ‘:iows how to build bridges between itself and up-and- 
coming powers, especially since the Arabs have vested interest in an international 

system based on cooperation and mutual development. 


Thus, we believe that the Jamahiriyah, whose diplomatic activities span the entire world, 
is not ignoring China. It has sent one of its prominent revolutionary leaders to China. 
We believe that 'Abd as-Salam Jallud's visit to Peking has a specific importance; special 
circumstances have made it essential and its timing is connected with special circum-
