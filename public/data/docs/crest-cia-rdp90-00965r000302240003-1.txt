Summary (AI): A New York Times article (27 August 1986) reports that US officials sought to play down rumors of new Libyan terrorism, saying they had no hard evidence of planned attacks while Ambassador Vernon Walters prepared a European trip to widen sanctions against Qaddafi's government. It is foreign-policy background with no specific Tripoli sites.
• US officials said they had 'indications' of planned new Libyan terrorist activity aimed at Americans but no 'smoking gun'.
• A Wall Street Journal report on Monday (25 Aug) about 'new signs' of Libyan activity was described by officials as unauthorized and premature.
• Egyptian-American military maneuvers were conducted along the Mediterranean coast; Navy sources called them routine and said they had been planned the previous April.
• Walters had flown secretly to Europe before the April 1986 US air attack to gain British permission for American bombers based in Britain to strike Libya.
• 1986-04-05: Terrorist bombing of a West Berlin discotheque killed 3 people and wounded 230; the US cited it when it raided Libya on 15 April. (3 killed)
• 1986-08-03: Mortar attack against a British air base in Cyprus; US officials could not confirm Libyan involvement.
