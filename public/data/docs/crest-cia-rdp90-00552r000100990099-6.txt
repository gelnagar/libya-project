Summary (AI): A Jack Anderson column (Washington Post, 26 October 1979) describes Billy Carter's 1978 trip to Libya with a Georgia delegation, the White House's concern about it, and State Department efforts to monitor and restrain him. Libya's hospitality and the Vesco transport-plane bribery allegations are also covered. Background on U.S.-Libyan relations, with no Tripoli sites named beyond the city itself.
• The Libyans had granted arms and asylum to some of the world's most notorious terrorists, according to the column.
• Eight transport planes bought by Libya from the US had been frozen by Washington; Billy Carter reportedly raised the issue privately with the Libyans.
• The Justice Department was investigating allegations that Vesco plotted to pass out $30 million in bribes (OCR reads 330 million) to Carter associates for transport planes for Libya.
• Eagleton's cable said there had been no negative fallout from the visit and that the local scene would rate it a very positive event.
• 1978: Billy Carter and a Georgia delegation of eight politicians and businessmen visited Libya; Libyans paid the costs, estimated at over $50,000 (OCR reads 350,000), and handed out gifts.
