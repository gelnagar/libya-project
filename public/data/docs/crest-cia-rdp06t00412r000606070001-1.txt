Summary (AI): This CIA memo explains why Libya cut its diplomatic staff in Western Europe in 1983-1986, linking it to economic strain and the 1986 US airstrike. It also says the cuts have not reduced the terrorist threat, since Libya may be moving operatives under other cover.
• Libya decided in late 1983 to reduce its official overseas presence, mainly to save foreign exchange for the $7 billion Great Man-Made River project.
• Libyan diplomatic and staff personnel in West European countries fell from over 450 to about 150 after the April 1986 strike.
• Libya's foreign exchange earnings were $11.5 billion in 1985, against a high of $22 billion in 1981; projected to fall to about $5-6 billion in 1986.
• The Foreign Affairs Secretariat ran Libya's People's Bureaus, which had largely replaced embassies since 1979.
• Libyan students are large in number in the UK, West Germany, Spain, Greece and Italy, and may be used as operational cover.
• 1986-04: US airstrike on Libya, after which West European governments expelled Libyan officials and Libya recalled personnel worldwide in May 1986
• 1984: Shooting from the Libyan People's Bureau in London killed a British policewoman and led to a break in relations (1 killed)
