TEXT OF AL-—QADHDHAFI LETTER TO PRESIDENT REAGAN 


LD270948 Tripoli JANA in English 0849 GMT 27 Jan 81 


[Text] Tripoli, 27 Jan [JAMAHIRIYAH NEWS AGENCY)--The leader of the great Fatah 
revolution, Colonel Mu'ammar al-Wadhdhafi, sent a letter to President Ronald Reagan 
of the U.S. 


The text of the letter reads as follows: 
President Ronald Reagan of the United States, Washington D.C. 


Your accession to the presidency of the United States shows the American voters’ 
confidence in you and is proof further of the failure of former President Carter's 
policies. 


The most singular failure in Carter's foreign policy was the signing of the Camp David 
accords. These accords were the affirmation of the use of force by the victor against 
the vanguished. They further angered the entire Arab nation for squandering the rights 
of an oppressed people--the Palestinians. 


These accords have also resulted in the boycott by the Arab nation of Egypt which is 
now severed from our nation. The accords further resulted in Arab anger at U.S. policy 
during Carter's administration. For it is with his encouragement that ‘the Israelis 
transformed Jerusalem into their capital, thus defying the islamic and Christian worlds. 


On this occasion we call on America, under your administration, to play a different 
role, respecting the will of peoples and ending oppressive U.S. intervention, both 
covert and overt, in the internal affairs of other countries. 


I would further hope that during your administration, the red Indians will gain their 
rights. What in fact makes me mention this subject which concerns America are two 
reasons, the first being that the red Indians throughout Carter's administration sent 
me letters calling for my help.

The second reason is that the majority of red Indians are of Libyan extraction. I hope 
the opportunity arises for me in the future to show you these letters and reports on 
archelogical findines proving the red Indians' Libyan extraction. 


The Libyan Arab people look forward to sound and equitable relations based on mutual 
respect and interests. My country looks forward to establishing such relations with 
your new administrations in the United States. 

Signed: Colonel Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi.

PAPER VIEWS LIBYAN RESPONSES TO FOREIGN PRESSURES 


LD261303 Paris L"HUMANITE in French 21 Jan 81 p 9 
[Robert Lambotte dispatch: "Tripoli Denounces Paris-Washington-Cairo Axis"] 


[Text] Tripoli, 20 January--It is with the greatest calm that the Libyan population 
is receiving the news from the south reporting the strengthening of the French 
military presence on Chad's borders. In the past 4 days Tripoli's streets have offer- 
ed the sight of a city enjoying the weekend break, extended by the Mawlid celebrations 
(birth of the prophet). The only visible crowds were forming in front of the toy 
stores besieged by children. A cold wind swept the capital and prompted everybody to 
return home. The radio and television were broadcasting their usual svograms. There 
was no sign of any military convoys. Observers seeking the least sign of tension 

were disappointed. Nonetheless, the Libyan leaders are still paying close attention 
to the development of the Chadian situation. 


The recent conference of several African heads of state in Lome has prompted some very 
strong reactions from them. "Our forces intervened in Chad," they say, “at President 
Goukouni Oueddei's request, not only because he is the head of the National Unity 
Transition Government but above all because he is the founder of the Chadian National 
Liberation Front and in that capacity removed Malloum's corrupt government from 
Ndjamena after 20 years of fighting." 


In reply to the statements issued by some African heads of state at a meeting in Lome, 
the Libyan officials are now stressing that the Libyan troops now stationed in Chad 
will only withdraw at Goukouni Oueddei's request. 


As regards the plan for union between Chad and Libya, portrayed in Western capitals as 
a coup by Colonel al-Qadhdhafi against Ndjamena, the Libyan officials state: "At 
present it is above all a question of mass unity, unity between the Chadian and Libyan 
masses. That is why we decided to open the borders. As regards political unity, it 
goes without saying that it cannot be achieved without a broad consultation of the 
Chadian people through a referendum."

The Giscardian military preparations in Africa and the troops sent to the countries which 
border on Chad, however, are certainly worrying the Tripoli authorities. "If there were 
to be a clumsy maneuver by the French Government," declare officials at the People's 
Bureau for Foreign Affairs here, "there is no doubt that the question would be submitted 
to our people's councils and they would have to decide on the appropriate response, 
whether economic or military." 


This hypothesis of a Giscardian "initiative" cannot be completely ruled out when you know 
that a Quai d'Orsay faction is still counting on an improbable future for Hissein Habre— 
the rebel extremist. However, current Libyan anxiety is mainly prompted by the attitude 

adopted by Egyptian President as-Sadat, whose threatening remarks suggest that, with U.S. 
support, he is planning an offensive against Libya. 


Speaking to the Arab People's Congress at a meeting in Tripoli on Monday evening [19 
January] Libyan head of state Colonel al-Qadhdhafi said that the Egyptian and Sudanese 
leaders, allied with the French leaders, were behind an operation to put Hissein Habre 
back in harness. He revealed that the weapons seized in Hissein Habre's camp in Ndjamena 
were of Israeli, Sudanese and Egyptian manufacture. "The Chadian war," Colonel 
al-Qadhdhafi said, "was being waged against the Libyan Jamahiriya. We were forced to 
cross the border to defend ourselves." 


Mentioning the union between Syria and Libya, Colonel al-OQadhdhafi said: "For our part 
we think it is the Libyans’ national duty to complete this unity."
