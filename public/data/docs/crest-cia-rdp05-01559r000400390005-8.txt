Summary (AI): A August 1980 State Department cable (from the London embassy) reports on New York Times journalist Youssef Ibrahim's visit to Libya and his interview with Qadhafi, including Qadhafi's remarks on Carter, U.S. diplomats, oil, and surveillance of foreign journalists in Tripoli. It also mentions Iranian activity at Tripoli's Beach Hotel and the Tobruk disturbances.
• Ibrahim said surveillance in Libya had become more obvious; the hotel telex operator refused to let him cable stories to New York.
• Qadhafi gave names of two Libyans in London whom the regime was tracking; Ibrahim passed them to the UK Embassy in Tripoli.
• Qadhafi said U.S. diplomats left Tripoli because he would not see the American chargé who carried a message from Carter.
• An American pastor reported detention without charge of Americans for up to 48 hours; the American school grounds were sought for September 1 celebrations.
• 1980-08-26: Youssef Ibrahim of the New York Times met Qadhafi for a two-hour interview; Qadhafi said he had given Ibrahim damaging information on President Carter.
• 1980-09-01: Planned celebrations marking the regime's eleventh anniversary; Ibrahim could not confirm rumors of dramatic announcements.
