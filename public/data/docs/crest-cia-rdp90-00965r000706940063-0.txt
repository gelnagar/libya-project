Summary (AI): A 1986 Jack Anderson column argues that European governments that criticized the U.S. raid on Libya ignored Qaddafi's record of sponsoring assassinations and terrorism against Libyan dissidents in Italy, Greece and elsewhere. It is about Libya's foreign terrorism rather than places in Tripoli.
• Column dated 27 April 1986 in the Washington Post, by Jack Anderson and Dale Van Atta.
• Italian police reportedly uncovered a plot by terrorist leader Oreste Scalzone, who was thought to have received Libyan funds for a radio station and cultural centers in the Rome/Naples area.
• The 1986 West Berlin discotheque bombing was cited as the immediate cause of the U.S. air strike on Libya.
• The column notes Libyan oil money financed antigovernment terrorist groups from the Philippines to Ireland.
• 1981-02: At least two possibly Libyan gunmen opened fire at Rome's Fiumicino airport, killing or wounding a number of Arabs, per an intelligence cable.
• 1984-01: Threatening letters from a group calling itself 'The Armed Arab Struggle' were received at AP and AFP offices in West Berlin, linked to Libyan involvement per a State Department cable.
• 1984-07: Two young Libyan students opposed to Qaddafi were shot in their Athens apartment; a cable describes one being beaten, strangled and shot twice in the back. (2 killed)
