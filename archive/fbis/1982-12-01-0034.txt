LIBYA

CHADIANS MARCH IN SUPPORT OF GOUKOUNI OUEDDEI 


LD292226 Tripoli Voice of Arab Homeland in Arabic 2115 GMT 29 Nov 82 


[Text] The Chadian community and its revolutionary forces in Tripoli staged a march this 
evening in support of the Transitional National Union Government led by Goukouni 
Oueddei. Participants in the march affirmed the Chadian people's adherence to the 
legitimate Government of National Union and declared their total rejection of all 
imperialist attempts aimed at installing agents and butchers over the Chadian people. 


The participants in this march issued a statement declaring that the Chadian people and 
their revolutionary forces do not recognize the presence of Habre in Ndjamena. The 
statement stresses that the sacrifices and the pure blood spilled on the soil of Chad 
will not be in vain, that the Chadian people adhere to the legitimate national 
government, which includes all factions of the Chadian people, and that we shall resist 
every attempt aimed at installing Hissein Habre over the Chadian people. 


FOREIGN MINISTER COMMENTS ON OAU SUMMIT 


LD271630 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1400 GMT 27 Nov 82 


(Text] Tripoli, 27 Nov (JANA) -- ‘Abd al-'Ati al-'Ubaydi, secretary of the People's 
Bureau for Foreign Liaison and chairman of the OAU ministerial council, has condemned 
Moroccan policy bent on hegemony over the occupation of the Sahara. 


At a news conference held this morning at the information center in Tripoli, he said 
that the OAU has stressed repeatedly that the people of the Sahara are waging a 
liberation struggle against Morocco and that this organization recognizes the right of 
the Saharan people to self-determination. The Moroccan regime disregards African 
resolutions and insists on continuation of its hegemony over the Sahara. 


He noted that the practices of the Moroccan regime in the Sahara are the same as any 
colonial practices on the African Continent. 


He said: We are confident that the Moroccan regime is leading its allies in a serious 
direction that is bound to actually contribute to the dismemberment of the organization 
and the obstruction of its march. We hope that the African leaders who were absent 
from the OAU summit conference in August and November will realize the gravity of this 
fact. 


"Abd al-'Ati al-'Ubaydi, in his news conference, which was attended by the OAU's 
official spokesman, Peter Onu, made it clear that the reality that emerged during the 
meeting of the African leaders in Tripoli was that the Chad problem is a political 
issue and an issue of a people divided by civil war -- a matter that demands that the 
OAU fulfill its role in finding a solution to realize the unity of Chad. 


He told the journalists that the Chadian capital, Ndjamena, has come under the control 
of one faction, Habre's; while there are still 10 factions -- which form the Transition- 
al National Union Government formed by the OAU under Goukouni Oueddei's leadership -- 
that control most of Chad's regions.

He added that the OAU was the one that dealt with and is still dealing with the Chad 
problem, and that the Lagos and Nairobi summit agreements are still in force and have 
not been rescinded. Therefore, the organization has not recognized any regime in 
Ndjamena and continues to affirm its recognition of the Transitional National Union 
Government headed by Goukouni Oueddei. 


He stressed that any attempt that comes from outside the African Continent to impose 
a specific regime in Chad will not be acceptable to the African leaders. He noted 
that Habre's acceptance in Kinshasa means nothing to the African leaders and the OAU. 


"Abd al-'Ati al-'Ubaydi dealt with the importance of the Tripoli declaration. He 

said that this declaration stressed the rejection of the conditions of the American 
Administration, which linked Namibia's independence to the withdrawal of Cuban forces 
from Angola. He added: “It is significant that the Tripoli declaration was the reply 
to the American Administration by 31 African states who were present in Tripoli at 

the time that the vice president of the American Administration, Bush, was visiting 

a number of African countries. He was visiting because the American Administration 
sensed that the 19th summit conference in Tripoli would seriously debate the Namibia 
issue and that it was determined to adopt a united and firm African decision." 


Regarding the support and backing for the liberation movements in South Africa and 
occupied Palestine, Al-'Ubaydi made it clear that the African leaders stressed the 
need to support the African liberation movements materially and morally in order to 
achieve the aims sought by these movements. 


He noted that the Tripoli declaration delegated the present chairman of the OAU and 
its General Secretariat to establish programs connected with economic and social 
development, especially health and self-sufficiency in Africa, according to the Lagos 
economic plan. 


He made it clear that the Tripoli summit of 31 African states debated Africa's pro- 
blems and matters preoccupying Africans. The issues of liberation and the racist- 
Zionist aggression on Arab territory were among the subjects debated by the African 
leaders. The Tripoli declaration also reflected the leaders’ interest in economic and 
social issues. 


JANA CITES PEREZ DE CUELLAR INTERVIEW ON UN 


LD260018 Tripoli JANA in English 1440 GMT 25 Nov 82 


[Text] Tripoli, Safar 9, Nov 25, Jamahiriyah News Agency -- The U.N. secretary gen- 
eral, Javier Perez de Cuellar, commented on the U.N. Charter and the role of the U.N. 
in a radio interview with "the Voice of the Arab World" (Sawt al-Watan al-Arabi). He 
said that it is difficult to say that 37-year-old U.N. Charter reflects the true con- 
dition of the world today or the international balance of political and economic 
forces. 


He added: "The charter was deviously prepared, for any amendment to carried out 
requires the approval of the five permanent members in the Security Council." 


De Cuellar went on to speak about the crippling effect of this comstitutional set 
up and pointed out that if the other member countries continued to exert their 
efforts the Charter may be changed in the future.

In answer to a question on the economic and social problems facing the industrial 
countries of the world and ways for bridging the gap between the rich and poor coun- 
tries of the world as a means for achieving development, Mr de Cuellar said: "World 
peace and security cannot be discussed as long as economic and social instability 
prevail in the developing countries. World peace and security are not specifically 
political and military issues but essentially economic and social. When the economic 
and social problems of the industrial countries have been overcome, then it is pos- 
sible to think seriously about peace and security on an international level." 


In the long run, Mr de Cuellar said, these economic problems of the industrial nations 
will be destabilising factors not only to the countries concerned but also to the rest 
of the world. This is why it is necessary, he said, for the industrial nations to 
achieve social and economic stability and thereby insure stability in the world. 


The U.N. secretary general condemned interference in the internal affairs of countries 
that have become independent recently. He said: "This interference is not merely 
condemned but is unacceptable by the U.N. Charter. Personally, I am opposed to every- 
thing that denotes direct or indirect foreign intervention in Africa or elsewhere." 


On the question of the right of veto enjoyed by the permanent members of the Security 
Council, Mr de Cuellar said this constitutes a serious problem which has preoccupied 
several countries, such as Libya, which is concerned with maintaining respect for the 
U.N. Charter. De Cuellar also conceded that this right has been misused by the coun- 
tries that enjoy this privilege. 


On the U.N. achievements for world peace and security, De Cuellar said: "The U.N. 
works for world peace and security by means other than simply finding solutions for 
international problems. Through its agencies it helps the developing nations to con- 
tinue their progress and growth. There are many activities all of which contribute 
to solve the social and economic problems of the developing countries and help estab- 
lish peace and security in the world." 


Mr de Cuellar also pointed to the U.N. other efforts in preserving peace in various 
parts of the world as those carried out by international U.N. forces. [sentence as 
received] 


Mr de Cuellar has said that he was very pleased to be in the Jamahiriyah and to meet 
Colonel Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi. He also expressed his gratitude towards the warm 
reception he was given by the Libyan people. 


Mr de Cuellar pointed out the Jamahiriyah's support and care for the U.N. and its con- 
cern to peacefully, through the organization, settle the world problems. He added: 

"The economic and financial resources of the Jamahiriyah are rationally and ideally 
exploited. I have witnessed for myself all important social achievements. I, as 

a secretary general of the U.N. and a citizen of this world am pleased to see the indus- 
trialization of the Jamahiriyah, the building of roads, refineries and dams are signs 

of such great achievements."

JOINT LIBYAN-CAPE VERDIAN COMMUNIQUE ISSUED 


LD292202 Tripoli JANA in English 1832 GMT 29 Nov 82 


[Text] Tripoli, Safar 12, Nov 29, Jamahiriyah News Agency -- A joint Jamahiriyah-Cape 
Verde communique was issued today in Tripoli and Praia following the visit of Cape 
Verde's prime minister, Pedro Verona Rodrigues Pires, to the Jamahiriyah. The text of 
the communique is as follows: 


In response to an invitation by the General People's Committee secretary, the prime 
minister of the Cape Verde Republic, Mr. Pedro Verona Pires, paid an official and 
friendly visit to the Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah between 11-13 Safar 
1392, 27-29 November 1982. 


Talks that were held during the visit between delegations representing the two sides 
that were headed by the General People’s Committee secretary and the prime minister of 
the Cape Verde Republic, Mr. Pedro Verona Pires, dealt with bilateral cooperation and 
common positions towards African, Arab and international issues. 


On bilateral cooperation, the two sides reviewed the progress of this cooperation and 
expressed their belief in the necessity of bolstering and developing it in ways that 
would serve the interests of both countries and realise the ambitions of both peoples 
for progress and prosperity. 


In this respect, an agreement was signed for regulating technical and economic coopera- 
tion between both countries. 


A cultural agreement was also signed for the exchange of experts and educational dele- 
gations, and for training. Furthermore, and in order to follow up and expand this 
cooperation, the two countries agreed on the formation of a joint committee at 
ministerial level. 


During discussions on Africa, the two sides expressed concern regarding increased 
foreign interference in Africa's internal affairs in ways that threaten the sovereignty, 
security and independence of the continent's countries. 


In this respect, the two sides affirmed the importance of economic, political and 
cultural coordination, solidarity and cooperation between African countries. They 
further affirmed the importance of the Organisation of African Unity (OAU) and its 
positive role in enhancing the unity and solidarity of African countries and of making 
the continent avoid the pitfalls of foreign interference, pointing in this respect to 
the OAU role against imperialism, neo-colonialism and all forms of racism. 


The two sides strongly condemned the attempts by imperialism and its agents in the re- 
gion to sow the seeds of discord amongst African countries, thus weakening the OAU 

and leading to the non-convening, for the second time, of the 19th African summit con- 
ference in Tripoli. 


Both sides expressed their regret that it was not possible to hold the 19th OAU summit 
conference and pledged full support for the resolutions taken in Tripoli regarding 
efforts to defend and consolidate the OAU and the African Continent. 


Both countries expressed support for the struggle of the Saharan people and commitment 
to the OAU resolutions regarding the acceptance of the Democratic Saharan Arab Republic 
as a member of the OAU and denounced attempts to rescind this resolution. They con- 
firmed the OAU resolutions and Lagos agreements on Chad.

Both sides discussed the situation in southern Africa, condemning the repressive policy 
of the apartheid regime of South Africa and pledging support for the struggle of the 
South African people to abort the policy of racial discrimination, repression and 
terrorism exercised by the apartheid Pretoria regime against the South African people. 


In respect to the situation in Namibia, both sides asserted their unwavering support 
for the struggle of the Namibian people led by their sole legitimate representative, 
SWAPO, and called for prompt implementation of U.N. resolutions and recommendations 
on Namibia's independence. 


Both sides condemned the repeated aggression of the apartheid South African regime 
against Angola, Mozamibque, and the other front line countries and stressed the need 
for other African countries to increase the defence capability and security of those 
countries. 


Both countries reviewed the grave and explosive situation in the Arab region as a result 
of repeated Israeli aggression against the Arab nation, the last of which is the 
barbaric invasion of the Lebanese territories, backed by international imperialism. 

In this respect both sides strongly condemned this aggression and the horrific 

massacres committed by the Zionist entity against the Palestinian and Lebanese people 
and called for an immediate and unconditional withdrawal of the Israeli forces from 

all Lebanese territories. They renewed their unlimited support for the just struggle 

of the Palestinian people and their inalienable right of return to their homeland and 
establishment of their own independent state in Palestine. 


The two sides agreed that the Camp David accords have led to the entrenchment of the 
Zionist occupation of Arab territories and the widening of its aggression against Arab 
countries in an attempt to forcefully impose its hegemony over the Arab nation and 
liquidate the Palestinian cause.
