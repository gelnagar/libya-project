SUPPLYING OF WEAPONS TO IRAN, IRAQ DETAILED 
Hamburg DER SPIEGEL in German 24 Nov 80 pp 142-146 
[Article: "Iran/Iraq--Who Supplies the Weapons") 


[Text] Soviet arms go to Iraqis and Iranians, U.S. planes 
provide supplies for the ayatollah from Libya, Soviet planes 
fly war materiel from Saudi Arabia to Iraq, Israelis with 
Syrian approval supply Iran via Beirut--the helpers in the 
wa being waged in the oilfields apparently are not governed 
by their old antagonisms. 


The attackers were taken by surprise. For weeks the Iraqis had been content to 
keep the port of the Iranian city of Khorramshahr occupied; the center of the city, 
they figured, would soften up on its own. 


But when they finally made their first attempt also to occupy the town proper, they 
were in for a surprise. 


The defenders did not surrender by any means. Even after having used up their 
ammunition, the men of the Iranian Revolutionary Guard fought on, this time with 
clubs and knives. "They were simply mad," an Iraqi major said later in amazement. 


Meanwhile the Iranians as a rule no longer have to fall back on such cut~-and-thrust 
weapons. Nor will they have to do so in the foreseeable future. 


On adventurous routes, enough war materiel is reaching Iran to enable its people 
to hold their own in the battle in the oilfields which has been rocking back and 
forth undecided for more than 9 weeks. The supplies, however, do not suffice to 
give Khomeyni's fighters the upper hand. 


The harassed realm of the ayatollah is getting help from the most varied countries, 
which in quite a number of cases definitely are enemies of one another. As if 
sometimes bloody differences all of a sudden did not matter any longer, Israel 

and also Israel's archenemy Libya, communist North Korea and anticommunist South 
Korea are supporting Iran. 


Ahead of all the rest, however, the Soviet Union is rendering daily aid. NATO 
intelligence experts are sure that, while untiringly protesting strict neutrality 
in the conflict, the rulers in the Kremlin are supporting both sides. 


18 











Iraq is being given direct support by the Russians, who are supplying munitions as 
well as spare parte for planes and armored vehicles on the basis of agreements con- 
cluded before the war. 


Iran, on the other hand, is being supplied by Moscow more or less discreetly 
through satellites and friendly countries. Western experts claim that transport 
for the most part is handled by the seven jumbos of Iran Air. The huge planes, 
each of which can carry 90 tons of freight, take on spare parts and probably also 
munitions in the GDR and transport them to Tehran via Poland. 


According to the same experts, Bulgaria is an important turntable in the supply of 
the two warring sides. They say that Sofia's official airline, the Bulgarian 
Airlines-Balkan, transports supplies both to the Syrian capital, Damascus, to be 
passed on to Iran, and to the Jordanian capital, Amman, to be passed on to Baghdad. 


Iranian jumbos take off from the runways of Libyan Chief of State Qadhdhafi, who-- 
involved in every crisis--has opened his well-filled arsenals and ammunition depots 
for the harassed mullahs. Via Bulgaria and through Soviet air space, the war 
materiel reaches Khomeyni's country unimpeded--small arms, armored planes and 
Soviet ZSU 23-4 Vierlings antiaircraft tanks. 


Even U.S. planes are flying on the ayatollah's side. According to NATO intelli- 
gence, the Flying Tiger Line, thoroughly experienced in delicate missions for 
decades, is transporting Libyan arms donations on official air routes of the 
Mediterranean and via Turkey to Iran. Does Washington really not know anything 
about that? 


Like Libya, Iran supporter Syria too is in a position to supply Western arms, such 
as Hot and Milan antitank rockets jointly produced by the Germans and French. 


The Israelis as well, who at one time were often cursed publicly by the ayatollah, 
are helping him with important spare parts, because Iran, enemy of its enemy Iraq, 
after all is less close and less dangerous. 


According to Western military experts, the Israeli supplies, with Syrian concur- 
rence, are trarsshipped in the Christian part of Beirut and transported to their 
destination by the Lebanese freight forwarders Trans-Mediterranean Airways. 


Even the distant North Koreans have their hand in it, loading Iranian jumbos in 
Pyongyang with supplies from Soviet arsenals. These are needed to a certain ex- 
tent because Reza Pahlevi for his shiny armor had also procured minor quantities of 
Soviet antiaircraft guns, infantry tanks and trucks. 


According to Western situation estimates, U.S. arms captured in Vietnam also are 
being shipped from the Far East to Iran. 


Whatever their new friends cannot come up with, the mullahs are purchasing with 
foreign currency from international arms dealers. For maintenance and repair they 
have hired Japanese and South Korean specialists who know how to handle the equip- 
ment, mostly of U.S. origin. 











The Far Eastern helpers are not having an easy time, for the one-time strongest and 
most modern war machine of the region is now but a shadow of what it once was.
