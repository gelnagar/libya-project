SUDANESE WORKERS IN QATAR--Last week, there was an exchange of documents 
ratifying an agreement to arrange for Sudanese workers to come to work in 
Qatar. The agreement requires that the Qatari Ministry of Labor and Social 
Affairs and the authorities concerned in Sudan to coordinate to bring the 
Sudanese workers to Qatar. The Qatari Ministry of Labor will solicit bids 
for services from employers in Qatar, provided that the Sudanese authorities 
comply with these bids within the limits of possibility. The agreement also 
requires that the two sides form a joint committee whose job will be to co- 
ordinate in order to implement the provisions of the agreement, to interpret 
them in the event they are disputed and to recommend that some provisions 

be reviewed, if the interests of both sides require that. This agreement

PERSONAL DIFFERENCES BETWEEN SARKIS, KAMIL AL-AS'AD VIEWED 
Kuwait AL-TALI'AH in Arabic No 745, 26 May 82 pp 24-25 
{Article: "Background of the Quarrel Between Sarkis and Al-As'ad"] 


[Text] It is no longer a secret that a quiet quarrel has been going on for 
some time between the President of the Republic, Ilyas Sarkis, and the 
president of the Chamber of Deputies, Kamil al-As'ad. This has emerged in 
numerous statements by al-As‘'ad on many occasions in which he criticized the 
regime severely and accused it of neglecting initiatives and actions needed 
to cope with the crises the country is experiencing. Recently this quarrel 
took on the form of a challenge when the deputies who support the president 
of the chamber submitted a petition demanding that a session be convened to 
discuss the government's policy and its inability to play a positive role in 
the incidents in Tripoli and the Lebanese South where hundreds had been 
killed and wounded. 


However the administration took a counter-position in which it took pains to 
abort the opposition's plan by instructing its supporters to be absent from 
the debating session thus preventing a quorum and stopping the session from 
being held. When the president of the chamber again called for a new session 
with the same result; only 32 deputies were present with at least 50 needed 
for a quorum. 


This split among the deputies over a position toward the government, and 
implicitly toward Sarkis himself, has aroused great interest in political 
circles because of its implied ramifications on the eve of the elections for 
a new president of the republic to replace the current piesident whose term 
expires on 23 August. Some feel that this is a sign of the numerous 
complications which will surround the presidential elections. 


Developments have revealed that there is an agreement between the president 
of the republic and the prime minister for the latter to remain in power on 
condition that he manifest complete cooperation with the former and major 
preparations to support him in the steps he reportedly intends to take at the 
end of his term. 


These steps involve the issue of the use of the army in the nationalist areas, 
removal of the Arab Deterrent Forces from Beirut and its suburbs and applying

HADDAD INFLUENCE LIMITED--A tacit agreement appears to be emerging among 
Haddad, the IDF authorities and the Lebanese Government to limit Haddad's 
sphere of influence to the area south of the Litani River and to encourage 
the local Lebanese Government to reassert its authority over the other 
areas controlled by the IDF. In an exclusive interview with the JERUSALEM 
POST, Haddad said his policy would be "to establish a reai, legitimate 
civilian authority. Intermal security should be handled by the civilian 
authorities through their police," he said. "The free army of Lebanon 
should not interfere in problems of internal security. Only when specifi- 
cally asked will I send soldiers to assist the civil authorities,'' Haddad 
said. Haddad thought the IDF should not withdraw from Lebanon until the 
last Syrian soldier leaves. "Only then can Lebanon really be free," he 
concluded. [Excerpts] [TA250854 Jerusalem THE JERUSALEM POST in English

INTERNATIONAL AID URGED FOR REFUGEES IN SUDAN 
Khartoum AL-SAHAFAH in Arabic 18 May 82 p l 


[Text] Dr Muhammad 'Uthman Abusaq, minister of state in the Ministry of 
Internal Affairs, announced in a statement before the People's Assembly 
yesterday, in response to a question from Member Sulayman Mustafa Ab-Bakr, 

that the total number of refugees in Sudan, according to statistics as of March 
1981, was 580,000. He said that the influx of refugees was basically from 
Ethiopia/Eritrea 446,000, Uganda 21,700, Chad 30,000, and Zaire 500. The 
minister underscored the firm obligation to care for and protect the refugees, 
out of humanitarian duty and in fulfillment of international charters and cus- 
toms. He indicated that the latest refugees are pouring into Sudan, especially 
from Eritrea and the Tigray region, at a rate of 100 a day. Moreover, Ugandians 
constantly cross into Equatoria at an average of 70 per day. The minister made 
it clear that there is also a stream of Chadian refugees. He added that the 
refugees are basically concentrated in the provinces of Kassala (297,000), 

the Red Sea (63,000), and Equatoria (107,000), and in the city of Khartoum 
(35,000) and some 34,000 in other cities. Moreover, there are 3,000 refugees 
in Jonglei, 2000 in the Upper Nile area and 7000 in the Blue Nile Province.
