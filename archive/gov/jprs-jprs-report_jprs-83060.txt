[Text] Tripoli, 27 Feb (SANA)--Salah al-Hajji, member of the Political De- 
partment of the Tunisian Communist Party, has affirmed that Syria represents

demned attempts to cast doubts and poison the clear, sound Syrian stand 
on the Arab struggle issues, particularly the Palestinian issue. He said 
that casting doubt on Syria means casting dbout on the noble stand which 
Syria and her friends, foremost among them the USSR and socialist bloc 
countries, represent. 


He affirmed his party's support for Syria, the Palestinian revolution and 
the Lehanese nationalist forces which are confronting the hostile designs 
against the Arab region. He reaffirmed his party's condemnation and denun- 
ciation of the U.S.-Zionist aggression against and hostility toward the Arab 
nation and its liberation and progressive forces. 


Al-Hajji denounced the Camp David accords, affirming that any attempt to es- 
tablish peace with Israel through any Arab party at the expense of the PLO 

is an attempt to liquidate the Palestinian issue. He stressed the need to 
preserve the unity of the Palestinian revolution and its independent decision 
to ensure that it avoids making mistakes and pitfalls and being dragged into 
league with reactionary stands. He pointed out that the Arab liberation 
movement must base its moves on common denominators which guarantee its 
progress and develop its struggle in service of our liberation objectives.

Conclusion 


Despite the shortcomings of the demographic data available on the Palestinians, 
we can say that, in general, the Palestinian people is a young people and the 
sex ratio is normal, although it is different in the countries that attract 
workers like the Arab Gulf and Libya because of residence in these countries. 


The Palestinians enjoy a high birth rate and a low death rate like the rest of 
the peoples of the developing countries. Moreover, their educational status is 
excellent and above that of many peoples of the developing countries. The level 
of participation in the work force is excellent compared with the developing 
countries, particularly the participation of the males. However, the partici- 
pation of the Palestinian women in the work force is still low. The Palestin- 
ian work force is regarded as a modern work force with a high percentage of 
scientists and technicians. 


The Palestinians have unfavorable rates of emigration from Palestine because l 
percent of the inhabitants, most of them young people, are forced to leave. It 
is urgently necessary that the PLO and all the Arab states try to halt this 
human hemorrhage from Palestine by adopting active measures to support the 
steadfastness of our people in the face of Israeli occupation and expansion.
