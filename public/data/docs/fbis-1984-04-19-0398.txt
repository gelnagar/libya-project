LIBYA

British ‘Attack’ on London People’s Bureau Condemned 
LD181856 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1530 GMT 18 Apr 84 


[Text] Tripoli, 18 April — The British Government has in a 
high-handed manner violated international law when the British 
police yesterday embarked on an armed attack on the Libyan 
People’s Bureau in London during their escort of a band of agents 
and hired men of various nationalities who marched in the 
direction of the Bureau. 


This ugly action by the British police who opened fire haphaz- 
ardly and arrested a number of workers of the Libyan People’s 
Bureau is a flagrant violation of all international covenants and 
an act of flagrant tyrannical and disgraceful behavior that dis- 
plays a detestable racist policy. 


This ugly action by the British police that was instigated by the 
British Government cannot be separated from all the criminal 
acts by the British Government, not only against Libyans but also 
against all the other Arabs and peoples who have been afflicted 
with occupation and British hegemony. 


The terrorist acts of pursuit and hunts against the Libyan rev- 
olutionary force in Britain, the harassment of Libyan patients 
and frightening of school children in Britain, as well as the 
deceitful and arbitrary arrests of Libyan travelers at British 
airports, are all acts which represent a racist British policy that 
appears at present to be increasingly vulgar. 


All the forces opposed to organized international terror must 
condemn and expose this arrogant conceit which cannot be 
justified under any circumstances. 


UN Secretary General Notified of Embassy Siege 
LD181949 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1855 GMT 18 Apr 84 


[Text] Tripoli 18 April — The People’s Committee of the People’s 
Bureau for Foreign Liaison has officially notified the UN Secre- 
tary General today of the British police imposition of a siege on 
the Libyan Arab People’s Bureau in London and arrest of some 
of its members, a matter that is considered a violation and a 
breach of the Vienna Convention regulating relations between 
states.
