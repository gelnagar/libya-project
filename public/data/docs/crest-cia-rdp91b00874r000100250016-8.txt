Summary (AI): CIA talking points for Ambassador Walters (28 August 1986) on how US trade and financial sanctions, combined with low oil prices, are squeezing Libya's economy. It covers oil revenue losses, frozen reserves, US oil company withdrawals, European trade reactions, and Libyan use of front companies and intelligence cover for subversion. No specific Tripoli locations are named.
• Each $1 decline in oil prices costs Tripoli about $33 million per month at 1.1 million b/d exports.
• At an average price of $12 per barrel, Libyan oil revenues would be about $4.5 billion, one-quarter the 1980 level.
• Libya still held an estimated $4.0 billion in foreign exchange reserves, about 40 percent in Japanese banks, plus $1.3 billion in gold.
• Libya sued a US bank in London to regain access to $260 million in frozen funds.
• Libyan intelligence operatives under Libyan Arab Airlines cover were reportedly involved in planned attacks against a US officers club in Ankara and the US Embassy in Nicosia.
• Oil production was about 68 percent of maximum sustainable production.
• 1986-01-07: US economic restrictions on Libya imposed (referenced as 'since 7 January')
• 1986-06-30: US oil companies Marathon, Conoco, Amerada Hess, Occidental and W.R. Grace ceased operations in Libya under Administration orders
