Summary (AI): A declassified CIA-era policy paper (catalogued 2008, apparently from around 1981-82) weighing political and economic measures against Qadhafi's Libya, including oil embargoes, withdrawal of US business personnel, and export controls. It gives figures on US oil purchases, Libyan revenues, Soviet arms deliveries and US-Libya trade, but names no specific Tripoli sites.
• In 1980 the United States purchased 40% of Libya's oil exports, paying Libya $10 billion in annual revenues.
• Libya was the largest recipient of Soviet weapons and accounted for one fifth of Soviet arms exports to the Third World, with deliveries estimated at $2.3 billion in the current year.
• Libya still had reserves totalling $16 billion, per the paper.
• US exports to Libya ran about $500 million a year, mostly machinery and transportation equipment.
• Oil companies' investment in Libya had an estimated replacement value of $2.5 billion, with book value possibly under $200 million.
• Exxon had 71 US citizens among its 1,920-person Libyan workforce; the paper proposed phased economic steps including a unilateral US oil embargo under Section 232(b) of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962.
