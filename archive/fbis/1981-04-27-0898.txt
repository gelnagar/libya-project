LIBYA

COMMENTARY ATTACKS U.S. FOREIGN POLICY, RDF 


[Unattributed commentary] 
LD261258 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 25 Apr 81 


[Excerpts] In the past 24 hours news agencies have reported statements by the Pentagon 

in Washington regarding the U.S. decision to set up an independent command of the Rapid 
Deployment Force. Reports say the Pentagon is now studying the possibility of transferring 
combat troops to the Arabian Gulf region. U.S. Defense Department sources have explained 
that the completion of the forces and their full deployment will take place in the near 
future. 


The question of the Rapid Deployment Force is not new to the United States’ foreign 

policy map, particularly in the last 3 years, because the U.S. administration's approach 
to this issue and its intention to implement it assumed a serious nature through practical 
steps which began with a speech by former U.S. President Carter in December, 1979, in 
which he presented the plan for what he termed the Rapid Deployment Force. In January, 
1980, came the U.S. declaration that it was prepared to embark on offensive military 
operations in the Arabian Gulf area within the framework of a project named the Carter 
principle--to which the former U.S. president resorted as a face-saving operation in 

the wake of the worsening crisis of the American hostages in Tehran, and at a time when 
the U.S. presidential elections were approaching. 


For 30 years U.S. policy on the Gulf region remained within the limits of muscle parading, 
military balances and statements of threats and intimidation, or raising the big stick 
without actually using it. It was a policy at whose limits former President Carter was 
anxious to stop; he was anxious not to go beyond those limits onto a dangerous path, as 
his predecessor Nixon did in the Vietnamese war, for example. 


Then Ronald Reagan came to the helm of the U.S. administration. The new American 
President's first steps toward the White House were a series of statements which were 
described in general as a military propaganda war. Soon he proceeded to put that old 
American concept into practice, through a number of trends. These began by interfering 
in the question of the security of the Arabian Gulf and the Indian Ocean region; then the 
trend toward open military interference in El Salvador--an attempt which was coupled 
with precautions so as not to slip into another Vietnam-like situation; and lastly, 

c me the tour of his secretary of state, Haig, in the Arab region, which took place at 
the time of the recent Lebanese events in Zahlah, Beirut and the south. In fact, the 
explosion of the situation in Zahlah is regarded as one of the elements which prepared 
for Haig's tour; a situation which the United States used to put pressure on some of 
the parties concerned when discussing the permanent or continuous presence of U.S. 
forces in the region, and in the Gulf in particular. 


During and after Haig's visit, the question of supplying Saudi Arabia with the AWACS 
spy planes was raised and is the subject of study and preparation.

In this the White House ignored the Zionist opposition and decided to hand the aircraft 
over to Saudi Arabia, under conditions, dictated by the U.S. Congress to the effect that 
the crew of these aircraft must be American with regard to administration and technical 
work. In other words, they must remain directly under control of the Pentagon; or perhaps 
there could be a compromise in which, by setting up a joint command, the American side 
would have absolute majority by virtue of its traditional military influence on Saudi 
Arabia and also because the AWACS aircraft is a strategic weapon of NATO, and the U.S. in 
particular, in the military confrontation between the two superpowers. 


The United States is now moving at record speed toward militarizing its foreign policy and 
imposing it by force of influence and terrorism on the peoples of the Third World and the 
Arab region in particular. The Reagan administration is (?implementing) this by means 

of the steps it takes every now and then to bridge the gap between deployment of the Rapid 
Deployment Force and the possibilities available, and especially outside the circle of 
U.S. [word indistinct]. 


The first stage in implementing this project requires the presence of 15,000 men from the 
82d Division in additon to 19,000 men from the 10lst Airborne Division, relying for their 
transportation on 971 huge transport aircraft. The question of making these aircraft 
available is still a problem besetting the full implementation of the project. 


One matter remains, an essential one: It is the United States insistence on placing the 
world, and the Arab region in particular, at the front of a line of (?resistance) and 


self-defense which is dictated by the honor of belonging to the homeland and by dignity. 


AL-QADHDHAFI LEAVES FOR VISIT TO MOSCOW 26 APR 


LD261542 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1524 GMT 26 Apr 81 


[Text] Tripoli, 26 Apr (JANA)--The leader of the revolution left for Moscow today for a 
visit to the USSR at the invitation of the CPSU Central Committee and the Presidium of the 
USSR Supreme Soviet, and in implementation of the resolution of the fourth summit confer- 
ence of the pan-Arab Steadfastness and Confrontation Front. He is to contact the USSR in 
the name of the front and discuss the means of developing and deepening the relations of 
friendship and cooperation between the USSR and the confrontation states. 


AL-QADHDHAFI CALLS ON ARABS TO FIGHT ISRAEL 


LD241328 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 24 Apr 81 


[Cable from Libyan leader Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi to Arab presidents and kings--date not 
given] 


[Text] The situation in Lebanon is one of external armed aggression on an independent 
state which is a member of the Arab League. This demands from the league's member states 
direct military action in repelling this aggression, in accordance with the league's 
charter. The league's member states must therefore send openly and in a regular manner 
naval, air and land forces and air defense units; fighting must start immediately against 
the invading Israeli forces, which are occupying Arab territories. 


Watching Israeli warships attack Lebanon's coast and its civilian ports and sink merchant 
ships of their choice and watching daily Israeli raids destroy Sidon, Tyre, Beirut and the 
refugee camps is the ultimate humiliation for anybody who has an Arab name. It is also 
the worse type of surrender by all those who hold Arab scepters. If the Arab governments 
do not decide to fight, then anybody, man or woman, who has some Arab dignity, origin and 
heritage must tread on the scepters, cross frontiers and head for Lebanon and Palestine 

to either kill or be killed. 


Your brother, Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi.
