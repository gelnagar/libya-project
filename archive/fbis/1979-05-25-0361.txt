AL=QADHDHAFI SENDS CABLES TO ARAB LEADERS ON LEBANON 


LD242036 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1845 GMT 24 May 79 LD 


[Text] Tripoli--Col Mu! ammar al-Qadhdhafi, leader of the great 1 September Revolution, has 
sent cables to the kings and presidents of the Arab States. The text of each cable is as 
follows: 


Fraternal greetings: In these critical moments in the life of our Arap nation, which is 
faced with the defiance and arrogance of its Zionist enemy and those who follow and support 
it, I have deemed it necessary to mention and draw attention to the killing and rendering 
homeless of our Arab people of Lebanon and Palestine by the Israeli enemy. To be silent on 
this subject would be most shameful. It is most astonishing to see the unarmed Lebanese 
and Palestinian peoples being mercilessly and relentlessly struck and killed while none of 
us moves a finger. 


The world of our age no longer recognizes any ethical logic or course of action, and it is 
obvious that the international community is incapable of resisting tyranny or of protecting 
Sanctuaries from being violated. Our world has already proved it is unable to check the 
aggressors and their dominantion. Otherwise, why haven't the destitute Palestinian people 
so far secured their right to live in their own land? We have other glaring examples in 
the peoples of Africa; consider Namibia, Zimbabwe and South Africa, The people who have 
become independent have attained this through their own strength. 


By this, I want to affirm that it is imperative for the Arab nation to face its destiny and 
fate. Indeed, it is a shame beyond all shame that the entire Arab nation should be stande 
ing by watching while our Arab brothers, the Palestinians and Lebanese, are being attacked 
and killed daily and their houses with their children and women inside are being destroyed 
While the enemy aircraft fly unchecked over Lebanese cities and Palestinian camps. 


For this reason, I call upon you to shoulder our pan-Arab responsibilities and check the 
aggressors by striking back twice as hard at them, thereby safeguarding the Arab nation*s 
dignity before the world. We cannot keep silent about what is taking place in Lebanon, 


Because of this open humiliation, what value will the life of the Arab nation have before 
the other peoples of the world? Indeed, what value does this life have now in this 
despicable situation? Death now is more proper and more honorable than this life of 
humiliation, submission and silence about what is being committed against our Arab brothers, 
the Lebanese and Palestinians in Lebanon, 


It looks as though the Arabs have accepted that they have been born only to be humiliated 
and humbled and that their faces are treaded upon and their backs are beaten. Shame unto 
this nation, of which I am one, for accepting shame as lunch and disgrace as dinner and to 
Sleep and wake up in disgrace. Signed: Your Brother Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi. 


Ve. 25 May 79 NORTH AFRICA

PDRY*S ISMA*IL DEPARTS, COMMENTS ON TALKS WITH AL-QADHDHAFI 
LD241032 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 0700 GMT 24 May 79 LD 


[Text] Tripoli--"'Abd al-Fattah Isma'il, member of the PDRY Presidential Council, left Tripoli 
at 0200 today to return home at the end of a brief visit to Libya. He was seen off at 

Tripoli international airport by Staff Major ‘Abd as-Salam Ahmad Jallud, a number of secre- 
taries of the general people's committees, the PDRY ambassador to Libya and by the chief of 
protocol of the Foreign Secretariat. 


In a statement to a JANA correspondent, 'Abd al-Fattah Ismatil described the results of his 
visit to Libya as important and objective. He said: I discussed with Brother Colonel 
Mufammar al-Qadhdhafi, leader of the revolution, and with Brother Staff Major Jallud the 
relations between the two countries and the means to develop these relations in the service of 
common goals, 


‘Abd al-Fattah Isma'il said that his tals with the leader of the revolution included the situe 
ation in the Arab region and the coordination of both countries! positions regarding the 
enemies of the Arab revolution and against imperialism, Zionism and reaction led by the 
defeated president of the Egyptian regime, 


He pointed out that the viewpoints of the two countries were identical concerning the coordi- 
nation of joint efforts, within the framework of the liberation movement in the Arab homeland 
and its national forces, to continue the struggle with increased perseverance and conviction. 


Regarding the unity of the two peoples in Yemen, ‘Abd al-Fattah Isma'il emphasized that this 
unity was a gain for the Yemeni people and for the Arab revolution movement. He expressed 
hope that the committees working for the implementation of agreements reached for the reali- 
zation of unity between the two Yemens will accomplish their task in accordance with the 
established timetable. 


‘Abd al-Fattah Isma'til expressed thanks and appreciation to the Arab people in Libya and 
their leadership for the great hospitality and warm reception afforded to him and his 
delegation.

[Text] Kuwait--Libyan Foreign Secretary Dr "Ali 'Abd aseSalah at-Turayki, has said in a 
statement published in Kwuait that the Libyan Jamahiriyah is not prepared to discuss the 
question of the disappearance last year of a Lebanese Shiltite leader, 


In a statement to the Kuwaiti AL-QABAS, he repeated that Imam Musa as-Sadr, president of the 
Supreme Shifite Council in Lebanon, has left Libyan territory. 


The Shitite leaders of the Iranian revolution raised the question of the disappearance of 
Imam as-Sadr with the Libyan leader Maj *Abd as#Salam Jallud when he recently visited Iran. 


However, in an interview today, Dr at-Turayki said: There is no call to reopen the question 
with Libya of the disappearance of Imam Musa aseSadr. The Jamahiriyah has clearly explained 
its stance on this issue, confirming that Imam as-Sadr_ had left Libya. 


The Libyan minister denied the presence of the toppled president of Uganda, Idi Amin, in the 
Jamahiriyah. He described Tanzania's invasion of the Uganda territory as a serious precedent, 
which could lead to new wars in the African Continent.
