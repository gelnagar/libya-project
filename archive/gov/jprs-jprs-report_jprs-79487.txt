PILOTS TRAIN IN FRANCE--Another group of Libyan pilots and technicians has 
arrived in France in the last few days for advanced training on the Mirage-5 
aircraft. The members of the group have been housed in the city of Bayonne, 
in the southwestern part of the country. Last night a report was made about 
a group of approximately 60 Libyan pilots and technicians who are staying in 
Biarritz, getting advanced courses in the main Dassault plant. These reports 
have received no official confirmation from Paris. Political circles in the 
French capital this evening told our correspondent that if it transpires that 
the reports are accurate Mitterrand's socialist government will be very much 
ill at ease, as it is demanding that the Libyan leader remove his forces 
from Chad. Paris had, however, already announced in the past that it would 
continue to honor the commitments undertaken by all the previous governments, 
even though some delays may occur in the dispatches to Libya. [Text]

PAKISTANIS RETURN FROM SYRIA--Islamabad, October 18--Libya has brought back 
from Damascus some 150 Pakistanis who were allegedly press-ganged and sent 
to fight Israelis in Lebanon, a Foreign Office spokesman said here today. 
Following protests by Islamabad at the treatment of Pakistani nationals 
working in Libya, a high-powered Pakistani delegation is now expected to 
visit Tripoli next month to investigate, the spokesman added. (A.F.P.)

FINANCIAL COOPERATION WITH FRG--A financial agreement was signed in Bonn on 

6 Oct 81 by the Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs, Mr Berndt von Staden, 

and the Moroccan Ambassador in Bonn, Mr Abdelhakim Iraqi. This agreement allocates 
a loan of 75 million DM for agriculturel projects in Morocco, and 10 million DM 

for the technical cooperation between the two countries. Furthermore, to alleviate 
the detrimental effects of the 1980-81 drought in Morocco, the government of the 
Federal Republic of Germany will provide the Moroccan government with 6,000 tons 

of wheat. [Text] [Casablanca LA VIE ECONOMIQUE 16 Oct 81 p 1]

SUDAN 


DEBATE HELD ON SECURITY MEASURES 
Khartoum SUNA DAILY BULLETIN in English 1 Oct 81 pp 5-6 


[Text] Khartoum, Oct 1 (SUNA)--The Society of Law, University of Khartoum, 
organized here Tuesday evening a debate on the recent measures taken against 
vagrants and tramps in Khartoum Province. 


A number of lawyers took part along with the Deputy Chief of the State Security. 


Advocate Taha Ibrahim said the measures were governed by a number of codes, 
the Constitution, the Criminal Procedures Act, the State Security Act and the 
Police Act. 


He said the measures could not be described as legal in view of Article 41 of 
the Constitution.
