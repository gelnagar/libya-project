Summary (AI): A 1983 Washington Post article reports that former CIA agent Edwin P. Wilson was acquitted in a U.S. District Court in Washington of conspiring in 1976 to murder a Libyan opponent of Qaddafi, Umar Abdullah Muhayshi. The article covers Wilson's earlier convictions for arms and explosives smuggling to Libya and the testimony of witness Raphael Quintero.
• Wilson was convicted twice in recent months of smuggling arms and explosives to Libya under multimillion-dollar contracts with Qaddafi.
• Wilson was serving prison sentences totalling 32 years and ordered to pay $345,000 in fines.
• The jury deliberated about five hours after a four-day trial.
• Wilson still faces a New York trial on a charge of conspiring to assassinate two federal prosecutors and seven potential witnesses.
• 1976-08: Wilson and Francis E. Terpil allegedly approached Raphael Quintero about an overseas murder contract targeting Libyan exile Umar Abdullah Muhayshi, which Quintero says he refused.
