Summary (AI): A December 1980 NPIC imagery report on Libyan military deployments in Chad, describing new airfields in Aozou and Ndjamena and Libyan aircraft and ground forces at several Chadian airfields. Nothing here is in or near Tripoli.
• Chad achieved independence from France in 1960.
• A coalition provisional government in Chad broke down earlier in 1980, after which fighting between Goukouni and Habre forces intensified.
• The report is based on satellite imagery and cites a Washington Post article of 7 Nov 1980.
• Libyan forces at Ounianga Kebir included an infantry battalion with BTR-60 APCs and a support battalion.
• 1980-06: An airfield was identified under construction about 51 nm north-northeast of Aozou; it is now serviceable.
• 1980-10: Since late October, Libyan F-1 Mirage fighters, SF-260 strike aircraft, CH-47 and HIND helicopters, tanks, APCs, rocket launchers and antiaircraft weapons were deployed to Chadian airfields.

LIBYAN MILITARY ACTIVITY IN CHAD, 

1. (S/D) This report summarizes Libyan military activity in Chad from 

During this period the Libyans increased their presence in Chad significantly, especially in the 

areas of Ndjamena, the capital, and Dougia, Faya Largeau, Fada, Oum Chalouba, Ounianga Kebir, 
and Aozou (Figure 1). 

2. (S/D) Chad, which achieved independence from France in 1960, has been divided by rival 
factions fighting a civil war for the past 16 years. Libya has offered support to various guerrilla 
groups during this time. When a coalition provisional government comprising representatives of 
all the political factions broke down earlier this year, fighting between the forces of President 
Goukouni Queddei, supported by Libya, and the forces of Defense Minister Hissene Habre inten- 
sified. ! 

3. (S/D) In late June 1980, an airfield was identified under construction 51 nautical miles 
(nm) north-northeast of Aozou in the Libyan-claimed and -occupied Aozou strip... The Aozou 
airfield is still under construction but is now serviceable, and Libyan aircraft have been observed 
there since (Figure 2). 

4. (S/D) A second airfield was identified under construction on the outskirts of Ndjamena, 

approximately 3 nm northwest of Ndjamena airfield on Ndjamena Airfield Northwest 
consists of a graded-earth runway with a line of defensive positions on the southeastern edge (Figure 3). 
The presence of three HIND helicopters on indicates that the airfield is occupied by 

Libyans. At Dougia Airfield, 35 nm northwest of Ndjamena, the runway has recently been extended and 
a new parking apron/support area has been observed. 

5. (3/D) Since late October, Libyan aircraft including F-1! Mirage fighters, SF-260 light 
strike. aircraft, and CH-47 and HIND helicopters have been deployed to various airfields in Chad 
(Figures 4 and 5), Libyan ground force equipment including tanks, armored personnel carriers, multiple 
rocket launchers, and antiaircraft weapons has been observed (Figures 6 and 7). Table | summarizes 
equipment and aircraft observed in Chad during this period. 

6. (S/D) Imagery of the Zouar, Bardai Zougra, Ogoui, Yebbi Bou, Koro Toro, Iriba, Ati, 
Adre, Am Timan, Odouge, Abeche, Pala, Bongor, and Moundou areas revealed no significant 
activity.
