LIBYA

AL-QADHDHAFI DENIES DIRECT AID TO CHAD'S GOUKOUNI 
AB280848 Paris AFP in English 0700 GMT 28 Nov 80 


[Excerpts] Tripoli, Libya, Nov 28 (AFP)--Libya has not yet given direct military aid to 
the transitional government in Chad, but would do so if asked, Libyan leader Mu'ammar 
al-Qadhdhafi told international journalists here on Thursday. 


Describing the transitional government headed by Goukouni Oueddei as the legitimate regime 
in Chad, Colonel al-Qadhdhafi noted that a Chad-Libya treaty signed last June 15 required 

both parties to join in defense against any foreign aggression and any threat to internal 

security. 


Colone! al-Qadhdhafi said that even if Libya supported the Transitional National Union 
Government, it would be doing no more than suppporting a legal government, and that is in 
keeping with the charter of the Organization of African Unity and with other African con- 
ference agreements. 


The Libyan leader said he was prepared to uphold Chad's territorial integrity, sovereignty 
and unity. He denied a report that France, the former colonial] power in Chad, had turned 
down a Libyan proposal to partition that country. 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi on the other hand, accused Egypt and Israel of aiding the rebellion 
by Hissein Habre's forces, and said the Transitional Government's troops had captured 
Egyptian and Israeli weapons being used by their opponents. 


FOREIGN SECRETARIAT EXPLAINS BOYCOTT OF SUMMIT 
LD261314 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 26 Nov 80 


[Text] The Foreign Secretariat has issued the following statement about the convening of 
the Arab summit conference in Amman: 


In view of the targets of the pan-Arab Front for Steadfastness and Confrontation and in 
order to implement its resolutions for and commitments to the aims of the pan-Arab struggle 
in facing the dangers threatening the Arab nation and its existence and in challenging all 
capitulation plots which have arisen to fulfill the purpose of colonialism and Zionism, the 
foreign secretariat in the Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah would like to stress 
that the convening of the summit conference in Amman in these circumstances without the 
participation of the PLO, the sole legitimate representative of the Palestinian people, the 
possessors of the main cause, and without the presence of Syria in its capacity as the 
first confrantation state, means that the conference would lose popularity and legality 

and be unable to express the will of the Arab nation. Hence the Socialist People's Libyan 
Arab Jamahiriyah has considered it its pan-Arab duty to boycott the conference being held 
in Amman and not to take part in it because the convening of this conference in this period 
without the particpation of the effective sides is considered in itself a plot against the 
Arab nation and the Palestinian question. 


NEW GUINEA-BISSAU LEADER EXPLAINS COUP TO AL-QADHDHAFI 


LD261948 Tripoli JANA in English 1757 GMT 26 Nov 80 


[Text] Tripoli, 26 Nov (Jamahiriyah News Agency)--Colonel Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi leader of 
the great Fatah Revolution, received a message from the president of the revolutionary 
command council in Guinea-Bissau, in which he explained the reasons which led to the 
toppling of the regime there by the revolution. He affirmed his concern for the unity of 
Guinea-Bissau territories. The Revolutionary Command Council will send a delegation to the 
Jamahiriyah, he said, to explain the latest developments in his country.

INFORMATION ON JORDAN BEING EXCHANGED WITH U.S. 
TA271145 Tel Aviv MA'ARIV in Hebrew 27 Nov 80 p 1 
{[Ya'aqov Erez report] 


[Text] The United States and Israel are exchanging information about the latest develop- 
ments on the Jordan-Syria border and over the last 24 hours there have been consulta- 
tions between U.S. and Israeli elements on this issue. 


Deputy Defense Minister Mordekhay Tzipori and Maj Gen Avraham Tamir, who are shortly 
leaving for Washington, will discuss with top Pentagon officials, among other things, 
the movement of forces on the Jordan-Syria border and the worsening tension in the 
region. The trip by Tzipori and Major General Tamir is intended as a resumption of the 
strategic dialog. 


Israel continues to follow the developments on the Syria-Jordan border and official 
sources said this morning that the tension was worsening because of the flow of forces 
of both sides. 


According to various reports, military aerial movement in the region of the Jordan-Syria 
border has increased and Syrian planes have violated Jordanian air space. Israeli sources 
said that so far it was impossible to decide whether the Syrian moves were intended for 
threatening purposes or whether Syria--with the approval of the Soviet Union--intended 

to initiate military action in northern Jordan, The Israeli sources emphasized that in 
this region the Syrians were threatening the land supply artery to Iraq--the Amman-Baghdad 
highway. 


RADIO VIEWS JORDANIAN, SYRIAN BORDER FORCES’ STRENGTH 
TA271734 Jerusalem Domestic Service in Hebrew 1700 GMT 27 Nov 80 
[From evening newsreel] 


[Text] The Jordanian Army, two divisions of which are deployed along the Syrian border, 
is well-prepared. It looks as though it will be able to stop the Syrians even without 
having to move additional forces northward. The Jordanian Army and Air Force are on high 
alert and the assumption is that Syria will not attack. Here is a report by our military 
correspondent, Shmu'el Tal. 


{Begin recording] The Jordanian Army is currently heavily deployed--armor and infantry-- 
along the Syrian border. Deep inside Jordanian territory, troops have been moved out of 
permanent bases and have taken up positions. The Jordanians are bringing units up to 
full strength, completing staff work, and have been patroling and digging in. There is 
no panic in their army, nor are there massive movements in the direction of the border. 
One can say that the Jordanians are prepared and ready to reinforce the front, but, 

to repeat, there still have not been many movements. 


The Syrian armored force is above division strength, namely it numbers more than 400 tanks 
deployed in an elliptical 60 km front with one point in the city of Dar'a. In all, the 
Syrians have armored, infantry, mechanized, artillery and antiaircraft forces along the 
Jordanian border. Western sources have said that at the moment around 20,000 Syrian troops 
are stationed there. 


The reasons the Syrians have moved is not yet clear, but one of the assumptions is that 
this is a movement which, for some reason, is typical of that army. In 1975, the Syrians 
moved two divisions toward the Iraqi border and later had them return for no obvious 
reason. In 1976 they moved a division toLebanon and returned it, too. In 1977 they 
transferred a division to the Iraqi border, and that unit also returned just as it went.

Last year they again moved a large force toward Aleppo. It may well be that this is 
one of the wide-scale movements characteristic of that army. There is no clear-cut 
explanation tor this, and one must not rule out the possibility that the present force 
will be withdrawn in the next few days. 


The prevalent assessment is that no flareup is expected, because Al-Asad's predicament 
is not too rosy: he is isolated; he has problems in Lebanon; he must be cautious on 
the Israeli front as well as the Iraqi border. Also, the Soviets have made it clear 
that the pact they signed with the Soviets has no secret clause. What can be inferred 
from this statement is that the Soviets are not too happy with the latest developments. 


In general, to repeat, the Syrians are faced with a well-armed Jordanian Army with high 
morale, and equipped with superb antiaircraft missiles. The lessons of the 1970 battles 
indicate that the Jordanian division--the lst division--was able to stop a Syrian division 
and even inflict many casualties in tanks and in human lives. In short, the Syrians are 
deployed in a wide front with many troops numbering over a division and the Jordanians 

are in defensive positions. As for Israel, it is following the developments intently. 
[end recording] 


MA'ARIV REPORTS LRAQI PROMISE OF AID TO JORDAN 
TA280734 Tel Aviv MA'ARIV in Hebrew 28 Nov 80 pp 1, 11 


{Arab affairs correspondent ‘Oded Granot report: "Iraq Has Promised Husayn Aerial 
Support if Jordan is Attacked by the Syrians") 


[Text] Iraqi President Saddam Husayn promised King Husayn to place the Iraqi Air Force 
at his disposal if the Jordanians are in a position where they have to ward off a Syrian 
military offensive. Western sources who followed the talks held by the Jordanian and 
Iraqi leaders in the framework of the llth summit believe that such a promise was indeed 
given. 


It has been learned that Saddam Husayn and the Jordanian monarch met in the course of 
the summit to discuss the significance and implication of the concentrations of Syrian 
forces along the Jordanian border. The Iraqis, grateful for the aid Jordan has extended 
to them in their war against Iran, have promised to not stand idle if Jordan is attacked 
from the north. 


Within the framework of those promises, they announced that they would permit the Iraqi 
MiG-23's (stationed on Jordanian soil) to be operated by Iraqi pilots against the Syrians. 


The assessment is that the principal aid Iraq can extend to Jordan is based on air 
power, since the Iraqi ground forces are in the main deployed on the Iranian front. 


King Husayn evaded the question on the possibility of Iraqi aid during the new conference 
he held last night, however, observers believe that the self-assurance he demonstrated 
("We will fight for every grain of sand of our soil, if necessary") stems, among other 
things, from both his recognition of the fact that the Jordanian Army can withstand the 
Syrians, but also from the Traqi backing, and most probably also the support of Saudi 
Arabia.
