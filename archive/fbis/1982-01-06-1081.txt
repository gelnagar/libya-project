LIBYA

RESOLUTIONS OF GENERAL PEOPLE'S CONGRESS 


LD051630 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1302 GMT 5 Jan 82 


{Resolutions of the seventh session of the General People's Congress, held at the Al-Amal 
Hall in Tripoli, read by the assistant secretary general of the General People's Congress -- 
name not given; live] 


[Text] The following are the resolutions of the popular congresses in its third regular 
session for 1391 from the date of the death of the prophet, corresponding to 1983, 
formulated by the general meeting of the popular congresses, people's committees, trade 
unions, associations and vocational cooperatives and the General People's Congress in its 
seventh regular session from 7 to 10 Rabi' al-Awwal, 1391, from the date of the death of the 
prophet, corresponding to 2 to 5 January 1982: 


I. Review of the decisions and recommendations of the basic popular congresses in their 
3rd regular session from 15 Muharram to 15 Safar 1390, from the date of the death of the 
prophet, corresponding to 22 November to 23 December 1980, formulated by the general 
meeting of the popular congresses, the people's committees, trade unions, associations and 
vocational cooperations and the General People's Congress, in its 6th regular session for 
1390, from the date of the death of the prophet, corresponding to 1980. 


The basic popular congresses reviewed the aforementioned decisions and recommendations 
which have been implemented and have expressed their appreciation for the progress in the 
implementation of these decisions and recommendations, especially in the sphere of 
development and have followed up the efforts exerted by the people's committee -- the 
implementation instrument -- in various fields and have stressed the need for continuation 
of work to complete that which has not yet been completed. 


II. Internal Policy 


1. To continue to implement the development and administrative budgets for 1390, from 
the date of the death of the prophet, corresponding to 1981. 


A. The basic popular congresses express their satisfaction with the progress in implementing 
the development plan despite the difficulties whichhave confronted it as a result of world 
economic developments -- along with the local ramifications. They draw attention for the 
need to curtail administrative and consumer expenditures in order to reduce the effect of 

the world economic crisis on the progress of the development plan projects. 


B. The basic popular congresses stress the need to continue work to increase the 
participation of national companies and establishments in implementing the development 
plan and to work to overcome the difficulties facing the implementation process. 


C. The basic popular congresses approve the extra expenditure spent on the development 
plan projects during the fiscal year 1390, from the date of the death of the prophet, 
corresponding to 1981. 


2. To prepare the development and administrative budgets for 1391, from the date of the 
death of the prophet, corresponding to 1982. The basic popular congresses debated the 
draft development and administrative budgets for 1391, from the date of the death of the 
prophet, corresponding to 1982, and decided upon the following:

A. To approve the development budget to a limit of 2.6 billion dinars together with an 
emphasis on the recommendations in the memorandum debated by the popular congresses, 
particularly the following: to refrain from adding any new financial burden except for 
productive projects and then only within the limits of available resources; to ensure 

that importing companies and establishments set up and operate productive projects 
particularly in the industrial sector; to continue to exploit available financial 

resources and to work to develop them to achieve the strategy and targets of the development 
plan; to lay down guidelines and strict regulations for the use of foreign manpower. 


B. To approve the draft administrative budget for 1391 from the date of the death of the 
prophet, corresponding to 1982, up to the limit of 1,255 billion dinars, emphasizing 

that the executive bodies curtail administrative expenditures and work according to the 
recommendations set in the memorandum debated by the basic popular congresses, which will 
ensure savings in general expenditures of public money. 


3. Agricultural sector: 


A. Study of the agricultural projects. While affirming their determination to realize 
the green revolution, the popular conferences stress the need for the establishment of 
agricultural projects on the basis of scientific studies and for aiming them at the 
realization of prosperity, meeting needs and protection of natural resources. 


B. Study of private farms and their lack of adherence to agricultural guidance. The 

popular conferences decided to review the agricultural situation through the organization 

of the agricultural activity in a manner which will ensure complete adherence to agricultural 
guidance pertaining to the cultivation of crops wherever such cultivation is feasible 
according to the society's need for these crops, provided that the agricultural conferences 
play their role in realizing this. 


C. Study of the policy of marketing agricultural and animal produce. The popular conferences 
approve the proposals set in the memorandum and stress the need to draw up a precise and 
effective marketing method to ensure the selling of agricultural and animal produce. 


D. Study of subterranean water and water resources. Draft law on water resources. The 
basic popular conference decided to adopt the proposed law after taking into consideration 
the remarks made in this respect by the basic popular conferences. 


E. 1. Draft law on the protection of pastureland and woodlands. The basic popular 
conferences decided to adopt the law after taking into consideration the remarks made 
in this respect by the popular conferences. 


2. Draft law on agricultural inspection. The basic popular conferences decide to adopt 
the law after considering the remarks made in this respect by the basic popular conferences. 


4. Public service sector: the policy pertaining to manpower and ways to derive maximum 
benefit from it. The basic popular conferences discussed the manpower question and 
decided to following: 


A. Work for the realization of the objectives and strategy of the development of the 
manpower in accordance with the development plan for 1981-85. 


B. To continue to work to reduce the number of those working for the popular administrative 
machinery and to direct them toward working in establishments, companies and other production 
places. 


C. To limit the employment of the non-national working force, particularly in the 
administrative machinery. 


D. To increase the work participation of all community elements,

E. The need to introduce mechanization and calculators, particularly in the popular 
administrative machinery. 


F. To work to simplify and shorten administrative procedures. 


G. To stress the necessity of using professional identity cards and to ask the bodies 
concerned to take the necessary measures in this regard. 


H. To prepare a study on reducing the number of administrative units, taking into consi- 
dieration that the number of municipalities will not be reduced until after completion of 
the transformation plan for 1981-85. 


5. Services sector: 


A. To reconsider education policy: The basic popular congresses decide the following: 
the need to reconsider the education system now functioning in the Socialist People's 
Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah; to create a new educational structure for the jamahiri [masses] 
community that realizes the demands of this community and is in line with its 
aspirations; early specialization and the establishment of specialized secondary schools 
instead of the current secondary schools, linking them with similar specialization in 
university education; to concentrate on the basis curricula required by the nature of 
specialization at every stage of education; to work to enable students to choose fields 
suited to their wishes and mental and physical capabilities; to pay special attention to 
Arabic, it being the principal container of the Arab personality; the integral responsibility 
of educational establishment and that of the family and the mosque; the realization of 
school militarization. 


B. Facilitating the obtaining of transporation means: the basic popular congresses 
discussed this subject and stressed the importance of transportation means and work to make 
it available in its various forms, giving priority to public transportation and establishing 
the basic [as heard] banks for this purpose. The economic solutions which are bound to 
preserve the national wealth and not to squander it must be taken into consideration. 

The specialized people's committees must take the necessary measures to realize these 

aims, including consideration of the costs when preparing (?estimates) for materials and 
services. 


C. Health service and organizing the dispensing of medicines: The basic popular congresses 
approve the following: To operate according to the system proposed by the General People's 
Committee for Health in its memorandum submitted to the basic popular congresses on 
organizing the dispensing of medicines; to approve restraint in the horizontal expansion 

{as heard] in the medical services sector and to work to improve the quality of the services 
sector and to improve the quality of the services; to support the program to develop the 
manpower operating in the health sphere; to concentrate on preventative medicine and to 
improve the environment and the basic health care programs. 


D. Oil: The basic popular congresses stress the need to preserve oil resources and the 
gains achieved by the Great 1 September Revolution in this sphere. They affirm complete 
readiness to accept all ensuing sacrifices and to face all consequnces. They also stress 
the need to work according to what was agreed during the third regular session between 

22 November and 22 December 1980. 


E. Social welfare: The basic popular congresses approve the memorandum put before them, 
providing that implementation is within the framework of available resources and does not 
entail increased financial burdens.

6. The armed people: 


A. The basic popular congresses approve that the defense budget should allocate funds for 
militarization. 


B. The basic popular congresses approve the preparation of a draft bill to implement the 
armed people's [word indistinct] and to submit it for debate at the forthcoming sessions so 
that it can be approved. 


C. To honor our martyrs who contributed to the liberation of the people of Chad and 

our other heroes being martyred in defense of freedom everywhere, according to the people's 
authority declaration, the basic popular congresses decided to collect 5 dinars from every 
Libyan Arab, male and female, to be used for the care of the martyrs' families, in addition 
to the honor, privileges and attention due them in accordance with current legislation. 


7. General issues: 


A. To issue resolutions by the popular committees without referring to the basic popular 
congresses. The basic popular congresses reaffirm the contents of the declaration of the 
establishment of the people's authority -- that the authority is for the people alone; 
the people exercise authority by way of the popular congresses, the popular committees, 
trade unions, the federations, the professional bodies and the General People's Congress. 
The law will define these organizations’ functioning. The popular committees will issue 
the executive decisions connected with the resolutions and recommendations of the popular 
committees and the laws in force. 


B. Draft law relating to the reorganization of the Supreme Court: The basic popular 
congresses decided to enact the law taking into consideration remarks made in this connection. 


C. Reports of the administrative trade union: The basic popular congresses have looked 

into the report submitted by the administrative supervision body. They realize the magnitude 
of the problems faced by the executive organs resulting from the great aspirations of the 
Libyan Arab people and reaffirm the need to work to overcome all difficulties and 
obstructions, stressing the need to bring to about all those who do not carry out their 

work as they should and to make efforts to avoid this. 


D. Draft law of marriage and divorce: The basic popular congresses endorse the enacting 
of the required legislation taking into account the remarks made by the basic popular 
congresses. 


E. Traffic law: The basic popular congresses aporove the enacting of this legislation 
and entrust the General People's Committee for Justice to prepare the endorsement. 


F. Law on the protection of the environment: The Basic popular congresses approve the 
enacting of this law, taking into account the remarks made in this connection. 


G. Law on organizing the use of ionized radiation: The basic popular congresses approve 
the enacting of the law taking into account the remarks made in this connection. 


H. Carrying out of autopsies: The basic popular congresses approve the enacting of a 
legislation taking into account the following: Except for carrying out an autopsy to 
ascertain the nature of an unknown disease, dissection for scientific purposes will be 
carried out according to the will of the diseased or with his family's approval. Use 
of the organs of the deceased for transplant purposes will be made according to his own 
will.

8. Professionai and productive congresses: The basic popular congresses confirm the 
necessity of submitting the recommendations of the professional congresses regarding 

the policy of society of those recommendations which entail commitments on society to the 
basic popular congresses so that they can be discussed and appropriate measures taken. 

The basic popular congresses reaffirm the speeding up of the development of higher studies 
in higher institutes and universities in the Jamahiriyah. They are of the view that the 
sending of students on educational missions abroad will be confined to the minimum except 
in the case of branches of studies not available in the Jamahiriyah. Funds should be 
allocated for this purpose. 


III. Foriegn Policy 


1. The basic popular congresses endorse the report on foreign policy, presented by the 
People's Bureau for Foreign Liaison. 


2. The basic popular congresses approve the agreements the Socialist People's Libyan 
Arab Jamahiriyah concluded or joined and which are detailed in the attached list. 


3. The basic popular congresses underline what the leader presented on the 12th anniversary 
of the Great al-Fatih Revolution regarding the policy of neutrality and they agree to act 
accordingly. 


4. The basic popular congresses agree on popular moves to realize unity between the 
Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah and sister Syria. They stress: 


A. The unified revolutionary leadership will start setting in motion all necessary measures 
for the immediate realization of unity. 


B. The need to set up a unified command for the armed forces in the two countries. 


C. To call the General People's Congress and the Syrian People's Assembly to meet at 
one pan-Arab conference. 


D. To set up one executive organ containing the Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah 
General People's Committee and the Syrian Council of Ministers. 


5. The basic popular congresses underline their resolutions which were formulated by the 
General People's Congress at its extraordinary session on 28 September 1981, regarding the 
Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah territory, which is a territory for all Arabs; 

they stress the importance of continuing to accept Arab volunteers to train them and 

prepare them for the battle of destiny against the enemies of the Arab people and the 

Arab nation with all available facilities in light of the measures taken for arming the people. 


6. The basic popular congresses agree to the conclusion of special agreements with states 
regarding the extradition of criminals, saboteurs and terrorists. 


7. The basic popular congresses decide to work for supporting the Chad people economically 
in line with the ties of fraternity between the two peoples, provided the bilateral agree- 
ments and treaties are honored. 


iV.- New Business 


1. To cement the authority of the people, the popular congresses decide that the following 
are to be observed when making legislation: 


A. The basic popular congresses will decide on the idea and subject of the law.

B. The General People's Committee with its specialized bodies will prepare the bills. 


C. The General People's Committee and its specialized bodies will rewrite the draft law 
and resubmit it to the basic popular conferences. 


D. After the draft law is adopted by the basic popular conference, the General People's 
Congress will undertake the task of putting it in its final form and promulgate it. 


E. Exempt from the above will be the laws pertaining to budgets. 

2. With regard to tranferring funds of custodianship to people's ownership, the basic 
popular conferences discussed the question of custodianship and the funds of the 
enemies of the people, who have taken refugee abroad, and have decided the following: 
A. All funds and property placed under custodianship will revert back to the people. 
B. All funds and property of the enemies of the people who have absconded outside the 
Jamahiriyah and who have refused to return in spite of repeated calls will also revert 
back to the people. 

C. Persons who have fled abroad will not be entitled to any compensation for the funds 
and property which has been reverted back to the people in accordance with the provisions 
of the law to be promulgated in this regard. 


D. TheGeneral People's Congress will issue resolutions pertaining to the specification 
of such funds and properties and names of their owners as governed by this law. 


E. It has been agreed to promulgate the law in accordance with the above. 
3. With regard to the drafting of specifications and conditions that should be met by 
thos. nominated for membership of the popular committees, anyone nominated for membership 
of the popular committees must have the following qualifications: 
A. Adherence to the revolutionary principles in words and deeds; B. Adherence to moral 
norms; C. Experience in the field to which he is nominated; D. Educational qualifications 
compatible with the demands of the position he is nominated to occupy.
