LIBYA

LAMBERZ BODY LEAVES TRIPOLI; CEREMONIES HELD 
LDO71926Y Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1830 GMT 7 Mar 78 LD 
{Summary] A Libyan aircraft left Tripoli this evening carrying the body of Werner 
Lamberz, who died yesterday in a helicopter accident. There was an official sendoff 
at the airport. Commander in Chief of the Armed Forces Abu Bakr Yunis Jabir was there. 


Officials Attend Funeral 


LDO71924Y Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1830 GMT 7 Mar 78 LD 


{Summary] Foreign Secretary 'Ali 'Abd as-Salam at-Turayki and Industry and Minerals 
Secretary Jadallah 'Azzuz at-Talhi left this evening for Berlin at the head of a Libyan 
delegation to offer condolences on the death of Werner Lamberz. They will alsc attend 
the official funeral. 


rT 4 NORTH APTCA 


Jallud Oration 
LDO81530Y Tripoli JANA in English 1405 GMT 8 Mar 78 LD 


[Text] Tripoli, March 8th, JANA--Speaking on the occasion of the tragic death of the 
secretary of the Central Committee of the German Socialist Unity Party, Werner Lamberz, 
and his accompanying delegation, and the death of the Jamahiriyah secretary of liaison 
and the head of protocol at the Foreign Secretariat, and the airline crew, all of whom 
perished when their aircraft crashed, S/Major Jallud said that those who have died are 
martyrs in the cause of improving international relations. 


S/Major Jallud began reciting verses from the holy Koran which stressed that man is but a 
transitory voyager on earth. He then spoke about the German secretary general, whose 
loss will be felt not only in Germany but here in the Jamahiriyah as well. "We felt as 

if we have always known our brother Lamberz. We shall miss a great revolutionary--a 
Jamahiriyah revolutionary.” Referring to the bereaved Libyan families he said: "Before 
we present our condolences to them we have to console ourselves and the whole people of 
the Jamahiriyah on the loss of great, devoted and active members of the Jamahiriyah." 


At the end of his oration, S/Major Jallud reassured that there is no greater honor than to 
die for one's country as a martyr on a mission for the people.
