LIBYAN-TUNISIAN AFFAIRS SHOW SIGNS OF IMPROVEMENT 
Parie AL-NAHAR AL~'ARABI WA AL-DUWALI in Arabic 29 Dec 80-4 Jan 81 p 15 


[Text] Tunisian-Libyan relations have begun to improve after last month's crisis. 

An official diplomatic source confirmed to AL -NAHAR AL~'ARABI WA AL-~DUWALI what was 
broadcast in Libya about an invitation extended by the Tunisian Foreign Ministry to 
Dr ‘Abd-al-Salam al-Turayki, secretary of the Libyan Foreign Ministry, to visit 
Tunis--an invitation arising out of the conviction of official Tunisian circles that 
any direct contact will lead to positive results for relations between the two 
countries. 


This relaxation of tensions between Tunis and Tripoli comes after intense discussions 
undertaken by the Libyan charge d'affaires in Tunis with officials in the Tunisian 
Foreign Ministry. These occurred after the Tunisian Government had delivered two 
weeks earlier a memorandum to the World Court in the Hague responding to the Libyan 
viewpoint which had been transmitted in a Memorandum of the Tripoli Government. 


The two regimes previously presented their viewpoints on the point of contention 
over the continental shelf in the Gulf of Qabis in two memoranda which they sent to 
the International Court of Justice last May 30. 


On the occasion of the Arab information ministers’ conference held two weeks ago in 
Tunis, the Tunisian minister, al-Tahir Belkhoja met with his Libyan counterpart. 
They agreed to step up meetings and talks between officials of the two regimes after 
the Libyan foreign minister's visit to Tunis. 


The new rapprochement between Tunis and Tripoli has arrested the tension noticed 

recently which showed up in reports published by the Tunisian Foreign Ministry on 
18 November 1980, that Libyan troops were massing on the Tunisian border and that 
Libyan army patrols had more than once penetrated into Tunisian territory. 


On another matter, sources in the Arab League affirmed to AL-NAHAR AL~‘ARABI WA 
AL-DUWALI that it was Chadli Klibi, secretary general of the Arab League, who had 
helped to achieve this new rapprochement by his efforts to straighten out the current 
Arab situation. It appears that the rapprochement between the two countries is 
likely to come about. This is shown through three new indicators that emerged last 
week, They are: 

















1) Most of the members of the Tunisian parliament during Foreign Ministry budget 
discussione called for settlement of differences with Libya and restoration of 
relations to their normal state on clear bases. 


2) The end of the internal struggle in Chad, which in recent months had contributed 
more than any other factor to troubled relations as a result of the position the 
Tunisian government took in opposition to the Libyan support for the central regime 
in Chad, 


3) The Franco-Libyan rapprochement that had begun with the visit to Paris of Ahmad 
al-Shahati, the chief of the Bureau of Foreign Liaison in the General People's 
Congress. After his talks with Francois—Poncet he announced that relations between 
Paris and Tripoli were moving in a positive direction. 


Accordingly, all the internal and external indicators point to a normalization in 
relations between both countries, Al-Turayki's expected visit to Tunis will be a 
decisive factor in this course if both sides can arrive at practical solutions to 
the comple, problems that still exist between them.

rock massifs surround the city, which is concentrated within a relatively 
confined space. So that airplanes, especially large ones, have to touch down 
almost in a nose-dive onto the rather cramped airfield situated at the edge of 
the city. Barren, rugged mountains like those around Kabul--at the most, relieved 
here and there by bleak highland steppes--are also just about the only thing 
which the traveler has seen on his flight into the Afghan metropolis, no matter 
whether he is coming from Teheran, Delhi, or Tash*ken.: He has spotted neither 
railway lines nor freeways nor broad, navigable .ivers, and aside from a few 
towns only the small scattered settlements, all of which seem to be clinging
