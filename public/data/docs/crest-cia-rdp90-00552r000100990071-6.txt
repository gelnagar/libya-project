Summary (AI): A Washington Star article from 31 July 1980 reports on the Billy Carter–Libya affair: Billy Carter reportedly told Justice Department investigators he had received low-classification State Department cables about his September 1978 Libya trip, and the White House said the president did not recall showing them to him. The story covers the political fallout and a House inquiry.
• Billy Carter told Justice Department investigators that the president had given him State Department cables about his Libya trips and that he kept them at his home in Buena Vista, Georgia.
• The White House said the only cables the president had discussed with Billy Carter were low-classification US Embassy cables from Libya on his September 1978 trip.
• The House Judiciary Committee and Foreign Affairs Committee approved a resolution of inquiry into Billy Carter's activities.
• Cable traffic on the trip consisted of six to eight messages, with the lowest security classifications.
• 1978-09: Billy Carter's first trip to Libya, covered by six to eight US Embassy cables, reported by William L. Eagleton Jr., senior US diplomat in Tripoli, as having no negative fallout.
