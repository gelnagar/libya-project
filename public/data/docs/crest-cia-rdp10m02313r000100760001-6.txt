Summary (AI): A 1982 CIA reading-room package with a draft National Security Decision Directive on economic measures toward Libya. It proposes banning US imports of Libyan crude oil and requiring licenses for exports of US goods and technology to Libya, with some exceptions. It has no Tripoli-specific sites, people in Tripoli, or killings.
• Draft NSDD would prohibit import into the US of crude oil from Libya.
• Draft would require validated licenses for exports of US goods and technology to Libya, except medicine, medical supplies, and agricultural commodities.
• General policy would deny licenses for export to Libya of nationally controlled goods and oil and gas technology not readily available outside the US.
• Draft includes provisions on third-country companies, reexports, and pre-existing binding contracts.
• Draft directs contingency planning on Libyan and Soviet reactions and continues a Task Force on Libya co-chaired by the Secretary of State and the National Security Adviser.
• Memo dated 2 March 1982 records the DCI opting for the language in paragraph 4a.
• 1982-02-25: NSC meeting at which recommendations on US policy toward Libya were made to the President
