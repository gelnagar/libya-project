PALESTINIANS SUPPORT CALL FOR MECCA LIBERATION 
PM281341 Beirut AL-SAFIR in Arabic 25 May 82 p 12 


[Unattributed report: "Three Palestinian Leaders Welcome al-Qadhdhafi Call 
for Liberation of Mecca and Medina"] 


[Text] Three Palestinian resistance leaders have welcomed Col Mu'ammar 
al-Qadhdhafi's call for the liberation of the holy places in Mecca and Medina. 
They maintain that there can be no liberation unless the Arab reactionary 
forces are defeated. Abu al-‘Abbas, assistant secretary general of the 
Palestine Liberation Front, has stated that the "call for the liberation of 
Mecca and Medina from Saudi political hegemony has accurately pinpointed 

the main danger threatening the Arab nation, that danger being the Arab 
reactionaries headed by the Saudi rulers." 


Taysir Qubba'ah, member of the Political Bureau of the Popular Front for the 
Liberation of Palestine, has stated that "there is no doubt that 

Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi's statement on the occasion of the anniversary of 
the prophet's ascension and night journey, calling for an end to Saudi control 
of Mecca and Medina and for the march to proceed from there to Jerusalen, 
tully expresses the opinion of all honest people, Arab nationalists, and 
progressive Muslims." 


iie added: "We consider it to be a call for struggle against reactionary 
policies at all levels. The Saudi regime is a strong and essential ally of 
imperialism. Saudi Arabia supported the Egyptian regime as far back as the 
early seventies and paved the way for that regime to strengthen its relations 
with imperialism." 


Taysir Khalid, member of the Political Rureau of the Democratic Front for the 
Liberation of Palestine, stated that "we consider the Jamahiriyah's battle 
against Saudi reactionary policies to be a real battle, and we treat it with 
a high degree of responsibility on the grounds that it is a hostile policy 
humiliatingly subservient to the aggressive U.S. policy." 


He added: "The front will always be in the front line in the battle against 
Saudi policy, which is a threat to the nation's present and future and 
particularly to the present and future of the Palestinian struggle."

"LE FIGARO' ENVOY VIEWS SOUTH LEBANON SITUATION 
PM211455 Paris LE FIGARO in French 12 May 82 p 4 
[Dispatch by Jean-Jacques Leblond: "Resurgence of Lebanese Nationalism"] 


[Text] Beirut--"He who touches my mosque strikes me on the head!" The voice 
of the Shi'ite leader of this little village in southern Lebanon is gentle 
but firm. It seems that here the spiritual dimension of the conflict arms 
eloquence with a redoubtable power. It would be rash to disregard it when 

it is based on a community of over 1 million, and, what is more, when it has 
just become aware of its power over this land scorched twice over by Israeli 
bombs and Palestinian shells. 


Resignation was in order in the past. Today it is no longer current. From 
1978 the "movement of the disinherited,'"' which became Amal (hope)--a powerful 
politicomilitary organization--has developed in this pounded Lebanon. Its 
leader was Musa al-Sadr, the missing imam, who was abducted in Rome and 
probably executed afterward by his Libyan kidnappers. From the outset, Amal, 
driven by a vigorous proselytims, set itself two goals: the defense of 
southern Lebanon, 90 percent of whose population are Shi'ites, and the 
reconciliation between communities already proposed by the Maronite Christians. 
It is on the basis of these two principles that Amal asserts its identity--a step 
which raises in particular the problem of the "stationing" of Palestinians in 
this south which they have virtually annexed. 


The movement has since spread. It covers three traditional regional areas 
(the south, the Beirut suburbs and al-Biqa'). It is composed of 14 sections 
which come under the Politburo and comprise some 10,000 militiamen, divided 
into self-defense units. It can mobilize more, but an executive committee 
member stated: "We do not want our arm to become more powerful than our 
heart!" 


"We no longer wish to fight among Lebanese," he added, "but to fight for 
Lebanese unity. We are hostile to the Palestinians only insofar as they wish 
to substitute their laws for those of our country. It is not for them but 
for our national army to 'protect' our border with Israel. That is why we 
demand its deployment in the south and that it take over from the garrisons 
of the PLO and its progressive allies."
