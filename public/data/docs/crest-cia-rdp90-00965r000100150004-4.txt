Summary (AI): A Washington Post column (23 December 1982) reports that Libya's Qaddafi and former CIA agent Edwin P. Wilson supplied hundreds of disguised "camera gun" assassination weapons, with 250 to 380 cameras allegedly sent to Tripoli for conversion. The account comes from a former Wilson associate and is denied by Wilson through his attorney.
• Allegedly more than 300 'assassination' camera guns could be in terrorists' hands; each capable of firing two .22 bullets through the lens.
• Between 250 and 380 cameras were reportedly sent to Tripoli to be converted into weapons.
• Wilson allegedly deposited more than $200,000 in a Swiss bank account for the inventor, who had no access.
• Wilson was on trial in Alexandria, Va., on weapons-smuggling charges.
• 1978-04: Wilson gave an associate a list of assassination devices for Libya; the associate and a companion flew to Tampa to arrange a prototype camera gun.
• 1978-05-21: The inventor and the associate took the same flight to Libya carrying the working camera gun for Qaddafi.
