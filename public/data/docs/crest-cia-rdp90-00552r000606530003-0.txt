Summary (AI): A 1980 Chicago Tribune article, reproduced in a CIA reading-room file, reports that CIA documents describe a multimillion-dollar Libyan effort to win favor with Americans through business deals, trips to Libya and lobbying. It concerns U.S. politics and Libyan influence-buying rather than conditions inside Tripoli.
• CIA documents reportedly outline a multimillion-dollar Libyan effort to influence U.S. policy and opinion, sent to the Justice Department.
• Organizer of one effort said he spent more than $225,000.
• Libya's efforts reportedly fell into three areas: lucrative brokerage offers for oil, peanuts, soybeans and wheat; trips to Libya; and business deals offered to Americans.
• Senate subcommittee of nine senators was to decide after Oct. 4 whether to examine Libyan influence-buying further.
• Justice Department sent a 'letter of inquiry' to Rev. Jesse Jackson the previous week.
• 1978-10: Richard Shadyac arranged an expense-paid junket to Libya in October 1978, days after Billy Carter visited the country
