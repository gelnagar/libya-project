LIBYA

MILITARY ENROLLMENT TERMS FOR STUDENTS OUTLINED 
LD141535 Tripoli JANA in English 1444 GMT 14 Nov 80 


["Announcement of the Acceptance of Arab Students at Different Military Colleges in the 
Jamahiriyah"--JANA headline ] 


[Text] Tripoli, 14 Nov (JANA)--Within the framework of the call for holy war (jihad) to 
confront military defiances and the fierce onslaught faced by our Arab nation from im- 
perialism, Zionism and Arab reaction, and in affirmation of the right of every free Arab 
to train on arms to participate in the honour of defence of the Arab nation, liberating 
her holy places from all kinds of imperialist foreign existence, working to achieve her 
comprehensive unity and to make the day of establishment of the armed Muslim people nearer, 
the General Staff Command of the Libyan Arab Armed Forces announces the opening of en- 
rollment for Arab students to join the following colleges: 


1) The military college 

2) The naval college 

3) Air force college 

4) Air defence college 

5) The military college for girls 


The following conditions should be observed by the applicant: 


1) To be an Arab 

2) His age to be no less than 17 years and not over 25 years 

3) To have the general secondary certificate or any other equivalent certificate 
4) To pass the scheduled medical examination 

5) Not to be sentenced for a felony or an immoral misdemeanor. 


The applicant should submit a written form enclosing the following documents: 


1) Educational qualification certificate 

2) Good conduct certificate 

3) Photostat of passport, or travel document or identity card 
4) Four photographs 

5) Address in full 


General Information 


1) The applicant is granted a monthly allowance throughout the period of study. 
2) The applicant will have a second lieutenant rank on graduation.

3) The applicant will be subject to all military laws and regulations applied in the 

armed forces. 

4) If the applicant cannot obtain some required documents he can submit the application 

on condition that he undertakes to complete the documents at a later date. 

5) Applicants are submitted to the General Staff Command or to the nearest camp or any 
Libyan Embassy or People's Bureau abroad. 

6) The places mentioned in the previous item should be referred to for further clarification. 


[signed] General Staff Command 

EDITORIAL CALLS REAGAN FOREIGN POLICY ‘'FASCIST' 

LD171818 Tripoli JANA in English 0912 GMT 17 Nov 80 
("Political Editor on Reagan Fascist Policy"--JANA headline] 


[Text] Tripoli, 17 Nov (JANA)--Our political editor commented on the fascist policy of 
U.S. President-elect Ronald Reagan reiterated during his election campaign. Regarding 

the position of the American administration since the beginning of the U.S. imperialist 
attack against the Arab nation, he said the policy of the step by step initiated by Kissinger 
was the first te violate Arab sovereignty, threaten Arab destiny and disparage the national 
confrontation front. This was a preliminary step for the downfall of the Arab nation 

with all its fronts and strength. This is the policy pursued by the Carter administration 
at an early stage of the imperialist tide and which was characterised by encirclement and 
containment. It led to the downfall of many areas and positions at the hands of soldiers 
of the reactionary regimes whose duty turned out to be the handing of the Arab nation's 
keys to the Pentagon. 


Despite everything the Carter era had stood for in the way of terrorism, flagrant inter- 
vention in the affairs of other countries and even direct military occupation of several 
Arab areas, Ronald Reagan insists that his administration will continue its fascist 
terrorist trends in the international area and in its dealings with other countries. This 
was clearly demonstrated in his statement and speeches during the recent presidential 
campaign. 


According to ‘Reagan the Arab Peninsula in particular, is the region which should serve as 
an advanced strategic position for U.S. military presence in the Middle East. He is 

also advocating that the Panama Canal should return under American control and impose U.S. 
control over the Carribean Sea by force. 


Reagan, moreover, is promising direct intervention in what Latin America had been witnessing 
[as received] alongside the dictatorial regimes in Chile, Salvador, Bolivia, Argentina and 
especially Nicaragua where Reagan has threatened to halt its progressive revolution. 


Some have interpreted these terrorist pronuncements as being mere rhetoric designed to 
win votes in the struggle for presidential power. Yet this interpretention has proven 
ill founded. The post-election victory (?period) has been characterised by intensive 
meetings of Reagan with military leaders and Pentagon officials known for their extreme 
views and (?commitment) to the manufacture of the neutron bomb. 


Reagan's leanings are based on a policy that relies fascist militarism in all walks of 
life including the manarement of the economy. In fact, Reagan has decided on proving a 
direct link between U.S. oil companies and the Department of Defense, slowly decreasing 
the attachment of the companies at present with the Department of Energy.

Reagan has also called for reconsideration of the SALT agreement signed with the Soviet 
Union, even hinting at their total abrogation. He has chosen to employ as his advisors 
military specialists known for their involvement in war, intervention and espionage 
activities. 


The looming danger to world peace and security heaalded by the new American president 

which threaten the freedom and independence of people has been met with considerable cri- 
ticism even inside America. U.S. unions expressed extreme anxiety at Reagan's election who 
is considered by the working masses as nothing but an organic extention to the capitalist 
police state. It is elementary that the Arabs should have a stand. We, the revolutionary 
Arabs, do have a daring and definite stance vis-a-vis the U.S. policy, not only before Reagan 
officially announed its military terrorist character but also when the American administra- 
tion cloaked itself with its traditionally hostile position towards the Arab nation. 


Policy has changed from the time of John Foster Dulles, whom Reagan intends to bring back 
into the White House and whose policy distinguished itself in hostility and deception, to 

the present with Reagan's plan to consolidate U.S. military presence which thereby deals a 
blow to the Arab nation. As if U.S. bases across the triangle of danger and terror in Egypt, 
Somali, Oman etc. is not sufficient (?for) Reagan. He still finds it necessary that American 
should have complete protection for its forces in the Arab region.
