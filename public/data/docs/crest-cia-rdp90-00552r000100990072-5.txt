Summary (AI): A Washington Post article (as reproduced in a CIA reading-room copy) reports that President Carter discussed low-classification State Department cables on Libya with his brother Billy Carter, who had claimed to Justice Department investigators that he received the cables from the president. Billy Carter's Libyan ties and his 1978 and 1979 trips are the subject of a congressional inquiry. The document is about US domestic politics, with little on Libya itself.
• The White House acknowledged that the president discussed low-classification State Department cables with Billy Carter concerning his first trip to Libya in September 1978.
• Jenkins said the cables were classified confidential or limited use only.
• Sawyer said Billy Carter told investigators he received State Department cable traffic from the president, and that he had them at his house.
• Justice Department records showed Billy Carter said he had received only a few gold bracelets and about $6,000 to $7,000 in expenses from the Libyans, while sources said he had got $200,000 by check.
• The House resolution of inquiry could not come to the House floor before Aug. 19, after the Democratic National Convention.
• 1978-09: Billy Carter's first trip to Libya, on which the embassy in Libya reported that the trip had gone well
• 1980-01-16: Justice Department interview of Billy Carter at his Buena Vista, Georgia home, in which he said the president gave him State Department cables
