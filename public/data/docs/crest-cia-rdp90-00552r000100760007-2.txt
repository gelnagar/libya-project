Summary (AI): A 1981 New York Times article, reprinted in a CIA reading-room copy, reports that 20 tons of C-4 plastic explosives were illegally shipped from the United States to Libya in 1977 through a deal arranged by former CIA agent Edwin P. Wilson. It says Wilson had set up facilities outside Tripoli and in Benghazi and Tobruk for making bombs.
• About 20 tons of plastic explosive (C-4) were reportedly shipped from the US to Libya in 1977.
• C-4 was manufactured in Canada, New York, California, Texas and Louisiana and shipped to Houston.
• Wilson reportedly arranged about $250,000 transferred from the United Bank of Switzerland to finance the purchase.
• Bombs made from the explosive were disguised as ashtrays, attaché cases, coat hangers and rocks.
• 1977-07: Accidental detonation of one bomb device killed three Libyans and wounded two Americans, one seriously; shortly after, Wilson began talks with a California explosives maker about obtaining a shipload of C-4. (3 killed)
• 1977-10: 40,600 pounds of C-4 explosive compound were flown from Houston to Libya.
