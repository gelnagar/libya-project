Summary (AI): A 1981 article (reprinted from The Nation) describes former CIA agents and U.S. suppliers allegedly training and arming Gaddafi's terrorists, drawing on Seymour Hersh's New York Times Magazine reporting. It describes a training lab near Tripoli and arms shipments to Libya, but places are mostly not in the city itself.
• Gaddafi reportedly wanted thousands of timers to set off explosives, ostensibly to clear Israeli mines left from the 1973 October War.
• About 40,000 lbs of RDX high explosive were reportedly shipped to Libya in 55-gallon drums marked 'industrial solvent'.
• Wilson and Terpil reportedly set up a training program in Libya in espionage, sabotage and psychological warfare, including a laboratory near Tripoli.
• Terpil reportedly had a $3.2 million contract to supply arms, explosives and torture devices to Idi Amin's Uganda.
• American Electronic Laboratories, Inc., of Falls Church, Va., reportedly agreed to build prototypes for Gaddafi's order.
• 1981-05: Reagan Administration ordered Libya's diplomatic mission in Washington to close and leave the US (stated as 'last month' relative to the article).
• 1976: Kevin Mulcahy was coaxed into the business of exporting communications and computer gear through Interarms/Inter-Technology, Inc.
