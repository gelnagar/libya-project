SRI LANKAN FOREIGN MINISTER ARRIVES FEB 18 
ID191200 Tripoli JANA in English 0930 GMT 19 Feb 79 LD 


(Text ] Tripoli, February 19, JAMAHIRIYAH NEWS AGENCY--The Sri Lankan foreign minister, 
Mr. Abdul Kadir Shahour Hameed, arrived in Tripoli yesterday afternoon on a several-day 
visit to the Jamahiriyah. Present at his reception and the accompanying delegation in 
Tripoli International Airport was the Foreign Secretariat's undersecretary, the director 
of the Asian Department in the Foreign Secretariat and a representative from the protocol 
department. In a statement to the JAMAHIRIYAH NEWS AGENCY's correspondent, Mr. Shahour 
expressed gladness for being able to visit the Jamahiriyah. He also expressed the Sri 
Lankan Government and people's deep regard for the great lst of September Revolution's 
stand towards all just causes and its active role in supporting the non-aligned movement. 


He lauded the important role undertaken by the Jamahiriyah for the establishment of a new 
world economic order. He said the Third World's peoples are united by one hope and ambi- 
tion which is freedom from foreign exploitation and domination. Im this aspect, he said 
that he lauded the Jamahiriyah's brilliant successes. Mr. Shahour praised the relations 
which linked his country to the Jamahiriyeh. He called for bolstering these relations 
and widening their scope in the interest of the two friendly peoples. At the end of his 
statement, the Sri Lankan foreign minister said he carried the Sri Lankan people's best 
wishes to Colonel Mu!ammar al-Qadhdhafi and the Libyan people who have achieved gigantic 
successes in yarious fields. 


Issues Statement, Departs 


LD210407 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1730 GMT 20 Feb 79 LD 


{Excerpts] Tripoli--Sri Lanka's foreign minister, Abdul Kadir Shahour Hameed ad-Din, 
who is the current chairman of the nonalined movement, left Tripoli this evening at the 
end of his 2-day official visit to the Jamahiriyah.

A press statement was issued at the end of the visit in which the two sides emphasized 
the need for the Palestinian people to retrieve their national rights and their right 
to self-determination in accordance with the resolutions underlined by the nonalined 
movement. 


The two sides condemned the attempts of colonialism, imperialism and other forces to 
liquidate the Palestinian question. 


When reviewing the situation in southern Africa, the two sides emphasized that the con- 
tinuation of the racist regime, represented by the white minority, continues to consti- 
tute a direct threat to international security and peace. The two sides called for the 
urgen. need to find a solution that would guarantee the freedom of the 27.5 million people 
of Zimbabwe, Namibia and South Africa and enable them to rule themselves. 


The two sides affirmed their conviction that the results achieved by the meetings of the 
Coordination Bureau in Maputo will make a positive contribution to the realization of 
this goal. . 


The two sides stressed the importance of supporting the nonalined movement so that it can 
perform its positive and effective role in the international issues. They agreed to con- 
tinue consultations and coordination between the two countries in preparation for the 
forthcoming nonalined conference in Sri Lanka and the sixth summit conference in Havana, 


The two sides reviewed with great concern the differences that have emerged within the 
movement and agreed on the need to overcome them for the sake of the unity and cohesion 
of the nonalined movement. 


The two sides welcomed the convening of the UN special session connected with disarmament. 
Their views were in agreement on the need for the nonalined states to continue their efforts 
aimed at achieving this goal. The two sides agreed that the political freedom of the 
countries of the Third World will never be complete without the achievement of economic 
freedom. They agreed to support efforts aimed at finding a new economic order. 


In connection with the Jamahiriyah's efforts to reach a peaceful solution to the bloody 
dispute between brothers in Uganda and Tanzania, the Sri Lankan foreign minister expressed 
his thanks and profound appreciation for the sincere efforts. The two sides agreed to con- 
tinue efforts within the nonalined bloc to find a peaceful solution to the problem. 


MAURITIAN PRIME MINISTER ARRIVES 19 FEB 
1D192335 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1715 GMT 19 Feb 79 LD 


(Text ] Mauritian Prime Minister Seewoosagur Ramgoolam arrived in Tripoli this evening for 
a 4-day official visit. The chairman of the General People's Committee, ‘Abd al-'Ati 
al-'Ubaydi, as well as secretaries, undersecretaries, the chief of protocol and the 
Jamahiriyah's charge d'affaires to Mauritius were at the airport to welcome the guest. 

He told JANA that he was happy to visit the Jamahiriyah, pointing out that the visit would 
contribute to the further consolidation of friendship and solidarity between the two 
countries, and added: I bring greetings from the people of Mauritius to the people and 
historic leadership of the Jamahiriyah. Concluding, he said he was deeply moved by the 
warm welcome accorded to him and the accompanying delegation.
