[Excerpts] For anyone who has any doubt whatsoever: The U.S. vote in the Security 
Council this week waseebesides the resolution itself which was passed thanks to that 
vote--one of the most serious things to befall Iraeli-U.S. relations in recent years. 
And not just for the Israeli side, but definitely for both sides in this alignment of 
forces. 


Anyone following the recent emphases in U.S. Middle East policy will discover a new 
orientation: Not only pro-Palestinian, with the philosophy of the Brookings Institution, 
which Carter adopted at the outset of his term; not only proeArab visea-vis countries 
termed "noderate" and pro-Western--but also pro-Muslim. This has been the Brzezinski 
approach ever since the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan: The crystallization of an anti- 
Soviet Muslim front (and the effort by the national security adviser to develop a romance 
with Iraq by means of far-reaching gestures involving engines for gunboats and Boeing 
aircrafte-this effort is part of that romance. 


Where the U.S. vote is concerned, at least the same impact that is attributable to the 
Begin government's actions and verbiage is attributable--without any connection to this-- 
to the Muslim school which prevails in the White House. It bears recalling that the 
recent Security Council resolution on Jerusalem was engendered at the initiative of the 
Muslim conference. Certainly ‘Arafat was part of that conference. But in the name of 
Islam, polar opposites, such as Saudi King Khalid and Iraqi President Saddam Husayn met-« 
the first meeting of its kind since the murder of the Iraqi royal house 25 years agoewand 
came out with a joint communique threatening every country represented in Jerusalem.

the fact that they no longer speak about 2 million Palestinians, or about 100 million 
Arabs, but, like AseSadat, about 800 million Muslims: Pakistanis, Bangladeshis, 
Indonesians, Malaysians, even Iranianse-everyone in sight. And all of them are, to the 
best of the White House's knowledge, anti-Soviet. 


Secretary of State Muskie came to the Security Council in order to speak in condemnation 
of the call to the countries represented in Jerusalem to pull out their embassies. Even 
if this in itself is nothing new at the UN, the argument was marked by considerable

{(Excerpt] Tel Aviv, 25 Aug--Interior Minister Yosef Burg, the head of the ministerial 
autonomy committee, said this evening that U.S. Special Ambassador Sol Linowitz will arrive 
in Israel on Sunday afternoon, 31 August, and will meet with Prime Minister Menahem Begin, 
Dr Yosef Burg and the ministerial autonomy committee. He will then travel to Cairo. 


Dr Burg, who spoke in the first annual party of the council of the Yisra'el Hatza'ir 
religious organization in the Tel Aviv Bet Sokolov, emphasized he does not know what 
Linowitz, Israel's friend, is bringing with him or what he will be carrying back from 
Israel, but that he nevertheless hopes that the autonomy talks will continue. 


"The task of opening the door is not in our hands since it was not us who closed it. 

Since 7 May Egypt has closed the door four times. Israel has made big and heavy sacrifices 
for peace, giving up both area and oil in the Sinai and the world should know this," 

Dr Burg said, noting that we are faced with a big struggle and an argument with friends 
which is sometimes more difficult than an argument with others. 


Dr Burg remarks that since May the Egyptians have increased the volume on three subjects 
at the same time: the situation in Judaea and Samaria, the settlements and Jerusalem. 
Over the last few months they placed the emphasis on Jerusalem. "I can think of a 
scenario where those same UN hands who voted or abstained on the Jerusalem issue will 
act the same when the debate will concentrate on the situation in Judaea and Samaria 
and on the settlements," he said. 


In this connection the interior minister stressed that in his UN appearance Secretary of 
State Muskie demonstrated "a distribution of work" whereby he gave us a wonderful speech 
against sanctions, but gave others an abstention. 


The minister then proceeded to firmly criticize the wording of the UN Security Council 
resolution regarding free access to the holy places in Jerusalem, noting that "such 
resolutions repel me since they indicate organized wicked hypocrisy. Where were the 
free access heroes when Jews could not go to pray at the Wailing Wall? Where else in 
the world do Muslims and Christians enjoy a freer access to holy places? The political 
lie of the whole issue lies in the very talk of free access."

TA251836 Tel Aviv ITIM in Hebrew 1755 GMT 25 Aug 80 


{Excerpts] Tel Aviv, 25 Aug--Deputy Defense Minister Mordekhay Tzipori said on Monday 
that Israel would not abandon the southern Lebanon arena to unimpeded terrorist 
activities without keeping an eye on it and the Syrians should know and accept this 
fact. This situation will continue so long as 15,000 terrorists, whose sole purpose 
is to attempt to hit Israeli citizens, remain in Lebanon, the deputy defense minister 
stated in an interview to the Israeli television Arabic program. 


Replying to a question on whether the harsh criticism voiced in Syria regarding Syrian 
military noninterference in the IDF's latest operation in Lebanon might not drive 

Syria into interference, Mr Tzipori said that he believed there were enough wise people 
in Syria who had enough problems in the domestic arena and with Iraq to deter them from 
becoming involved in an issue connected with the terrorists and their criminal attempts 
against Israel. “If logic does not work, then Israel will always be ready to supply 
the answer so that the Yom Kippur events do not recur."
