LIBYA

Authorities Ready To Allow Access to UK Detainees 
NC141721 Paris AFP in English 1717 GMT 14 May 84 


[Text] Paris, May 14(AFP) — Libya is prepared to give consular 
access to two Britons held since April 17, the day of the shooting 
incident at the Libyan Peoples Bureau (embassy) in London 
which led to the breaking of diplomatic relations between the two 
countries. 


A diplomatic source in Tripoli contacted by telephone said that 
the Libyan authorities had passed the information to Alessandro 
Quaroni, the Italian ambassador, who is looking after Britain's 
interests. 


The whereabouts or reasons for the arrest of the two Britons, 
Douglas Ledingham, who is head of the British Caledonian 
Airways office in Tripoli, and John Campbell, a businessman, 
have not been divulged. 


The source said that Mr Ledingham had been arrested at Tripoli 
Airport by the local Revolutionary Committee in reprisal for the 
siege of the Peoples Bureau by the British police following the 
shooting of a policewoman from within the building during a 
demonstration. 


Mr Quaroni was said to have asked the Libyans on several 
occasions to give the jailed Britons the same rights as Libyans 
arrested in Britain. 


The Libyan news agency JANA said yesterday that Tripoli had 
protested at “support” from Britain and the United States for 
“terrorists” who had infiltrated Libya to Mr Quaroni and the 
Belgian charge d° affaires, who represents Washington’s inter- 
ests. 


A diplomatic source in Tripoli said the authorities there had 
demanded that London prevent Libyan exiled opposition move- 
ments from carrying out political activities in Britain.
