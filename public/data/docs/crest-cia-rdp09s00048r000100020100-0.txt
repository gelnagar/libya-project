Summary (AI): A 1981 New York Times article (reprinted in CIA reading room) reports that Robert W. Hitchman, a former CIA employee, directed American pilots and mechanics maintaining helicopters for the Libyan Air Force for Edwin P. Wilson, who was a fugitive living in Libya. It also covers the Chinook maintenance contract and Hitchman's Air America and Vietnam background.
• Wilson obtained a contract in late 1979 to maintain 20 Chinook transport helicopters for the Libyan Air Force.
• British pilots and mechanics were employed to fly and maintain Libyan-owned C-130 Hercules cargo planes, which were used in Libya's intervention in Chad the previous year.
• Hitchman reportedly flew search and rescue missions over North Vietnam and helped evacuate Americans from the US Embassy roof in Saigon in 1975.
• A man identifying himself as Hitchman answered Wilson's telephone in Tripoli and cursed a reporter.
• Hitchman reportedly claimed to be a millionaire who owned an apartment complex in San Bernardino.
• 1979-11: Robert W. Hitchman arrived in Libya to work for Edwin P. Wilson's contract maintaining Chinook helicopters for the Libyan Air Force
• 1981-10-01: Wilson's Chinook maintenance contract with the Libyan government expired and, according to Americans cited, had not yet been renewed
