SKEPTICISM EXPRESSED ABOUT UNLTY PLANS BETWEEN ARAB COUNTRIES 
Kuwait AL-SITYASAH in Arabic 4 Sep 80 p 1 
[Editorial by Ahmad al-Jarallah: "Dangers of Escapist Slogans of Unity") 


[Text] Ever since unity between Egypt and Syria failed, projects of unity between 
one Arab country and another have lost their glitter. To the Arab citizen now 
unity of material interests takes precedence over unity of religion, language, 
poetry, music, hopes and dreams. 


Unity between Egypt and Syria could have succeeded had it been preceded by success- 
ful preparation. The case is the same with regard to unity between Iraq and Syria 
which was subject to inaccurate considerations. 


By the same token, the Jordanian-Syrian cooperation, which came close to unity, 
could have been firmer and could have lasted longer, had it not been for the 
feelings of caution, suspicion which plagued it as of its early days. 


Likewise, talk about unity between Libya and Tunisia, or even Libya and Algeria 
could have served practical purposes had not the calculations made in each instance 
been inaccurate. The long historical dialogue about unity between the two parts 
of Yemen is not too different. This dialogue remained so confined to the leaders 
that it could make no progress. 


Thus, talk about unity occasionally provokes laughter, because it is not based 

on serious thought and precise cal.lation. All successful experiments of unity 
on the European and American continents have shown that talk of unity will not be 
effective unless it is based from the very beginning on practical considerations 

of common economic interests. The daily life routines of candidates for unity have 
to be similar and based on identical theories, legislation and practices. 


Without such a foundation, Arab unity proposals will remain tantamount to conversa- 
tions between two deaf people, or to speeches made by leaders on ceremonial 
occasions, when they try to escape from the problems of the present by talking 
about the future. People are no longer interested in chasing a mirage or a 
beautiful dream. A mirage does not quench thirst; it makes it worse. 


Unity means a network of interests that ultimately leads to joint political 
action. Unity does not mean putting on an act in the media. No one believes

[Text] Nur al-Din Ahmad ‘Ali, Member of National Political 
Office of Polisario Liberation Front Says, "We Accept Any 
Mediation with Morocco, and We Accept Unity With It." 


Nur al-Din Ahmad ‘Ali, member of the National Political Office of the Polisario 
Liberation Front said that his visit to Kuwait is in the context of a tour that 
includes Arab capitals to explain the front's point of view in the struggle with 
Morocco. 


In an interview with AL-QABAS, Nur al-Din Ahmad ‘Ali added that the front is fully 
prepared to accept any Arab mediation whatsoever, provided that the nature of 

the struggle between the front and Morocco and not the struggle between Morocco 
and Algeria be investigated. 


He said that the OAU had agreed to hold negotiations through the Committee of 
African Arbitrators this month, but it seems that Morocco changed its mind. 


Ahmad ‘Ali said chat the front is prepared to unite with any Arab country what- 
soever. He emphasized that "Unity must take place between us as two states. 

It must not be imposed on us. We are prepared to unite with Morocco through a 
democratic, constructive dialogue, not through an accomplished fact imposed on us." 


Ahmad ‘Ali said that the purpose of the aid offered by colonialist countries 
is to preoccupy the Arab Maghrib [Morocco] with secondary questions at the 
expense of the real Arab problems. 


He affirmed that the front had advocated unity between Libya, Algeria, Morocco 

and Mauritainia, and said: “This is because we are advocates of unity. We believe 
that the Arabs will not have any strength, unless they unite. This is the basis 

of our outlook. The Sahara was divided in 1884, and remained occupied until 

Spain left it. We wanted independence, but Morocco and Mauritania wanted to 

annex the people of the Sahara, and we did not want that. We are fighting now 

so that we can assert our will." He added: “Mauritania has become convinced 

that the people of the Sahara have rights, and it has withdrawn from its agreement 
with Morocco." 


He said: "We want to put an end to Arab bloodshed. We want to have direct nego- 
tiations with Morocco so that we can establish a just and permanent solution 
similar to that which happened with Mauritania."
