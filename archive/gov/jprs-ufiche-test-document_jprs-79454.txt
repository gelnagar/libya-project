TWO FRENCH ENGINEERS KIDNAPPED BY IRAQI COMMUNISTS 


French Intervention Sought 
Paris LE MONDE in French 26 Sep 81 p 40 
/AFP despatch/ 


ftext/ Two French engineers working for SOGREAH, a French firm specializing 
in dam building and irrigation, together with their Iraqui driver, were kid- 
napped in Iraq on 22 July by members of the Iraqi Communist Party. The 
party's Central Military Bureau (PCBMC) disclosed the act in a communique 
published in Beirut on Thursday 24 September. 


In the communique the PCBMC states that the two French engineers, Maurice 
Harlet, 43, and Guy Maure, 39, were taken hostage between Baghdad and Kir- 
kuk in northern Iraq, that they are "in excellent health and very well 
treated," and that their families may contact them through the International 
Red Cross Committee. 


The PCBMC, in its communique, asks France to use its "influence with the 
Iraqi regime to obtain liberation of political prisoners in Iraq." It 
stresses that release of the engineers will "depend on the efforts made by 
all concerned to induce the Iraqi regime to accede to our demands." 


Upon questioning, a SOGREAH official refused to make any comment.

LIBYA, LEBANON FRONT RAPPROCHEMENT DISCUSSED 
Beirut THE ARAB WORLD WEEKLY in English 10 Oct 81 pp 9-10 


[Text] Last Saturday, prominent leaders of the Lebanese Front alliance attended 
an evening of songs, dances and poetry--a cultural soirée--at the theatre of the 
Casino du Liban, in Maameltein, near Jounieh. The event in itself would have 
hardly been worth mentioning in the press--except in the social pages--had the 
group which performed not been Libyan. 


The event made headlines and front pages and prompted Lebanese Front president Ca- 
mille Chamoun, who attended the evening along with Lebanese Forces’ commander 
Bechir Gemayel, to declare that no political conclusions were to be drawn from the 
fact. 


But political conclusions were drawn and the main one was that to put a Libyan team 
on stage in a region under Lebanese Front control must have taken quite a lot of 
behind-the-scenes efforts and talks. 


It all started over a month ago, when Libyan leader Colonel Moammar Qadhafi an- 
nounced that he had an initiative for the settlement of the Lebanese crisis. Where- 
upon the Lebanese Front invited him to come and see for himself the reality of the 
Lebanese situation. 


Contacts between the two sides were conducted through the Libyan representative 
in Beirut, Abdel Kader Ghougqa. 


Questions were bound to be raised for Libya is an ally of Syria and also a member 
of the Steadfastness and Confrontation Front which is headed by Syria. For this 
reason, observers stated that the Libyan intervention on the Lebanese scene must 
necessarily have taken place in coordination with Syria. 


The contacts between the Lebanese Front and Libya were a source of concern, how- 
ever, to the members of the quadripartite Arab Follow-up Committee particularly 
Kuwait and Saudi Arabia. They wondered whether the Libyan move was not the prelude 
to a freezing of the AFC. 


Indirect contacts between Libya and the Lebanese Front began several months ago 
but it was not until Libyan leader Colonel Moammar Qadhafi's recent visit to 


132 








Damascus that the contacts took a more serious turn. Observers believe the Libyan 
leader explained what he had in mind for Lebanon to Syrian President Hafez Al 
Assad and received the latter's approval. 


Since that visit, the Libyan representative to Beirut, Mr Ghouqga has met with 
Lebanese Forces commander Bechir Gemayel three times and with Lebanese Front presi- 
dent Camille Chamoun two times. Talks centered on the Libyan initiative for Leba- 
non whose details have remained undisclosed. 


If certain sides fear that the Libyan initiative may come at the expense of the 
other Arab efforts that are being done for the settlement of the Lebanese crisis, 
other sides believe there are no grounds for such fears, for the following reasons: 


1) the fact that before establishing contacts with Libya, the Lebanese Front had 
first consulted the other main sides concerned with the situation in Lebanon. 

On the other hand, contacts between the Front and Libya help settle the thorny is- 
sue of collaboration with Israel. 


2) the fact that the Lebanese Front has clearly asserted that if any direct con- 
tacts and relations are to be established with any exterior side, it will insist 
the Lebanese State be a partner in the matter so that it may reap the benefits. 


3) the fact that there is hardly any hope of success for any unilateral and in- 
dividual initiate in Lebanon, Libyan or otherwise.

SOVIET LOAN FOR RAILWAYS--Damascus, (ST)--The Syrian government has recently rati- 
fied the economic agreement between Syria and the Soviet Union. The agreement 

was signed in Moscow on May 14, 1981. According to the agreement, the Soviet Un- 
ion is to offer Syria a 10-year loan of 40 million rubles for the financing of the 
following projects: 1. building a 150 km railway between Deir ez-Zour and Abu 
Kamal; 2. building a 90 km railway between Tartous and Lattakia, and a 10 km minor 
railway leading to the Tartous Cement Factory; 3. preparing a study for governmental 
farms projects at the Euphrates' center which will cover an area of 4,000 hectares; 
4. preparing a study for the economic and technical feasibility of the use of hy- 
dro-electric resources; and 5. constructing vocational training centers and a tech- 
nical institute. According to the agreement, Syria will pay back its debt in its 
products. [Text] [Damascus SYRIA TIMES in English 10 Sep 81 p 2) 


INDUSTRIAL EXHIBIT IN LIBYA--Damascus, (ST)--The Syrian industrial exhibition in 
Libya has been very successful. Sales at it amounted to $50 million and contracts 
signed there are expected to total $500 million, according to the Minister of In- 
dustry, Dr. Hussein Qadi. The exhibition was opened on September 20. Libya, which 
has 3 million inhabitants, besides a number of foreign employees and workers, is an 
important market. It is also a centre for third-country trade, because of its 
exports to many African countries. The exhibition was held during the anniversary 
of the Libyan Revolution. It was organized in cooperation with Libyan officials 
who helped to make it a success. Libyan people and firms were encouraged to sign 
contracts for imports from Syria. The popularity of Syrian goods proved their high 
quality and the skill of Syrian craftsmanship. Exhibitors also had the chance top 
acquaint themselves with the requirements of Libyan markets and coordinate with 
trade firms there for meeting their requirements. Dr. Qadi said that the most im- 
portant products displayed were textiles, carpets, refrigerators, various metal 
products and handcraft items. [Text] [Damascus SYRIA TIMES in English 28 Sep 81
