It lists questions on Libya's internal stability, security forces, foreign orientation and the security of the bases, but gives no specific facts about Tripoli.
• The terms of reference were agreed at an IAC representatives meeting on 6 April 1956.
• Contributions were due by close of business on 27 April 1956.
• The paper is to supersede the Libya section of NIE 71-56, 'Probable Developments in North Africa' (31 August 1956 per OCR; likely 1955).
• The paper names the King and Ben Halim as key figures in the regime's power base.

THE PROBLEM 

To estimate probable developments in Libya over the next 
few years and their impact on US interests, particularly US 

base rights. 

SCOPE 

We envisage a short paper concentrating on those probable 
developments which will have a significant affect on US interests 
generally and on the status of US bases in Libya in particular, 
Although the paper will include a general survey of trends and 
probable developments in Libya and will supersede the section on 
Libya in NIE 71-5), "Probable Developments in North Africa," 31
