al-'Atiqi Saturday called for diversification of Arab investments in the

He also called for concerted Arab economic efforts to check under-

of Arab countries. 


In a statement to AL-RA'Y newspaper here, al-'Atiqi said such moves were

this context a Latin-Arab bank has been established with headquarters 
in Lima, Peru. 


Shareholders in this joint venture are four Arab states--Kuwwait, Saudi 
Arabia, the UAE, and Libya--with 60 percent shares, and six Latin 
American states--Peru, Chile, Brazil, Argentina, Ecuador, and Colombia-- 
with 40 percent shares. 


Al-"Atiqi, who left Doha for (word indistinct] Friday, said he was going 
to Manama to open a branch there for the Latin-Arab bank. He said an 
Arab was chosen to go (?’as) president of the new bank. 


He said Bahrain was chosen as the site of the bank's Gulf headquarters 
because of the strategic and geographical situation of the country in 
the region. 


Al-'Atiqi praised the close cooperation between the various economic and 
financial institutions in the Gulf countries he has so far visited and 
said this would help the economic development of those countries.
