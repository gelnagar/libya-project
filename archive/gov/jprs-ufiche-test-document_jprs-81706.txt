Gulf sources reported that the ministers who met believed that directing a 
call for the convocation of an emergency session by Arab foreign ministers 
would not receive sufficient response and would not allow participants to 
reach a unanimous resolution over the war which has begun to menace the secu- 
rity of the whole Gulf and consequently the security of the entire Arab re- 
gion. 


The sources also added that the view had been limited to the settling of the 
Arab feuds generated from the Iraqi-Iranian war, or which had been intensi- 
fied by the outbreak of this war. This would be the first task on the road 
to putting an end to the war itself. Maintaining these differences will have 
its own reflection on the level of compliance in the discussions that will 
take place during the extensive Arab ministerial meeting. 


The most prominent of the aforementioned Arab feuds is the Iraqi-Syrian feud 
that reached its peak with the severance of relations, closing of borders, 
the shutting of the Iraqi pipeline passing through Syrian territory and the 
exchanze of harsh propaganda campaigns which endanger the fate of both the 
Damascus and Baghdad regimes. 


Among these disputes is also Libya's tacit support to the Iranian side in 
the war. Baghdad has severed its relation with Tripoli in retaliation for 
this support. However, relations have become more relaxed lately though on 
a small scale. 























Then came Jordan's strong support to the lraqiside in the "Gulf War" to cause 
further strains in relations between Amman and Damascus, though this aspect 
of the disputes was not given attention in the official propaganda campaigns 
exchanged. 


Algeria has recently abandoned its passive neutrality status, which was mis- 
construed, in favor of a position of active neutrality. This new position 
resulted in its proposal to mediate between Iran, Iraq to achieve a peaceful 
end to the existing differences. Algeria also affirmed its determination to 
continue its mediation, despite the accidental killing of its ex-foreign 
minister Muhammad Al-Sadik Bin Yihia on his way to Iran to start his media- 
tion. 


Iraqi sources say that Algeria did not come up with any tangible proposals 
when it offered Baghdad the idea of mediation. Baghdad has supported and 
still supports that idea. Algeria attributes this move to its good relations 
with Iran because of its help in resolving the crisis of the American hostages 
last year.
