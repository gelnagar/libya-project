TEHRAN ENVOY VIEWS IRAQ-IRAN WAR, U.S. HEGEMONY 


LD281027 Tehran KEYHAN in Persian 14 Feb 81 p 15 


[Undated KEYHAN correspondent report: "Member of Libyan People's Bureau in Iran As-Sayyid 
al-Marghani Says ‘Iraq Has Lost $10 Billion Worth of Arms in Iran'"] 


[Text] In an exclusive interview with KEYHAN, Libyan People's Bureau member As-Sayyid 
al-Marghani, who was in Shiraz for the seminar on Arabic revolutionary literature and 
poetry, replied to questions put by Hamid ‘Attar, KEYHAN correspondent for Fars Province. 
Al-Marghani explained the Libyan Government's position regarding American imperialism and 
its influence in the Persian Gulf region and on the dispatch of milit2ry forces to the 
area. He said: "Our position in this respect is quite clear: When che American AWACS 
planes were delivered to the reactionary Saudi Government brother Mu'ammar al-Oadhdhafi 
summoned the people to a holy war against world-devouring America. Now these aircraft are 
flying above the holy Ka'bah and the tomb of the prophet. How can one accept that AWACS 
aircraft, which have gone into action with the sole purpose of destroying Islam and the 
Muslims, should overfly the land where the prophet once walked and led the people against 
oppression and tyranny. The presence of American forces in the Persian Gulf is a direct 
threat to all governments of that area, and America has no right to come here and act as 
the policeman or overlord in the Gulf. We have declared that wherever America happens to 
be we will fight her, whether in the Persian Gulf, Egypt, Somalia or the Sudan." 


On the reception given to the Islamic revolution by the leader and people of Libya 
Al-Marghani said: "The leader of the revolution and the Libyan people have made their 
position clear from the start. Brother Colonel Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi has announced not 
once but many times that Iran is the only Islamic country in the world and he has declared 
on several occasions that any attack on Iran or the Islamic government would constitute an 
attack on Libya. Our relations with the Islamic government in Iran are governed by an 
injunction from Almighty God, who commanded that ‘the believers are brothers and your 
community is a single entity." At any rate, we have proved that we do not support his 
revolution with words alone; in fact we do so with all our might. There is no country 
whose relations with Iran equal ours. Our relations are of a strategic nature and we are 
trying to establish them on a permanent basis. In other words we are striving to ensure 
that they are clearly defined, unlike the relations between other countries of the world." 


On the subject of the At-Ta'if conference and the Libyan Government's view of it, Al- 
Marghani said: "One month before the conference began we announced that we would not 
participate: How could we attend when, as everyone knows, it was convened at the behest 
of America and was taking place in a courtry where American AWACS planes were patrolling 
overhead and spying on Muslim nations; in short, the conference of Arab heads of state and 


similar conferences solved none of the problems.'

On the dispatch of French Mirage fighter aircraft to Iraq, which as everyone knows 

are to be used to kill defenseless people in Iran, Al-Marghani said: "France claims that 
is was necessary to implement contracts previously concluded with the Iraqi Government 
for the sale of Mirages. This means one of two things: Either the French Government 
approves of the present ruling regime in Iraq, but not in Iran, or that in some way, 
together with the EEC countries, it has decided to sell arms to both countries in order 
to milk the two nations, Iran and Iraq. It is therefore possible that, following the 
delivery of the Mirages to Iraq by France, certain other countries of the EEC which 
have contracts with Iran will deliver weapons previously sold. It is merelv commercial 
considerations which govern the desire of arms-supplying countries to derive maximum 
advantage from the war between Iran and Iraq." 

Asked how he saw the future course of the Iran-Iraq war, Al-Marghani said: "like all 
the other Muslims of the world, we have no doubt whatever that Islam is victorious and 
that those defending Islam will win. Against them, the agent of America, its infidel! 
and traitous lackey Saddam Husayn and his shaky and corrupt regime, purveyors of false 
values, is doomed to defeat. The war will soon be over, and the result is now clear. 
This is exactly what God promised us. [Iraq is now trying to extricate itself from the 
territory it has occupied, even after the destruction of $10 billion-worth of arms--and 
how much better it would have been if these weapons had been used to liberate Jerusalem 
and not to kill the defenseless people of the cities of Abadan and Khorramshahr and 

to destroy homes, mosques and hospitals." 


FORMER LIBYAN ENVOY SAYS OPPOSITION UNITING 


NCO30916 Paris AFP in English 0901 GMT 3 Mar 81 


[Text] Rabat, 3 Mar (AFP)--Libyan opposition movements were preparing to unite under 
a single banner, Ahmad Ibrahim Ahwas, a fomer Libyan diplomat and now an ‘opposition 
leader said here. There are some 50,000 Libyan exiles, most of them belonging to the 
country's elite, regrouped within professional groups, Mr Ahwas, who quit his post 

as charge d'affaires to Guyana to join the opposition, said. The 1,000 strong group 
numbers about 1,000 [as received] and constitutes the nub of the opposition in exile, 
"the one which can act", he added. An underground opposition movement within Libya 
"has the support of 70 percent of the Libyan people," Mr Ahwas said. 


Of the 12 members of the 1969 Revolutionary Council, two have sought refuge abroad, 

four are still in Libya, one is dead and only four remain in the government along with 
Libyan leader Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi, Mr Ahwas said. He added that one of the main 
opposition leaders was Muhammad al-Magrif, former ambassador to New Delhi, who was the 
target of an assassination squad at Rome's Fiumicino Airport on February 24. Mr al-Magrif 
was sentenced to death by a Libyan revolutionary tribunal the day after the attack, he 
said. 


"Al-Qadhdhafi's tenaciousness in trying to eliminate Mr al-Magrif, who was professor 
of economy, is due to his popularity within university circles," Mr Ahwas said. "As 
chairman of the Audit Office, Mr al-Magrif was the first to publically criticize the 
shortcomings and corruption of the Al-Qadhdhafi regime in a report which led to his 
removal to New Delhi," Mr Ahwas added. 


The former charge d'affaires, who said that Italian terrorism was financed by Colonel 
al-Qadhadhafi's "international revolutionary committee," added that most Libyan oppositon 
leaders had left Western Europe and the United States to settle in Arab capitals because 
their safety was not assured.
