NIGERIA-LIBYA TALKS ON CHAD BEGIN IN TRIPOLI 


AB111425 Lagos Domestic Service in English 1200 GMT 11 Feb 81 


[Text] Nigerian and Libyan officials have begun talks in Tripoli for proposals for a 
minisummit conference on Chad. The officials will also examine proposals for the with- 
drawal of Libyan troops from Chad (?to be replaced by an OAU peace-keeping force). 

{Words indistinct] understands that the outcome of the talks will determine the date for 
the proposed conference. 


At its meeting in Lome, Togo, last month the OAU ad hoc committee on Chad recommended 
the immediate establishment of the peace-keeping force in Chad. It also called for the 
withdrawal of Libyan troops from that country, the holding of general elections to 

be supervised by the OAU by the end of April this year. The OAU peace-keeping force 
will be made up of troops from Togo, Guinea, Benin and Congo. 


AL-QADHDHAFI RECEIVES VISITING CHADIAN DELEGATION 


LD111749 Tripoli JANA in English 1505 GMT 11 Feb 81 


[Text] Benghazi, 11 Feb (JANA)--Colonel Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi, leader of the great 
Al-Fatah revolution, yesterday received the Chadian popular delegation currently visit- 
ing the Jamahiriyah. 


The Chadian delegation expressed the thanks and appreciation of the Chadian people 
towards the role of the Libyan people in crushing the rebellion in Chad. The delegation 
hailed the humanitarian stand and aid offered by the Libyan people which reflects the 
deep-rooted cultural and historic ties between the two peoples. The delegation stressed 
the determination of the Chadian people to defend their freedom and establish their own 
people's authority. 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi greeted the delegation and confirmed the close ties between the 
two brotherly peoples in the Jamahiriyah and Chad. 


PAPER VIEWS WESTERN PRESS ON LIBYA, ARABS 


LD111433 London AL-ZAHF AL-AKHDAR in English 6 Feb 81 p 2 
[Untitled editorial] 


[Text] The thing that cannot be denied is the growing economic recession facing the world 
today and the fact that capitalism is in severe crisis. This situation has boosted the 
growing power of the right, who in turn are bent on consolidating their grip on 
exploitative relations, upon which their domination depends. 


Part of this consolidation incorporates a world-wide attack on revolutionary forces who 
have consistently resisted the encroachments of the right. At a national level, the 
attack takes the form of internal subversion of those regimes that have been struggling 

to keep their revolutionary potential on course. The American intervention in Central 
America and tlie recent hullabaloo over the alleged maltreatment of American prisoners by 
the Iranians, are simply two illustrations of this ploy, the aim being to divert attention 
from the realities of the deteriorating economic and social situation in capitalist 
countries and its effect on the masses of the people. 


V. 12 Feb 81 NORTH AFRICA 


Side by side is the now standard anti-Libyan hysteria in the West that is clearly aimed 
at isolating the one nation that has not cowered in the face of this right-wing assault. 
Daily the world's press focus on Libya, seeking to trivalise and belittle its attempts at 
forging necessary political alliances, needed for a strong anti-imperialist front in 

the developing world. 


Under guises of ‘analysis', political commentators proffer anti-Libyan propaganda by 
snide references, innuendos and blatant lies. One does not expect a fair press for 
ideas and policies that strike at the very heart of imperialism, but the spate and 
intensity of the recent attacks cannot be correlated to the initiatives taken by the 
Libyan Jamahiriyah. 


The only excuse for such attacks lies in the capitalists’ perception of the threat to 
their status quo and the need to contain and mediate the crisis of capital by subverting 
the potential for revolutionary change both within their own boundaries and among those 
that set the best example. Consequently, international slander becomes the order of the 
day. 


That is why the press can talk of 'Al-Qadhdhafi's honeymoon turns bitter' and 
"Al-Qadhdhafi move sparks new nuclear fears'. In fact an analysis of the major inter- 
national newspapers over the past month, reveals that each had at least three news items 
on Libya and one political commentary per day. Almost all were based on assumed 
references to internal developments dubious 'facts' or alleged statements of Libya's 
leader. 


There is indeed a general anti-Arab lobby prevalent in the world's press and the response 
to the Iranian release of the American prisoners and the massive publicity exercise 
engineered by Reagan's Hollywood propagandists, took specific swipes at the Arab nation. 
Asimilar analysis could be made of the coverage of the Islamic summit at At-Ta'if where 
invariably nuances of scepticism and incredulity permeated all the news stories, as 

if underlying the popular notion that Arabs and people of the developing world are 
incapable of managing their own affairs. Why else would the French president try to 
upstage the summit by announcing his own grandiose plan for a settlement of the so-called 
Afghan question? 


Visions of impending doom from 'the slumbering giants of the Middle East’ and "the 
third universal plan' as stated by the Western press, however, cannot frighten away 
the political will and determination of a people that have their desinities firmly on 
the horizon. Column inches of Western propaganda are no match for a sound political 
theory or the authority of the masses.
