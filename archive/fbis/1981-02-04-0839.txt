LIBYA

MEMORANDUM ON CONTINENTAL SHELF ISSUE LODGED 


LD031328 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 3 Feb 81 


[Text] The Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah yesterday lodged at the International 
Court of Justice at The Hague a memorandum which included its reply to the first Tunisian 
memorandum lodged at the same court on 30 May 1980 concerning the continental shelf be- 
tween the two countries. The Jamahiriyah and Tunisia agreed at the beginning of June 1977 
to submit the question of the continental shelf to the International Court of Justice for 
its judgment on this subject. 


PAPER VIEWS PENDING 'COUNTERREVOLUTIONARY' ATTACK 


LD031349 London AL-ZAHF AL-AKHDAR in English 30 Jan 81 p 2 
{Untitled editorial] 


[Text] The world hysteria over the decision of two independent countries of Africa to merge 
took a sinister twist recently as the major imperialist nation and its Middle-Eastern 
surrogates, Egypt and Sudan, began rattling their sabres and threatening the Libyan masses. 


The American cowboy President has stated that his top priority is to ‘end Libya's role as 

a sponsor of terrorism and subversion on a global scale'. With the memory of Vietnam 

firmly emblazoned on his mind, Reagan is not expected to take the vanguard role in confront- 
ing Libya and it is to her satellites that she must turn in order to carry out her threats. 


The main American lackey, the traitorous Egyptian ruler, Anwar as-Sadat, has expressed 
his eagerness to embrace this role by promising to use the so-called ‘Libyan National 
League’ led by the equally despicable Mustafa ad-Durki to foment a full-scale uprising 
in Libya. As-Sadat is expected to use the pretext of defending Sudan and so-called 
assassination plots against him to mount an invasion. 


Speaking on French television, As-Sadat said that the recent merger threatens the Sudan 
and what threatens the Sudan threatens Egypt. He also promised reporters that they have 
not heard his last word yet on the merger. With 100,000 troops stationed near the Libyan 
border As-Sadat is lulled into a false security that he can stem the revolutionary tide 
against traitors for the Arab nation and surrogates of imperialism. Even with American 
military support this cannot be done. 


V. 4 Feb 81 NORTH AFRICA 


Another serious threat has been launched against the Libyan masses by the murderous regime 
of Saddam Husayn. The renowned butcher of Baghdad has begun to channel support to a 
motley bunch of enemies of the Libyan revolution in an effort to destabilise the country. 
Iraq's directorate of general intelligence, Mudiraiyat al-Muhabarat al-A'ama. has re- 
cruited some 200 such traitors and arranged for them to be transported to Iraq for special 
training in guerrilla warfare and clandestine operations, 


The whole programme is under the personal direction of Barazan Ibrahim at-Takriti. the 
vperational chief of the Iraqi Muhabarat. The camp is based in central Iraq and some of 
the mercenaries have already been deployed in the war with Iran. 


Suddenly Libya's territory becomes fair game for all the counter-revolutionary forces in 
the area. No doubt the right-wing Reagan administration has given these forces the courage 
and temerity to enact their expansionist dreams at the expense of the real struggle against 
Zionism and imperialism. But because Libya has unswervingly committed itself to the just 
struggles of the oppressed masses, it is expected that the cornered wolves will lash out 

at their hunters. But the hunted will only become the hunter if political will and de- 
termination are allowed to give way to fear and compromise. 


Iraq will use its agents to foment unrest in the civil administration of Libya as well as 
spread despondency among the masses. But the revolutionary gains made during the past 11 
years cannot be blown away by a few dissidents, regardless of the power of their paymasters. 
Libya is strong and its policies are based on the fundamental principles of revolutionary 
Islam and there is no substitution for that. 


But no revalution is totally secure as counter-revolutionary forces are forever on the 
lookout for the half-chances to create an opening to undermine progress. Any and everybody 
can become an important ally in this instance. Libya expects and will continue to do so, 
the incessant plottings of its enemies but not for once will that deter us in our aims or 
cower [as published] us into compromises.
