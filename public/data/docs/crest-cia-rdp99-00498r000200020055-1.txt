IN JULY 1977, 10 AMERICAN MEN, BELIEVING THEY WERE INVOLVED IN A COVERT OPERATION AUTHORIZED BY THE U.S. CIA, TRAVELED TO LIBYA WHERE THEY WERE INFORMED THEY WERE TO GIVE COMMANDO TRAINING TO TERRORISTS

Summary (AI): A 1981 Philadelphia Inquirer column by Edwin Guthman discusses the 1977 Green Beret mission to Libya, recruited by Edwin Wilson and Francis Terpil, and questions whether the CIA authorized it. It covers Libyan intelligence, Billy Carter's ties to Libya, and the Gulf of Sidra shoot-down, but names no specific Tripoli sites.
• Nine of the ten Americans were retired Green Berets; the team was recruited by Master Sgt. Luke Thompson, then on active duty.
• Thompson said he alerted Army counterintelligence at Fort Bragg after accepting the assignment.
• Billy Carter reportedly received a $200,000 loan for future services from Libya.
• The column notes a shoot-out over the Gulf of Sidra in which two US Navy fighters downed two Soviet-built Libyan jets.
• 1977-07: Ten American men, nine of them retired Green Berets, travel to Libya believing they are on a CIA-authorized mission, and are told they are to train terrorists in commando tactics.
• 1977-04-26: CIA Director Stansfield Turner fires two middle-level CIA employees for giving Edwin Wilson unauthorized help shipping explosive devices to Libya.
• 1980-04: Edwin P. Wilson and Francis Terpil are indicted by a District of Columbia grand jury on charges including illegal explosives exports and acting as unregistered agents for Libya.
