ISLAMIC AFFAIRS 


PROGRESSION OF ISLAM IN AFRICA DISCUSSED 
Paris LE MONDE in French 16 Jan 81 p 5S 
[Article by Paul Balta: "The Progression of Islam in Black Africa" ] 


[Text] The conversion--sincere or not--to Islam of several black chiefs of state in 
recent years, the interest aroused by the Iranian revolution among the Arab and Afri- 
can masses, the reactions to the plan to merge Libya and Chad, are a few examples 
illustrative of the expanding role played by that "religion that is conquering 
Africa,"' according to Vincent Monteil's expression”. 


Often based on evaluations and despite their inaccuracy, the demographic statistics 
nevertheless make it possible to get an idea of the progression of the Islamic phe- 
nomenon on the black continent. This continent has a little over 400 million inhab- 
itants, nearly half of whom are Muslims--some 85 million in white Africa and 100 mil- 
lion in black Africa. 


The density of the disciples of the Prophet Mohammed diminishes from north to south, 
on the continental scale as well as in most of the countries south of the Sahara. 
Except in Egypt, where the Coptics--Christians implanted also in Ethiopia and former- 
ly in Nubia--r-present a strong minority, the African peoples bordering the Mediter- 
ranean are almost 100-percent Muslims. Their co-religionists are clearly in the ma- 
jority in the states bordering on or neighbors of the Arab countries, especially in 
Somalia, Senegal, Gambia, Niger, Mali, the Sudan and Guinea, and they form powerful 
minorities in Ethiopia, Sierra Leone, Guinea-Bissau, Nigeria, Chad, Tanzania, Liber- 
ia, Upper Volta, the Ivory Coast and Cameroon. Farther south, their presence is weak 
where it was nonexistent 10 years ago. 


Less than a century after the death of Mohammed (632), Islam had made a violent 
breakthrough to the east, as far as the Valley of the Indus, and subsequently pro- 
gressed farther and, to the west, as far as Spain by way of the Maghrib, even reach- 
ing Poitiers temporarily (732). On the other hand, the penetration of black Africa 
was later, the horses of the Arab conquerors of the seventh century having been 

laid low by the tsetse fly.

century.

IMPLICATIONS OF LIBYAN PRESENCE IN CHAD DISCUSSED 


Rabat L'OPINION in French 18 Dec 80 p 10 
{Editorial by Ahmed al Fadilli) 


[Text] With the signing of the cease fire agreement by Hissene Habre on Tuesday, 
the battle of Ndjamena seems to have come to an end. However, this is not true 
of the civil war in Chad--far from it. 


In fact, disregarding the reservations issued by the leader of the Niger Armed 
Forces at the very moment of the signing of the documents at Lome (whic! ‘eads 

us to believe that this agreement will be no more successful than its predecessors) 
and che statements of Habre to the effect that he intended to renew the fighting, 
the most serious threat to peace in Chad consists of the presence of Libya in 

the country. 


Therefore, as long as the dispute only concerned the opposing troops of Hissene 
Habre and the united forces of the GUNT organization, it was always possible to 
hope for a settlement between rival “brothers.” However, Libyan intervention 
has complicated matters and made it almost impossible to achieve a viable treaty 
and peaceful conditions within the country. 


The problem now is to know whether Africa will react to this threat or, on the 
contrary, will bury its ostrich head in the sand, a policy which it has been 
pursuing up to this time. 


After the seizure of Ndjamena by the Libyan forces, all of Chad is within the 
grasp of Qadhdhafi. Now, Chad is only a milestone in the path of the conqueror. 
The African nations, which up to this time have treated Qadhdhafi's threats as 
the raving of a maniac, now know what they are up against. The question that 

it should now consider is: "What will be the next target of the Libyan Army 
and, above all, where will it stop?” 


There is a saying to the effect that eating whets the appetite. Are the African 
nations familiar with this phenomenon? Are they therefore conscious of the 
dangers and the perils represented by Qadhdhafi's morbid appetite for many 
countries that are neighbors of Chad and even some that are distant? Have they 
forgotten the Gafsa and Kampal~ affairs? Have they forgotten the statements 
made by the Tr_poli colonel regarding his famous plan for the "United Sahara 
States"? 





78 








Alas, we are obliged to conclude that the African nations have indeed forgotten 
the abovementioned events for, although it has been more than a month since 
Libyan troops and armored units invaded Chad, no secious African reaction of a 
nature that could stop the advance of the Libyan forces has been recorded, 


To make the situation even worse, the Committee on Chad of the Organization of 
African Unity itself consists of heads of state whose allegiance to the Libyan 
government is no secret. Hence the reservations (well founded, in our opinion) 
expressed by Habre. 


The fall of Ndjamena and therefore of Chad, which is within the grasp of the 
Libyan forces, constitutes definite evidence of the resignation to its fate of 
the African continent which, satisfied with ite minor role as a spectator, opens 
wide its doors to the insatiable and morbidly hungry Qadhdhafi.
