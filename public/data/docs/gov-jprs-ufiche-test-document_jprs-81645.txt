MASSIVE ACHIEVEMENTS OF REAL ESTATE BANK EXTOLLED

[article by Mukhtar Suwaysi_and Daww al-Shaybani: "The Real Estate Savings and

[fext/ The housing shortage must be considered a worldwide crisis. It arises 
from the oppressive relationships which make it legitimate for man to exploit 
his fellow man and diminish his freedom by controlling his essential needs, 

such as food, clothing, transportation and housing. The freedom of people who 
live in other people's housing must be considered threatened by exploitation at 
every moment by the owners of buildings, lessors and usurpers to whom tyrannical 
laws give the right of absolute discretion to expel and dispossess families 
through rents, which must be considered the trump card in the hands of the bour- 
geois class by means of which that class exerts all forms of pressure and carries 
out all inhumane measures against tenants. 


Perhaps the fact that millions of people in the world have gone out on obscure, 
angry demonstrations against the class of bourgeois tenants who control these 
millions' freedom and happiness is the most eloquent expression of people's 
need for housing that can make them feel complete freedom and happiness, free 
from control by others. That is an expression of the permanent anxiety which 
the toiling people, whose urgent housing needs are controlled, experience. 


In the Socialist Libyan Arab People's Jamahiriyah, homes belonging to the people 
who inhabit them have been achieved through the application of the juridical 
statement in the first part of the Green Book — concerning the third worldwide 
theory, "homes for the people who inhabit them." No one should control the 
happiness and freedom of other people, and an end has been put to rents, which 
the bourgeois class would impose every month and with which it would threaten 
toiling people and their families. In the framework of the jamahiriyah's 
attention to housing, we will turn to talk about the Saving Bank and the extent 
to which it has been contributing to the housing sector a year after its estab- 
lishment. 


A Glance at the Real Estate Savings and Investment Bank 


In our conversation with him, our brother Sa'id al-Lishani, the director general 
of the bank, told us that after the section on housing was presented to the 
people's conferences at their third ordinary session of 1980, which the general 
meeting of the people's conferences, federations, unions and vocational societies

(the general people's assembly) formulated at its sixth ordinary session of 
3-7 January 1981, a law was drawn up establishing a Libyan joint stock company 
to be called the Real Estate Savings and Investment Bank. This would have a 
legal personality, would be subordinate to the Housing Secretariat and would 
exercise its activities in accordance with its charter in a manner that was 
not contradictory with the provisions of this law. 


The bank's headquarters are in the city of Tripoli and it may establish branches 
in the Socialist Arab People's Jamahiriyah as its activity requires by decree of 
the bank's board of directors. 


Our brother Sa'id went on to say that this bank was established in two depart- 
ments, an industrial department and a real estate department. The real estate 
department was to offer real estate loans to people with limited incomes only. 


Since there were other bodies that offered loans to citizens, such as housing 
societies, most of which complain of citizens' non-payment of loan instalments 
after the construction process is completed, because of the importance of saving 
and investment in moving the wheels of progress forward, and so that the citizen 
may feel that he has participated effectively in building his residence, does 
not feel that it is just a present given to him, and will look after it, be con- 
cerned with it and maintain it: 


Our brother Sa'id :aid that the bank's actual activity began on 1 June 1981, 
the date on which the acceptance of applications for citizens' loans started. 
The total applications submitted in the course of a year, that is, up to 1 June 
1981, came to about 30,000. The average loan was abcut 20,000 dinars, affirn- 
ing that the amount required for these loans excecic. 600 million pounds, at a 
time when the bank received only 75 million pounds during the abovementioned 
period. 


This amount covered only 12 percent of requirements. 


There is no doubt that the rate of applications increased in an unexpected 
fashion as a result of the suspension of loans, or, more accurately, the 
acceptance of loan applications that had been submitted over a number of pre- 
vious years. 


Within the limits of its resources, the bank was managed to cover applications 
for just 4 months, that is, from June to September 1981. 


Agreement will gradually be given to the other applications on the basis of 
their priority and the resources available. 


Our brother Sa'id went on to say, "As I mentioned above, these applications 
were submitted at a very great rate, which cannot be nc-mal. In the last 2 
months the rate of applications has declined; the Real Estate Savings and 
Investment Bank estimates that the normal rate of applications is between 
12,000 and 15,000 a year, which would require an annual budget estimated at 
about 250 to 300 million Libyan dinars."

Our brother the bank director said that the bank's permanent fixed resources 
in ordinary circumstances should be estimated at about 120 million dinars a 
year, which requires that an equivalent amount be allotted to the bank in 
the annual conversion budget. 


if the bank is able to obtain an amount of this sort, the pileup of applications 
will drop; but, in any event, even if this condition is met, processing the large 
volume of applications that has accumulated will take more than 4 or 5 years. 


This is as far as the bank's lending activity goes. Naturally there is another 
activity, which is the construction of housing and administrative units. The 
bank now has a number of projects that are being carried out, while others are 
being contracted for; these consist of a group of residential and administrative 
buildings in all the towns and villages of the jamahiriyah. 


The bank has granted loans to agricultural cooperative societies to construct 
oilmresses and buildings for industrial services such as shops and so forth. 


The Industrial Real Estate Bank has also carried out a number of projects: 


The project for the medical cotton and bandage factory in Janzur; itscosts 
come to 6,235,259 dinars, and its goal is to provide the jamahiyah's medical 
cotton requirements. 


The Benghazi bicycle factory project; its costs come to 58,739 Libyan dinars, 
and it has the objective of covering the requirements of the local market. 


The cardboard carton factory project in al-Zahra'; its costs come to 8, 2345, 984 
[sic/ Libyan dinars and is aimed at covering the requirement of the local market. 


The perfumed soap factory project in al-Gharayan; its costs come to 2,413,150 
Libyan dinars. 


The central workshop project in Tripoli. its costs come to 5,687,329 Libyan 
dinars. In addition, there is the central workshop project in Benghazi, 
costing 5,797,329 Libyan dinars, which is aimed at providing technical services 
for various dairy products and mechanical equipment and manufacturing the ne- 
cessary spare parts for them. 


There is the plastic box and container plant project in Surman, whose costs 
come to 10.5 million Libyan dinars. This ha- the goal of using local raw 
materials from the Abu Kamash chemical plant and increasing the volume of 
plastic products put out in the jamahiriyah, for household use and the materials 
needed for agricultural and industrial packaging. 


Conditions for Applying for Loans 


Among the conditions the Real Estate Investment and Savings Bank has set forth 
for accepting loan applications are:

The land, plans, and permits needed for the application must be available. 


The loan and consequently the building area must be determined in accordance 
With the family's needs. 


There are no objections to the first group of conditions that must be met in 
the application; however, the second group raises many questions and arguments 
which arise from the citizen's failure to be persuaded that the area should be 
determined. However, the bank, in determining areas, has relied on general 
housing policy and the areas the Secretariat of Housing has set out. In our 
opinion, the areas are very reasonable and are appropriate for families; it is 
not reasonable for a citizen to build a home for 10 people if the number of 
people in the family does not exceed three or four. 


Some citizens say that in 10 or 15 years their families will amount to 20 people. 
This is a fruitless argument, and a waste, loss and squandering of everyone's 
money. To say that a fumily will grow is not be questioned; the response 

to that is that the life of the house might be shorter than the period in which 
the family is developing and growing! This is on the one hand. On the other, 
one can plan to build a big house, a "duplex," provided that the construction 

be restricted to a portion of it, such as a floor, for instance, which will be 
cnough to house the family at present; then expansion in the construction of 

the other floors and levels can be done when the family grows. 


In addition, for information's sake, the Real Estate Investment and Savings 
Bank does not in reality determine the area to be put under a roof so much as 
it determines the amount of aid offered t© the applic.iion for construction 
of a house. If citizen S, for example, is given : loan to build a house of 
160 or 180 /square/ meters, that does not mean tnaat he cannot build a "duplex" 
of 250 or 300 meters. The citizen who has the loan has the right to build any 
area he himself wants, on condition, however, that he build what he wants at 
his own expense, while the society will just meet his own needs. As regards 
the loans that the bank gives, they are adquate for building houses, because 
the bank makes annual measurements and the cost level changes according to the 
vrices that prevail during construction. 


As regards the failure to accept loan applications, the bank strives earnestly 
through every means to give loans to people who have presented previous appli- 
cations, and then declares that new applications from citizens are being 
accepted; after this they are discussed individually, regardless of whether the 
person presenting the application has a limited or average income. Thus, the 
citizens are equal in the way they obtain loans, in terms of time periods and 
the monetary value of the loan.
