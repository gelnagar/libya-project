LIBYA

ECONOMY, LIGHT INDUSTRIES COMMITTEE ISSUES VARIOUS RESOLUTIONS 
Tripoli AL-MUNTIJUN in Av‘abic 16 Oct 82 p 3 


[Article: "Resolutions and Recommendations of General People's Committee for 
Economy and Light Industries During Third Ordinary Session of 1982" ] 


[Text] ‘he General People's Committee for Economy and Light Industries held 
its third session of 1982 at the committee headquarters in Tripoli from 

4-6 October 1982. It reviewed the implemented part of the resolutions it 
adopted at its second meeting of 1982 and the accomplishments outlined to it 
‘ by the statements of the committee secretary, in light of the field visits he 
made to the municipalities and the production sites, and in light of the 
observations and impressions he expressed regarding what he had seen on the 
spot. 


The committee expressed its satisfaction with the standards for resolution 
implementation, both at the level of the committee secretariat and the level 
of the municipal secretariats. The committee has underlined the following 
instructions: 


1. The economic conditions being experienced by the Jamahiriyah and the plots 
surrounding it and aimed at undermining its freedom and its revolutionary 
gains require us all to exert greater efforts and to engage in creative action 
in order to establish an economic base founded on increased and diversii ied 
production and aimed at meeting the essential needs of society's members. 


2. The limited financial resources must be an incentive to us to raise pro- 
duction and to enhance society's production capability through the ideal 
utilization of the resources in a way that enables us to meet our essential 
needs at the lowest cost. 


3. The shaky conditions engulfing the world economy which affect our economy, 
either negatively or positively, and the economic pressures aimed against the 
Jamahiriyah people require all of us to enlighten the citizens through the 
people's congresses and the people's and revolutionary committees in order to 
crystalize their position vis-a-vis the challenges facing them through sound 
consumption habits and through contributing to enhanced production. 

















Proceeding on this basis, the producers’ and professional unions and associa- 
tions and the revolutionary information media are called upon to make the 
citizens understand their role in this regard, in streamlined consumption and 
in achieving the set production targets of the various plants and production 
units. 


4. While keeping in mind the above-mentioned instructions and given facts, 
the General People's Committee for Economy and Light Industries pledges to 
exert further efforts and work to establish a strong socialist economy founded 
on reduced consumption and increased production to meet the needs. In this 
respect, the committee has adopted a number of recommendations and resolu- 

tions, including the following: 


First, Followup Resolutions: 


Tic committee reaffirms the resolutions it adopted in its second session of 
1982. 


The departments concerned have been instructed to continue their followup and 
to make sure that the unimplemented part of the resolutions is carried out, 
especially the recommendations and resolutions adopted regarding wool 
gathering and processing, the distribution of carbonated drinks, cooperation 
with the trucking company to transport local production and regarding main- 
tenance of the production lines of the flour mills and fodder plants. 


The General People's Committee for Economy .and Light Industries has also 
decided that the brother municipal secretaries shall initiate immediate and 
constant followup on the plants and production units within their juris- 
diction to make sure that the equipment and machinery already received but 
not yet installed is installed and put into operation, and that damaged 
equipment is repaired and that its production capacity is enhanced. They 
shall also ensure the implementation of the instructions issued by the 
meetings of the revolutionary committees on increasing production and 
operating at least two shifts in the plants and production units in order to 
increase local production of the various commodities and products. 


Industrial Development 


The committee has underlined the importance of the approved industrial pro- 
gram. The companies and establishments concerned shall be instructed to take 
immediate practical measures needed to implement the program at least by the 
end of 1983. 


Fish Farming 
The Marine Resources Agency has been instructed to draw up a specific and 


complete program for fish farming in the Jamahiriyah in light of the indi-

Second, Commodity and Production Budgets: 


The committee reviewed the 1983 commodity budget and industrial budget and 
has expressed its satisfaction with the efforts exerted in this respect. I 
stressed the need for compliance with this budget within the framework of 
set priorities and with serious efforts to increase the local production of 
various goods and commodities, to utilize in an ideal manner the capacity of 
the plants and production units and to achieve surpluses in them in order to 
serve the national economy. 


The committee also stresses that the companies and establishments engaged in 
importation must take into consideration the best prices, the quality of the 
goods and the actual need for the imported commodities. Consideration must 
also be given to finding new markets within the framework of the Jamahiriyah's 
relations with the various countries of the world and to getting the best 
terms for bolstering the economic structures and making use of technical 
expertise in return for the purchase of goods and commodities. 


The committee has also expressed its ubservations and recommendations in this 
regard, stressing in particular the need to enlarge the areas of fruit culti- 
vation, to draw up the agricultural programs capable of supplying fruits in 
quantities capable of meeting the citizens’ needs and the need to form a © 
strategic store of wheat and food supplies. 


In implementation of the resolutions of the 1981 third ordinary sessior of 
the people's basic congresses to increase production and to enhance the 
production capability, and in appreciation of the responsibilities entrusted 
to the committee members and to the workers of the sector's agencies, con- 
panies and plants to implement the resolutions. The committee urges all the 
other departments and agencies concerned, especially banks, customs, public 
service, electricity, passports, ports and the Urban Mobilization Command ro 
cooperate with the industrial companies and plants and to facilitate acqui- 
sition of requirements for operation and importation of foreign expertise 
that is not available locally. They must adapt their procedural regulations 
to make it possible for the industrial companies and plants to supply the 
needs of the industrial sector promptly and to insure the continued operation 
of these companies and plants in accordance with the set production targets. 
The committee has also issued its resolutions and recommendations in this 
regard, including: 


Approval of the 1983 production targets, taking into consideration the com- 
mittee's observations on these targets. The committee urges the people's

committees in the companies and the factories to exert utmost efforts to 
achieve the set production figures. The industrial companies must exert 
efforts to operate their plants at the rate of at least two shifts daily, 
unless there are objective reasons acceptable to the committee which bar 
such operation. 


The committee also urges constant efforts to raise the plants’ productivity 
and to reduce the production costs. 


The 1983 Draft Commodity Budget for Industrial Companies: 


The committee has reviewed the draft commodity budget of the sector's 
industrial companies and the 1983 draft budget for commodity imports and has 
requested that the two drafts be referred to the resources committee, along 
with its observations which include: 


A. Considering the sums al'’ocated for the industrial companies as the mini- 
mum sums necessary to operate the plants under its control. 


B. Warning that any reduction in the sums allocated for importing essentials 
for operating the plants will necessarily lead to reduced production and to 
failure to achieve the figures set for the national industrial production in 
1983. This production was taken into consideration when determining the 

volume of imports of similar commodities in the 1983 commodity imports budget 
or determining not to import such commodities in view of the national industry's 
capability to meet the local market needs of such commodities. 


C. It recommends that the decision approving the budget grant adequate powers 
to the budget committee to increase the allocations for the industrial com- 
panies’ commodity budget beyond the set figures if the request for an increase 
is due to the need to meet the requirements of an increased number of shifts 
in the plant or plants controlled by the committee, to expansion in these 
plants, or to the installation of new production lines or units listed in the 
light industries production development program, if such lines or units are 
put into operation ahead of the timetable set for them in the program. 


D. It recommends that the resources committee and the commodity budget com 
mittee instruct commercial banks to facilitate credit, to develop procedures 
for opening credit accounts as long as the remittances volume is within the 
company's approved commodity budget and advancing the necessary credit 
according to the volume of the work which the companies are assigned to 
implement.

The committee reviewed the industrial production report for the first half of 


1982 and (discussed with the industrial companies the figures recorded during

Regarding Industrial and Commercial Companies and Establishments i 


The committee also reviewed what has been done to supply vehicle spare parts 
and maintenance workshops and to spread the services of the vehicles sector 
to the various parts of the Jamahiriyah. The committee has decided the 
following: 


Work hours in the distribution centers belonging to the General Engines 
Company and to the (Tibesti) Car Company will be during the morning and 
evening hours. 


In distributing the budget allocations for the various items, consideration 
must be given to assigning priority in allocations and imports to frequently 
and quickly consumed items. 


The General Engines Company and the Tibesti Car Company will be assigned to 
draw up an administration program to begin manufacturing small spare parts 
that can be manufactured locally. 


The Tibesti Company and the Engines Company will be assigned to set up a 
larger number of workshops, to offer maintenance work throughout all parts of 
the Jamahiriyah and to exert efforts to supply maintenance requirements and 
spare parts systematically and regularly. 


Resolution No 10 


The committee discussed the activity of the National Markets Company and 
examined the relationship binding the company to its branches and offices and 
to the specialized municipal committees in light of the resolutions of the 
committee's second session of 1982. In light of the memoranda, projections 
and explanations presented, the committee has adopted several recommendations 
and resolutions, including: The need for the distribution companies and 
establishments to be present in all the municipalities through distribution 
centers established for that purpose in all the municipalities, in addition 
to the public markets in the area. The companies directorate and the pro- 
duction and distribution directorate shall follow up with the various 
companies on the implementation of this resolution, provided that this is 
done before the end of the current year in cooperation and coordination with 
che municipal secretaries of economy and light industries.

Followup on Transformation Plan 


The committee reviewed the followup reports on the economy and light indus- 
tries sector and adopted the necessary measures. 


Mines and Quarries 


The committee reviewed the memorandum o¢ the Public Directorate of Industrial 
Organization and Services regarding the mines and quarries and the memoranda 
of a number of brother secretaries on the issue and adopted the necessary 
resolutions on them, including the decision to set the sale price of a cubic 
meter of gravel at the quarry site at 3 dinars. The secretary of economy 

and light industries in every municipality shall study the gravel production 
cost in his area and reset its price at no more than 3 dinars per cubic meter. 
A committee has also been formed to study the issue of organizing and uti- 
lizing the quarries.
