Algerian  Surpluses  for  Maghrebian  Neighbors 

The  interconnection  between  the  Maghrebian  countries, 
particularly  between  Morocco,  Algeria,  and  Tunisia,  is 
no  recent  phenomenon.  In  1988,  the  three  systems  had  a 
combined  total  of  over  10,000  km  of  very  high  tension 
lines,  an  installed  power  of  7.4  million  kW  [kilowatt]  for 
a  peak  power  of  4.7  million  kW,  and  an  annual  produc¬ 
tion  of  25.6  billion  kWh.  Experts  say  the  combined 
production  systems  are  complementary  and  thus  create 
“an  inertia  effect”  enabling  the  Maghrebian  system  to 
meet  all  contingencies.  In  addition,  yield  is  better 
because  the  power  plants  in  each  of  the  countries  operate 
at  a  normal  rather  than  a  weak  load. 

The  proposed  first  line  between  Morocco  and  Algeria 
connecting  the  Oujda  station  and  Ghazaouet  (46  km 
long,  12  in  Moroccan  territory)  was  begun  in  the  1970s.
