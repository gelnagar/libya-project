LIBYA

FRENCH TV INTERVIEWS AL-QADHDHAFI ON CHAD ISSUE 


NCO51934 Paris AFP in English 1927 GMT 5 Feb 81 


[Excerpt] Paris, 5 Feb (AFP)--Libya would intervene in other African countries only 
if asked to, as had been the case in Chad, Libyan leader Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi said 
in an interview broadcast by French television today. 


Col. al-Qadhdhafi also said that Libya had wanted to pull its troops out of Chad after 
their intervention had brought an end to the civil war there in December, but stayed 
put at the insistence of Chadian President Goukouni Oueddei. 


Chad fears a French military intervention, and "if France intervenes in Chad, it would 
confront us in that country," Col. al-Qadhdhafi vowed. 


"There is no direct problem between Libya and France," Col. al-Qadhdhafi continued. 
"But colonialist French interventions in Africa provoke confrontations between France 
and its friends and, in the future, will go against French interests in Africa." 


Concerning the announced plan for Libya and Chad to merge, Col. al-Qadhdhafi said the 
proposal was “neither a political union nor a fusion". It is "a union of the masses" 
and would only take place after a general referendum, he said. 


Col. al-Qadhdhafi said Nigeria was mistaken when saying that it was in danger due to 
Libyan forces in Chad. "It was drawn into a trap," Col. al-Qadhdhafi said of Nigeria, 
calling the country politically inexperienced.

Col. al-Qadhdhafi also dismissed suggestions of political divisions within Chad, 
blaming a "French plot" for such reports. "France wishes to sacrifice Chad," he said. 
He said that all sides of Chad's political spectrum were represented in the country's 
provisional government and called Mr. Goukouni a "legitimate revolutionary". 


About Chadian Rebel Leader Hissein Habre, the former defence minister, Col. al-Qadhdhafi 
said that Libya would not be "hostile" towards him if he struck an agreement with Mr. 
Goukouni.
