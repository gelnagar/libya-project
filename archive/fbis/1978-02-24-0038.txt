TRIPOLI TALKS REACH AGREEMENT ON CHAD SITUATION 
LD232232Y Tripoli Voice of the Arab Homeland in Arabic 2115 GMT 23 Feb 78 LD 


[Excerpts] Today's closed session of the quadripartite talks, which was held at the 
People's Hall at Sabha, ended at 2100, The session was attended by heads of states 
participating in the talks which began at 1700 today. Members of the official dele- 
gations of the Jamahiriyah, Niger, Chad and Sudan participated in the session. 


The official spokesman of the conference, brother Muhammad Abu al-Qasim az-Zuwayy, 
secretary for information and culture, held a press conference at the end of the 

closed session, He declared that the heads of state held a closed meeting lasting 
about 2 hours in a constructive, cordial atmosphere, During the meeting, the following 
was agreed: 


1. The four countries meeting at Sabha have agreed not to permit any ovher side to talk 
about matters pertaining to the internal situation in Chad and to help the Chadian 
brothers to solve their problems. In other words, no party other than the countries 
meeting at Sabha will have the right to intervene or discuss this subject. 


2e The heads of states have agreed to prepare a draft provisional agreement for the 
holding of a meeting between the rebels and the Chad Government at Sabha under the 
supervision of Sudan and the Jamahiriyah, The agreement is being drafted, The agreement 
will provide details of matters pertaining to the meeting. 


3. It was agreed that the Jamahiriyah ambassador ill return to Ndjamena and that the 
decision to freeze political relations between the two countries will be canceled, 


Brother Colonel al-Qadhdhafi has made a statement. He said: We are meeting here to 
solve an internal problem in a sisterly state, Chad. We in the Jamahiriyah, Niger 
and Sudan are neighbors of Chad. We are meeting to solve an internal problem and this 
proves that Africa is truly for the Africans and that the Africans are able to solve 
their own problems, 


This meeting frustrates the hopes of those who want to spark the fire of war in Africa 
particularly in the underdeveloped African countries which need peace and stability.

DAYAN COMMENTS ON PEACE NEGOTIATION ISSUES 

Possibility of Jordanian Role 
TA230920Y Tel Aviv HA'AREZ in Hebrew 23 Feb 7% pp 1,2 TA 
[By HA'AREZ political correspondent } 


[Text] Foreign Minister Moshe Dayan said yesterday to the Knesset Foreign Affairs and 
Security Committee that he can see no chance of Jordan's joining the negotiations. 


Dayan said that Husayn is not prepared to make any compromise to Israel and he is 
demanding a total Israeli withdrawl, including a withdrawal from East Jerusalem. 
Dayan addea that the prime minister was willing to discuss a Jordanian proposal for 
territorial compromise but no such proposal has been forthcoming. Dayan said that if 
we get one, we will not reject it outright. He added that the efforts to be made 

by Alfred Atherton toward formulating an Israeli-Egyptian declaration of principles 
might not be acceptable to Jordan. As-Sadat is unwilling to reach a separate peace 
settlement with Israel. 


Discussing the settlement issue, Dayan said that the existing settlements in the Rafah 
approaches should be strengthened (thus implying that new settlements should not be 
added), while the agreed settlement plan for Judaea and Samaria should be implemented 
(which includes the establishment of two more settlements in the framework of military 
camps). 


Dayan pointed out that in his talks in Washington it was not the settlement issue which 
was central in the deliberations but As-Sadat's conditions for the renewal of the 
negotiations. The Egyptian president's positions are tough, said the foreign minister. 


The foreign minister was also asked on the intervention by the Israeli Embassy in 
Washington in the plans of Jewish leaders to meet with As-Sadat. Dayan told the members 
of the committee that there had not been any intervention initiated by the Israeli 
Embassy. When asked, it had merely expressed its opinion against helping As-Sadat in 
his efforts to split the Jewish leadership with respect to Israel..

JERUSALEM POST SAYS PLO 'REGULARS' FOUGHT AT LARNACA 
TA24O634Y Jerusalem POST in English 24 Feb 78 p 1 TA 
[By military correspondent Hirsh Goodman] 


[Text] Sixteen PLO terrorists took part in the attack against Egyptian commandos 
in Larnaca on Saturday night, sources confirmed to the Jerusalem POST yesterdzy. 


According to Western intelligence sources, the 16 were flown into Cyprus from Beirut 
with the prior consent of the Government of Cyprus in order to provide a security 
belt for the two Palestinian hijackers who had been granted free passage out of 
Larnaca by the Cypriots in return for releasing the plane, its crew and the hostages 
they had taken earlier, 


All 16 were regular members cf the Palestine Liberation Organization and not a special 
commando force, as published in various papers to date, They arrived in Cyprus armed 
and were charged only with providing an escort for the two hijackers, whom the 

PLO apparently feared would be prevented from leaving Gyprus by "hostile forces" 

on the island, 


It was this special group of PLO regulars who took the major role in the battle against 
the Egyptian troops, shooting the attackers in the back from positions at the main 
terminal. 


The confirmation of the participation of PLO regulars in the fighting explodes the 
PLO's earlier protestations that it had nothing to do with the hijacking, and makes 
a mockery of Yasir 'Arafat's personal assurances and expressions of regret at the 
incident,

JOURNAL COMMENTS ON BREAK IN RELATIONS WITH KEWYA 
LD231404Y Teheran FARS in English 1100 J“) 23 Fe LD 


{Text] Teheran, 22 Feb, PANA (PARS NEWS AGENOY)--In an editorial on Wednesday, 
February 22, the English language morning newspaper Teheran JOURNAL comaented on 
the following article by saying that... 


A pane has shattered in Iran's "window on Africa" policy with the recent decision to 
break off diplomatic relations with Kenya. But the severance of ties, while unfortu- 
nate, is not without justification. Iran was forced into a position where the longer 
Ambassador Ahmad Tavakoli remained at his post in Nairobi, the more tarnished diplo- 
matic pride would become and this nation would be seen as accepting unjustified 
criticism without taking appropriate action, That action came on Saturday when Foreign 
Minister Abas Ali Khalatbari announced that diplomatic ties with Kenya had been 
stretched beyond their limite and Iran had decided on the final cut. He described the 
move as provoked by “unwarranted” criticism of Iran's support for Somalia in its dispute 
with Ethiopia. fhe Kenyan press had been attacking Iran for some time, erroneously 
alleging Iranian involvement in the Ogaden war. 


Iran has declared its desire to bring about a peaceful settlement in the Ogaden con- 
flicts and has not taken any measures against Ethiopia, Iranian aid to the Somalis 
has consisted merely of medical supplies delivered by the Red Lion and Sun Society. 


Yet this is a natural concomitant of Iran's commitment to the inviolability of inter- 
natioally recognized borders everywhere, as a member state of the United Nations. As 
well, Iran is understandably concerned with the fate of Somalia as a predominantly 
Moslem nation and member of the Islamic brotherhood, It is also natural for Iran to 
worry at the prospect of unsettled conditions in the Horn of Africa and the possibility 
of a hostile controlling influence disrupting sea routes for Iranian oil transported 

to Europe and the United States, 


Inevitable though the diplomatic rift was between Iran and Kenya in view of the 
continued press and finally official accusations, it is nevertheless not welcome, 

Iran will not lose a great deal as diplomatic contact was simply part of general policy 
to open a window on Africa and expand cooperation with Third World countries. Trade 
relations were one-sided, with Iranian oil mostly reaching Nairobi via the oil 
consortium. The Moslem minority in Kenya, including some 28,000 Arabs, will be unhappy, 
and the Kenyan economy is not so healthy that the government of President Jomo Kenyatta 
can afford to alienate potential sources of aid or trade, On reflection, Kenyan 
authorities may well wish they had not been so hearty or heavy-handed. But it is now 
up to this African state to make any moves toward rapprochement with Iran, And that 

is unlikely until a peaceful settlement is achieved in the Ogaden,
