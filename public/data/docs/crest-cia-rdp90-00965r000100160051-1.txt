Summary (AI): A Washington Post column (25 September 1981) reports on Billy Carter's dealings with Libya, including a $220,000 "loan" from Libyan officials and an alleged plot tied to ex-CIA agents Frank Terpil and Edwin Wilson. It concerns Libyan influence-peddling and arms/terrorism-linked figures rather than specific places in Tripoli.
• Billy Carter reportedly received a $220,000 'loan' from the Libyans, an advance on expected commissions, requested as $500,000.
• Qaddafi reportedly believed Billy could persuade the president to release eight C130 transport planes embargoed over Libyan support for terrorists.
• Terpil reportedly tried in 1976 to arrange a similar oil-commission deal for himself.
• Wilson reportedly discussed hiring a professional assassin to kill Billy Carter by planting explosives in an oil painting.
• 1978-09: Billy Carter first visited Libya with Georgia associates, ostensibly to promote commercial ties
• 1979-09: Terpil invited Billy Carter to the 10th-anniversary celebration of Qaddafi's coup; apparently a deal was struck giving Billy a commission on Libyan crude delivered to Charter Oil Co.
• 1980-01-16: Billy Carter told a Justice Department official he had met Terpil on the 1979 trip; he later recanted this statement two months later
