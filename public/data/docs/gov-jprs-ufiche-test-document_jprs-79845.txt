"JEUNE AFRIQUE' SAID SEEKING CONTRACT WITH LIBYA 
PM091503 Paris LE MONDE in French 28 Nov 81 p 4 


[Text] In its latest edition, dated 25 November, LE CANARD ENCHAINE reveals that 
the weekly JEUNE AFRIQUE (circulation 133,000), whose editor is Bechir Ben Yahmed, 
has offered Tripoli a series of contracts worth $4 million to "create a current 

of opinion favorable to Colonel al-Qadhdhafi's work and to give millions of Africans 
and people throughout the world a better understanding of it." Ome of features 
designed to this end, which was to be published in the "Platform" series, apparently 
comprised 72 pages at $5,800 per page. In addition, the weekly envisages 48 pages 
in JEUNE AFRIQUE ECONOMIE, 12 in the ANNUAIRE DE LA DEFENSE ET DE LA DIPLOMATIE, 
which belongs to the same group, as well as a supplement entitled: "Let's Get 

To Know Sport in the Jamahiriyah." 


In the plans submitted to the Libyan authorities, Henry Descleves, assistant editor 
of the weekly and director of JEUNE AFRIQUE promotion, states: "It is understood 
that the word ‘advertising’ will not be featured on the texts published by JEUNE 
AFRIQUE;"" and, according to a photocopy published by LE CANARD ENCHAIN®, he also 
states that the articles would be "written by several well-known journalists or 
writers" chosen from a list which he supplied. In addition, JEUNE AFRIQUE suggests 
placing versions of these texts in major French and foreign newspapers including 

LE MONDE. LE CANARD ENCHAINE recalls a JEUNE AFRIQUE cover showing Colonel 
al-Qadhdhafi and headed: "Beware, He Kills." 


An Explanation From Mr Ben Yahmed 


Several of the writers and journalists mentioned, including our contributors Paul 
Balta and Eric Rouleau, were not consulted or approached. The same applies to our 
advertising department. In a note to us JEUNE AFRIQUE editor Ben Yahmed criticizes 
LE CANARD ENCHAINE for not checking its information with his weekly. He also 
explains that he has "so far not signed any contract with Libya" and that "if he 

did so he would inform his readers of it." He suggests the formation of an honorary 
committee which could "be appointed to examine the respect shown for professional 
ethics and ethical rules by JEUNE AFRIQUE and LE CANARD ENCHAINE over a period of
