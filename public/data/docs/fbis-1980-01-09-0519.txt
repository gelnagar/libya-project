At-Turayki on Foreign Policy 
LDO42254 Tripoli Voice of the Arab Homeland in Arabic 2015 GMT 4 Jan 80 ID 


[Excerpts] The General People's Congress ended its evening session a short while ago 
at the People's Hall in Tripoli. The congress resumed its meetings at 1800 [1600 GmT] 
today. 


After the secretary general of the General People's Congress, Brother 'Abd al-'Ati 

'ubaydi, opened the session, the congress began its review of item three on the agenda, 
which deals with the foreign policy of the Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah, 

and paragraph one, which covers a brief report on the implementation of the recommendations 
of the popular congresses. 


The foreign secretary then spoke about Libya's foreign policy. He reiterated that the 
foreign policy of any country is a reflection of its domestic policy. Socialist People's 
Libyan Arab Jimahiriyah foreign policy is a reflection of the great achievements and 
revolution which has been and continues to be witnessed in the Jamahiriyah. 


He pointed out that the Great 1 September Revolution has made great achievements and 
continues to do so in the domestic field. The Jamahiriyah people can feel proud that 
since the spark of their revolution, they have been leading the Arab nation's struggle 
against the Zionist enemy and exploitation and reaction represented by the Egyptian 
regime, which betrayed the cause of the Arab nation and concluded an unnatural and unholy 
alliance with the Zionist enemy and imperialism. 


He also stated: We can say that during the past year, the Jamahiriyah's foreign policy 
has witnessed major moves in the pan-Arab field. I can say that at the Baghdad conference 
of Arab foreign ministers, the Jamahiriyah was the one behind the resolutions adopted at 
the Baghdad conference, by which the entire Arab nation abided in boycotting the Egyptian 
regime, its expulsion “rom the Arab League and denunciation of its capitulationist policy, 
and consequently denun ation of imperialist policy in the area. 


On the African level, the foreign secretary said our policy is based on bolstering relations 
with fraternal African countries and the African liberation movements.

The following are the resolutions and recommendations adopted by the popular congresses 
during their third ordinary session for 1979, which were formulated by the general 
conference of the popular congresses, committees and trade unions and vocational unions-- 
the General People's Congress--in its fifth session held 1-6 January 1980: 


In consolidation and affirmation of the people's authority, and with success from God, 
the general conference of the popular congresses, popular committees and trade unions 
and vocational unions--the General People's Congress--convened in its fifth ordinary 
session in the period 1-6 January 1980 to draft and formulate the resolutions and 
recommendations of the popular congresses for the first time since the separation of 
the revolution and authority. 


The congress greets the historic leadership of the Great 1 September Revolution headed 
by the commander, teacher and revolutionary thinker, Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi 
[applause], and has adopted the following resolutions: 


I. In review of the resolutions and recommendations of the popular congresses in their 
third ordinary session on 21 October 1978, which were formulated by the general con- 
ference of the popular congresses and popular committees and trade unions and vocational 
unions--the People’s General Congress--in its fourth session for 1978. 


The basic popular congress have reviewed their resolutions and recommendations and re- 
affirmed them and expressed satisfaction with what has been implemented. They also call 
for speeding up what has not yet been implemented, particularly the resolutions and 
recommendations pertaining to the establishment of general popular committees in the 
municipalities, the duties of these committees and the role of the revolutionary medical 
professional and the revolutionary agricultural adviser, the role of the agricultural 
coastal strip, the people's ownership of the seashore and employment of Libyan teachers 
during the summer vacations. 


IIe Domestic Policy: 


1. To follow upon the implementation of the development budget and the administrative 
budget for 1979: 


A. The popular congresses wish to register their satisfaction with the projects that 
have been implemented to date within the framework of the development plan and hope that 
steps will be taken to overcome the factors that have delayed the implementation of 
other projects. They also stress the need to speed up the implementation of the pro- 
jects that have not been implemented yet, whether these projects are behind schedule, 
at a standstill, at the stage of being contracted out or if they have not yet been 
placed under contract. These projects are defined in Appendix No l. 


B. The popular congresses hereby approve the excess in expenditure on projects during 
fiscal year 1979. 


2. The development budget and the administrative budget for 1980: 


A. The popular congresses have discussed the draft development budget of change for 
1980 and have approved the draft budget as defined in Appendix No 2, provided that the 
following factors are taken into consideration:

NEW PETROLEUM SECRETARY CITED ON EXPORTING POLICY 
LD071122 Kuwait KUNA in Arabic 0716 GMT 7 Jan 80 ID 


{Text] Kuwait, 7 Januarye-Libyan petroleum secretary 'Abd as@Salah aze-Zaq'ar, in a press 
interview published today, has denied that his appointment to his new post indicates a 
change in Libya's oil policy. 


In an interview with AI-WATAN--his first since his appointment to succeed former Libyan 
Petroleum Secretary 'Izz ad-Din al-Mabruk, who was relieved of his post on Saturday by the 
General People's Congress--AzeZaq! ar said that Libya will continue together with other 
OPEC member countries to provide energy to the world but, according to the paper, he dis- 
counted the likelihood of any new changes taking place in the field of energy. 


The Libyan Oil Secretary added that his country will continue with its oil policy which 
is based "on the liberation of the Arab will, the creation of a national oil industry and 
placing the oil in the service of the Arab cause and the central cause, namely, Palestine." 


Aze-Zaq!' ar declined to give any direct reply when asked whether he supported the halting of 
oil supplies to the United States because of its support for Israel in the Middle East 
conflict. All he said was that the Jamahiriyah "wants the Arab nation to adopt a unified 
stand on this matter." He added that "we are prepared to adopt a hard line which will 
weaken the enemies of the Arab nation should they continue to aline themselves with Israel." 


AzeZaq' ar called on the Arab oil-producing countries to close ranks, saying that such a 
unified Arab stand would cause Western governments to adopt a better attitude toward the 
Palestine question and the rights of the Palestinian people.
