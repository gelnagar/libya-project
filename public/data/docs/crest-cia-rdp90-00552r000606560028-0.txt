Summary (AI): A New York Times article (5 December 1981) reports that a former Green Beret, Eugene Tafoya, was convicted in Fort Collins, Colorado, of assault on Faisal Zagallai, a Libyan-born Colorado State University student and critic of Qaddafi. Prosecutors alleged Tafoya was paid by the Libyan government, possibly through former CIA agent Edwin P. Wilson.
• Tafoya was convicted of third-degree assault and conspiracy to commit third-degree assault; sentencing was set for January 5, and he faced up to 24 months in jail.
• Prosecutors alleged the Libyan government paid Tafoya to assassinate Zagallai; no evidence was introduced as to who hired him.
• Zagallai was reportedly urged by Libyan students to stop making broadcasts injurious to Israeli-Egyptian detente.
• Tafoya fled the United States after the shooting and visited London and Tripoli, according to prosecutors.
• 1980-10: Eugene Tafoya, posing as a job interviewer, visited Faisal Zagallai and shot him in the head with a .22-caliber pistol; Zagallai survived but lost the vision in his right eye.
• 1981-12-04: Jury convicted Tafoya of two misdemeanor assault charges (third-degree assault and conspiracy to commit third-degree assault) and acquitted him of attempted murder.
