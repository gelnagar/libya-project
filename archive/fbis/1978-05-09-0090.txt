Despite the rhetoric of the Jamahiriyah, the limits to its influence are no surprise-- 
Libya has a great deal of money and the propensity of the rich to make extravagant 
promises. But with a population of only two-and-a-half million, its armed forces 

are in no position to back up words with muscle--a fact of life, already demonstrated 
by the collapse of the Libyan-engineered ceasefire in the Philippines, which will 
doubtless be well remembered by Mauritania, if Ould Daddah's visit to Tripoli leads 

to Libyan attempts at a Saharan solution, 


President al-Qadhdhafi's many critics, ranging from Western pundits and King Hassan 

of Morocco, say the new interest in Africa is all part of a Soviet grand design spreading 
from the Western Sahara to the Horn of Africa and from the Mediterranean to the borders 
of white-ruled southern Africa. 


As evidence they cite Libya's enormous purchase of Soviet arms (some 2,000 tanks and
