Summary (AI): A 1984 New York Times article reports US plans to consult allies, especially Britain, to turn concern over Libyan activities into international pressure on Qaddafi after Britain cut ties following a London shooting from the Libyan Embassy. It describes earlier US measures (1981 expulsions, oil import ban, American evacuation) and allied reluctance, and notes reports of assassination attempts and crackdowns on dissidents.
• US barred importation of Libyan oil, restricted technology flow to Libya, and told Americans to leave Libya.
• Italy has about 20,000 workers in Libya; Britain has about 8,000 (per a State Department official).
• Reports of explosions and assassination attempts against Qaddafi and close collaborators, including cousins; one injured a close relative.
• NATO foreign ministers were to meet in Washington at end of May 1984; economic summit in London in early June.
• 1984-04-23: Shooting of a British police constable in London by someone inside the Libyan Embassy, after which Britain severed diplomatic ties with Libya the following Sunday
• 1980: American Embassy in Libya was burned down
• 1981: US expelled Libyan diplomats and advised American companies and personnel to quit Libya
