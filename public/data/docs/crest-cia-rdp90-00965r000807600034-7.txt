Summary (AI): A Washington Post article (by Bob Woodward) reports that the CIA concluded a December 1981 assassination attempt against Qaddafi was carried out by Libyan army officers, possibly returning from Chad, and that Qaddafi was probably nicked by a bullet. It also describes purges, executions, and rising opposition, but does not place any events in Tripoli itself.
• The CIA reportedly concluded the attempt was carried out by members of Qaddafi's army, probably one or more officers who had just returned from the Libyan invasion of Chad, with no external support.
• Qaddafi said on Jan. 5, 1982 that 30 of the 4,000 Libyan students in the US had been recruited by US intelligence.
• Reports said between four and 640 Libyans allegedly involved in the attempt had been executed; US officials said some executions likely occurred.
• Qaddafi denied on Dec. 6, 1981 in an ABC interview that Libya had sent hit squads to kill Reagan or others.
• Libyan unsuccessful foreign adventures in Uganda and Chad; troops were withdrawn from Chad recently.
• 1981-12-19: Reported assassination attempt on Qaddafi while he was visiting a headquarters area; initial reports said eastern army commander Col. Khalifa Khadir was killed along with Qaddafi's driver. The article describes these reports as unconfirmed.
• 1982-01-05: Qaddafi resurfaced publicly to address the final session of the General People's Congress in Tripoli.
• 1982-01-09: Sudan's SUNA reported that Qaddafi had put down a coup attempt in Benghazi earlier in the month.
