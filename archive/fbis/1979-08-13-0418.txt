LIBYA

U.S. MANEUVERS NEAR TERRITORIAL WATERS OBSERVED 
LD110240 Tripoli Voice of the Arab Homeland in Arabic 2315 GMT 10 Aug 79 LD 
[Text] The U.S. 6th Fleet on 8 and 9 August carried out military maneuvers in the area 


north of 32.5 degrees latitude in the Mediterranean, that is, opposite the territorial 
waters of the Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah.

Previously, the U.S. 6th Fleet on 27, 28, 29 and 30 July carried out similar maneuvers 
in the same area north of latitude 32.5 degrees in the Mediterranean, that is, at the 
edge of Libyan Arab territorial waters. 


Warning Issued 
LD121632 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1400 GMT 12 Aug 79 LD 


{Text ] Tripoli--A source at the Foreign Secretariat has said that the American charge 
d'affairs in the Jamahiriyah has been summoned and invited to give clarifications on 
whether there is any substance in the reports carried by the paper NEW NORFOLK, published 
in the U.S. state of Virginia, and the New York TIMES on 10 August 1979. Quoting Anerican 
sources, the papers said that the United States would challenge, by force, those states 
whose territorial waters extend over more than 3 miles. 


The same source at the Foreign Secretariat said that the American charge d'affaires was 
told that the Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah would defend its rights to its 
territorial waters fixed at 12 miles, and also its territorial waters at the Gulf of 
Sidra which it regards as an internal sea and an integral part of its territories under 
its sovereigty, in accordance with the proclamation issued in 1973 in this respect and 
conveyed at the time by the Jamahiriyah to all states and international organizations. 


AFP REPORTS FURTHER DEVELOPMENTS IN IMAM AS-SADR CASE 
OW112247 Paris AFP in English 2233 GMT 11 Aug 79 WW 


[Text] Paris, Aug 11 (AFP)--The mystery of the disappearance almost a year ago in 
Tripoli (Libya) of Lebanese Shi'ite Moslem leader Imam Musa as-Sadr deepened today with 
a claim that Italian legal authorities had established that, contrary to Libyan 
declarations, he had never gone on to Italy. 


A Lebanese Shi'ite delegation visiting here accused Libyan leader Mu'ammar al-Qadhudhafi 
of keeping the imam prisoner along with his two companions, Sheikh Muhanad Yaqub, 
another religious leader, and Abbas Badrad-Din a Lebanese journalist. 


A member of the delegation, professor of international law Husayn Kana'n, told AFP that 
the Italians “had announced their findings after an inquiry involving French legal 
authorities and the international criminal police organisation (INTERPOL)." 


The imam, 52, vanished after leaving Beirut for Libya on Aug 25 last year. A statement 
by the Libyan Government on Sept 17 said he had left Libya on Aug 31 by plane for Italy, 
but this was denied by the [Italian airline, Alitalia. 


The Shi'ite delegation said Libya was holding the imam because of "conflicting political 
viewpoints on the war in Lebanon, in that the imam has announced he accepts Syrian nediation 
as a preliminary to a national Lebanese reconciliation," and "divergent religious viewpoints 
concerning Colonel al-Qadhdhafi's interpretations of Islamic law." 


The delegation added that a conference due to be held on the As-Sadr affair here last 
Monday involving representatives from Iran, Syria, Algeria, Libya and the Palestine 
Liberation Organisation (PLO) did not take place "because the Libyans failed to turn up."

The efforts of the Iranian ambassador to persuade the Libyans to take part "met with a 
categoric refusal" the delegation said. It accused Colonel al-Qadhdhafi of "serious 
violation of human values and principles of Islam, which considers holy the intangible 
character of men of religion" and called on countries throughout the world and interna- 
tional organisations, as well as Arab and Islamic heads of state to "end" this situation.
