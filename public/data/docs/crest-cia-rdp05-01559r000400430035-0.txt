Prime Minister al-Mahdi on Flood, Aid Plans 
NC2208153588 Cairo MENA in Arabic 1500 GMT

[Text] Khartoum, 22 Aug (MENA)—Sudanese Prime 
Minister al-Sadiq al-Mahdi has criticized the way in 
which the foreign press has reported the recent events in 
Sudan. He added that some correspondents described 
Sudan as if it were drowning, as if it were plagued with 
epidemics, and as if locusts were threatening it for the 
first time in 1,000 years. 

Most of Al-Mahdi’s criticism was directed at the BBC. 
He accused that corporation, as well as newspapers and 
other press establishments, of exaggerating the situation 
in Sudan in their reports. 

He affirmed that the Sudanese Government welcomes 
all journalists of whatever nationality, provided that 
they commit themselves to reporting facts. The prime 
minister stressed that Sudan is open and ready to pro- 
vide them with whatever they demand. 

Ina statement before the Constituent Assembly today on 
the recent floods and torrential rains in the country, 
Al-Mahdi pointed out these disasters had left 73 persons 
dead and 215 others injured. He added that 120,705 
buildings, including houses, schools, and other installa- 
tions, have been demolished in Khartoum, not to men- 
tion the damage which has been caused in the Northern, 
Eastern, and Central Provinces. 

The prime minister said that structural flaws primarily 
contributed to the extensive damage to buildings in the 
capital. He added that technical committees are now 
working on a plan to redesign and reconstruct the 
capital. 

The Sudanese prime minister disclosed in his statement 
that Sudan has 22 planes at its disposal to fight the 
spread of the locusts and has enough pesticides to last 15 
days. 

Al-Mahdi said the locusts initially infested an area of 
§00,000 hectares but have now spread to an area covering 2 
million hectares. He emphasized that Sudan, supported by 
friends, will exert more efforts to fight this danger.

Answering allegations made by foreign radios that the

Turing to the issue of the floods, Al-Mahdi disclosed. 

that the water level of the Nile reached 16.8 meters 
today. However, he added, it is expected that the damage 
resulting from these floods will be less severe than that 
incurred in 1946, thanks to the big projects which have 
restricted the flow of water as well as the construction of 
the Al-Rusayris Reservoir, facilities that did not exist in 
1946. 

Pointing out that Khartoum is 360 meters above sea 
level, Al-Mahdi said that if we add to this the highest 
water level ever recorded—17.14 meters in 1946—then 
the highest the water level could reach is 377.14 meters. 
Theoretically, this means that all parts of Khartoum 
would be covered by water, he added. The prime minis- 
ter noted however that most of Khartoum’s installations 
have been built at 382 meters above sea level, thus 
rendering them safe from the threat of flooding. 

Al-Mahdi said that he has received a report on the 
readings of the water level in the Al-Daym area on. the 
Ethiopian hill. The report indicates that the level has 
dropped 10cm. This, he added, means that regardless of 
how big the flow might be today and tomorrow, the 
water level will recede after 3 days, because any drop in 
the Al-Daym readings will reflect on the Nile within the 
next 3 days. He noted that the volume of water in the 
Al-Rusayris Reservoir has dropped from 643 to 603 
cubic meters at the moment, adding it is expected to 
drop further in the coming 3 days. 

The Sudanese prime minister said that the [current] 
readings of the water levels do not portend a great threat. 
He acknowledged, however, that some islands, towns, 
and other areas have been flooded by the Nile inunda- 
tion and that damage has been incurred as a result. The 
prime minister pointed out that a reassuring situation 
will develop should the Nile water level recede in the
