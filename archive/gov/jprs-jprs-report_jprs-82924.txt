[Excerpt] Tripoli, 9 Feb (JANA)--In an interview carried by French television 
channel one Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi said: We have not heard that Ahmed Ben 
Bella has decided to use violence. However, we do know that Ben Bella is 
carrying out a campaign for the upholding of human rights in the world. This 
was noted in the course of his reply to a question on the discovery by French 
police of weapons in the house of the aide-de-camr of former Algerian President 
Ben Bella and claims that these weapons were from Libya and that Libva is 


giving all means of support to sen Bella. 


The leader explained: According to what we have heard, Ben Bella is against 
violence and the use of weapons. We do not believe that Ben Bella has been 
engaged in any political activity, especially in opposition work against 
regimes in the region: we have become aware of the so-called Islamic opposi- 
tion. However, this is an attempt to terrorise the living force in the Islamic 
and Arab worlds and to terrorize the Aram nation. The Arab nation is facing 
pressures and provications at various levels, and any initiative on our part 

is described as terrorism and violence. 


The leader went on tc say: Imperialism and Zionism are trying, through a 
psychological terrorist campaign they are waging against the Arab nation and 
the peoples of the Third World, to conceal the real terrorism they constantly 
practice in order to distract world attention and to allege that the Arab 
nation and the peoples struggling for freedom are the ones engaged in inter- 


national terrorism.

"In politics,’ ‘Abd al-‘Ati al-'Ubaydi explained to me, “there are no permanent 
good relations and no permanent bad relations." When he asked Colonel 
al-Qadnhdhafi to explain his policy toward the United States, he stressed that 
the break had not been made on Libya's initiative. "At the beginning of our 
revolution,” he said, "we thought we could establish good relations with the 
Western countries which, like us, are 'people of the book,’ whereas we were 
very hostile to the Eastei:n Bloc countries. History has shown that the United 
States deliberately declared itself our enemy, going so far as to directly 
threaten our territorial waters 18 months ago in the Gulf of Sidra affair, 
whereas the USSR and its allies have never done anything against us." 


Al-Qadhdhafi nonetheless denied that he has become a "Satellite" of the USSR. 

"We buy from that country arms which are necessary for our self-defense, and we 
have relations of friendship and cooperation in keeping with our mutual interests. 
Anybody who respects our sovereignty can have such relations with us.” 


Of course the Libyan leader thinks that the United States intrinsically ceserves 
condemnation for its imperialist policy throughout the world and its "submission 
to Zionist interests'’ in the Arab world. The USSR however, al-Qadhdhafi added 
while conceding that, being a superpower itself, its strategy is not always in 
keeping with its principles, “respects other countries' independence, supports 
progressive movements, does not establish military bases outside its borders and 
does not threaten any country's sovereignty.” 


Not even that of Afghanistan? We asked. 


"T think,'' al-Qadhdhafi replied, "that the Soviet intervention in Afghanistan 
is 4S legitimate as our forces’ intervention in Chad in 1980. In both cases a 
lawful government asked its ally to give it help against foreign interventions. 
I would regard the USSR as being in the wrong if the Afghan government asked it 
to withdraw and it did not, as we did in Chad." 


Behind the oratorical gloss one nonetheless has the impression that "something 
has changed” in diplomatic circles in Tripoli, and it is justified by the need 
for Libya to break its isolation and remove the risk of exclusive dependence on 
the USSR by diversifying its relations and trade. Some people think that al- 
Qadhdhafi, faced with his relative African failure after the fiasco of the two 
OAU summits, with the collapse of the Arab world which is pushing his dreams of 
unity into the limbo of an improbable future and also with his own economic 
difficulties, is seeking not only new partners but also other interlocutors 
with whom to discuss the future of a threatened planet, of which he thinks he 


is now one of the great men. 


Defining his foreign policy principles, al-Qadhdhafi told us that it was 
characterized mainly by frankness and truth. Many people doubt that. But as 
one French diplomat said: 'Why not take him at his word?" After all, when 
Claude Cheysson came to Tripoli he immediately saic that there were major 
areas of agreement between the two countries, and just as many areas of dis- 
agreement, including the near east question on which the French and Libyan 
positions were diametrically opposed. It is therefore pointless to discuss 
that problem, our minister apparently then stressed.
