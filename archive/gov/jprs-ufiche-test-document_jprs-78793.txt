GERMAN COMPANY SEEKS TO SELL ROCKETS TO ARAB COUNTRIES 
Tel Aviv HA'ARETZ in Hebrew 1 Jun 81 p 2 
/Article by Yossi Melman: "German Rocket Company Negotiating With Muslim Countries"/ 


[Text/ The German Rocket Company, OTRAG, is trying to crange giant transactions for 
the sale of rockets of Muslim countries including Pakistan, Syria, Libya, and Saudi 
Arabia, according to a special interview which will be broadcast tomorrow (today) on 
the BBC program "Panorama." 


OTRAG's involvement in the region began after its plan to establish a rocket site in 
Zaire in Africa failed after a mysterious explosion in which six German technicians 
were killed. Because of pressure from company investors, its president, Lutz 

Kaiser, was forced to seek new locations for test sites. In May 1979 Kaiser proposed, 
through a German businessman, Klaus Nickel, that the President of Pakistan, Zia- 
ul-Haq, purchase information from OTRAG despite his knowledge of Pakistan's intentions 
to procure nuclear weapons. However, because of financial problems, the president 

of Pakistan did not agree to this. 


Afterwards, OTRAG proposed to the Syrians to set vy a rocket launching site in their 
country and to build for them, according to the text of the contract which became 
available to the producers of the program, 2-stage tactical rockets with a range of 
300 km and 5-stage rockets with a range of 2000 km, however these contacts also were 
fruitless. 


An approach to the Saudis 


The German company then approached Saudi Arabia. Through the German General 

(Retired) Harold Wust, contact has been established between OTRAG and Saudi military 
personnel. It is evident from documents that on 20 Jun 1980 a meeting took place in 

the offices of the German company Miflan in Frankfurt at which OTRAG people were present. 
It was proposed at the meeting that the company establish a rocket launching site in 
Saudi Arabia and manufacture hundreds of rockets in a deal valued at about $4 

billion. Other companies such as Telefunken and AEG were also to have been involved. 


When the plan failed, the Germans approached Libya. At the conclusion of the 
negotiations, an agreement worth $100 million was signed. Qadndhafi granted OTRAG 

a test site about 800 km south of Tripoli, and the Germans will soon present him with 
the first rocket. 











"Only for Peaceful Purposes" 


The German company maintains that some of the documents presented are spurious and 
that it is involved only in building rockets for peaceful purposes such as the 
launching of communications and weather satellites. However, Fred Weimer, an 
international businessman who has served as an intermediary between OTRAG and 
Zairian President Mobutu, said on the program: "We are not children. These toys 
can be easily converted to weapons." 


The program also points out the fact that the distance from the test site in 
Libya to Tel Aviv is exactly 2000 km, the range of the rockets which OTRAG offered 
to the Syrians. 


Prime Minister Menahem Begin says in an interview for the program: "Every supply 
of weapons to countries like Libya is a crime against humanity." 


The producers of the program worked on its production about 1 year and were assisted 
by tens of interviews, some of which were conducted also by HA'ARETZ reporters in 
Germany and Britain. 


The producer of the program, Christopher Olgietti, told HA'ARETZ that while collecting 
the material, he obtained information also on attempts by OTRAG to offer deals to 
Jordan and Iraq, however, he was unable to validate this information and therefore 

it was decided not to include it in the program.

SAUDI LOAN TO SOMALIA--The Saudi Fund for Development has granted Somalia a 
soft-term loan of Riyals 33.3 million ($10 million), the Somali news agency 
reported last week. At 2 per cent interest, the 15-year loan is to finance 
the completion of the Juba sugar project at Marerey, in the south of the 
country, the report said. The third from the Saudi Fund, the latest loan 
brings the fund's total lending to Somalia to SR332.6 million ($100 million).

[Text] On 1 July, the term of Robert McNamara as head of the World Bank ended 
and the term of Clausen, formerly president of the Bank of America, began. The 
ceremony marking the change of office is expected to include discussions of the 
major problems handled by the World Bank and the need for continued attention 
to these problems on the part of the new president within the framework of his 
implementation of loan programs for fiscal years 1981 through 1985. 


In view of McNamara's respect for Lebanon and his admiration for the implementa- 
tion of Lebanese projects despite unstable security conditions, he promised that 
the issue of Lebanese reconstruction and Lebanon's applications for additional 
loans would be among the issues addressed in the discussions he will conduct 
with his successor, Clausen . He pledged to recommend that the bank be pre- 
pared to go to the maximum limit in granting loans to Lebanon short of fear for 
the safety of its investments. 


McNamara made these remarks to Dr Muhammad ‘Atallah, president of the Reconstruc- 
tion and Development Council in Lebanon, in a recent meeting in New York. The 
two men discussed the Lebanese Government's application for a new loan to finance 
a number of construction projects, including certain additional works at the port 
of Beirut, expansion of the port of Tripoli, and participation in a project to 
recondition the country's highways which will cost an estimated total of 1.5 bil- 
lion Lebanese liras. 


The World Bank was surprised by the completion of the works financed by the pre- 
vious loan of $50 million. These works included the project to renovate the 
port of Beirut which was completed during the war years amid falling shells and 
DShK (heavy machine gun) fire and despite the violence of the battles that took 
place in the area.

resources of international institutions during the months of April and May while

LOAN TO YUGOSLAVIA--The Libyan National Bank has granted Yugoslavia a $150m finan- 
cial credit, repayable over seven years. The loan, which follows the visit to Bel- 
grade, earlier this month, by Libya's second-in-command, Major Abdelsalam Jailoud, 
will help finance Yugoslavia's balance of payments deficit, anticipated this year 
to be $1.8bn. Yugoslavia wants to borrow just over $2bn this year to finance its 
deficit and top up its foreign currency reserves. Deputy Prime Minister Zvone 
Dragan announced last month that Kuwaiti-based banks would lend the country $250m.

Paris AFRICA AFP in English No 2816, 4 Aug 81 p 11 

[Text] Rabat, August 2--Mohamed Bensaid, a long-time Moroccan opposition figure 
who recently returned from 18 years’ exile in France, has been detained for 


24 hours and had his Passport seized, sources close to him said today. 


Mr. Bensaid, a former leader of the pre-independence Moroccan Liberation Army, 
was held by police in Casablanca from Friday until yesterday, the sources said. 


They said Mr. Bensaid had just come back from Iraq, where he attended celebrations 
of the 13th anniversary of the accession to power of the Baath Party. 


The police questioned him about his activities while in Baghdad, the sources 
Said, and "temporarily confiscated" his passport, 


Mr. Bensaid lived in France from 1963 until last March 8, when he returned to 
Morocco after an amnesty was decreed by King Hussan II. 


The opposition leader had been sentenced to death in absentia in November 1963,
