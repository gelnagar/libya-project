Reason for Carter Defeat 
LDO51402 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 5 Nov 80 


[Excerpts] Ronald Reagan has become the 40th President of the United States. Carter 
has conceded defeat. 


The Carter administration's defeat is due to a number of things. The most important is 
his political problems, among which is the case of the American hostages--the case 

which Carter deliberately held on to until the last moment as the last key for his entry 
into the black house. Political observers believe that an immediate reply to the Iranian 
Majlis for the release of the hostages could have saved Carter despite his many fail- 
ures in strategic issues, foremost of which is the Arab-Zionist conflict. 


Will the new American administration (?listen), will the new American President benefit 
from the record of American policy and will he take a lesson which would greatly benefit 
him in laying down the bases of the American people's relationship with the rest of the 
peoples of the world founded on [words indistinct] and ridding the American side of the 
Zionist influence? 


Thus, Carter's downfall is considered a judgment by the American people on the policy of 
terrorism pursued by the American Government, a cry against imperialist intervention in 
the world and a protest against the expansionist policies hostile to the masses. Wil] 

this downfall be the beginning of the end of the unjust agreements, those of Camp David 


and will the new American administration realize that the policy of repression and in- 
justice will not last long?
