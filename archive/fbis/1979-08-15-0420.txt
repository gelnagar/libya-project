LIBYA

POLISARIO, SAHARAN LEADERS ARRIVE FOR TALKS 
LD141834 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1730 GMT 14 Aug 79 LD 
[Text] Tripoli--The secretary general of the POLISARIO, Mchammed Abdul Aziz, arrived 
in Tripoli this evening for talks on the latest situation in the Sahara, following the 
signing of the agreement with Mauritania. The prime minister of the Sahran Democratic 
Arab Republic, Mohamed Lamine, also arrived in Tripoli. 
The secretary general emphasized to JANA the appreciation and respect of ail revolutionaries 
in the world for the First of September Revolution, because of its courageous and 
consistent stand toward the cause of mankind and freedom, its opposition to imperialism 
and the enemies of peoples and its absolute support for the national liberation movements 
that are struggling for freedom and dignity. 


They were welcomed at the airport by a representative of the Arab liaison office and the 
head of the POLISARIO office in Tripoli. 


JANA: U.S. SECRETLY PLANS TO DROP SUPPORT FOR AS-SADAT 


LD141118 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 0700 GMT 14 Aug 79 LD 


[Text] Washington--"He is not required to stay any longer"--this is the expression 
Which has been repeated more than once in American White House circles in the past week. 
What this refers to is the vanquished Egyptian president himself.

Information that has leaked from some of those who are close to the White House says that 
the American intelligence service in actually busy, with President Carter's authorization, 
searching for a new agent in place of the vanquished to implement the remainder of the 
[words indistinct] American plai against the Arab nation. 


This information underlines the disappointment that is sweeping the American administra- 
tion after it has lost its gamble on the vanquished as a trump card in the region for 
implementing its plot against the Arab cause. 


The United States based its calculations on the expectation that the Arab leaders would 
flock to the White House to offer their support for its schemes after the Egyptian 
President had signed his document of surrender to the enemy and accepted its conditions, 
which the Arabs have continuously rejected, having condemned in advance any who are 
inclined to accept it. The United States was encouraged in its reliance on indications 
to this effect by the vanquished president's suggestion that he represented the center 
of gravity and the magnetic pole of the Arab region and that all the Arabs would follow 
him to the White House expressing fealty for his treachery and his signatures of liqui- 
dation. 


The information, described as "top secret," stresses that the American administration 

is convinced that these claims by the vanquished were no more than hallucinations, which 
he used to repeat to his friend Kissinger after the vanquished had had his daily dose of 
cannabis. 


The information adds that what has increased the conviction of the American administration 
that it has actually backed a losing donkey is this categorical rejection with which the 
Arab people have confronted all American schemes for which the vanquished was their 

tool, a rejection which has turned into a comprehensive and total boycott of the vanquished 
Egyptian president which is represented by the expulsion of the Egyptian regime from the 
Arab League--which has moved from the Egyptian capital to Tunis--and also by the suspen- 
sion of Egypt's membership of all Arab organizations and the possible suspension of its 
membership of other international organizations including the organizaticn of nonalined 
states. 


This information, which has been obtained from a source close to the White House, notes 
that the European countries do not share the U.S. policy in the Arab region nor its 
feeble dependence on the Egyptian president to implement its policy. Hence, a feeling 
of caution prevails throughout the American administration, which is strengthened by an 
intention not to gamble with American interests in the Arab region for the sake of the 
vanquished Egyptian president, who, it has been proved, holds no weight or appeal, as 
he claimed, and subsequently will not be able to draw in the Arabs behind hin. 


This information finally concludes that to find an alternative for "As-Sadat" has 
recently become the most urgent preoccupation of the American administration.
