‘stic Service in Hebrew 1500 


Text] Another round of the oil talks between Israel and the United States has ended with- 
out results. This has been reported by our correspondent, Gadi Sukenik. The talks dealt 
with the conditions according to which the United States will supply Israel with fuel 


for emergency cases. The joint communique issued by the two sides says that the talks 

were helpful and certain progress was achieved but the two delegations expressed concern 
at the fact that no agreement has yet been reached. Our correspondent comments that a 
certain disappointment was expressed by the Israeli side at the intransigence shown by 
the Americans in the deliberations. Th il talks will continue in Washington at the 


beginning next month.

in Hebrew 23 Sep 80 p 1] 


iffairs correspondent ‘Oded Granot report: "Israei Accepts Jordan's Explanation 
the Iraqi Planes' Landing Is Not Aimed Against It"] 
LT Israeli sources are inclined to accept 1.e Jordanian explanation transmitted via 
the United States that the landing of the Iraqi Air Force cargo planes at the airfield 
northeast of Amman over the last 24 hours is not an anti-Israeli act but rather a measure 
adopted by the Iraqis to remove these planes from the battlefield arena where they may be 
hit by the Iranian bombardments. 


sources confirmed the report broadcast on the late television news yesterday in which 
sported that several Iraqi Air Force planes, most of them Soviet-made heavy 
s, landed at a Jordanian airport and that Jordan asked the United States to 
clear to Israel that this is not a move against Israel. 


was reported that the information on the Iraqi planes' landing in Jordan was yesterday 
rought to Prime Minister Begin's attention at a meeting with U.S. Ambassador in Israel 


Samuel Lewis. 

Israel’ sources say that regardless of the Iraqi-Iranian conflict a significant rapproche- 
ment has occurred over the last few months in the relations between Baghdad and Amman, 
including the sphere of military cooperation, and it is thus clear why the Iraqis chose 

to transfer the planes to a Jordanian shelter. 


Ya'aqov Erez, MA'ARIV military correspondent, reports: Growing disquiet is prevailing 
within the Syrian Government due to the Iraqi-Jordanian military rapprochement and the 
threat inherent in this to Syrian borders in the south and in the east. According to 
Israeli estimates Syria will not be able to be passive when a practical alliance is 
developing and forming between Iraq, putting at the latter's disposal aiz bases that serve 
as shelter and refuge to the Iraqi planes.
