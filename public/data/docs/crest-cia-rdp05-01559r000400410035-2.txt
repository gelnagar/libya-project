Summary (AI): A Tripoli radio and television broadcast of Qadhdhafi's 16 April 1986 speech, two days after the U.S. raid, defiant about the attack on his home and urging Arab unity and revolution. The text names no Tripoli locations beyond his house and tent, and gives no casualty figures.
• Qadhdhafi said the raid used 33 attack aircraft, escorted by about 100 fighters, with about 30 tankers for refuelling, and claimed the target was his house and tent.
• Qadhdhafi claimed Libya had munitions stockpiled and could fight with 1 million fighters.
• Qadhdhafi said he decided not to include the Mediterranean in an exclusion zone, and warned Italy and Spain to stop the U.S. 6th Fleet using their ports.
• Qadhdhafi said France had denied overflight rights, forcing the U.S. aircraft to fly about 4,000 km.

[Text] First, we deal with the situation in Libya where Colonel 
al-Qadhdhafi has made his first public appearance since Amer-

ended speculation about his own personal safety following the

injured two of his sons. Yesterday, there was also speculation that 
a coup attempt against Col al-Qadhdhafi was under way with 
reports on gun fire and unexplained disturbances in Tripoli, but 
the Libyans have firmly denied this. To find out whether the 
Libyan leader’s television address has defused any of the tension 
inside libya, Mann Price spoke a short time ago to one of the 
BBC’s correspondents in Tripoli, David Willie. 

[Begin recording] [Willie] Well, it had the curious and absolutely 
immediate effect of causing some demonstrations in the street 
outside our hotel of young men who were dancing and carrying

off their lights, we light our streets and we sing and go about our 
business, and this is what they were trying to show was the correct 
attitude now, they realized that there was to be no further 
retaliations by the colonel in a military sense and, therefore, the 
risks of a general conflict, perhaps, has diminished.
