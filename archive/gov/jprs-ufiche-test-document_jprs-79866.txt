a new one. In the past year, there were mediation efforts and initiatives--in 
short, it was a year of nonsense talk. However, what we hope is that the new 
year will be one of work and self-determination for Lebanon. I hope that the 
new year will be a good one for all my brothers in free Lebanon and for all the 
Lebanese people and Lebanon. I also hope that it will be a year of deliverance 
[from the crisis], liberation, unity, sovereignty and independence. 


Anyway, as I said, the new year will be full of events, including the current 
talks about the presidential elections or extending of the term of office in 
occupied Lebanon. On our part, our views on both things are well known. What- 
ever they do in occupied Lebanon, be it elections or extending the term of office 
or anything, it is illegal because the Chamber of Deputies lost its legality 

long ago and no longer represents the people. It is unreasonable for an illegal 
body to [claim to] issue legal things.

NATURE OF LIBYAN REPORTS TO HALT CIVIL WAR DESCRIBED 
Paris AL-NAHAR AL-‘ARABI WAL-DUWALT in Arabic No. 236, 9-15 Nov 81 pp 8,99 


/Article by Rashid Fayid: "The Government: The Damascus Tripatite Summit-- 
Tripoli Did Not Offer and Ba‘abda Did Not Welcome a Libyan Initiative''/ 


/Text/ The minister of tourism, Mr Marwan Hammadah, was in the coastal tourist 
village of al-Andalus, "getting prepared" psychologically to greet the waiting 
period, which he had expected would be long, for an appointment to receive Col 
Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi, when a messenger brought him the news setting the date for 
that evening. 


The Lebanese minister, who had gone to Tripoli from Nouakchott, the capital of 
Mauritania, by way of Rabat, bringing letters on his trip from the president to 
the heads of the states he visited, had expected that he would not meet the Libyan 
president before 2 September, that is, 4 days after he arrived, because the Libyan 
Arab Jamahiriyah was celebrating the 12th anniversary of its revolution. 


Just as the scheduling came as a surprise, so did the interest al-Qadhdhafi showed 
in the Lebanese issue. He presented the minister with the broad outlines of what 
he declared the following day in his speech at the main celebration of the anni- 
versary of the revolution concerning a Libyan initiative to end the war in Lebanon. 


However, the initiative had originated before that. On 11 June, in the Evacuation 
Day speech, al-Qadhdhafi declared that he would launch an initiative to end the 
war in Lebanon, and on the 19th of that menth at the Nairobi summit of the Organi- 
zation of African Unity the foreign relations secretary of the Popular Committee 
of the Popular Bureau, Mr ‘Abd-al-‘Ati al-'Ubaydi, declared that al-Qadhdhafi 
would follow up on his initiative to restore relations with Saudi Arabia, Iraq and 
Morocco on an Arab and African tour to put an end to the wars in Lebanon, the Gulf 
and the Sahara. 


The deputy Col Fu'ad Lahhud says that he was the first one to indicate to Lt Col 
al-Qadhdhafi to make a move to end the war in Lebanon and that that dated back to 
the revolution celebrations of 1 September 1980. Meanwhile a Libyan diplomatic 
source points out that the idea was raised after he, personally and through 
friends, had sounded out the situation from more than one Lebanese party. 








However, whatever the origins may have been, the Libyan party's efforts so far do 
not constitute the initiative at issue; rather, one can sense its features by seek- 
ing out the common denominators among the local parties to the crisis following 
Syrian agreement to the Libyan move. 


Before the “celebrations of the first," Col al-Qadhdbafi visited Damascus and in a 
meeting with the regional and national commands of the Ba'th Party presented his 
idea and received agreement, especially since he had founded the idea on three in- 
tegrated principles whose priorities were interconnected: 


Ending the war in Lebanon and attaining a national reconciliation. 


Protecting the Palestinian resistance and supporting its struggle to achieve its 
goals. 


Strengthening Syria's perseverance, guaranteeing its frontal terrain in Lebanon 
and putting the Syrian army back on the front with Israel. 


In another meeting, the colonel discussed his plan with the leaders of the National 
Movement and all detachments of the Palestinian resistance. Although he had set 
his mind on support, more than one person present expressed a fear that the move 
was fated to be suppressed and warned of a Lebanese quagmire. 


At the same time, indirect contact was made with the Lebanese Front, which showed 
its support. 


The Premises of Sounding out the "Situation" 
In any event, the Lebanese move was launched, on the basis of the following points: 


l. The withdrawal of Syrian forces from Lebanon and the gradual handing over of 
security affairs to the security forces designated by the legitimate authorities-- 
meaning, in practice, the army (which would lead to a discussion of the status of 
the army). 


2. The acquisition by Syria of security and military guarantees on the protection 
of its frontal terrain in Lebanon. 


3. The shift of Palestinian resistance personnel from Lebanese towns to the south, 
while granting /the resistance/ freedom of media and politicai action. 


4. Severance, by those parties dealing with Israel, of their relations with it. 


Under these headings, the Libyan Brotherhood Bureau, in its activity, broke itself 
up into two teams, on the one hand the secretary of the bi:veau, Mr Salih al-Daruqi, 
whose task basically was to meet the president, the premier, the minister of for- 
eign affairs, the Maronite patriarch, the papal emissary, the mufti of the republic 
and Shaykh al-'Aql, and, on the other, Messrs "Abd-al-Qadir Ghuqah and Muhammad 
al-Faytwri, who made visits among the other parties. 





The upshot of the visits was a concurrence of views among the parties, even on the 
points in dispute, as follows: 


1. A desire to put an end to the current situation, with all its aspects. 


2. The fact that the Lebanese situation is complex and §. connected to te Middle 
East crisis. 


3. The need to carry out political and constitutional reform. 


In the political sense the most prominent meetings were those which took place with 
the president, Shaykh Pierre al-Jumayyil and the Islamic Grouping. 


Lebanese sources reported that President Ilyas Sarkis welcomed the Libyan move in 
principle, /stating/ "Lebanon welcomes any initiative or assistance that will lead 
to ending the hemorrhaging of its territory and its people." He pointed out that 
the Lebanese consensus required now is a consensus on holding the presidertial 
elections on time and providing suitable conditions for them, and said "Any Arab 
party looking for a positive role in Lebanon must participate in this approach and 
provide a role for the army, since that is the instrument of the Legitimate author- 
ities. What is desired, as a result of this, is to create a climate for the next 
president which will enable him to achieve a reconciliation." 


The Tripartite Summit? 


After meeting with the secretary of the Brotherhood Bureau, Mr al-Daruqi, President 
Sarkis was asked about the possibility that a trilateral summit might be held in- 
cluding Presidents Sarkis, al-Asad and al-Qadhdhafi in Damascus, and he replied, 

in a statement to journalists at Ba'abda Palace, that some parties had raised the 
issue but that "there has been nothing official since." 


Syrian sources say that the idea of the trilateral summit is not a Libyan product 
but is a recommendation Shaykh Pierre al-Jumayyil presented to Messrs Ghuqah and 
al-Fayturi when they met with him, and that he prefaced his recommendation by say- 
ing that he believed that a meeting that included him and the Syrian president 
would result in frank talk which would put an end to current Syrian-Lebanese prob- 
lems. The Libyan party's response was that that was out of the question, at which 
point, as Syrian sources say, al-Jumayyil recommended that three presidents meet 
and that he, that is, the commander of the Lebanese forces, be part of the Lebanese 
delegation. The answer again was that that was out of the question. The final 
formulation of the proposal was that the trilateral summit would meet in Damascus 
and the parties to the Lebanese crisis, including Bashir al-Jumayyil and the com- 
manders of the National Movement, would be invited. 


At this meeting, and in similar ones with the Libyan party, the commander of the 
Lebanese forces expressed a position whose gist is as follows: 


l. A desire to spell out the Syrian role in Lebanon. 


2. Preparations to accept Libyan forces in place of the Syrian ones. 








3. The fact that the Palestinian issue is common to all Lebanese but that the 
Palestinian presence must lie within a framework that does not infringe upon 
Lebanese sovereignty. Pierre al-Jumayyil was quoted as saying, "I am prepared to 
defend the Palestinians if they discipline themselves and commit themselves to the 
agreement, and do not support some Lebanese parties against others." 


4. Support for anything that would guarantee Syria's security and reassure Syria 
about its security zone in Lebanon. 


5. The relationship between Lebanon and the Arabs, based on conditions that are 
taken for granted and are not open to discussion. 


The Chill Has Its Justifications 


In meetings with the other parties, /former/ President Sulayman Franjiyah, the 
Islamic Grouping, and the Islamic Council, the Libyan impression was positive al- 
though the Islamic Grouping, for example, expressed a negative point at the first 
meeting-- justified by one of its members, who was a former prime minister--that 

was based on the relationship between Libya and the National Movement and Tripoli's 
long neglect of the grouping that had arisen from that. 


These dialogues and meetings, and others which will be repeated, were conveyed and 
will be conveyed to Tripoli before consultation with Damascus and the Palestinian 
resistance, preparatory to the launching of the Libyan initiative itself. 


However, Lebanese milieux have observed a chill in Libyan activity in recent weeks. 
Sources in the Libyan Brotherhood Bureau attribute that to the desire to draw con- 
clusions from the meetings that have taken place up to now. Sources in the Pales- 
tinian resistance have stated that the reason is the Libyans’ reservations regard- 
ing the "media climate" in Beirut, which contains "an offensive against the Arabs 
and Arabhood," and that "the material being written against Syria is not compatible 
with the Libyan approach.” 


These sources quoted Libyan leaders in Tripoli as saying that their desire to have 
the Syrian forces leave Lebanon was "to serve the Syrian role in confronting Israel, 
so that the Syrian army could occupy its forward positions, not to get them out of 
Lebanon in the guise of a defeated party. In addition, the Libyan activity is on 
Lebanon's behalf and at the same time is on behalf of Syria and the Palestinian 


cause." 
What is the next stage in the process? 


The secretary of the Brotherhood Bureau, Mr Salih al-Daruqi, said that the process 
is still going through a state of crystallization of views among the parties and 
added “It would be better if one could say that we have offered the draft of an 
initiative, rather than that we have moved and failed." 


Al-Daruqi's statement does not negate information, emanating from the Palestinian 
resistance. to the effect that Libyan activity will slow down in anticipation of 
the presidential elections, the ripening of circumstances, and the firming up and 
consolidation of the national movement, so that the solution can be politically 
balanced, the way the military situation was balanced in the previous period.

Paris LE MONDE in French 28 Nov 81 p 34 
[Article by Roland Delcour: Morocco: On the Brink of Ruin"] 


[Text] Rabat.--Will Morocco go through a second year of drought? Millions of 
growers, and especially the small fellahs (peasants), gaze worriedly at the sky in 
this month of November 1981. 


The spectacle of the Moroccan countryside is distressing. Already transformed into 
a quasi-desert all the way to its richest provinces such as the Meknes plain, 
Morocco now presents its farmers with a soil which is resistant to the plow. In 
most of the northwestern regions, usually the most fertile, farmers can neither 
plow nor plant.
