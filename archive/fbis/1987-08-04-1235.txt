Prime Minister To Lift State of Emergency 
EA031226 Addis Ababa Domestic Service in Amharic 
0400 GMT 3 Aug 87 


[Text] Prime Minister al-Sadiq al-Mahdi yesterday announced 
that the state of emergency he declared 9 days ago was a [word 
indistinct] measure taken to curb illegal acts, and that he would 
therefore lift it as soon as possible. Al-Sadiq al-Mahdi made his 
statement on lifting the 1-year state of emergency at a news 
conference in Khartoum. The prime minister commented that 
the purpose of the state of emergency, which was declared by 
Sudan’s State Council 9 days ago, was to combat illegal dealing, 
armed robbery, and illegal possession of weapons. 


The [words indistinct] Khartoum, the nation’s capital, and other 
areas were shaken by a wave of student demonstrations and 
l-week strikes. Opposition parties and trade unions have 
denounced the state of emergency as unnecessary and an attempt 
to deny the people their freedom by strengthening the law. 


Al-MAJALLAH Interviews Foreign Minister Tawfiq 
PM031547 London AL-MAJALLAH in Arabic 
29 Jul-4 Aug 87 p 11 


[Interview with Foreign Minister Muhammad Tawfig by Sa‘idah 
Ramadan in Cairo; date not given] 


[Text] [Ramadan] There are those who say that your visit is due 
to recent visit by Dr al-Turabi [secretary general of the Sudanese 
National Islamic Front], especially following reports that there 
are attempts to open a channel with Egypt at the expense of the 
[Democratic] Unionist Party [DUP]. What is your reaction to 
this? 


[Tawfiq] The unionist ministers’ meeting in Cairo was an acci- 
dent more than a planned visit. I was on a special visit, but I can 
confirm that the National Islamic Front’s attempts and its 
adoption of the DUP’s slogans are an assumption on their part 
that the party is on its way to extinction and that they can replace 
the DUP. However, they are completely deluding themselves, 
since there is complete awareness that the Front’s attempts to 
establish relations with Egypt are a tactical rather than a strate- 
gic policy. 


[Ramadan] You met with Egyptian Foreign Minister Dr ‘Ismat 
‘Abd al-Majid during your visit to Cairo, which was the first 
meeting between the two nations’ foreign ministers since Egypt 
officially asked Sudan to define its position toward their joint 
defense agreement. So, can you describe the government's view 
on this agreement? 


[Tawfiq] Repeated talk about whether to cancel or maintain the 
joint defense agreement has become a dead letter. This is because 
the relationship between Sudan and Egypt has passed beyond this 
stage and is now more than a joint defense agreement. There 
should be consideration of a security plan for the Nile Valley. 
The word security itself is comprehensive and deep since it means 
both defense and nondefense. Actually, both today and when I 
was minister of information, I never heard of an official request 
from Egypt to Sudan to define its position toward the joint 


defense agreement. These matters concern Sudanese foreign 
policy and are the business of the Foreign Ministry and no one 
else. Therefore, if any opinion concerning foreign policy is 
expressed by someone else, it will be considered a personal 
opinion. 


[Ramadan] The media released press statements of your 
statements confirming that the joint defense agreement had been 
canceled. These statements, however, conflict with other declara- 
tions you made confirming that it will remain in effect. We would 
like this matter to be clarified. 


[Tawfiq] A false statement was attributed to me, one which 
quoted me as saying that the joint defense agreement had lapsed 
with the fall of Al-Numayri, just as other agreements had lapsed. 
The truth is that when I was asked about the meaning of Al-Sadiq 
al-Mahdi’s statements concerning this agreement, I stated that 
with the fall of Al-Numayri, all agreements concluded during his 
era had now lapsed. I said this simply to explain Al-Sadiq 
al-Mahdi’s statement; my opinion is known, since I have already 
declared it. I am one of those who believe that the Nile Valley’s 
security is a single whole that cannot be divided. 


[Ramadan] Are there any differences between the Council of 
Ministers and the Foreign Ministry over who should manage the 
nation’s foreign policy? 


[Tawfiq] The time has come for the Foreign Ministry to assume 
the responsibilities and powers of which it was deprived. During 
Al-Numayri’s rule, the Foreign Ministry had no effective role. 
During the transitional period and the period of democratic rule, 
the Foreign Ministry's powers remained distributed among the 
Ministerial Council, the Presidential Office, and other min- 
istries. Therefore, the Foreign Ministry must restore its respon- 
sibilities and powers so that it can implement the policy drawn 
up by the political leadership, indeed, participate in formulating 
this policy, which is in fact the essence of its existence. Otherwise, 
any policies formulated without the Foreign Ministry's partici- 
pation will be considered personal ideas. 


[Ramadan] Your meeting with Dr ‘Ismat ‘Abd al-Majid prior to 
the African summit coincided with reports on the success of 
Egypt’s mediation between Sudan and Ethiopia. What is the 
latest on this situation? 


[Tawfiq] The Sudanese prime minister on 6 April presented an 
initiative to those that have taken up arms in southern Sudan. 
President Mengistu Mariam then paid a visit to Egypt and held 
talks with President Mubarak on normalizing Sudanese- 
Ethiopian relations. President Mengistu also sent a message to 
Al-Sadiq Al-Mahdi on this same topic; it was delivered by the 
Ethiopian minister of information. This confirmed that Ethiopia 
agrees in principle with mediation. However, the success of these 
endeavors via the African summit rests on good intentions, due 
to the lack of trust between Sudan and Ethiopia. If this can be 
eliminated during the meetings between the parties concerned, 
the initiative will then succeed. 


[Ramadan] During his visit to Egypt, President Mengistu denied 
the existence of mediation between Sudan and Ethiopia. How do 
you explain this? 


[Tawfiq] What was meant was that the Sudanese-Ethiopian 
dispute does not require mediation. However, Sudan’s concern is 
that security should include the Horn of Africa, since this would 
convert the Red Sea into a zone of peace. 


[Ramadan] Is there coordination between Egypt and Sudan on 
some issues that concern the two countries at the conference? 


[Tawfiq] Yes, there is coordination on all issues such as the Gulf 
war that concern both countries. In this conflict, we seek a 
solution by approaching quarters that support peace — the 
Security Council or the ICO — make every possible effort for 
peace, and provide every type of assistance in this regard. 


[Ramadan] You accompanied the Sudanese prime minister dur- 
ing his recent tour. Al-Sadiq al-Mahdi, however, prolonged his 
stay in London. What was the reason for this? 


[Tawfiq] Al-Sadiq al-Mahdi’s visit to London is a personal visit 
that will last 1 week.
