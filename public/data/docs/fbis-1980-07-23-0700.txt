LIBYA

AL-QADHDHAFI DISCUSSES LIBYAN-EGYPTIAN TENSION 


DW211310 Hamburg DER SPIEGEL in German 21 Jul 80 pp 92-99 


{Interview by editors Adel Elias and Dr Bernhard Mueller-Huelsebusch with Libyan head 
of state Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi at an unidentified Libyan military camp: "Then I Will 
Turn Off the Oil Tap" ] 


[Text SPIEGEL: Colonel al-Qadhdhafi, no other chief of state persecutes his adversaries 
in exile as mercilessly as you do. Why did Libyan commandos kill Libyan emigrees in 
Bonn, London, Rome and Athens? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: First of all I want to make it clear that these are not harmless Libyans 
living abroad. They are pecple who have committed crimes against our people; they are 
thieves, corrupt businessmen and spies for the enemy. This is why they are being per- 
secuted by our revolutionary committees. 


SPIEGEL: You yourself, Colonel al-Qadhdchafi, have said: "Either the enemies return, 
or they are doomed to die." 


Al-Qadhdhafi: I did not say that. I gave these people a deadline, namely 11 June. 
SPIEGEL: But by that time the killer commandos had liquidated 10 Libyans living abroad. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Well, it is hard to keep the zeal of the revolutionary committees under 
control. I set this deadline because I wanted to anticipate further actions of the 
committee. Once the ultimatum had expired, I could no longer guarantee the lives of 
the enemies of the people. 


SPIEGEL: You cannot rid yourself of the responsibility just like that. Nothing hap- 
pens in Libya without your approval. According to an Arab saying, not even an ant 
moves here without your permission. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: That is true only in part. You see, the revolutionary committees are 
strong and powerful. Their aim is long and they have struck violently. 


SPIEGEL: So, if the committees decide that this or that Libyan is a traitor, he will 
be liquidated without a trial. Do you call that justice? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Personally I would naturally prefer a trial of the culprit. Only when 
his guilt has been clearly proven can he be executed. Once again, I am against liqui- 
dating the enemies of the people the way the committees have done it. 


SPIEGEL: The murder cf exiled Libyans ordered by Tripoli, Libyans living, say in 
Germany or Italy, is a gross violation of the sovereignty of these countries. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: These states can cooperate with our revolutionary committees by ex- 
traditing them to us. Why do these countries become so excited, complaining about 

the violation of their sovereignty like a maiden losing her innocence? Do they forget 
that their territory is a playground of the Israeli intelligence service? Has the 
Federal Republic, for example, forgotten that in 1960 the Israelis kidnapped a German 
from Argentina?

SPIEGEL: Do you mean Adolf Eichmann, the murderer of Jews? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Yes, he's the one. He was kidnapped from Argentina and executed in 
Israel. The Federal Republic approved of it. Why did it not protect Eichmann, a 
German, and why did it not make him face a German court? 


SPIEGEL: In this case the Israelis did not violate German sovereignty, but rather 
Argentine sovereignty. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Then I will give you another example: Tito sent some emissaries to the 
Federal Republic to liquidate his Croatian adversary there. But Tito's reputation in 
Germany did not suffer from that. Why can Tito do it and I cannot? I did not give the 
order myself, as has been mentioned before, to liquidate anybody on foreign territory. 


SPIEGEL: What would you say if Egyptian President as-Sadat, for example, were to have 
commandos chase and possibly kill his exiled adversaries living here in Libya? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Let him try ite But he ought to bear in mind what risk he would be 
running. Hundreds of thousands of Egyptians live in Libya, some of them as guest 
workers. If I were to send them back to their homeland, where there is mass unem- 
ployment anyway, As-Sadat would be in trouble. 


SPIEGEL: How many Libyans have obeyed your order to return home, and what happened to 
them? 


Al-Qadhdhafi: There have been several hundred so far. We have given them a chance 
to reintegrate into our society. They have uttered public self-criticism at the 
people's congress building and have promised that in the future they will work for 
our revolution. 


SPIEGEL: Yet there still are many exiled adversaries of Al-Qadhdhafi. There are 
allegedly several thousand of them in Egypt alone. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: By Allah, that is very exaggerated. Many Libyans who have stolen money 
from the people here have fled to Egypt. But they are fearful because they have a bad 
conscience. As-Sadat should not foster any hopes that these Libyans will support his 
treacherous Camp David policy. 


SPIEGEL: Your adversaries in Egypt are even supposed to have merged--under former 
Maj Omar (Meheishi), who staged an unsuccessful putsch against you in 1975. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: (Meheishi)? As-Sadat is responsible for him going insane. He is in an 
insane asylum right now. 


SPIEGEL: We would like to hear more of that story. 


Al-Qadhdhafi: Then I will tell you the story. (Meheishi) was raging mad that As-Sadat 
suddenly visited Jerusalem in 1978. He toré a portrait of As-Sadat to pieces, threw it 
into a toilet and urinated on it. The Egyptian intelligence services learned about the 
matter and tortured (Meheishi) until he really went insane. Now he is in a Tunisian 
insane asylum. 


SPIEGEL: Yet there are reliable reports that the Libyan opposition abroad is forming 
to act against you. Western diplomats say that resistance against your regime is grow- 
ing in Libya, too. You have locked up more than 2,000 political adversaries in prison.
