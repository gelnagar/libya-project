Summary (AI): A 1950 Naval Institute article recounts the 1804 loss of the U.S. ketch Intrepid in Tripoli harbor and the 1950 discovery and reburial of five American sailors' graves on a cliff overlooking the harbor. It is about the Barbary Wars, not Gaddafi-era Libya, so it has little bearing on the Tripoli map.
• Intrepid carried about 15,000 pounds of powder in bulk plus 100 thirteen-inch and nine-inch shells.
• Search for the graves was started in 1938 by harbormaster Mustafa Burchis and renewed in 1949.
• The original 1938 report was among American papers burned by the US embassy in Rome in 1941.
• The 1950 ceremony included a Scottish band and a British Army unit, and Marines fired volleys.
• The Spokane (CL-120) was the flagship of Rear Admiral Cruzen during the visit.
• 1804-09-04: Ketch USS Intrepid exploded in Tripoli harbor, killing thirteen officers and men. (13 killed)
• 1950-04-02: Five American sailors from the Intrepid were reburied with honors in a ceremony attended by the U.S. Navy and Tripoli officials, including the mayor Joseph Karamanli.
