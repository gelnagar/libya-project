LIBYA

Jallud Marks Egyptian Revolution Anniversary 
LD241124 Tripoli Voice of Greater Arab Homeland in Arabic 
1908 GMT 23 Jul 84 


{Editorial Report] Voice of Greater Arab Homeland at 1908 
GMT on 23 July broadcasts a 15-Minute speech by Major ‘Abd 
as-Salam Ahmad Jallud at the Jamahir Theater in Tripoli during 
a festive meeting to mark the anniversary of Egypt’s 23 July 
Revolution. 

In his address, Major Jallud said: ‘Free brothers! Rev- 
olutionary brothers 


“To live on the heritage of ‘Abd an-Nasir and on the glories of 
‘Abd an-Nasir or to attempt to gain glory at the expense of the 
heritage of ‘Abd an-Nasir is contrary to revolutionary ethics 
{applause}. Brothers, what is left of the 23 July Revolution? The 
attempt to create an opportunity and material for a speech to sing 
the glory of ‘Abd an-Nasir and the 23 July Revolution is an 
escapade. What has remained of the 23 July Revolution when we 
see the Zionists touring Cairo and Asyut and placing their feet 
on the tomb of ‘Abd an- Nasir? What has remained of the 23 July 
Revolution? What has remained of Arab dignity? We see the 
Zionists, out of their desire to annoy the Arab nation, celebrating 
in an Arab capital the anniversary of the occupation of Palestine. 
Brothers, what has remained of the peasants of Egypt? The 
workers of Egypt? The students of Egypt? The soldiers of Egypt? 
What has remained for them when Egypt has abandoned its 
dignity?” 


Major Jallud added: “The real celebration of the 23 July 
Revolution should be in carrying a gun and fighting from one 
quarter to another, from one street to another, from one school 
to another, from one college to another, and from one garrison to 
another to regain dignity and honor [applause].” 


After paying tribute, Jallud recalled that ‘Abd an-Nasir had once 
addressed Al-Qadhdhafi as being the trustee of Arab national- 
ism. This placed responsibilities on our shoulders, Jallud added.

In the Jamahiriyah, he said, we have chosen to celebrate the 
anniversary by holding our gun tightly in our hands and by 
training the people on the use of weapons as well as by reaffirm- 
ing the people’s authority. To celebrate the anniversary in a 
traditional way is an insult to ‘Abd an-Nasir, Jallud added. ‘Abd 
an-Nasir was history and a historic epic. To celebrate the anni- 
versary we should be faithful to the principles of ‘Abd an-Nasir. 


Major Jallud said: “Despite the fact that the people of Lebanon 
are torn by civil war and sectarian fighting, they did not join the 
Camp David agreement as the second Arab state. Instead, they 
put an end to the series of unilateral agreements which the enemy 
wants to impose on us, one Arab country after another.” How- 
ever, Jallud added, the Egyptian people joined Camp David. 
Instead of being our leader, Egypt became sword drawn on our 
heads. Egypt, with all its capabilities, placed itself on our enemy’s 
side, like Iraq. To celebrate the anniversary, we should resolve to 
carry a gun to defeat the forces of surrender, exploitation and 
dictatorship, as well as capitalism and reaction, against which 
‘Abd an-Nasir fought and in return for which they waged battles 
against him and against the Pan-Arab line he pursued. 


Major Jallud added: “The victory of the Lebanese masses is a 
victory for the Libyan people. When women, children and old 
people struggle, it is a beautiful picture. (?We saw) unarmed 
people, without a government, struggling. The unarmed Leb- 
anese masses, especially the Shi‘ite and [word indistinct] masses, 
defeated the [word indistinct] and fascist forces. The victory was 
a victory for the Libyan masses. The victory of the Lebanese 
masses is the beginning of the emergence of the [words indis- 
tinct}.” The real celebration of this anniversary, Jallud noted, is 
the celebration of the Lebanese national resistance. “The heroes 
who drive the booby-trapped vehicles — the vehicles loaded with 
dynamite and explosives — and who storm the NATO command 
garrisons and the commands of the Zionist forces have every 
right to be proud of celebrating ‘Abd an-Nasir because they are 
faithful to him” 


Major Jallud later said: “Free brothers, we were surprised to 
see the Egyptian national movement entering the elections bat- 
tles. We were surprised. They said that they would achieve 
victories through the elections. But what was the result? We said 
that this futile method and this traditional way of thinking cannot 
restore to the Egyptian people their dignity. Reagan and the 
Western circles sent cables to Husni Mubarak congratulating 
him on the sound democracy in Egypt. Egypt has become an 
example of democracy! Whose democracy? Is it the democracy 
of feudalism and the bourgeoisie forces and the dead party forces 
against which ‘Abd an-Nasir rebelled? What democracy is this? 
The mere fact that Reagan has congratulated Mubarak on the 
democracy in Egypt should make the people of Egypt and their 
living and national forces rebel. This is their democracy! “We in 
the Jamahiriyah, Major Jallud added, were not taken by sur- 
prises. We knew the inevitable result in advance. This should be 
a lesson to the Egyptian national movement and to the Nasirite 
forces.
