Casey’s assault on Libya leaks 

By SANDY GRADY 
Knight-Ridder Newspapers Writer 

DON’T usually work up a sweat defending 
the honor of The Washington Post. That 
rich, fat paper has a Murderer’s Row of 

legal hired guns to handle its feuds. 

Same thing for Newsweek, Time, The New 
York Times, and The Washington Times. All of 
those folks have six-figure lawyers to do their 
heavy lifting. 

But when CIA Director Bill Casey talks 
about throwing newspapermen in jail, he’s got 
my attention. 

I’m not sure who Casey has in mind for the 
hoosegow — Post publisher Katherine Gra- 
ham, editor Ben Bradlee, or one-time reporter 
Bob Woodward. Hard to tell because when 
Casey gets mad, his jowls shake and he 
stammers like a hot Model-T Ford. 

But Casey’s threatening to invoke a 1950s 
secrecy law that could cost somebody $10,000 
and 10 yearg in the slammer. 

Like the President he serves, Casey blows 
his stack about officials spilling secrets and 
reporters puntos them. If Ron and Bill had 
their druthers, the town would have a lie 
detector behind every file cabinet. 

But Casey’s attempt to pin a rap on The Post 
and four other news outfits is laughable. 

“We've already got five absolutely cold 
violations,” Casey told The Post’s editors. 

The way I understand it, Casey and the 
President are burned because The Post and 
others spilled the beans that the United States 
has been “reading Khadafy’s mail." Precisely, 
they're mad because reporters wrote that U.S. 
code-breakers intercepted cables between 
Moammar Khadafy’s headquarters and the

Casey’s right. Somebod Piabbed about

The leakers were at the top. In truth, the 
U.S. Government, trying to bulld support for 
its Libyan air strike, took the extraordinary 
gamble of disclosing intelligence picked up by 
National Security Agency (NSA) experts who 
broke Khadafy’s diplomatic codes. 

Ronald Reagan himself was the Chief

from Tripoli... to conduct the terrorist attack.
