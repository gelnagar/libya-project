[Text] Not too long ago, a delegation of Soviet social organizations headed 
by Candidate Member of the CPSU.Central Committee. and Deputy Chairman of 

the USSR State Committee for Inventions and Discoveries, Yu. N. Pugachev, 
visited Libya's capital, Tripoli. Our good friend Yegipta Abdurrakhman 
al~Khamisi, laureate of the international Lenin prize "For strengthening 
peace between peoples" and revolutionary poet, flew with us to Tripoli. Our 
delegation was flying to a conference dedicated to the 15th anniversary of 
the Libyan revolution. 

The flight was like any flight...A stop in Vienna. A cup of hot CORECR es 
Then a leisurely trip in search of our little waiting room... "Amsterdam,' 
"Paris," "Tel-Aviv" and finally our waiting room from where we must set out 
to Tripoli... 

I remember a military parade at a seaside square in Tripoli. Powerful tanks 
and artillery moved forward, column after column, along the seashore which — 
was visible from our platforms. The words which Mu'ammar Gedpanaed ees 
there at the square stayed in my mind. 

M. Qadhdhafi sharply criticized the aggressive, adventurist policy of 
American imperialism in various areas on the planet and, in particular, 
in the Near East. 

"Libya," M. Qadhdhafi emphasized, "is in favor of creating a broad front of 
the powers wich are struggling aentagt the intrigues of American imperialism 
the world over." 

Having been in Tripoli scarcely more than a week and participating directly 
in the conference itself and a great number of other friendly and business 
meetings, I.no longer thought that we would meet directly with Qadhdhafi 
himself. Everyday on television, literally until nighttime, we saw how 
busy Qadhdhafi was, how much he had to appear. But my friend, Abdurrakhman

al-Khamisi, an untiring optimist, assured me: "There will be a meeting." 
And the meeting did indeed take place. In the early evening, the day before 
our flight to Tunis, Omar Khamdi, one of Qadhdhafi's aides for public 
organizations, suddenly came to us at the hotel and invited us into his 
small mottled car. Dhamdi himself was behind the wheel. Just like 3 years 
ago, we went by Tripoli's noisy streets and now quiet alleys to a meeting 
with Qadhdhafi. 

And, like 3 years ago, having been delayed for a moment near the sentries, 
we drove up to a broad tend.. The tent was open and we went into it. 
Qadhdhafi rose from his desk and came to meet us. He was in a flying suit 
and greeted us like old friends. Firm handshakes. And here we are, already 
sitting around the wide table on which there are a telephone, manuscripts 
and files. Qadhdhafi smiles politely and says: 

There is a lot of work now. More than there was during your last arrival. 
. Many things have gotten complicated, but we haven't grown weaker. I can 
say frankly, we've grown stronger. 

They see all this with their own eyes, says Khamisi. Our conference is 
going successfully. 

Qadhdhafi nods his head. 

Yes... "Successfully... They are reporting to me on it. I myself was not 
allowed to properly finish speaking at the conference. 

There was a warm welcome... 

Our main task now is unification. If we are together, it is hard to separate 

us and we are stronger... I repeat, our main goal is. to strengthen the unity 
of all Arab states. Unity is our strength for achieving victory... 

[Question] How do you see your future? 

[Answer] We see our future in socialism. We have no other path, nor can 

there be one. We think that our Libyan revolution has played, is playing and 

will play a large role in this. We are glad--and this is not the only 
evidence for this--that the socialist countries support us. The threat of 

imperialism in the form of nuclear rockets is equal for our countries and for 

the socialist countries. The American rockets in Europe are such a direct 
threat to us. Together with the socialist countries, we rank with those who 
struggle against the nuclear threat. 

Qadhdhafi said all this calmly, stopping from time to time to give a chance 
fcr translation.

- [Question] What are Libya's economic prospects? 
Here Qadhdhafi smiled again. 

[Answer] I don't want to overload your memory; they'll give you all the 
materials...I can say one thing: we are associating more and more closely 
with the socialist countries, but at the same time we are trying to conduct 
trade which is advantageous for us also with capitalist countries. But 

the main thing for us is to always be with friends...it is hard for Libya to 
live without friends. 

[Question] And what is the role of writers in these great and crucial 
matters? 

Qadhdhafi clearly did not expect this question, but, navane smiled again,

[Answer] It is great, of course...You vourcels know that perfectly well...

[Question] May I, like last time 3 years ago, ask you to write some words
