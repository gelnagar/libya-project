Summary (AI): A April 1973 White House memo to Kissinger reports that a Libyan reconnaissance flight went off without incident and recommends interdepartmental review of contingency papers on protecting Americans in Libya. The attached contingency paper covers evacuation plans, the US Embassy in Tripoli, and the roughly 2,200 Americans in Libya, about 2,180 of them in Tripoli and vicinity.
• About 2,200 Americans in Libya, approximately 2,180 living in Tripoli and vicinity.
• Americans elsewhere: Marsa Brega (350), around Benghazi (169), and oil ports of Zueitina, Ras Lanuf and Sidra on the Gulf of Sirte.
• Embassy has single side band voice circuit communications with Benghazi, Tunis and Valletta.
• Evacuation plan has phases: warning, evacuation of dependents, non-essential staff, closure of post, with a standfast recommendation if threatened.
• Libyan Government unlikely to promote all-out assault on Americans due to concern for oil industry, but isolated violence possible especially in Tripoli suburbs.
• 1973-04: Libyan reconnaissance flight occurred without incident, prompting the contingency review.
