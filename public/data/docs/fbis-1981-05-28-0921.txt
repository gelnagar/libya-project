JN272045 Abu Dhabi EMIRATES NEWS AGENCY in Arabic 1900 GMT 27 May 81 


[Text] Abu Dhabi, 27 May (ENA)--The following is the text of the press conference held 
today by his highness the president: 


Reports say that the Sultanate of Oman and the State of Kuwait held different 
the Gulf summit conference. Can Your Highness explain what the situation was? 


Answer: Views may differ, but the objective and the interest are one. There are 
different ways to achieve an objective. I would like to assure you that, judging by 
the meetings I had with my brother leaders and the opinions I heard from Saudi Arabia, 
Kuwait, Qatar, Oman and Bahrain, they all aim at general benefit and real and sincere 
cooperation among all these states. Opinions may differ even if the aim is one. Such 
differences are not suprising. What is surprising are differences that have ulterior 
motives that do not serve the common interest, destiny and happiness of all. As the 
saying goes, not every endeavor achieves its goal. In the end, everyone agreed on 
one opinion, which is cooperation, solidarity and cohesion in all fields--the economic 
field, the security field and any other field suggested by any brother or state, 
provided that such cooperation is earnest and sincere. 


westion: Your Highness the Presicent, you have stressed in your meeting the need to 
keep the area outside the world power struggle, remove foreign fleets and cancel 
military facilities. voes this apply to the facilities supplied by Oman and Bahrain? 


Answer: This applies to everyone. Not resorting to any foreign power applies to 
everyone. This was heard from the Gulf states before the conference by everyone, far 
and near. The conference was the final bond. The Gulf states sha!l dispense with any 
foreign power or foreign protection. They are ruled out. 


Question: The conference's final statement spoke of the rejection of foreign bases 
in the Gulf states, but it did not refer to the military facilities given to some 
powers. What is your position on that? 


Answer: I have already said that the Gulf states can dispense with the protection of 
any foreign power. They are capable of protecting themselves with the means in their 
power and with the cooperation of their brothers, both those who attended the conference 
and those distant brothers who did not attend. The Gulf states do not have an obvious 
enemy. They do not seek to antagonize any quarter. This is their real position as 

I know it for certain. 


Question: The Gulf summit's statement tackled the current serious and urgent topic-- 
namely, the situation in Lebanon, the Israeli threats against Syria and the Gulf's 
supporting stand. How can this stand be put into practice--by holding an Arab summit 
on Lebanon? 


Answer: Actually, whatever is needed by Lebanon, Palestine,. Syria or any other Arab 
state is felt by every Arab in every Arab country. I would like to assure you that 
efforts have been concerted right from the beginning with regard to Palestine and the 
frontline states. With regard to Lebanon, the Arabs will spare no efforts to prevent 
usurpation and aggression. I would like to say that the Gulf states are in the fore- 
front in aiding their fraternal Arab states. 


Question: Some states, such as Somalia and the YAR for example, have expressed the desire 
to join the Arab Gulf Cooperation Council. Is the door open for other states to join it? 


Answer: Everything has a beginning. The foundation of a citadel or building begins 
with one brick, and God wanted the brothers in the Gulf states to take a step toward 
mutual assistance and cohesion among themselves so that all steps will be completed.

Everything has appendices and there murt be some appendices after the foundation is 
laid down. Man should look at his convitions at first and eventually there will be 
other things. 


The Cooperation Council's leaders alone undertook this cooperation and this is evidence 

of the fraternal ties that link them together in any predicament or domain. These states 
do not want to alienate or isolate any brother. The member states‘ conviction is that this 
cooperation should be a support for all brothers in any near or remote part of the Arab 
States. However, we have heard remarks that this council is in isolation from the Arab 
League, but the one who says this is biased and what he says is wrong. The Gulf states’ 
cooperation is a backstop for the whole Arab nation and particularly for the Arab League. 
It is an effective backstop and power for this nation. It also consolidates ties with 
friends. 


The Arab states should seek to rebuild themselves, establish cooperation among themselves 
and follow the example of the Gulf states in cohesion and cooperation, whether these 
states are in the Middle East or the Maghreb. All these facts constitute the foundations 
and reinforcements of this citadel. We bless all cooperation among brothers once and ten 
times and we sincerely call on our brothers to cooperate, because the rights of our Arab 
brothers cannot be restored except through cooperation and cohesion. God willing, this 
cooperation will be the beginning of good for the Arab nation. 


Question: Does Your Highness expect a closer cooperation among the armed forces 
council's states in the field of armament, planning and training; 


Answer: This cooperation is in its infanc and it is meant to achieve cohesion in all 
issues, predicaments and everything that a brother might need from his brother whether this 
be in the economic or military field; security or life. 


Question: Does Your Highness expect a joint coordination of oil policy within the framework 
of the Cooperation Council? 


Answer: We did not discuss this matter during the leaders’ deliberations. It is left to 
oil specialists and experts. Once they agree on something, they will submit it to the heads 
of state. 


Question: In the final statement you stressed the need to keep the region away from 
international struggle. It has recently been announced that Soviet war vessels are visiting 
Aden port. What do you think of this? 


Answer: This is the thing which made us avoid foreign states whatever their size. This 

is also the thing which led to cooperation among the Gulf states. We have the right, first 
and last, to our lands and property. We also have the right to rally our efforts in order 
to safeguard our homelands, peoples and security. We do not want any small or big state 

to interfere in our affairs, nor do we want foreign states to clash with their adversaries 
in our territories, skies and seas. We must confront the one who antagonizes us. We 
reject and strongly condemn the states which quarrel among themselves on our territories. 
No Arab or Gulf state is in need of foreign states to interfere in its private affairs 

or to interfere with the aim of protection. We do not need any foreign state. The big 
powers’ conduct is not satisfactory to mankind in general. 


Question: French President Francois Mitterrand has announced that he will visit Israel 
and will send envoys to the Arab Gulf states. In light of this, how does your highness 
view the future of the Arab-French relations? 


Answer: This is something depending on the two sides. If the French side desires to 
strengthen and improve relations with the Arab states and if this desire agrees with the 
Arab states' desire, there will be a benefit and an interest in strengthening and 
bolstering friendship between the Arabs and France. 


28 May 81 ARABIAN PENINSULA 


Question: What is your stand on the Iraqi-Iranian war ard on the issue of the three Arab 
islands that was put forth by Iraq as one of the b.isic conditions for having a settlement 
with Iran? 


Answer: The hope was that the war would not continue until now. We also hoped that 

there would be no war. However, this is God's will. No one knows what is good or what 

is the alternative. The only result of war is unhappiness. It causes destruction and is 
against progress. In any case, we are affected by this war, as we also are by any confront- 
ation between Islamic Iran and fraternal Iraq, because they are neighbor and brother 
countries. Des. ‘te the fact that we are not fighting, we are suffering from this war 
because both coun .ries are our neighbors. 


We shall be happy the day we hear that rapprochement, peace and a cease-fire have taken 
place between the two neighbors, Iraq and Iran, because these two countries are linked 
by common interests. We ask God Almighty to reconcile them and have them become an aid 
to each other and to Islam in accordance with the Holy Koran's dictum that a Muslim must 
aid his brother Muslim. We also hope that the efforts to halt the war and achieve peace 
will be crowned with success. 


We and Iran are linked by relations stemming from Islam and neighborliness. Iraq 

is a fraternal country. Both are our brothers in Islam and Arabism. We are greatly 
affected by this dispute. We hope that this war will end because Iraq and Iran and any 
Muslim or Arab have nothing to gain. We also hope that each party will have its rights 
restored and be content with these rights, thus ending the troubles and the damages, and 
that the two countries resume their cooperation through thick and thin. 


With regard to the issue of the islancs, we have announced our stand and proof exists 
that the islands are Arab ones. We, however, shall not seek to seize these islands in 
a nonbrotherly manner. This is our intention and this is what we have announced to our 
neighbors since the first day. They know our stand. 


This is a right and the individual must be committed to it, because this is 4a vight of 

a state and a nation which cannot be relinquished. We do not want estrangement from our 
neighbors. The desire not to fight has been the only reason which made us pursue our 
rights in the manner we deemed best, in order not to give the enemy the chance to gloat 
about us. 


Question: There are differences on the issue of the borders among Gulf countries. Were 
these differences discussed within the framework of the summic conference? 


Answer: Cooperation has been established now and the council, which is made up of Arab 
Gulf countries’ leaders, has been set up. What is the importance of one or two kilometers 
if they decide to cooperate? Thus, this issue will no longer be important nor have 

any effect because there is now a framework for cooperation. This cooperation will solve 
minor and even major problems. I hope that this will have no effect on the brothers, so 
that they will be an aid and support to one another. 


Question: Gulf countries are granting assistance to developing countries. Will this 
assistance increase in the wake of the establishment of the Gulf Cooperation Council? 


Answer: These matters were not discussed at the summit level among the leaders of the Gulf 
Cooperation Council. They were left to the competent council bodies to be studied in 

light of the ideas that are put forth. The finance ministers of the Gulf Cooperation Council 
member states will meet soon to discuss the joint scheme for foreign assistance. 


Question: Sudanese President Ja'‘far Numayri has called for convening an Arab summit in 
which Egypt would participate, despite the fact that President as-Sadat has not changed 
his stand on Camp David. Has this matter been discussed within the framework of the 
Gulf summit?

Answer: We have heard of something like that which has been reported by ths press too. It 
has not been communicated to us by a president or an official. Egypt is a fraternal Arab 
country and in the end will have to come back and believe in its Arab character and its 
brothers. Any behavior, however, which is not approved by the brothers causes division. 
Egypt is a fraternal country and its leader isahumanbeing. Human beings make mistakes and 
wise human beings should review their mistakes and try to avoid them. 


We hope that Egypt will be close to its brothers and an aid to them, no matter how long 
this takes. 


Question: Do you think that the Arab nation has a role to play in protecting the security 
»9f the Gulf, and can one separate the security of the Gulf from Arab security in general? 


Answer: A brother usually helps his brother. It is natural that he help him if the need 
arises. This is not a strange or far-fetched thing. Both the small and large parts of 
the Arab nation must help one another in times of need and regardless of the differences 
in viewpoints. 


Question: Yuu have asked the United States to seriously work for the establishment of just 
and lasting peace in the Middle East. Do you expecc tine United States to respond to this 
request, and what would your position be if it did not? 


Answer: If the United States did not respond this time to the Arab request and failed to 
adopt a fair stand, it would not be the first time. The United States is a superpower, 
which considers its »wn interests and objectives. Therefore, we do not blame it for not 
seeking the correct path. But, as a friendly state we would be surprised if it did not 
adopt a fair stand. Its stand would be denounced by everyone, because injustice by the 
big power encourages others to emulate them. Such an unacceptable and unfair stand on 
Arab rights would be denounced by Arabs andnon-Arabs. What is expected of the United 


States is to adopt a fair stand. We are not asking it to support the Arabs. We are only 
asking it to support justice. 


Question: Do you expect Iraq to join the Gulf Cooperation Council when the Iraqi-Iranian 
war is over? 


Answer: There is a time for everything. Iraq is a fraternal state and the cooperation 
council is composed of six leaders. They must consult with one another, and any decision 
they adopt must be a unanimous one. This is the best way to avoid any mistakes. 


Question: France has concluded new arms deals with the Arab states. Will the Gulf 
Cooperation Council continue the armament policy pursued by the Arab Gulf states? 


Answer: A friend who seeks friendship will remain a friend and dealing will continue with 
him. As you all know, the diversification of the sources of arms is useful so that we 
will not confine our dealing to one quarter and thus become subject to pressure. 


Question: How do you view the future of the region in light of the establishment of the 
Gulf Cooperation Council? 


Answer: As you know, cooperation existed before the declaration of the council. Gulf 
cooperation was not haphazard, it was preceded by meetings and studies. We only declared 
the establishment of the council after making sure that everyone wanted this cooperation 
because it was for the general good. Thus, we have established the council and we hope 
that it will be an additional strength for the Arab nation and proof to our fraternal 
states that this cooperetion is for their benefit. We also hope that the council will 
serve as a model for all brothers s~ that they may follow suit once they have seen the 
achievements of Gulf cooperation.

[Text] Cairo, 23 May ‘ The Egyptian F ign Ministry has characterized the meeting 
held in the Chadian capital of Ndjamena, atten y some African states along with Libya, 
which is occupying Chadian territory, as an acceptance by a minority group of African 
states of a policy denounced by all African states--namely, disrespect for the territorial 
integrity and safety of the African states and interference in their domestic affairs as 
well as an attempt to scheme against the African people. 

In a comment by a responsible source at the ministry today, the Foreign Affairs Ministry 
affirmed that the meeting creates an astonishing novelty in diplomatic relations--namely, 
that a minority of states should determine the fate of the Chadian people without referring 
to the Chadian people or to the neighboring states or to the African states. 


The following is the text of the statement by the responsible source at the Foreign 
Ministry commenting on the meeting held in the Chadian capital of Ndjamena and attended 
by some states along with Libya, which occupies Chad's territory: 


This meeting amounted to an acceptance by a minority group of states of a policy denounced 
by all African states. The meeting was inconsistent with the OAU Charter and with the 
Lagos and Lome conferences concerning respect for the territorial integrity and safety 

of the African states and noninterference in their domestic affairs as well as an attempt 
to scheme against the African people.
