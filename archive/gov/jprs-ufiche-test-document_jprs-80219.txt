[Question] One of the most prominent issues to be brought before the conference 
is the Arab position vis a vis the Saudi plan, or the so-called Prince Fahd plan. 
Do you think it essential that there be a unified Arab position regarding the 
plan? 











[Answer] So far these proposals have not been officially submitted to us. When 
we receive them officially, we will seriously study them. In principle, we 
support the Palestinian position. 


[Question] The Palestinian position welcomed the proposals in principle. 


[Answer] When the time comes, and when they are officially proposed, we will 
submit our views at that time. 


[Question] Do you think that the fate of the AWACS aircraft deal to Saudi 
Arabia will have an effect on American-Arab relations? 


[Answer] In my personal opinion, there is a contrived uproar around this matter, 
because we feel that our peoples do not need sophisticated weapons like these at 
this time. We need to follow other roads, if we in fact want to triumph over the 
main enemy, Zionism and imperialism. 


[Question] What do you mean by "following other roads"? 


[Answer] I mean that we must depend on our people, that we must. support the 
Palestianian revolution, that we must support movements of national liberation, 
and that we must also use our people's energies, in preparation for the real 
battle against our foes. 


[Question] What were the exigencies which led to the tripartite alliance between 
Aden, Addis Ababa and Tripoli? 


[Answer] Before all else, the common political, economic and social goals among 
these three revolutions compelled us to make a specific move in our relations. 
This specific action is to coordinate political action and ecenomic advantages 
among the three countries. We will then have periodic meetings between the 
three heads of state that will give them the opportunity to review, coordinate 
and organize various matters. In addition to this, during this difficult stage, 
our area is subject to vicious attack by American imperialist forces. We feel 
that the planting of military bases in Muscat, Somalia and Kenya, the deployment 
of rapid intervention forces in Egypt, and the direct threat to occupy the oil 
fields, an increasing threat to overthrow national regimes, should make us 
coordinate our forces to combat these possible dangers. 


[Question] Why did you choose to be a tripartite axis under the Soviet Union's 
aegis? 


[Answer] Any action outside of the American framework is interpreted as in the 
interests of the Soviet Union. Our countries are sovereign, independent nations. 
However, we can boast that the Soviet Union stands beside us. We consider the 
Soviet Union to be a sincere, loyal friend to our peoples, our Arab and African 
people. We are not embarrassed to declare this. The Soviet Union will remain 
loyal to its Arab friends. On this basis, we see a loyal friend who will help 
us to face these dangers from the real enemies.

[Question] The relationship of the two halves of Yemen is one of war or "between 
the teardrop and the blood" according to the well-known saying. Is there no 
middle ground to bring together the two sides, and from there to unite? 


[Answer] We are pursuing a program of other factors, which many do not know 
about. For nearly 10 years, all the leaders of both Yemens have been doing some 
serious thinking about the form for a future unified Yemen on the economic, social 
and political levels. We have several committees working on the form that Yemen 
will have as a unified state, and which will have no direction, regime or 
ideology located in the north or the south. It will be a form appropriate for 
contemporary circumstances, and for the requirements of the region, and which 
will not contravene our sacred things as Muslims nor our pan-Arab obligations. 
This form will go well with our backwardness and our striving for development in 
our country. For some time past, several joint projects have been established 
between the two Yemens, in the area of transportation, communications and 
industry. We are now taking a basic and excellent step toward finding the ideal 
program to bring the two Yemens together in unity. 


[Question] Do you join in Oman's condemnation of the tripartite agreement 
between Aden, Tripoli and Addis Ababa? 


[Answer] Every nation has its specific position. Our relations with Ethiopia 
are excellent, and we have no problem with it. So far we have not formulated a 
specific position with respect to condemnation or otherwise. However, we condemn 
anything that leads to divisions or gives scope to create tension or foreign 
intervention or that is a source of unrest and tension in the region. If it is 
something different than this, perhaps cooperation to find economic and develop- 
mental relations to fulfill the needs of the countries of the region, in order to 
overcome growth problems, and t» create bonds of friendship, then this is noth’ 1g 
for us to fear.

But this question remains: What will the magnitude of losses be, due to the 
increase in the two country's oil bills? 


The Jordanian al-Zaraqa' refinery's capacity is currently about 45,000 barrels 
a day. It is expected to rise to 50,000 barrels next year, i.e., 18 million 
barrels a year. The cost of its bill will be $600 million, an increase of 

$72 million. This increase can be broken down as follows: 


* $26 million is the cost of the Saudi crude oil price rise from $32 to $34 
per barrel. 


*About $20 million difference from the increased barrel shipping fee from Saudi 
Arabia to the al-Zaraqa' refinery in Jordan, which would increase from $ .40 to 
$1.48 per barrel. 


* $16 million that the company claims it loses currently from assuming the 
costs due to the long period for payment of bills, on the basis of $ .88 per 
barrel. In the event its demand is complied with, to make the period 1 month 
(as is done by the Saudi government), it will have passed these losses on to the 
Jordanian government. 


With regard to Lebanon, it is expected that the cost of its oil bill will rise 
from $495 million to $537 million next year, i.e., an increase of $42 million. 
Since the cost is different between the al-Zahrani refinery in South Lebanon and 
the Tripoli refinery in the north, the details must be explained: 


1. The al-Zahrani refinery has a capacity of 6 million barrels a year. Since 
its relationship with the Tapline Company is the same as that of the al-Zaraqa' 
refinery, its bill with regard to a barrel of oil will be the same. Therefore, 
it is expected that its annual bill will rise from $194 million to $218 million, 
an increase of $24 million. This increase can be broken down as follows: 


* $12 million from the crude oil price increase from $32 to $34. 
* $7 million due to the increased barrel shipping fee from § .40 to $1.48. 
* $5 million in losses stemming from reduction of the grace period. 


2. The Tripoli refinery has a capacity of about 9 million barrels annually. 
Since its relationship with the Tapline Company differs from both the al-Zahrani 
and al-Zaraqa' refineries, it has been subject for some time to the higher 
shipping rate of $1.48 per barrel, nor does it benefit from the longer grace 
period. Therefore, the change in its oil bill for next year will be limited to 
the increased crude oil price only, from $32 to $34 per barrel. This would 
result in an increase from $301 million to $319 million.

However, will the oil states pay the balance of assistance due soon enough to 
permit the Lebanese government to spend it at the appointed time? 


It is clear that there are some states that have not yet paid a single dollar 
of their original share of Arab assistance to Lebanon, such as Algeria and 
Libya. If the former was excused from paying for reasons of the financial 
straits from which it suffers, since the other brother states pledged to pay its 
share, the latter state had promised to pay as soon as contacts were made with 
its officials concerned. 


There are states that have not fully paid their share of the first installment 
for 1980, such as Saudi Arabia. Consequently, it has also not paid any of its 
share of the 1981 installment. However, Lebanese officials are hopeful that 
the Kingdom will pay its share shortiy. 


There are some states that have paid their share of the 1980 installment, but 
have not as yet paid any of their share for 1981, such as Iraq. It is also in 
the process of paying. 


As for the fourth category of states, such as Kuwait, it is the best. It has 
paid the full installment for 1980, and the equivalent of half the installment 
due for 1981. 


It is worth pointing out that the annual installment of Arab assistance to 
Lebanon, some $400 million, is allocated as follows: Saudi Arabia $114 million, 
Kuwait $63 million, Libya $63 million, Iraq $58 million, the UAE $47 million, 
Algeria $29 million, and Qatar $26 million. 


Dr Muhammad ‘Atullah, director of the Development and Reconstruction Council, in 
a report issued recently under the title "Progress of Work in the Reconstruction 
Project,’ pointed out that "reconstruction has begun, after having been slightly 
delayed. It is dependent on the support of the Arab world, the international 
community and several friendly nations. We are grateful for the assistance that 
we have received, but we must stress that the thrust of the support must be 
increased, if we want to achieve our goal of rebuilding a stable and revived 
Lebanon. 











"Frankly," Dr 'Atullah added, "our impression is that our brothers in the Arab 
world and our friends in the international community, who support the principle 
of assisting Lebanon to rebuild, are still less appreciative of the need for 
speed in its reconstruction. We are not rebuilding a factory or a road, or 
even a whole economy. On the contrary, we are trying to reconstruct an entire 
society, a society that had a share in the past and that is calling for a share 
in the future, in support of stability in the Middle East."
