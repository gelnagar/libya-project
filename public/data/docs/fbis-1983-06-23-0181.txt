LIBYA

Commentary on ’Arafat’s Allegations Against Libya 
LD230909 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 0758 GMT 23 Jun 83 


[Text] Tripoli, 23 Jun (JANA) — The U.S. Herald Tribune 
yesterday published statements by Yasir ‘Arafat in which he 
falsely accused Libya of being behind the widening of the new 
revolutionary movement in Fatah. The paper reported his 
statement that Libya’s backing of the rebels was no longer a 
secret. 


It is clear that "Arafat is at his wit’s end just like anyone who 
lacks courage to face reality and admit that he is unable to face 
up to it. Therefore, his accusations against Libya are based on 
his feelings of impotence in the face of the developing revolution 
which has shaken up his position and has put him in a vortex of 
powerlessness. There is no doubt that ‘Arafat is in a bad way, and 
this is a logical outcome dictated by a series of abominable 
mistakes, his capitulatory policy, his dubious contacts, and his 
abandoning the gun out of avidity for presidency [ri’asah] and 
leadership. He admitted this in his statements which were pub- 
lished by the Herald Tribune where he said: 1 am the leader, I 
am the president [ar-ra’is], | am the one who is responsible, and 
I shall remain. Anyone in a situation like "Arafat's would not 
hesitate to look for a sacrificial lamb on whose shoulders all the 
{words indistinct] Naturally Libya was his objective in this, 
although Libya extended a helping hand and aid to him. He could 
not, while wandering aimlessly, but bite the generous hand 
spitefully and without any adherence to morality or to a position 
of honorable gratitude. Involving Libya’s name is a blatant 
attempt to cover up the great defeat suffered by ‘Arafat and his 
leadership. He will not be able to ignore this or escape from it, 
because it is the truth that twists the necks of the impotent, 
conspirators and common people. 


JANA Reports Hijacking of Libyan Plane 


LD230745 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 0715 GMT 23 Jun 83 


[Text] Tripoli, 23 Jun (JANA) — A Romanian Boeing 707, 
chartered to the Libyan Arab Airlines was hijacked at noon 
yesterday on its way back from Athens to Tripoli, with 23 
passengers aboard in addition to the crew. 


it was reported that the aircraft has been hijacked by two 
Lebanese nationals. However, their identities have not been 
ascertained yet. The hijackers diverted the plane to Ciampino 
Airport in Rome, where it landed and was supplied with fuel 
before taking off for an unknown destination. 


AFP: Further on Hijacking 
OW230231 Hong Kong AFP in English 0218 GMT 23 Jun 83 


[Text] Nicosia, June 23 (AFP) — A hijacked Boeing 707 of the 
Romanian airline Tarom, taken over by two Lebanese, landed 
this morning at Larnaca airport in southern Cyprus at 0020 
GMT, informed sources said here. 


An authorized Cypriot source said that the two hijackers had 
indicated their wish to go to Tehran, and the airliner was being 
refueled at Larnaca early this morning. 


After the plane left Lebanese airspace without landing, Lebanese 
Transport Minister Pierre al-Khuri said that the Cypriot author- 
ities had agreed to let the hijacked Boeing come down at Larnaca 
for “humanitarian reasons,” since its fuel was running short. 


He also said that after an initial refusal of the Cypriot authorities, 
Syria had agreed to let the plane land at [word indistinct] but the 
hijackers refused to go there. 


The tam [as received] plane, chartered by Libyan airlines and 
making an Athens-Tripoli flight, had been hijacked yesterday 
afternoon and first landed in Rome. It left Rome in the evening 
with a number of passengers — as few as 23 according to one 
source, or as many as 122 according to another. 


Some sources said that the hijackers were partisans of Shi'ite 
imam Musa as-Sadr, who disappeared in Libya at the beginning 
of September 1978. The Italian [word indistinct] agency ANSA 
said that they might be members of a Maronite Christian orga- 
nization, the Black Berets. Aviation sources in Beirut said this 
morning that the plane was carrying 21 passengers of four 
different nationalities. 


They said there were 13 Egyptians, 4 Libyans, 3 Thais and | 
Sudanese. There are 11 crew members.
