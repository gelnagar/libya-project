Summary (AI): A 1961 U.S. State Department boundary study covers the Algeria-Libya border, about 610 miles long, running through arid desert. It notes desert routes through the Libyan oases of Ghudamis and Ghat, but nothing about Tripoli or Libyan repression.
• The Algeria-Libya boundary is approximately 610 miles long.
• The boundary is demarcated for about 20 miles between Fort Saint and Ghudamis and delimited southward between Ghudamis and the tripoint with Niger.
• About three-fourths of the boundary passes across rocky or pebbly surfaces; the rest crosses sandy surfaces, some with shifting dunes.
• A Franco-Libyan agreement of 1956 changed part of the boundary.
