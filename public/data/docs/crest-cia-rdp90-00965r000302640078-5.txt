Summary (AI): A New York Times article (8 Dec 1981, datelined Washington, 7 Dec) reports that Reagan and national security advisers weighed punitive economic and political measures against Libya, including an oil import embargo and a travel ban, amid reports of Qaddafi-sent assassination squads. It also notes Libya's oil exports had dropped to about 600,000 barrels a day and that all American diplomats had left Tripoli in 1980 after a mob burned the US Embassy.
• Administration policy review on Libya had been under way since summer 1981.
• Libyan crude exports had fallen to about 600,000 barrels a day, down from as much as 1.5 million barrels a day at one time.
• Libya's sale of crude oil provides virtually all its foreign exchange.
• About 1,500 Americans, mostly in the oil industry, were employed in Libya.
• Exxon had announced plans to bring its personnel home; Mobil was said to be doing the same.
• Qaddafi denied involvement in assassination plots in an American TV interview; the State Department said it had strong evidence of planned murders of American officials.
• 1980: Mob burned the American Embassy; American diplomats left Tripoli.
