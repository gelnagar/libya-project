Summary (AI): A 1981 press article (OCR-garbled) reports former CIA agent Edwin Wilson, under US indictment for illegally supplying arms to Libya, denying he was an arms merchant. In a People Magazine interview given in Tripoli, Wilson describes his import-export business, Libyan training camps in Benghazi, and his links to Frank Terpil and Eugene Tafoya.
• Wilson said his import-export company, OSI-SA, was based in Tripoli and was one of the few foreign firms still doing business in Libya after nationalization.
• Wilson said he employed 150 to 200 people over five years, about half American, and gave up the pilot and helicopter contracts retroactive to October 1.
• Wilson said the Benghazi camp trained Libyan draftees in basic soldiering, including about 12 hours of basic demolition, and that four or five former Green Berets worked there.
• Wilson said his Virginia farm of about 2,000 acres was worth around $9 million.
• Wilson denied selling Redeye missiles, bribing US military personnel in Iran, or supplying Libya with embargoed equipment.
• 1976: Wilson says he first met Frank Terpil at a party in 1976 and that Terpil came to Libya; Wilson later says he had no contact with Terpil after December 1976.
• 1977-04: Two high-ranking CIA agents were fired in April 1977 because of their association with Wilson and Terpil, according to the article.
