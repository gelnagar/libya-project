During his participation, the revolution's leader made remarks on the memorandum of 
the revolutionary programme regarding neutralisation. He said this point is very 
important and should be fully absorbed by the Basic People's Congresses (B.P.C.'s) 
so that the priorities of confrontation with enemies of the Arab nation are put into 
perspective. 


He said: "The main enemy of the Arab nation at this stage and for this generation 


is so-called Israel. This enemy must be destroyed... 


"If the B.P.C.'s resolve to confront the Zionist enemy, this will therefore call on us 
to cooperate with other Arab countries, in spite of all contradictions between us 

and them at this stage. I personally do not see any utility in settling with reac- 
tion. The guardians of Israel are more dangerous than Israel itself. Whatever it 

may offer, Arab reactioncannot realise anything useful. Arab reactionaries are being 
clearly and totally utilised to serve American and Israeli interests. It is they who 
are responsible for undermining the capabilities of the Arab nation. 


"But the matter is in the hands of the free Libyan people who are capable of taking the 


appropriate decisions and specifying the priorities in confronting enemies of the Arab 
nation. If the B.P.C.'s see any utility in joining forces with Arab regimes for 
mobilisation and confronting the enemy, then all other considerations should be 

dropped and contradictions between us and these regimes should be disregarded. If 
such utility is seen in this effort, then the B.P.C.'s shall take the necessary 
measures they see fit." 

The leader urged the B.P.C.'s to express their opinion regarding neutralisation so 
that any decision in this regard may be taken as a basis for political action by the 
People's Bureau for External Liaison in 1983. 


The revolution's leader ended his participation in the discussions by affirming: "The 
Libyan Arab People have shown that they are truly practising people's authority, that 
the people are the masters and that ordinary individuals have started feeling that 

they rule themselves directly...The B.P.C."s are above all...even the General People's 
Committee is considered at an end with the establishment of General People's Committees 
at municipal level. The masses are therefore free. They are no rer ruled by 
government of parliament."
