SAHARAN PRIME MINISTER GIVES PRESS CONFERENCE 
LDO51408Y Algiers Domestic Service in Arabic 1200 GMT 5 Jan 78 LD 


{Text} Muhammad al-Amin, prime minister of the Saharan Democratic Arab Kepublic, held 
a press conference in Tripoli yesterday. He stressed that the POLISARIO's decision to 
release the French prisoners was to maintain the front's relations with the French 

people and their democratic forces. But the response of the French Government to that 
initiative was a wide-scale military intervention against the Saharan people. 


Referring to the statement made by the French defense minister, the Saharan prime minister 
said that those statements that the French forces would protect French subjects ail over 
the world show that France would interfere militarily in the internal affairs of other 
countries. 


Muhammad al-Amin added: Our military operations are a part of the struggle being waged 
by the Saharan people for freedom and liberation. The people will continue the struggle 
regardless of who the enemy is and of what extent his aggression takes. Concluding, he 
said the POLISARIO supports the Jamahiryah's initiative to convene an: African summit 

to study the situation in the Western Sahara.

PRIME MINISTER'S ADVISER SHEMU'EL KATZ RESIGNS POST 
TAO51828Y Jerusalem Domestic Service in Hebrew 1705 GMT 5 Jan 78 TA 
{From the Evening Newsreel] 


[Text] We just heard that the prime minister's adviser on publicity affairs, Shem 'el 
Katz, who was considered one of Prime Minister Begin's confidants, resigned his post 
this afternoon. our political correspondent Shalom Qital is on the telephone and will 
give us details about the resignation; 


(Qital] This afternoon Shemu'el Katz submitted a letter of resignation to the prime 
minister's office. The prime minister was not in the office at the time, but the letter 
of resignation was submitted there. The prime minister has not yet reacted to it. 


Apparently there are three reasons for the resignation, First, Shemu'el Katz disagrees 
with the cabinet's political line and with the peace plan which the prime minister, 
Menahem Begin, recently submitted. Second, he does not think he was allowed to do his 
job. Immediately after beginning his job there was a dispute when Foreign Minister 
Moshe Dayan wanted to keep publicity matters, which are at the very heart of the Foreign 
Ministry--that is how he then put it, "the very heart of the Foreign Ministry"--in the 
Foreign Ministry. Shemu'tel Katz, who had been appointed adviser on publicity in the 
prime minister's office, found his job devoid of content and attempts to settle these 
problems failed. And so the fact remained, which Katz complained of, that he was not 
allowed to do his job. Now when this reason is added to the first, that is, the cabinet's 
political line, it led Katz to resign. 


There is also the third reason, which is Prime Minister Menahem Begin's support on Sunday 
of Hayyim Landau as the Herut movement's candidate. Shemu'sel Katz wants to run as a 
candidate himself against Hayyim Landau. 


In addition to these facts there is another. Recently Shemu'el Katz was offered the post 
of Israel's ambassador to the United Nations, replacing Hayyim Herzog, who will complete 
his job in the coming months. Shemu'el Katz, who was involved in a dispute on the issues 
we have already mentioned, refused to accept the post and submitted the letter of resig- 
nation. 


NRP, DMC INCREASE PRESSURE TO SIT IN ON PEACE TALKS 


TA060710Y Jerusalem POST in English 6 Jan 78 p 3 TA 


(By POST Reporter Asher Wallfish] 


[Excerpts] The Likud's two coalition partners, the National Religious Party [NRP] and 
the Democratic Movement for Change [DMC], have intensified their pressure for 
representation in the peace talks with Egypt. 


Each in its own way, the NRP and the DMC are acting to get one or more of their cabinet 
ministers to sit in the two working committees wnich President Anwar As-Sadat and Premier 
Menahem Begin decided to set up during the course of their Ismailia summit. 


If the pressure exerted by the NRP and the DMC to get their men into the committees does 
not bear fruit, they will probably demand that no ministers other than Dayan and Weizman 
be in the committees--specifically Ari'tel Sharon, the agriculture minister. 


6 Jan 78 ISRAEL 


In the Knesset restaurant Wednesday, a self-confident and smiling Sharon icld the PQST, 
in extremely amicable tones, that he could not comment on reports tnat he might serve 

as Dayan's deputy. "Mr Begin knows quite weil whet are the capacities of his ministers, 
and you can leave all the decisions about the composition of the negotiating committees 
to nim,” Sharon said. 


(The reports about Sharon being a possible candidate for the political committee have 
been traced in part to Foreign Ministry sources and partly to Sharon himself.) 


Meanwhile Lhe National Religious Party Knesset faction met Wednesday and passed a 
unanimous resolution that "the NRP must be represented in all the peace negotiation 
moves." The NRP decided that this resolution be conveyed to Premier Menahem Begin at 

the earlist opportunity, well before the Sunday cabinet session, when the composition 

of the delegations--political and military--is likely to be decided. NRP Chairman 

Yehuda Ben-Meir, chatting with correspondents after the caucus was over, left them with 
the impression that the NRP ministers would not vote on Sunday in favor of Sharon serving 
on a working committee. If another mooted candidate, Me'ir ‘Amit, the DMC minister of 
transport and communications, was also proposed in the cabinet, they would only agree 
if an NRP minister were coopted as well. 


The Democratic Movement for Change played its cards a bit close: to its chest, and took 
no formal decisions about participation. Nevercheless, tie POST was reliably informed 
that Deputy Premier Yiga'tel Yadin would be seeing Begin before the next cabinet meeting 
on the representation issue. Yadin's message to Begin, the POST was told, would be that 
the four DMC ministers will not vote on Sunday for delegations that do not give them due 
representation. 


The DMC is anxious to ensure that no widespread settlement campaign is launched in Judaea 
and Samaria at present. 


Although Aritel Sharon tried only this week to get high-pressure settlement plans discussed 
in a top-level forum, Premier Menahem Begin nipped Sharon's attempt in the bud very fast, 
the POST was reliably informed. Later, the failure of Sharon's bid was described to newsmen 
in Sharon's confidence as having resulted from a row raised by the Liberals in the Likud 

and by the DMC--who undeniably oppose settlement initiatives now--and not as the result 

of the premier's refusal to grant agenda time.

airfields under UN supervision and the third as a military field.

POST: WORK STARTS ON NEW SETTLEMENT NEAR AL-'ARISH 
TAOG6O810Y Jerusalem POST in English 6 Jan 78 p 2 TA 
[By POST reporter Zvi Arenstein] 


{Text] Moshav Haruvit--Two Jewish National Fund [JNF] bulldozers began groundwork just 
west of here yesterday for what apparently will be a new settlement provisionally named 
Haruvit B. The location is about 10 kilometers east of Al-'Arish, in a region whose 
future has been acknowledged in recent weeks as being politically uncertain. 


JNF foremen at the site would not answer questions about the operation and claimed not 
to have information about what will be built there. 


David Perez said that he had been told the site was intended for agricultural use, but 
his superior, who would not give his name, said that a “meteorological station" would 

be built to serve the local moshavim. He could not explain why the two local moshavim 
would require such a large weather station.
