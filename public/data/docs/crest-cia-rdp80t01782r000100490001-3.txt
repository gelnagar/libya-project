• The LONG TRACK radar was connected to a computer/control van by data-link cable, as were THIN SKIN B and FLAT FACE radars.
• The LONG TRACK and THIN SKIN B were served by an auxiliary power unit.
• If the LONG TRACK was not under Soviet control, it would be the first known Soviet export of the LONG TRACK radar outside the Warsaw Pact.
• The LONG TRACK radar would enhance the mobility and performance of Libyan SA-6s and make the system harder to nullify through jamming.
• 1980-09: Imagery revealed a LONG TRACK radar in operational mode at the Tobruk SA-6 SAM support facility South, connected by data-link cable to a computer/control van with THIN SKIN B and FLAT FACE radars.

FIRST IDENTIFICATION OF LONG TRACK RADAR IN LIBYA (S) 

1. (S/D) Imagery of revealed a LONG TRACK radar in an operational mode at 25X1 
Tubrug (Tobruk) SA-6 SAM Support Facility South Figure 1). The LONG TRACK 25X1 
was connected to a computer/control van by data-link cable, as were a THIN SKIN B and FLAT 
FACE radar. The LONG TRACK and THIN SKIN B were served by an auxiliary power unit. 

2. (S/D) This is the first identification of a LONG TRACK radar in Libya. If the LONG 
TRACK is not under the control of Soviet personnel in Libya, it would be the first known instance 
of the Soviets exporting the LONG TRACK radar outside the Warsaw Pact countries. The LONG 
TRACK radar will enhance the mobility and performance of Libyan SA-6s. It will also make the SA-6 
system harder to nullify because of the difficulty in jamming the LONG TRACK.
