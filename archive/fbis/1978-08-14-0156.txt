Talk on Diplomatic Aims 


L.i21034Y Tripoli Voice of the Arab Homeland in Arabic 1715 GMT 11 Aug 78 LD 


ttributed commentary: "Active Diplomacy"] 


[Text] Active diplomacy of the kind practiced by the Jamahiriyah is constantly seek- 
ing new fields; it defines its aims and then sets out to achieve them with balance and 
Ss tr ngth. 


In our modern age, diplomacy is the means and the language which people use to fulfill 
their hopes, ambitions and needs, and to facilitate the solving of difficulties. From 
this standpoint the observer of Libyan political and diplomatic activities will note 
that its activity has spanned the whole world with the exception of Southeast Asia. 
Now Jallud and his delegation are there, knocking on the door of the great China, and 
from there penetrating Southeast Asia, exploring that area, defining their purpose in 
going there and then acting in accordance with the development and needs of the 
Jamahiriyah. 


Modern diplomacy is not and cannot be merely a matter of tourism, pleasure or even 
exploration. It is a dialog based on real motives and the needs of people. If this 

is true, the matter is not limited to political, trade or economic motives or reasons 
of development. A mixture of social values is invclved. When the values of peoples 
are close and their problems and needs are similar, they find themselves driven toward 
getting together and cooperating. If it is true that the needs of one who is under- 
developed or developing are to be found at the door of one who is advanced or who has 
long been on the road to development, it does not follow that the balance need remain 
tipped in favor of the strong. Modern needs are complex, far-reaching and difficult. 
Thus one group of states cannot fulfill all needs or grant all requests. The family of 
nations today lives in two important paradoxes. The first is that countries inevitably 
come close together by virtue of world development, scientific revolutions and the 
intermingling of their futures and their interests. The other is that nations are 
still distant and hostile on the level of national needs. They live in a state of 
near isolation. some believe that the future of civilization depends on the extent of 
rapprochement between nations and on their ability to find a solution to the existing 
paradoxes. 


As far as the Jamahiriyah is concerned, there are several considerations which move it 

in its international relations. The first is Arab security and the future of the Arabs. 
Closely linked with that is the desire to achieve all-round and rapid development of 
Libyan society. The first aim necessitates confronting the enemies of the Arabs and all 
those who obstruct Arab destiny. The second aim leads to building bridges with any 

state or group of states capable of granting the requests of the Jamahiriyah in the field 
of development. The third aim is to play an active part in world events with a view to 
establishing peace and cooperation; to bring about solidarity among nations in order to 
preserve our civilization and to prevent all evils. Hence the importance of supporting 
liberation movements, particularly in Asia, Africa and South America, 


What distinguishes Libyan diplomacy is that it is practical, direct and goes to the heart 
of a problem. It does not beat around the bush.

It is not hostile to any party on factional grounds. In deciding its attitude for or 
against anyone, it takes into consideration the mixture of values, interests and aims. 

It is this seriousness in establishing relations and building bridges, urging positive 
cooperation, and finding material and moral justification for involving others in joint 
action for peace that distinguishes our active diplomacy. It is the diplomacy of a big 
state, not a small one. Today, however, states do not suffer from being big or small; 
they are measured in terms of their effectiveness, activity, creativity and their presence 
in the spotlight of world events. The success of the Jamahiriyah in building bridges 

with all nations is directly attributable to the Jamahiriyah and it is also a gain for 

all the Arabs; it seeks to make new friends and consolidate old friendships. 


Jallud Cables Thanks to Teng 


LD121711Y Tripoli JANA in English 1531 GMT 12 Aug 78 LD 


[Text] Peking, August 12, JANA--Staff Major Jallud sent the following telegram to Mr. 
Teng Hsiao-ping, the Chinese vice-premier, following his visit to China: 


"Upon leaving your friendly country, I would like to extend my heartfelt thanks to you 
personally and to the people of the People's Republic of China, as well as my apprecia- 
tion of the generosity and warm welcome given to me and my accompanying delegation 
during our stay in your friendly country. 


This visit has reaffirmed the nature and depth of the relations among the Arab nation, 
the great Chinese people, and the Libyan Arab people which after the Great Al-Fatah lst 
September Revolution has been playing a leading role in its nation's movement and is 
trying to make the most of the two people's common civilization in its relations with 
the Chinese people. The historical and civilizational relations which have brought us 
together over the centuries are the best means to assist us in conforming [to] the new 
role awaiting the Arab nation and the great Chinese people, 


Pully aware of the greatness of the Chinese people since old times, the Arab peopl 
sailed overseas and oceans and crossed long distances to get in contact with your peoples 
and to establish together the strongest civilizational and trade relations, The exis- 
tence of millions of Chinese Muslims nowadays is nothing but a proof of ‘1is spiritual 
relationship, and our present role having achieved freedom is the embodiment of this 
relationship and role. Both the Arab nation and the great Chinese people have suffered 
from colonialism and evil campaigns in the same Way. 


The great Chinese people has succeeded in taking its own destiny in its own hands and 

is now ready to establish its role in the world whereas the Arab nation is still conti- 
nuing its struggle to assert itself and overcome its enemies. The Arab nation considers 
the great Cninese people as the best ally. I was assured of this during my negotiations 
with Mr. Hua Kuo-feng, chairman of the Chinese Communist Party and premier, and when you 
reaffirmed that the Arab nation's causes were also yours. The results we have reached 
have brought our relations to a new phase which expresses our people's joint aspirations 
to achieve better cooperation. Please accept my sincere greetings and respect. 


Staff-Major 'Abd as-Salam Jallud.
