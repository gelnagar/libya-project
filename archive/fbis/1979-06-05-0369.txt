CABINET: ELON MORE TO BE BUILT ALONGSIDE NABULUS 


TAO41030 Jerusalem POST in English 4 Jun 79 pp l, 2 TA 


[By Asher Wallfish and Joshua Brilliant] 


[Text] Voting eight to five, with two abstentions and in the absence of Premier Menahem 
Begin, tie Cabinet yesterday made final the decision to build a Jewish settlement on the 
outskirts of the West Bank's biggest town, Nabulus. The settlement, to be called Elon 
More, was urged by the Gush Bnunim settlement movement, which has been demonstrating and 
agitating to found a village near Nabulus for several years. 


Yesterday's Cabinet decision rejected an appeal against setting up Elon More in the 
Nabulus area, which was filed by the Democratic Movement leader, Deputy Premier 
Yiga'tel Yadin. Yadin was appealing a decision of the Ministerial Defense Committee. 


The creation of Elon More will entail the taking over of some 800 dunams of privately 
owned Arab land. Defense Minister 'Ezer Weizman, who has refused once to sign the 
military order seizing these 800 dunams, will now presumably sign, though yesterday 
he voted with Yadin on the abortive appeal. 


Foreign Minister Moshe Dayan also voted with Yadin, as did the other two DM ministers, 
Social Affairs Minister Yisra'el Katz and Justice Minister Shmu'el Tamir. Finance 
Minister Simha Ehrlich and Industry, Trade ani Tourism Minister Gid!'on Pat, both 
liberals, abstained. The absentees, apart from Begin, were Transport Minister Hayim 
Landau and Health Minister Eli'ezer Shostak, who would assuredly have voted for the 
requisition had they attended. 


Procedures for seizing the land may well be put into motion this week, although the 
landowners can still appeal to the High Court. Ina recent land takeover case, the High 
Sourt ruled that requisitions, as distinct from acquisitions, were justifiable where 
security is a chief factor. 


Last month the Cabinet decided that one of the principles of future land settlement, t< 
be negotiated in the autonomy arrangement, was non-expropriation of private land. The 
prevailing view in the Cabinet now favours seizure or requisition for a period of years 
by military order; this means that the owner still keeps title, and may even get c ompen- 
sation, though he cannot enter or use his land. 


In yesterday's debate, Yadin said he and his DM party would not agree to private land 
being used for settlement in the areas. 


Weizman said that if the site had strategic importance, then it should be used for a 
military installation and not for a settlement established as a result of Gush Emunim 


pressure, 


Dayan said the land seizure was out of place when the autonomy talks were just getting 
underway. The site was not vital from the security standpoint, he said. 


Agriculture Minister Ari'el Sharon said he personally had favoured a different site, but 
he claimed that defense experts wanted this particular one. Sharon said that that 
Attorney-General Yitzhaq Azmir endorsed the seizure of the 800 dunams. 


After the vote, Yadin noted that Dayan and Weizman were both against the move, and 
described it as "a thunderous flop." 


The Elon More group was Gush Bnunim's first settlement nucleus,

Formed some five years ago, the group squatted several times at the Sebastiya railway 
station and elsewhere near Nabulus, demanding permission to settle near that town. 


Moved to Camp Qaddum, its encampment was subsequently recognized as a settlement called 
Kedimim, Some original members and later arrivals want to stay there, but 25 families 
renewed their demand to settle near Nabulus about 18 months ago. 


Immediately after the Camp David accords were signed, in September 1978, they climbed to 
a rocky hill near Rujayb to settle there. They were removed by soldiers, but returned 

a few days later and were again evicted by soldiers arriving by helicopter shortly before 
Shabbat. 


Another Rujayb settlement attempt failed when Gush members were stopped at a military 
roadblock at the entrance to Kafr Qaddum. They squatted there until January 7 when the 
Ministerial Defense Committee recognized them as "a candidate for settlement" and 
undertook "to consider the group's preferences (regarding the settlement site) as far 
as possible." The group's leaders said that the chairman of the Ministerial Settlement 
Committee, Sharon, and Education Minister Zevulun Hammer had made it clear to them, in 
private talks, that they would be allowed to settle there. 


But implementation was delayed. Sources close to Sharon recommended a different site, 
while the central command reportedly favored Rujayb because of its strategic value.

TAO41307 Tel Aviv HATZOFE in Hebrew 4 Jun 79 p 1 TA 


[Text] The murder of Imam Hashim al-Khuzandar will further toughen the position of the 
extremist Palestinians because °f fear of assassination. These remarks were made yes- 
terday by Defense Minister 'Ezer Weizman at the Cabinet meeting. 


The purpose of the murder was to deter moderate elements from cooperating with the autonomy 
institutions and to encourage them to act in accordance with PLO instructions. 


The defense minister reported that the military government had offered protection to the 
deceased after he had received threats, but he rejected the offer. 


Meanwhile, no arres:s have been made in connection with the murder of Imam al-Khuzandar, 


The atmosphere in the towns of the Gaza Strip is detering many public figures from

Khuzandar. He was murdered by an anonymous person of Friday night. An investigations 
team composed of Israeli policemen and policemen from Gaza has been set up. Our 
correspondent Rafiq Halabi was in Gaza today.
