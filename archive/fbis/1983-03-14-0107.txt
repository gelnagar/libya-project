LIBYA

CSSR'S DZUR MEETS WITH JABIR, DEPARTS 11 MAR 


LD111830 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1330 GMT 11 Mar 83 


[Text] A working session was held this morning between Brigadier General Abu 
Bakr Yunis Jabir, commander in chief of the Armed Forces, and Martin Dzur, 
Czechoslovak defense minister. Brigadier Gen Jabir gave the Order of Good Work 
medal to the latter. He also decorated members of the Czechoslovak delegation 
accompanying the minister with the medal of the Great 1 September. 


The Czechoslovak minister and the accompanying delegation left Tripoli this morning 
after a visit to the Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah. They were seen 
off by Brig Gen Jabir, several officers from the Armed Forces, the Czechoslovak 
ambassador and his staff, and a large crowd from the expatriate Czechoslovak 
community in the Jamahiriyah. 


SURINAME LEADER ARRIVES FOR VISIT 12 MAR 


LD122255 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1900 GMT 12 Mar 83 


[Text] Tripoli, 12 Mar (JANA) -- Daysi Bouterse, leader of the revolution and 
commander in chief of the Armed Forces of the Republic of Suriname, arrived in 
Tripoli this evening on a visit to Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah. 


Brigadier General Abu Bakr Yunis Jabir, commander in chief of the Armed Forces, 

met Daysi Bouterse and the delegation accompanying him on their arrival at Tripoli 
International Airport. A number of members of the General People's Committee, 
officers of the Libyan Arab Armed Forces and some members of the diplomatic missions 
in the Jamahiriyyah participated in the welcome.

14 Mar 83 NORTH AFRICA 


FORMER PRESIDENT CARTER'S MIDEAST TOUR ASSAILED 


LD130018 Tripoli Voice of Arab Homeland in Arabic 2115 GMT 12 Mar 83 
{Station commentary] 


[Excerpts] Following the escalation in the U.S. wave of propaganda attacks on Syria 
alleging that it has set up Soviet missile bases and in the wake of the unsuccessful 
attempts by the U.S. Administration to implement the capitulationist Reagan plan in the 
Arab homeland, suddenly Jimmy Carter began a tour of the Arab homeland including Egypt, 
Jordan, some Gulf states in addition to the Zionist entity. 


The visit was accompanied by a campaign of publicity and statements which have been 
interpreted by observers to show that this visit has three aspects. On one hand the 
visit, which is called the Palestinian campaign, is undertaken by Carter who was a U.S. 
president and it followed the start of the preliminary preparations for the presidential 
elections in which a hot contest is taking place between the Republicans and the 
Democrats, and the U.S. Administration wants to assert its stand on the Zionist state 
and the capitulationist Reagan plan. 


All the statements which Carter made in the occupied areas cannot be understood except 
in the framework of Zionist influence inside the United States. These statements have 
caused surprise and bewilderment because they contained exposed contradictions of the 
former U.S. President who is playing the role of the messenger of U.S. imperialism, and 
they were contrary to his former stands. In those stands he used to call for the 
establishment of a Palestinian state as a solution to the problem. This is correct if 
one assumed that Carter played an influential role in the Camp David agreements. 


Another aspect of the visit is that the observer cannot separate the visit of Carter 
and the propaganda campaign of the U.S. press and radios against the deployment of 
missile bases in Syria. The visit has come after the frenzied attack against Syria and 
after the statements of U.S. Government officials on the increase in Syrian-Soviet 
cooperation. This explains Carter's statement in Cairo in which he said that he pre- 
ferred that there be no independent Palestinian state as it would become an extremist 
state and probably fall into the clutches of Libyan or Soviet influence. This explains 
the bilateral meetings in New Delhi between the Egyptian foreign minister and the 
Iraqi, Jordanian, and Sudanese foreign ministers and their call for support for the 
capitulationist Reagan plan in the framework of U.S. strategy. 


The reactionary Arab regimes are trying their best to implement the Reagan capitula- 
tionist plan in circumstances when the Arab homeland is witnessing the increase and 
expansion in U.S. imperialist influence following the Beirut battle. As regards the 
current conditions Carter's visit comes within the framework of U.S. strategy in the 
Arab homeland because his statements have accompanied capitulationist calls from the 
mouthpieces of treason which have been made from New Delhi this time after the end of 
the summit conference of the nonaligned states and governments. The visit has also 
comes after the hostile attempts against the Jamahiriyah, and this is an affirmation of 
these reactionary Arab regimes falling at the feet of imperialism, or how else can we 
understand Carter's statement in Cairo in which he said that he did not prefer an 
independent Palestinian state as it might turn into an extremist entity which would 
probably fall into the clutches of Libyan or soviet influences?
