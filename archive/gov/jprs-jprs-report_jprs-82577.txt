Second, the Shi'ite experience with Haddad's government has not been very good. 
During the Litani operation, in 1978, Haddad's forces expelled the entire 
population from the Shi'ite town of Al-Khiyam, which was then pillaged by 
Christians from nearby Al-Qulay'ah, and Marj 'Uyun with Haddad's tacit approval. 
In the months since the Peace for Galilee operation, with Major Haddad's 
holding authority for the entire area south of the Al-Awali River, his forces 
have harassed the Shi'ite population in various ways: attempting to recruit 
new militiamen by force, levying taxes, arresting people at their own dis- 
cretion, forcing unwilling villages to accept their presence, sequestering 
arms, interfering in local clan conflicts, and behaving arbitrarily at check- 
posts and in the villages. The Shi'ites have found that, although they were 
liberated from PLO control by the IDF, they in fact only got rid of one 
humiliation in order to suffer another. 


Many Shi'ites fought against the PLO during the past 4 vears, under the lead- 
ership of the Shi'ite popular movement, Al-Amal. In battles waged to prevent 
a PLO presence in Shi'ite villages, Al-Amal casualties amounted to some 300 
dead and 600 wounded. Its victories over the PLO--as in Al-Qhaziyah, Al- 
Saksakiyah, Burj Rahhal, Ansar and Libyah--became instant legends, related in 
daily conversations throughout southern Lebanon, and always with a sense of 
pride. When the IDF entered Lebanon, on June 6, Al-Amal laid down its arms. 
Therefore, many Shi'ites now ask why the IDF put Major Haddad over them as 

if they were enemies, and chose him, instead of Al-Amal, to prevent a reap- 
pearance of the PLO in the area. 


The answers to these questions are simple. High-ranking Israeli officials 
and officers indeed met with leaders of Al-Amal, and were impressed by their 
directness and moderation, but, although the movement did not adopt anti- 
Israel positions, it was also not quick to engage in active cooperation with 
the IDF. The reasons for this reticence were both immediate and long-term. 


The immediate reason--valid until the evacuation of the PLO from Beirut in 
September--was that the lives of some 200,000 Shi'ites living in West Beirut 
were in danger of revenge by the PLO and the Sunnis, if it were to become 
known that Al-Amal was cooperating with the IDF. Similarly--though of lesser 
importance--approximately one fourth of all Lebanon's Shi'ites lived, and 
still live, under Syrian occupation in the northern Al-Biqa’. 


The more fundamental reason was that Al-Amal was very keen on the rehabilita- 
tion of the central government in Lebanon, and thus wished to refrain from 
undertaking any authority--such as policing southern Lebanon--that was a

by Moroccan sovereign King Hassan II ended its visit to Washington, the

Ronald Reagan told the members of the Arab delegation that the Arabs must 
first recognize the legitimacy of Israel's existence, and Washington 

would then pledge that it would put pressure on Israel to enter into 
negotiations to discuss the subject of the rights of the Palestinian people.

asked his foreign minister, who was a member cf the Arab delegation, to 
return to his country and submit a report on the details of the Arab 
delegation's meeting with the American President.
