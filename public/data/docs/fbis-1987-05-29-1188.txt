LIBYA

Al-Qadhdhafi Addresses University Students 
LD281226 Tripoli Television Service in Arabic 
2141 GMT 27 May 87 


[Remarks by Libyan Leader Mu‘ammar al-Qadhdhafi at a meet- 
ing with Al-Nasir University students; date not given — 
recorded] 


[Excerpts] [Passage omitted] Colonialist political propaganda 
concentrates on driving a wedge between the Arabs and the black 
Africans. During the colonial era, European propaganda suc- 
ceeded, and it is still successful. This is because most of the 
independent African states — especially those that achieved 
independence after 1960 — enjoy pseudo-independence; particu- 
larly the francophone states. These are still French colonies. The 
states that achieved some degree of actual political independence 
are the former Portuguese colonies. This is because Marxist 
movements were established in these states and are still in power 
there. Also, Portugal is a small state that grew weaker; it no 
longer had the ability to influence its former colonies. The latter 
no longer had a master; therefore, they became independent and 
began to rule themselves. As for France, it is a major power, 
capable of attracting its former colonies and capable of providing 
officials with salaries. It can train and provide weapons for the 
armies of its former colonies; it can support these former colonies’ 
budgets. The former French colonies have therefore remained 
under French influence until now. 


The anti-Arab Western colonialist propaganda concentrated on 
driving a wedge between the Arabs and the Africans. It suc- 
ceeded. This means that many Africans show hatred toward 
Arabs, more so than toward Europe, which colonized, humil- 
iated, and destroyed them. The Europeans do not resort to 
propaganda against themselves; they make themselves popular 
with the Africans. Consequently, the African loves the European 
but hates the Arab. The Europeans colonized the Africans, but 
there is no anti-European feeling among the Africans. Yet the 
Europeans spread anti-Arab propaganda among the Africans. 
We did not colonize these states; they did not hear our voice, and 
we did not defend ourselves. They [the Europeans}told them [the 
Africans} | Those who occupied North Africa — the area 
extending to Damascus and the Middle East — are Arabs. They 
are dangerous; they are colonialists, and it is possible they will 
march against you. 


This type of propaganda is now successful in Chad. That is to 
say, the Chadian politicians may hate the Libyans but do not hate 
the Americans and the French. They seek assistance from the 
French forces and fight against the Libyan forces. Why? Because 
Western propaganda succeeded in making the Africans hate the 
Arabs. It told them that the Arabs are colonialists. Now Chad- 
ians regard Arabs as colonialists — they regard Libyans as 
colonialists — while they do not look upon the French or the 
Americans as such. 


Actually, the real colonialist is the European, the Westerner, the 
French, and the American. These are the people who are really 
colonizing Chad, Africa, and Libya. [as heard] But because the 


European and the Westerner are the masters and are successful 
in their propaganda, they rule. The Arab homeland should 
therefore be isolated from the south, as the anti-Arab movements 
come from the south. There is now an anti-Arab movement in 
southern Sudan. [passage omitted] 


Now there is an intensified hostility toward Libya created by the 
Western states. They talk about Libyan missiles which can reach 
southern Europe. This is an exaggeration. A Libyan missile 
might cover a distance of 100 km, but they say that it can travel 
1,000 km. They try to magnify the ugly picture they want to 
implant in the minds of the West, so that the West will prepare 
to invade and persecute the Arab homeland. They say: Look, 
there is a new power emerging on the southern shores of the 
Mediterranean, and this will mean a new series of conquests. 
They want to remind the West of the Arab conquests of southern 
Italy, Spain, and the islands of the Mediterranean. They say that 
the Arabs will rape women and destroy towns and churches. 
Their books are full of such stories. We could not have done such 
things. Our books have nothing like that. These books speak 
about our victories in this or that battle. But their books are 
terrible. [passage omitted] 


Now the blacks are being incited by the colonialist Western 
states, the colonialist circles. They are pushing the blacks to act 
against the Arabs. There are movements south of the Arab 
homeland — south of Sudan, Libya, Mauritania, and Algeria — 
and Europe and the United States are behind all of them. The 
West is behind these movements. The West wants to bring 
pressure to bear on the Arab homeland. Why is there such 
enmity? Why does this homeland always face such pressure? The 
West has no ambitions with regard to the people, the Arabs, men 
or women. The West wants the land, the bridge. The Arab 
homeland by its shape constitutes a land bridge. All the powers 
that have imperialist interests — interests outside their own 
borders — want to control this bridge. 


The Suez Canal, the Bab el-Mandeb. [Al-Qadhdhafi here 
addresses one of the people who is close to the map] Please point 
to these on the map, Salih. Please come here, one of you. This is 
the Suez Canal; it is a gate from the north to the south. Then 
there is the Bab el-Mandeb, which is the second gate. Then the 
Strait of Hormuz — it is also a gate — and the Strait of 
Gibraltar. Look at these international gates. Europe is to the 
north, and Africa is to the south. The Arab homeland is a bridge, 
from west to east and from east to west. The aim is to seize this 
bridge. On the one hand, they want to seize these gates and these 
international strategic points. If there is resistance in these parts, 
they cannot seize them. How can resistance succeed? It succeeds 
through the activities of the population and the military. 


This is why they concentrated on keeping this region backward, 
so that the defensive capability of this area is weakened. Thus 
the major conspiracy is to deceive us, particularly with regard to 
oil. The reactionary, ignorant, and backward political regimes — 
against which there have been revolutions that have not yet 
succeeded — and those who have established these regimes are 
deceiving us by transforming us into a consumer market. They 
kept us away from genuine production, and cultures based on 
consumption spread. Shops in the Arab homeland spread. There 
are 100,000 merchants in Damascus. There should be 100,000 


farmers, blacksmiths, carpenters, and electricians in Damascus, 
not 100,000 merchants. [passage omitted] 


Generally speaking, there is a real danger to the region between 
the ocean and Gulf. The oases will run dry as a result of the use 
of pumps. The overpopulated towns that have no resources will 
face problems and become dilapidated. There is continuous 
enmity toward the homeland by an advance from the south. 
Hostile European propaganda has succeeded in creating armed 
movements against the Arab homeland and its southern parts: 
southern Libya, southern Sudan, southern Mauritania, etc. 


The West is getting ready, and what we have seen are only the 
preliminaries — the NATO attack against Libya, Israeli mili- 
tary capability, the destruction of the Arab homeland from 
within, the destruction of Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, Egypt, and 
Tunisia — all these are preliminaries. What some call Israel is a 
bridgehead. Israel sends a force to the area it wants to occupy in 
order to establish a foothold, a bridgehead, so that the bigger 
force can cross over. Western colonialism will inevitably come. 
These are preliminaries leading to the military phase. First 
propaganda is used, lackeys are sent, and coups are hatched or 
crushed. 


But now the military phase to occupy the Arab homeland has 
begun. Do not rule out the possibility that utterances will change 
and that they will begin to talk about the necessity of occupying 
this or that place. Now the United States is talking about the 
Gulf. The Gulf does not belong to it. The United States says that 
the Gulf affects its security, and it declares a state of emergency 
during the Reagan era because Libya threatens U.S. interests at 
home and abroad. This state of emergency means that the U.S. 
President has the power, without consulting with Congress, to 
make any decision, such as to attack Libya. The state of emer- 
gency finds its own justifications. Tomorrow they may say: We 
fear for international communications from attacks by terrorists; 
therefore, the peace-loving and rational power that is responsible 
for security is seizing such communications. We will then see the 
United States occupying Gibraltar, the Suez Canal, Bab el- 
Mandeb, etc. 


Imagine the Gulf of Sidra — they left all other parts of the sea 
and came to the stretch which lies between Misratah and Ban- 
ghazi! The United States says at international forums that 
international navigation in the Mediterranean is being 
threatened by Al-Qadhdhafi and that it is inevitable that the 
United States should impose its presence in the interest of 
international navigation. We told them: Suppose a ship travels 
through the Gulf of Sidra heading for Ghadamis or Jufrah, how 
can you consider this international navigation? Have we barred 
the way to all those crossing the Mediterranean or said that one 
can no longer travel in the Mediterranean from cast to west or 
from north to south? 


Look at their excuse! They fought us. There was a battle that 
took place between them and us at the line of death. We downed 
four of their aircraft. They hit two of our ships. Scores of Libyan 
sailors died at the line of death. Americans were killed; their 
aircraft were downed. They came here and said that it was in 
defense of their freedom. [passage omitted]
