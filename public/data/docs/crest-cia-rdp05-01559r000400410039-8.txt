Summary (AI): A April 1986 FBIS compilation of broadcasts, mainly Algerian and Libyan. Qadhdhafi gives a long interview to Soviet journalists in Tripoli, condemning the U.S. air raids on Libya and praising Soviet support. Other items cover Libya's reported talks to join the Warsaw Pact and Soviet naval visits to Tripoli and Benghazi.
• Qadhdhafi said the U.S. raids were a failed aggression and that Libyan forces shot down U.S. aircraft, including F-111s.
• Al-Ittihad (UAE) reported talks on Libya joining the Warsaw Pact, a report presented as unconfirmed.
• Soviet naval units were reported to have been sent to the ports of Benghazi and Tripoli and the Gulf of Sidra for joint exercises with Libyan naval units.
• The Soviet cruiser Druzhba visited Libyan ports; the Libyan naval chief of staff toured it on 24 April 1986.
• A Nonaligned Movement delegation visited Libya and saw the dead and wounded.
• A JANA report alleged CIA involvement in a bombing of the British Airways office in London.
• 1986-04-15: U.S. air raids on Libya, described as aggression against Tripoli and Benghazi (referenced in the interview; the date is not stated in the document)

[Text] The visit by the Brother President Chadli Bendjedid to 
Sweden constitutes a crowning of long-standing Algerian- 
Swedish relations. We must not forget that during our way of 
liberation many Swedes believed that the revolution was a 
prominent event in relations between the north and south and in 
the Third World’s march in history. The Swedish students who 
were in Algeria at the time included the Swedish prime minister, 
in whose era Algerian-Swedish relations developed. It was not by 
accident that the first visit the president made was to [word 
indistinct]. 

As to the political talks, they covered matters of interest to the 
Algerian and Swedish peoples. First, the talks touched on the 
situation in the Mediterranean and the U.S. aggression against 
the Libyan people. Like us, the Swedes have felt that this 
aggression constitutes a serious precedence in international rela- 
tions and that, whatever the differences and conflicts, problems 
should be solved through dialogue so that a peaceful solution can 
be found. 

The talks also covered the situation in the Arab world, the 
Palestinian cause, the Lebanese cause and the Iraq-Iran war. The 
talks showed identity on closeness of views in regard to these 
matters. The Swedes also feel that the solution of the Palestinian 
question is the heart of the Middle East problem and that the 
PLO should participate in all efforts to solve this problem, which 
involves self-determination for the Palestinian people. 

As to the Iraq-Iran war the Swedes, like us, feel that the 
fratricidal war is a tragedy and that it should be solved by 
peaceful means and by a return to internationally recognized 
borders. 

The talks also covered the situation on the African continent and 
the hotspots there, from South Africa to the Western Sahara. 
There is an identity of views between Algeria and Sweden with 
regard to Namibia and South Africa and the need for condemna- 
tion of the policy of apartheid and for Namibia’s attainment of 
independence, and for showing faith in the future of the people 
of South Africa.
