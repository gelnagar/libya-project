LIBYA RESIGNS AS CHAIRMAN OF ICATU 
Valletta JAMAHIRIYA MAIL in English 24 May 80 p 1 


[Text] The leader of the first of September revolution 
Colonel Muammar al-Qadhdhafi has met the Central Council 
of the International Confederation of the Arab Trade Union, 
ICATU. He addressed the council describing the resolutions 
of the Arab trade unions as mere ink on paper and of no 
effect. 


"In April 1979, you passed several resolutions including a boycott of the 
Egyptian regime, its companies, ships and airlines and similar boycott 
against the U.S., but latter, it was clear that you showed no ability to 
carry out these resolutions and many others before, thus deceiving the 
masses. 


"Arab workers continue to service Egyptian and American airlines, thus 
serving the enemy, for Egyptian planes fly from Cairo to the occupied ter- 
ritories and to other capitals, providing the enemy with vital land, sea 
and air links. 


"Those who offer services to the airlines of the defeated Egyptian regime 
are in fact offering direct services to the enemy as there is no difference 
between the enemy and the defeated Egyptian regime. 


"Arab workers still maintain links with the U.S. by commemorating the work- 
ers days, which mark the events that took place in Chicago. This day ended 
when workers found liberation from the slavery of being wage workers on the 
first of September 1978 in Libya, and becoming partners. 


"Workers are still oppressed as wage-workers offering these services to the 
enemy." He gave as example of this the Moroccan workers who offered direct 
services to the Israelis. 


"There is no shame like that which has befallen the Acab nation. This 

shame can only te removed through a popular revolution that would remedy 

the present deteriorating situation and injustice, exploitation, slavery and 
oppression. We regret that Libya should be heading the ICATU under such a 


deteriorating situation and we therefore find no option but to resign as 
chairman of the confederation, as it is no longer an honour to hold that 


post."
