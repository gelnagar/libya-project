DAILY REPORT 

MIDDLE EAST & SOUTH ASIA REVIEW 

4 SEPTEMBER 1985 

North African Affairs 

Algerian-Tunisian Cooperation — Commenting on the results 
of talks between the Algerian and Tunisian chiefs of staff, a 
Tunisian source stated that bilateral military cooperation has 
begun to assume a “practical nature” in order to confront any 
development that may take place, KUNA reported. 

Egyptian View — Any Libyan aggression against Tunisia would 
be Colonel Mu‘ammar al-Qadhdhafi’s “biggest mistake ever,” 
Egyptian President Husni Mubarak stated in remarks reported 
by MENA following a 3 September meeting with a visiting 
Algerian youth delegation. Meanwhile, Egyptian Interior Min- 
ister Ahmad Rushdi denied that Egypt has massed troops on its 
border with Libya, the agency noted. Rushdi also revealed that 
over 30,000 Egyptians have returned from Libya since January, 

10,585 of them having been expelled since 8 July.

ociety that respects human dignity, according to JANA. 

Moroccan Trial — The Court of Appeal in Casablanca passed 
sentence against the group of saboteurs arrested while infiltrat- 
ing from Algeria in July, MAP reported, noting that 14 people 
were sentenced to death, 9 in absentia, while the others received 
various prison terms. 

Israel 

A curfew was imposed in Hebron’s Casbah area after one Israeli 
soldier was killed and another was wounded in a knife attack, 
according to Jerusalem radio. Two local residents were injured 
by gunfire from other Israeli soldiers who fired at the attackers, 
the radio reported. A Palestinian military spokesman cited by 
Baghdad Voice of PLO radio said a Palestinian group carried out 
the “brave operation” against two “Zionist intelligence men.” 
Reacting to the stabbings, Prime Minister Shim‘on Peres said 
that at a time when Israel wants to strengthen peace, the PLO 
wants to exacerbate war, Jerusalem radio disclosed. He added 
that Israel will “coldly contemplate” adopting military and other 
means to tackle any wave of terrorism. 

In another development, seven more residents of Judaea and 
Samaria were placed under administrative detention, Jerusalem 
radio announced. 

PLO-Palestinians 

Greece Bombing — An unidentified caller told a local newspaper 
that the Black September organization claimed responsibility for 
a grenade attack on a hotel near Athens that injured 18 people, 
mostly British tourists, Athens radio reported. The attack was a 
warning to the Greek authorities to release a detained Palestin- 
ian, the radio added. 

Khalaf Remarks — Reporting Fatah Central Committee mem- 
ber Salah Khalaf’s 2 September press conference in Tunis, 
Baghdad Voice of PLO radio cited his statement that “there is 
no glimmer of hope from any meeting with the United States,” 
which is working to split the PLO and has stipulated conditions 
that no Palestinian leader can accept. The PLO radio’s report of 
Khalaf's remarks on his Moscow visit did not mention his 
acknowledgement that the Soviet Union opposes the Jordanian- 
PLO agreement. 

Lebanon 

An outbreak of fighting between Amal Movement and Palestin- 
ian gunmen in the Burj al-Barajinah area of southern Beirut left 
5 people killed and 12 others wounded, the clandestine Radio 
Free Lebanon reported. Acknowledging the fighting, an Amal 
statement broadcast by Beirut radio accused “gunmen” of firing 
indiscriminately at houses in the area. 

As for the continued closure of crossing points between the two 
halves of the capital, which has caused food shortages in the 
western sector, a Lebanese Forces official cited by Radio Free 
Lebanon explained that the closure is in response to the kidnap- 
ping of at least 70 people by the Amal Movement. 

The Arab Socialist Ba‘th Party Organization, the pro-Syrian 

Ba‘th group in Lebanon, claimed responsibility for another sui- 

cide car-bomb attack against an Army of Southern Lebanon 

(ASL) roadblock in the south early 3 September, Beirut radio 

reported. The bomber killed only himself in the attack, which 

occurred near Jazzin, according to ASL sources cited by AFP. 
GCC Ministerial Council 

The GCC Ministerial Council reaffirmed its preparedness to 
undertake any action that will help end the Iran-Iraq war, 
according to a statement issued by the council and carried by 
SPA following the conclusion of its 16th session in Riyadh. The 
council also regretted Iran’s “continued refusal” to respond to 
efforts made to end the conflict through negotiation and rec- 
onciliation. As for the current Arab situation, the council 
attached “special importance” to the tasks of the Arab commit- 
tees formed at the Casablanca summit to solve problems among 
Arab countries.

Iran 

Members of the government should not be offended if they are 
dismissed and other people are appointed to perform their duties, 
Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeyni said in remarks to Cabinet mem- 
bers broadcast by Tehran radio. He stressed that the aim should 
be to serve and not to hold a certain post. Khomeyni said that 
while some people claim they could do better than current 
officeholders in top posts, most of these claims are “just talk and 
no action.” 

In a separate development, Madrid radio reported that an air- 
craft carrying Spanish Prime Minister Felipe Gonzalez to the 

PRC was forced to leave Iranian airspace after Tehran author- 
ities warned that the military air defense had just prohibited air 
traffic in the zone. The prime minister’s aircraft landed in 
Ankara before continuing its journey. 

Afghanistan 

Soviet and Afghan forces have suffered some 1,000 casualties 
during a major offensive to break a siege of the garrison at 
Khowst in Paktia Province, according to Western diplomatic 
sources cited by AFP. Meanwhile, Kabul radio reported that 
government forces crushed rebels in Khowst and several other 
regions of Paktia Province.
