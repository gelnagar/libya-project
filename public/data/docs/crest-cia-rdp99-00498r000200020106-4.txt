Summary (AI): The New York Times reprint of the Justice Department's final report on Billy Carter's dealings with Libya (April 1981) describes interviews with President Carter, Brzezinski, and others about Billy Carter's 1978 and 1979 trips to Libya and his role in the Iran hostage contacts. It concerns US-Libya relations and the Carter family rather than places or security sites inside Libya.
• Investigation ordered by Justice Department Order No. 907-30, dated Aug. 1, 1980.
• A Status Report was sent to the Solicitor General on Oct. 29, 1980.
• The office was precluded from interviewing high-level Libyan officials because of the state of US-Libya relations.
• Billy Carter reportedly refused to register as an agent for Libya; the Justice Department disagreed with his position.
• Billy Carter's Libyan ties included prolonged business negotiations and a substantial loan sought from Libyans, which the President said he learned of in summer 1980.
• 1978: Billy Carter's first trip to Libya
• 1979: Billy Carter's second trip to Libya
• 1979-11: Billy Carter used in contacts with Libyan diplomats to help secure release of American hostages in Iran
