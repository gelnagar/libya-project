TRIPOLI BACKS TUNISIAN OFFICIAL'S COOPERATION CALL 


LD031522 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 3 Jan 81 
[From the "Reports and Dimensions" feature presented by JANA political affairs staff] 


[Text] At the end of the work of the Tunisian National Assembly on 29 December 1980 
Brother Mohamed Mzali dealt with Tunisia's relations with the Jamahiriyah. He stressed 
the need to work to cement the bonds of affection, fraternity and cooperation between the 
two countries, Tunisia and the Jamahiriyah. The prime minister also called for the need 
for dialogue in this sphere and called for the implementation of the agreements signed 
between the two countries in a number of spheres, stressing that this would be considered 
a major step on the path of cementing fraternal relations between the two countries. 


Without any doubt, such an initiative by Prime Minister Mohamed Mzali will prompt Libyans 
to open a new page with the present government headed by Brother Mohamed Mzali after the 
page of the previous government, which upset the relations of the two peoples and the 
two countries and endeavored to cast doubts so that a number of joint cooperation agree- 
ments were hindered and stopped, a matter which conflicts with the interest of the two 
fraternal peoples. This is because the speech of Prime Minister Mohamed Mzali clearly 
stresses the true intentions of his government to move to build a basis of genuine 
fraternity between the masses of the two countries so as to ensure the creation of real 
ceoperation which would ensure for joint relations strong continuity along the path of 
realizing the fraternity of the two peoples, a fraternity which continued for centuries 
and helped create a joint history of struggle against vicious colonial attacks which did 
not distinguish between the two peoples in their aims and plans. 


According to the call of Mohamed Mzali to work to strengthen the bonds of affection and 
fraternity and for joint action between the two countries, the masses of Libyans of 

both sexes will welcome with interest and attention this initiative and the opening of 
the door of dialogue which was closed by the previous Tunisian Government. This fraternal 
hand extended by the new Tunisian Government to the Libyan Arab people confirms that that 
government genuinely realises the importance of fraternal relations linking the two 
fraternal peoples and their effective positive results to the two peoples. If the pre- 
vious Tunisian Government had harmed this relation then without any doubt their brethren 
in Tunisia are aware that that government had also harmed the Tunisian people. We hope 
that the coming period will witness a significant and beneficial development for the two 
peoples of Tunisia and the Jamahiriyah.

JANA REPORTS POLISARIO MESSAGE TO MOROCCAN PEOPLE 


LD051746 Tripoli JANA in Engiish 1557 GMT 5 Jan 81 


[Text] Algiers, 5 Jan (JANA)--The Polisario addressed a message to the Moroccan people on 
the new year. The message said the Polisario is ready to negotiate peace and any force in 
Morocco able to give the Saharan people their legitimate rights. 


King Hassan II, who deceives the Moroccan people by promising victory over the Polisario, 
will not be able to achieve either victory or peace through his dirty war. 


The message referred to the dark image of the internal situation in Morocco caused by the 
aggressive war against the Saharan people on the expense of the brotherly Moroccan people. 
The Polisario called upon the Moroccan people to stop bearing the heavy burden of King 
Hassan's policies which made the Moroccan people their first victims. 


GENERAL PEOPLE'S CONGRESS VIEWS EGYPT RESOLUTION 


LD051410 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 5 Jan 81 


[Excerpts] The General People's Congress resumed its work at 0930 this morning. It dealt 
with the resolution connected with avoiding confrontation with the Egyptian army and with 
allowing Egyptian Arab workers to enter the Jamahiriyah to work. The congress secretary 
pointed out that 171 basic congresses have approved avoiding confrontation with the 
Egyptian Army and allowing the Egyptian workers to enter the Jamahiriyah to work. The 
secretary pointed out the need to take into consideration the new developments since the 
leader's speech commemorating the llth anniversary of the September revolution, and this 
has been done by the congresses in their resolutions and remarks. It has become apparent 
to the participants of the congresses that the Egyptian regime has turned Egypt into a 
military base and that it is still amassing its forces along the Jamahiriyah border. It 
is also carrying out joint maneuvers with the American forces. Moreover, the Zionist 
enemy forces are not far away.

The two sides expressed their determination to ensure the consolidation of fraternal 
relations between the Tunisian Republic and the Kingdom of Morocco and they stressed the 
need to pursue their consultations with a view to strengthening cooperation, stepping up 
exchanges between the two fraternal countries in all spheres and working to implement pro- 
grams drawn up on this question, 


The two sides also stressed the need to continue efforts to create a suitable climate in 
which to build the great Arab Maghreb on bases of fraternity, cooperation and solidarity, 
the preservation of common interests and mutual respect, and to make every effort to bring 
the region's countries closer to the objective through the things which unite them, such 
as unity of religion, language and civilization, while remaining loyal to their peoples' 
common struggle to regain dignity, freedom and independence, 


Palestine and Jerusalem 


The two sides reviewed the current situation in the Arab world and reaffirmed in particular 
their determination to pursue action with a view to consolidating Arab solidarity and 
closing ranks to confront the Zionist enemy and support the struggle of the Palestinian 
people under the leadership of the PLO--their sole legitimate representative--to win back 
the rights of which they have been stripped, to create an independent national state and 

to secure Israel's withdrawal from all the occupied Arab territories, including the holy 
city of Jerusalem, They also condemned Israel's deicision on the Judaization of Jerusalem 
with a view to making that city the capital of the Zionist entity, destroying its Islamic 
character, 


In this connection the Tui ians stressed the great efforts being made by His Majesty 
King Hassan IT, chairman ot the Jerusalem Committee, to protect the Arab nature of that 
city and preserve its Arab and Muslim heritage. The Tunisians also expresed their support 
tor all the efforts which the Jerusalem Committee makes in that sphere. 


Traqi-Tranian War 

The two sides expressed their concern at the continuation of the Iraqi-Iranian war and 
emphasized their hopes for the cease-fire calls to be heeded and the conflict settled by 
negotiation in keeping with the Islamic conference's charter and the UN Charter. 

Southern Africa 

In addition the two sides reaffirmed their full support for the Namibian and South African 
peoples in their struggle against colonialism and apartheid, and they expressed their deep 


conviction that the last bastions of colonialism will soon be removed from Africa, 


Atghaniatan 


The Tunistan Republic and the Kingdom of Morocco, while expressing their concern at the 
growing number of hotbeds of tension in the world and their effects on the atmosphere of 
international detente, their attachment to the principles of the UN, Arab League, Islamic 
Conference and OAU charters and the nonaligned movement's basic principles, just as they 
reiterate their determination to consolidate them on the basis of fruitful cooperation 
within the framework of respect for [word obscured] and noninterfererce in internal 
affairs. [as published] 


In this connection the two sides expressed their concern at the foreign intervention in 
Afghanistan, an Islamic country and a member of the nonaligned movement. They expressed 
the hope for the sovereignty of states to be respected and for there to be no interference 
in internal affairs, in keeping with the principles of the UN Charter.
