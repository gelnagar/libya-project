INTERNATIONAL COMMUNIQUES ON SAHARAN SITUATION 


Casablanca AL~MUHARRIR in Arabic 11 Oct 79 LD 
{[Editorial: Are We Up to the Required Level?"] 


[Excerpts] The recent attack on the town of Semara has raised and continues 
to raise many questions, particularly among people following developments 
in Northwest Africa. 


The first question concerns the press scoop which the adversaries achieved 
as a result of the Moroccan media's attitude during the battle. The min- 
istry of information's communique on Monday night came more than 24 hours 
after the communique issued by the adversaries in Algeria; as a result of 
this the adversaries’ propaganda had a field day in the international press. 
When the Moroccan communique was issued the foreign press and radio had al- 
ready given wide coverage and publicity to the adversaries’ viewpoint and, 
as a result, the Moroccan viewpoint was denied the publicity it would have 
enjoyed had we taken the initiative to break the news first. A press scoop 
during wartime is a weapon that should not be ignored or belittled. Why 
and for what reason was the ministry of information late in informing the 
national and international public of the truth, failing to do so before the 
adversaries could herald their “victories”? 


We leave this question to be answered by the ministry of information not 
with words and empty justifications but with action in the future to beat 
the adversaries in the propaganda war, whose importance no one can deny? 


There is another question that is more important. It concerns the strategy 
of the adversaries. The attack on Semara involved more than 5,000 men--a 
fact confirmed by the ministry of information communique as well as the ad- 
versaries' communique. The question is: Why attack with such a large num- 
ber of men? Some people, prompted by false hopes, explain this by saying 
that the adversaries or some of their supporters are tired and therefore the 
operation was suicidal and similar to the sudden flare of a candle before it 
burns out. 


Such talk generally reflects the wishes of its originators more than it 
expresses the truth. 


119 











The attack on Semara is no different from the previous attacks on Tantan, 
Famm al-Husn, Bir Enzaran and Bouirat. The objective of these at‘acks is 
clear: to establish a “strong” presence internationally and to inflict 
heavy losses in lives and material on Morocco so as to “weaken” it and 
create “confusion and chaos" in its ranks. 


Another strategic objective to to prepare the ground at the United Nations 
in the same way that it was prepared at the Monrovia conference and the Ha-~ 
vana conference, the opponents of our territorial integrity are hoping to 
obtain from the United Nations a resolution condemning Morocco and demanding 
it withdraw from the Sahara. Should they succeed in doing so, they would 
then move toward the next stage in their plan--namely, launching simultan- 
eous attacks with large numbers of men on several towns and positions with 
the help of "volunteers" from here and there. With this plan the opponents 
hope to secure a number of positions and then begin “liberating” the land 
and imposing a military solution. 


This, in our view, is the strategy of the adversaries. ‘ny “reassuring” 
talk would only be deceiving. We must face the disturbing truth and be 
prepared for the worst. 


The fact is that the war is reality and is imposed on us. The only question 
that we must ask is: Are we equal to the war imposed on us or not? 


Frankness forces us to answer in the negative, at least with regard to one 
important aspect of the war--namely the information front, in which we are 
lacking. Are we also up to the required level on the other fronts, such as 
the diplomatic front, for example?
