HASSAN PROFESTS TAW-TAN ATTACK IN ITETTER TO OAU CHAIRMAN 


LD291330 Rabat Domestic Service in Arabic 1200 GMT 29 Jun 79 LD 


[Text] Mercenary forces tried to attack the town of Tan-Tan on 27 June 1979. The Royal 
Armed Forces stopped them more than 50 kms from the town and prevented them from reaching 
the town, ur armed forces were able to repulse the mercenaries and pursue them for 


many kilometers, 


V. 2 Jul 79 Se NORTH AFRICA 


During the battle, which went on for the whole day, the mercenaries left behind them on 
the battlefield more than 60 dead and many seriously injured, in addition to a signifi- 
cant quantity of arms. Our forces lost 20 dead and 10 injured. 


Following this attack on our national soil, His Majesty King Hassan sent the following 
message to His Excellency Muhammad Ja'far Numayri, the current chairman of the OAU: 


His Excellency Muhammad Ja'far Numayri, president of the Democratic Republic of Sudan 
and current chairman of the OAU, Khartoum; 


Your Excellency Mr President ; 


Morocco was subjected during the period [date indistinct] and 4 June 1979 to two [word 
indistinct ] attacks against towns which have been under Moroccan sovereignty for ages. 
The seriousness of this situation compelled us to resort to the Security Council, where 
we lodged a complaint against Algeria, whence the agressors come and whether they return 
after carrying out their acts of aggression. While the Security Council was studying 
our complaint, you personally intervened and asked us to leave the whole matter to the 
OAU alone, particularly as the wisemen committee was at that time meeting in Khartoum 
under your esteeméd chairmanship. Because we appreciate every African solution, we 
responded to your request immediately, and thus asked the Security Council to postpone 
the examination of our complaint in order to avoid hindering the work of the wisemen 
committee. 


The wisemen committee, at the end of its meeting, addressed an appeal to all sides to 
respect a general cease-fire as a prerequisite for creating a suitable atmosphere for 
the establishment of peace in the region. 


We, on our part, intended to respond to this appeal and were convinced that the other 
sides would respond to it too. But on this day, 27 June 1979, the town of Tan-Tan was 
again the target of a treacherous attack--the town which is on indisputed national soil. 


While informing you of this new act of aggression, and this attempt to resort to armed 
force yet again against our state's territorial integrity, we would like to draw your 
attention particularly to the following: 


1. Because the mercenaries ignored in a manner which is beyond argument and deliberately 
the recommendations of the wisemen committee, by this attack they are clearly violating 
even the legitimacy of the wisemen committee and the legitimacy of its recommendations 

or resolutions; 


2. Because Morocco cannot naturally be held responsible for the consequences of these 
criminal acts of aggression, those who carried out these acts of aggression against our 
national soil will now and in future be held fully responsible for all consequences at 
all levels. 


We would be very grateful, Your Excellency, if you would arrange for the circulation of 
the contents of this letter, particularly to the members of the wisemen committee. 


Your Excellency Mr President, please accept our profound esteem. 


[Signed] Hasan II, king of Morocco.

INCREASED SYRIAN THREAT IN LEBANON PREPARED FOR 


TA302008 Jerusalem Domestic Television Service in Hebrew 1830 GMT 30 Jun 79 TA 


[Text] Reports from foreign sources show that the Syrians have accelerated their 
preparatory measures during the weekend anticipating the possibility of further clashes 
with Israel. This step was taken in view of reports from Lebanon, claiming that the 
Israeli Air Force continues its daily routine aerial presence in south Lebanese skies 
for purposes of patrols and surveillance. 


Defense elements in Israel estimate that the next few weeks, perhaps even the coming 
week, will be the test period for Syrian intentions. Here is a report by our military 
correspondent 'Amiram Nir on the sources of tensions and on possible developments. 


{Nir--live] The continuation of the Israeli overflights in southern Lebanon presents a 
clear challenge to the Syrians, a challenge they cannot overlook. And this is the 
message. Israel will not give up its aerial presence in Lebanese airspace. Air bombing 
are the most immediate, convenient and inexpensive way to hit terrorist bases whenever 
intelligence calling for this is received. It may be possible to reduce the bombing 
sorties a bit and increase the land or maritime raids, but it is virtually impossible 

to give up bombing from the air completely, and that is why it is not inconceivable 

that in the next few days Israel will once again face the need to bomb Lebanon, also in 
light of the new Syrian threat. 


What is even more serious, however, is the threat to the patrol and surveillance flights. 
A large part of the intelligence material gathered on the terrorists! and the Syrians! 
activity has been collected in this manner. Without this information, the IDF's capabil- 
ity to defend the State of Israel will be hampered. These flights therefore will be con- 
tinued and they must continue. 


How will the Syrians react? According to the preparations made by Syria at present, 
Israeli elements estimate that Damascus has no intention of leaving the track it has 
begun. The immediate purpose remains to substantially limit Israel's aerial freedom, 
this being the key to Israel's status in Lebanon. This is the first step toward settling 
the Lebanese problem in the way the Syrians desire. 


The question remains regarding the method and the timetable: The next time Israel bombs 
terrorist targets in Lebanon, will the Syrians again engage in a dogfight? This is 
possible, but not necessary. A possibility which is no less likely is the gradual 
reinforcement of the aerial early-warning system and the introduction ground-to-air 
missiles into Lebanese terrotory. This does not necessarily have to happen in the next 
few days, but Israel may suddenly find that Lebanese skies are defended by missiles and 
that its freedom in Lebanese skies has at least partially been reduced. If the IDF 
attacks the missile batteries to maintain this vital freedom of activity, the fighting 
will move to the land dimension, and the way toward escalation will be very short. 


It is, of course, reasonable to assume that the United States would intervene even 
before things reach this stage, but when fire begins even such reasonable assumptions 
are of limited value. In any case, the upcoming week or perhaps the next few weeks 
constitute the test period.

revolutionary elements or groups who have agents in the telephone exchanges in the 
communications company; or people who have the keys to the exchange centers located in 
the main streets could tap some of the telephones lines they are interested in.

MINISTER OF STATE EXPLAINS THIS YEAR'S BUDGET 
LD011124 Tehran Domestic Service in Persian 0330 GMT 1 Jul 79 LD 


[Text] ‘Ali Akbar Mo'infar, minister of state and the supervisor of the plan and budget 
organization, in a telephone interview with PARS last night described the characteristic 
aspects and priorities of this year's state budget. He said: This year's budget was 
submitted to the cabinet council yesterday afternoon and was considered at a 5-hour-long 
meeting so that it could be submitted to the revolution council for consideration. 


'Ali Akbar Mo'infar then referred to the priorities of this year's budget and said: 
[read by announcer] 


This year's budget amounts to 2,440 billion rials of which 880 billion will be spent on 
development projects and fixed investment; 60 billion rials have been allocated to special 
regional projects and road construction in rural areas which had already been authorized 
by the revolution council. 


In answer to the question: If you have prepared a balanced budget this year, how much is 
the deficit and how will this deficit be compensated for, the supervisor of the plan 

and budget organization said: This year's budget is not a completely balanced one as it 
has a deficit of 330 billion rials. Having said this, the total budget which last year 
amounted to 3,200 billion rials is 2,440 billion rials for this year. In view of the fact 
that the sum of 240 billion rials is caused by the relevant resulting increase from the 
change of the coefficient from 80 to 90 and from 90 to 100, as well as the general 
increase in the pay of the government employees, we have had to extensively economize in 
other respects. Hence, an effort has been made to omit excessive expenditure by as much 
as possible. 


In answer to the question on the level of the credit sources of the budget, and as to 
what proportion of taxes, oil revenues and non-oil exports have on the whole been 
contemplate therein, Mr 'Ali Akbar Mo'infar said: Despite the fact that the level of 

oil exports is far below that of last year, the predicted oil revenue for the current 
year amounts to 1,472 billion rials which is slightly less than revenues in the previous 
year. Of course, this figure takes into account the increase in oil prices at the recent 
OPEC conference. 


He said: A sum of 82 billion rials in the current year's budget has been predicted as 
special revenues for some organizations such as the Awgaf organizations, and the social 
security organization whose expenditures amount to similar figures. Mr Mo'infar said: 
This year's budget proposes the allocation of 80 billion rials on account of the price 
differences of the commodities which have been purchased at higher prices inside the 
country or abroad and are being supplied at lower prices to the consumer. Similarly, 

a sum of 80 billion rials has been predicted as compensation for the loss incurred by 
government companies. 


Ve 2 Jul 79 R 16 


'Ali Akbar Motinfar went on to refer to the issue of the nationalization of the banks 

and other financial units and said: In view of the fact that the banks are self- 
sufficient institutions and have had considerable profits so far, the adoption of Islamic 
principles by the banks will not necessarily lead to losses, Gradually a number of 

banks will be amalgamated, and economy measures will be taken on a great number of 
expenditures, especially on rents and other excessive expenditures. And secondly, the 
banks will, in actual fact, render commercial transactions and will become self-sufficient, 
and probably will add considerable sums to the state revenues which would not necessarily 
be procured through bank interest. Of course, this year's budget allocates no sum either 
as revenues of the banks or as expenditures, 


In answer to the question as to what sums have been allocated in this year's budget to 
industrial projects, Mr Motinfar said: In this year's budget, 30 billion rials have 
been envisaged for the expansion of plants; 20 billion rials for industries and modern- 
ization; 20 billion rials for petrochemical industries; 20 billion rials for the trans- 
portation of gas inside the country; and 30 billion rials for other industries, 


He said about the situation of some of the private factories which have now been put at 

the disposal of the government: As an example, the [word indistinct] factory has virtually 
become a government property because of its indebtedness to the government, He added that 
a major part of its shares belonged to the previous Pahlavi Foundation, and since its 

major shareholders have fled Iran, it has in effect been nationalized, 


He added: We were struggling to prepare a logical budget. Under the conditions of 
repression in the past, there was no urgency that a complete balance should be attained. 
We insisted on economizing in current expenditures and thus have open possibilities for 
spending money on development projects, There will be no difficulty having a tolerable 
budget deficit in view of the favorable revenue from oil. 


He said at the end: By having a reasonable budget deficit and by lowering the overall 
budget within reasonable limits and expending about 880 billion rials on development 
projects, we endeavored to set the wheels of the economy in motion, We hope that 
pouring this amount of money into the Iranian economy will not cause much inflation. We 
will especially encourage the people to render effective assistance to anti-inflation 
factors through savings and investment,

In the name of God, the compassionate, the merciful, The limit on the number of decisim - 
making centers in the country, which had top priority in the general session between the 
leader of our revolution, the Council of Ministers and the Revolutionary Council, is 

one of the vital and important issues which should be noted and investigated as soon as 
possible, This measure will show results quickly and will enable the Provisional 
Government of the revolution to occupy the foremost position on the political decision- 
making pyramid of the country, thus granting the government the decisive power to fulfil 
the inner wishes of the revolutionary nation of Iran, 


V. 2 Jul 79 R 17 


Following the appointment of the provisional government after the victory of our 
revolution, and because of the exceptional characteristics of Iran!s revolution, suddenly 
and inevitably the seat of power was divided between several main groups such as the 
Revolutionary Council, the committees, the government, and several minor and national 
groups which considered themselves worthy of power, Thus, the system of centralized 
power collapsed. The pressing need of our revolutionary nation is centralization and 
harmony of the elements of power all over the country, The time has come, therefore, 
that all existing structures in our society should move toward this aim. But it has 
become clear that the foundations of government which any government--and especially 

a revolutionary government--should rely on in order to act with power have somehow 
become unstable. Several [word indistinct] which at present act as the decision-making 
centers should undoubtably be removed, and leave the governing power to the government 
of the revolution, because the Provisional Government was approved by the leader of 

the revolution and, at least, its honesty and truthfulness is not and has not been 
hidden from anybody. 


On the other, the government as an acceptable political element, and at the level of a 
revolutionary government, should prove its existence on the political stage. It should 
take part in the political activities of the country as a political force, The reason 
is that we live under certain conditions whereby the most important problems are over- 
shadowed by the political activities and positions that people adopt in the country. 
Under these conditions, our government cannot function only as a service machine and be 
indifferent to political jockeying. But up to now, such has not been the position of 
the government, 


In less than 5 months since our victorious revolution, the book of deeds of the Bazargan 
government has many blank and unwritten pages, every one of which could have included 
important decisions and great changes such as the nationalization of banks, unused lands 


and insurance companies, and the ratification of the formation of regional councils. 
Even in these cases, the government has found it sufficient to announce the plans and, 
in the case of progress made in the projects and the existing problems, it has been 
silent up to now, and has not involved people in the ongoing events. 


Now there is the question of why the Provisional Government has not yet been able to 
achieve its revolutionary goals. The answer can be summarized in two items, and the 
answer refers back to government itself. The revolutionary government has not so far 
published a formalized and detailed program of the actions it intends to undertake, nor 
has there been any mention of the government!s program in speeches by the prime mi:..ster 
or his deputies or the government spokesmen, What today are the provisional government's 
plans on the policy toward foreign countries and domestic affairs? What are the economic 
and social plans adopted by the government and what are the problems and difficulties 

it faces in carrying out its plans? In other words, the government has not submitted 
any clear or formulated programs, 


On the other hand, the disharmony in the elements of this government, which we have 
repeatedly witnessed through their contradictory comments, is another reason for its 
internal weakness, 


V. 2 Jul 79 R 18 


The second answer is that despite the good intentions of other decision-making centers 
outside the Council of Ministers to enforce their own views, because of the disharmony 
between the decisions taken by these groups and the views of the Provisional Government, 
and the resulting overlap in the execution of these decisions and other actions, the 
hands of the Provisional Government are practically tied and it is prevented from carry- 
ing out its essential projects, The government, therefore, instead of dealing with its 
current affairs, is forced to answer for hastily made decisions which are unsuitable and 
discordant and which have been made by other power centers, sometimes concerning foreign 
policy and relations with other countries, 


We should keep in mind that our country is going through a sensitive period, and the 

need for a decisive political power other than the leadership of our revolution is un- 
deniable, Now we should consider the location of this political power which should 
originate from the nation, Is any power other than that of a revolutionary government 
believing in the revolution worthy of such a position, especially while we are in the 
middle of a revolution? Of course not, because as we have said before, the government 
enjoys the overwhelming support of the leader of the revolution, the people and the 
decisive majority of social and political groups, societies and actions; it is the 
government which should be the main center of decision-making and power, In addition 

to the above, and considering the present position of the country, the need for such a 
power is felt more and more. We are now on the verge of elections for the council, 

which has to ratify the constitution, and after that there will be the referendum and 
then an election for the appointment of a president. These steps, from the point of 

view that they comprise the replacement of a rotten regime, whose roots should completely 
dry up, with a revolutionary regime, are very difficult, sensitive and worthy of de- 
liberation, Of course, we should not forget the doomed efforts of the counterrevolution- 
aries at this juncture, because each counterrevolutionary act requires repair and 
reconstruction of the damage resulting from it as well as suppression, and this point, 
too, takes a great deal of government time, 


Therefore, reduction in the number of decision-making centers and the limitation of the 
authority invested in them, discussed in the general session of the leadership of the 
revolution, the government and the Revolutionary Council, will guarantee opportunities 
for the Provisional Government and will enable it, having the necessary power, to carry 
out the aims of our revolution,
