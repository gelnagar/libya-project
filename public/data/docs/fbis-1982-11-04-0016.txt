LE MONDE ON LIBYAN ROLE IN CHAD, CAR 


PM031335 Paris LE MONDE in French 3 Nov 82 p 4 


[Jean-Claude Pomonti report: "Colonel al-Qadhdhafi Supports Rebellion in Northern Chad 
and Supplies Military Aid to Central African Republic"] 


[Text] Despite the withdrawal of his troops in November 1981 to the extreme north of 

Chad, Colonel al-Qadhdhafi has apparently not abandoned a "foreign policy based on force" 
in central Africa. Not only is Libya supporting Mr Goukouni Oueddei's rebellion in 

Tibesti -- he has just announced the formation of a "Provisional Government of National 
Salvation" opposed to Mr Hissein Habre's government in Ndjamena -- but it has just supplied 
arms to the CAR. 


This Tuesday, 2 November, [the Paris weekly] LIBERATION's special envoy confirmed that Chad's 
former National Unity Transition Government [GUNT] president, ousted by Mr Hissein Habre on 

7 June, visited Bardai, in the heart of the Tibesti region, on 28 October on board a Libyan 
Arab Airlines Fokker F-27, and announced there the formation of a "government of national 
salvation." He was accompanied by Colonel Kamougue, former GUNT vice president and former 
leader of the "southern" Chad Armed Forces [FAT] and by Mr Acheik ibn Oumar, who became 
leader of the Democratic Revolutionary Council [CDR] after Acyl Ahmat's death. 


The Libyan troops, stationed in the Aozou Strip for more than 10 years, offer logistical 
support to the 2,000 or so Toubous on whom Mr Goukouni Oueddei can count in occupying 
Tibesti -- a virtually impregnable fortress. Faced with that danger Mr Habre, who was 
himself invested as president of Chad and formed his government in Ndjamena on 21 October, 
has sent reinforcements north. On 24 September a FAN detachment occupied Ounianga 

Kebir in the northern part of the Borkou region. Nontheless, Ndjamena's troops do not 
seem to have regained a foothold in places like Zouar or Gouro on the fringes of the 
Tibesti region. 


Mr Hissein Habre has said several times, notably since his arrival in Ndjamena on 7 
June, that he would safeguard "Chad's territorial integrity within the borders inheri- 
ted from the colonial period." While denying that arms had been supplied to him from 
Sudan and Egypt, he clearly expressed the hope that the United States might give him 
military aid. On the diplomatic level, his participation in the French-African 

summit in Kinshasa ensured him a firm basis in pan-Africam circles, France has again 
demonstrated that it regards him as the only legitimate Chadian interlocutor by sending 
him Development and Cooperation Minister Jean-Pierre Cot, who return to Paris this 
Tuesday evening after a 36-hour visit to Ndjamena. 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi, while in principle hoping to host the OAU annual summit in Tripol# 
at the end of November, seems to have no hesitation in maintaining a rebellion in 
northern Chad which would have very limited chances if it did not enjoy that support. 


Another less important but more surprising element in Tripoli's interventionism is the 
military aid sent to Bangui via the Congolese port of Pointe Noire. That aid was decided 
before the announcement on 15 September of the resumption of diplomatic relations between 

the two countries. On 22 October, 62 Libyan advisers arrived in the CDR. They are installed 
in 3 Bangui hotels -- to take delivery of 4 T-62 tanks (Soviet-type heavy tanks), 8 light 
armored vehicles and transport vehicles. After leaving Paris on 28 October, CAR President 
Kolingba, for his part, went to Tripoli for a "technical stopover," and it is not yet 

known how long he spent in the Libyan capital.

To avoid incurring the wrath of their Chadian neighbors and to calm the discontent of 
their Zairian and Gabonese allies, the leaders are playing down the importance of that 
Libyan military aid. The reason for it is all the more difficult to understand since 
1,200 French soldiers stationed in the CAR form a strong guarantee against any threat on 
the borders. 


The Chadian authorities are not slow to wonder about the destination of those weapons. 
In September, when Mr Hissein Habre's supporters easily occupied southern Chad, which 
borders on the CAR, some 250 GUNT fighters (CDR and FAT members) took refuge in the CAR, 
where they were disarmed. Although Bangui gave an assurance that they would be given no 
help, Mr Hissein Habre now teels constrained to maintain troops in the south which he 
badly needs to face Mr Goukouni Oueddei's rebellion in Tibesti.
