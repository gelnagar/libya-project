Summary (AI): A 1981 Boston Globe Magazine article describes former CIA employees Edwin Wilson and Francis Terpil recruiting Americans to train terrorists and make bombs for Qaddafi's Libya. It says a bomb-camouflaging workshop was in the basement of servants' quarters at a former royal palace about twenty miles south of Tripoli.
• Over twenty Americans, including five former CIA employees, a Green Beret on leave, five ex-Green Berets, five former army explosives experts, and two civilian naval engineers were allegedly flown to Libya.
• Millions of dollars of military hardware, including 25,000 pounds of explosives and a Redeye missile, were allegedly contracted for shipment to Libya without required US approval.
• The project allegedly ran from 1976 through at least 1979.
• King Idris was ousted in a bloodless coup in 1969 led by Muammar Khadafy.
• 1976-09: First Americans recruited for the Libyan affair informed the CIA of the operation; the agency notified the FBI.
• 1979-12: Operation reportedly continued at least until late December 1979, according to a federal investigators' report.
