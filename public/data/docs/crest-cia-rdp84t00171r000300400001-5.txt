Summary (AI): A November 1983 NPIC imagery report says Czechoslovak-designed Dana 152mm self-propelled guns were shipped to Libya by the ship BLANIK via Ploce, Yugoslavia, and were seen at the Tarabulus Army Barracks Prinn. The report is about arms exports and does not describe Tripoli sites in detail.
• Five Czechoslovak Dana probable 152mm guns/howitzers were on flatcars at Ploce port railyard, Yugoslavia.
• The ship BLANIK, a Czechoslovak Krakow-class merchant cargo ship, transported the weapons to Libya.
• The Dana SP gun is manufactured at the Dubnica Nad Vahom metallurgical and munitions plant, mounted on a TATRA-815 truck chassis.
• A sixth Dana gun was observed at Tarabulus Army Barracks Prinn.
• 1983-11: Five Dana 152mm SP guns/howitzers observed, plus a sixth gun observed separately

2. The Czechoslovakian Krakow-class merchant cargo ship BLANIK was present at Ploce Port 
Facilities Yugoslavia, on (Figure 2). On the latter date, five 25X1 
Czechoslovakian designed and produced Dana probable 152mm guns/howitzers were on flatcars 
in the railyard that serves the port (Figure 3). Four days later, the BLANIK was at Tarabulus Port 
Facilities in Libya, where it remained until (Figure 4). On that date, five 25X1 
Dana guns/howitzers were at Tarabulus Army Barracks Prinn. Although the guns were not actu- 
ally observed being loaded at Ploce, their subsequent arrival in Libya and the presence of the 
same ship at the Libyan port leave no doubt that the BLANIK was the ship that transported the 
weapons. (S/WN)

4. The Czechoslovakian Dana SP gun/howitzer, in production probably since at least Sep- 
tember 1981, is manufactured at the Dubnica Nad Vahom MTLG A Munitions Plant 25X1 
(Figure 5). The chassis is the TATRA-815 heavy truck chassis probably equipped with three 25X1 
stabilizer jacks, one on either side of the chassis and one at the rear. A wheeled chassis will give 
this weapons system reduced off-road performance but higher road speed than a tracked chassis. 
Maintenance on a wheeled vehicle is easier and less frequently required than on a tracked one. 
This vehicle mounts a probable Czechoslovakian-produced cannon. (S/WN) 

5. The Dana SP gun/howitzer has had limited deployment within Czechoslovakian forces, 
which makes it unusual that this piece of equipment has been exported. It has been identified at 
the following Czechoslovakian installations (keyed to Figure 5):
