ASSOCIATE OF EX-CIA AGENTS ADMITS AIDING LIBYA

Summary (AI): A Los Angeles Times article from February 1982 reports that Douglas Schlachter, an associate of former CIA agents Edwin Wilson and Frank Terpil, pleaded guilty to shipping explosives to Libya and helping Libya train saboteurs and terrorists from 1976 to 1978. Schlachter agreed to testify against Wilson and Terpil, who were living abroad as fugitives.
• Schlachter pleaded guilty to conspiracy and Arms Export Control Act charges; six other charges were dropped.
• Schlachter could receive a maximum of seven years in prison and a fine.
• Explosives were reportedly purchased from Jersey and disguised in ashtrays, alarm clocks and tea boxes.
• Wilson and Terpil were indicted in April 1980 and had lived abroad as fugitives since then.
• Schlachter had conferred with two high-ranking CIA officials about Wilson's Libyan activities at meetings in the Washington area in 1976 and 1977.
• 1976: Wilson and Terpil signed a multi-year contract with Col. Muammar Kadafi to train Libyan terrorists; Schlachter helped set up an explosives school in Libya.
• 1982-02-12: Douglas Schlachter pleaded guilty to conspiracy and Arms Export Control Act charges in federal court.
