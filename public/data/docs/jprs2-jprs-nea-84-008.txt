The moment there is an outage, it is automatically posted on a 
huge lighted map that covers an entire wall in the enormous room. 
The map shows the entire electric power system of Algiers. For 
each sector of the system, there is a measuring device showing 
the level of consumption at any given moment. 

This monitoring team is backed up by a second team that springs 
into action the moment an outage appears on the map. Sometimes 
there is a mad scramble, and other times there is absolutely no- 
thing happening. The peak hours come between 1800 and 2100 hours. 

Outages occur most frequently in foul weather, generally following 
a sudden gust of wind that downs the lines. There are other 
causes for outages: traffic accidents in which a car runs into 

a utility pole, or short-circuits usually attributed to flooding 
in poorly maintained basements. 

The engineer who runs the center tells us that, over the last few 
years, there has been a very sharp trend toward increased consump- 
tion of electricity. This is the natural consequence of the grow- 
ing use of household appliances. Says he: "I've watched it hap- 
pening on my amp-meters." There hasalso been an increase in the 
number of customers, with all these new apartment buildings on 

the outskirts of Algiers. "But," he concludes ruefully, "So long 
as they've got electricity, people don't even think about it. 

It's only when it's not there that they realize how precious it is."

LIBYA 

MISMANAGEMENT OF FUNDS REPORTED 
Tripoli AL-ZAHF AL-AKHDAR in Arabic 5 Dec 83 p 12 
[Article by Muhammad al-Miladi: "If You Feel no Shame, Do What You Wish!"'] 

[Text] Revolutionary supervision had yielded a large [repertory of ] practices 
by those who did not keep their hands off society's wealth. These people have 
been trying to enrich themselves by helping themselves to the public treasury at 
the expense of the large development projects that are going on in Libya. And 
here we are setting forth in front of the public [as we do] every time new lists 
of accounts: Funds are being smuggled in foreign currency [out of the country] as 
salaries and different expenses. The smuggling is done in a manner more curious 
than we had imagined. We are setting forth these bare facts because they 
constitute a model and a symbol. What remains as a matter of importance and 
necessity is that a law for economic crimes [be enacted] and those hands that 
infringe upon [public funds} be cut off. 

The folly {of those who infringe upon public funds] has reached such a point that 
they issued an authorization to disburse 4,589.35 dollars [four thousand and five 
hundred and eighty-nine dollars and thirty-five cents) in accordance with a dis- 
bursement form. The authorization was issued from account number 80171 which is 
registered under number 6284, file number 4:3:5 and dated 21 November 1983 A.D. 
This amount was for the balance of the salaries due to the member of the People's 
Committee for the months of November and December 1983 A.D. even though that 
individual was in Libya. This was done for no other reason than to indicate that 
they are powerful and that what has been published about them has not and will 
not affect them. The transfer of funds abroad is continuing. (If you feel no 
shame, do what you wish!) It is enough that the honorable member of the People's 
Committee has been guaranteed a living until the month of December 1983. That 
individual's salaries were transferred in advance. Let the hero become a hero in 
the eyes of the People's Committee! 

In order for |that individual] to be a hero in the eyes of the public, we cite 
the following expenses that the honorable member charged to us in Spain. These 
expenses came to a total of 142,000 pesetas, for hotels, telephone calls, therapy 
and tickets from 19 to 25 November 1982 A.D. In addition, there were the fol- 
lowing charges: 

--Another invoice for 753 pesetas for telephone calls made during the months of 
October and November 1982 A.D. 

--Another invoice for 15,080 pesetas for a round-trip ticket from Barcelona to 
Madrid and back. 

--Another invoice for 15,830 pesetas for a round-trip ticket from Barcelona to 
(Santa Nadrid) and back with a total [for both tickets] of 30,910 pesetas. 

--A loan to the honorable member in the amount of 285 Lityan pounds. In Spanish 
currency that amounted to 147,630 pesetas. | 

--An invoice for 79,756 pesetas for a ticket from Barcelona to Madrid, Tripoli, 
Tunis and back to Barcelona. 

--An invoice for 15,080 pesetas for a round-trip ticket from Barcelona to Madrid 
and back. 

--A cash payment of 65,60C pesetas made to the honorable member. 

--A cash payment of 13,055 pesetas made to the honorable member. 

--A payment made for rental car expenses: a total of 182,669 pesetas. 
--Medical expenses: 8,000 pesetas. 

--Telephone calls: 11,937 pesetas. 

--A total of 35,767 pesetas. 

--An invoice for 67,927 pesetas for the honorable member's expenses. 

--An invoice containing the expenses of the ball team and the member; that came 
to a grand total of 843,912 pesetas. 

Maritime Shipping paid all these invoices except the rent of two apartments. That 
was to be paid by the tenants. The total spent by the honorable member came to 
thousands of dollars. We don't know how he could allow himself to charge to us 
all these expenses despite the fact that more than 30,000 dollars and approxi- 
mately 12,000 dollars were transferred. These funds were for salaries, and we've 
already referred to them. Isn't this theft, and shouldn't the perpetrator of such 
theft by punished for his crime? 

The other member of the People's Committee charged to us the sum of 740 dollars 
for his meals aboard a foreign ship. There were 10 other persons with him. The 
firm that owns the ship sent the Maritime Shipping Organization an invoice reques- 
ting payment. That invoice was turned over to financial affairs for action. Isn't 
this also an infringement on public funds, and doesn't it deserve to be exposed 
and to be punished as theft is punished? 

In accordance with the foregoing the People's Committee of the Maritime Shipping 
Organization paid no attention to the organization's affairs because it was quite 
preoccupied with the task of acquiring any amount of foreign and local currency 
and travelers’ checks at the expense of the Libyan people. This lack of interest 
in the organization's affairs is evident to us in the following: 

10 

1. The manager of the Maritime Shipping Organization in Britain became involved 
in shipping goods to Libya on board foreign ships. He had forgeji the stationery 
and the seal of the organization and suggested that the organization agreed to 
have goods shipped on a competitor's ship. The manager was questioned, and he 
admitted committing the forgeries. But the secretary of the organization's 
People's Committee was satisfied with [issuing] the following statement: ''The 
investigation with the manager showed that he had taken action without per- 
mission. He was warned not to repeat such an action. A search for a replacement 
is being considered."' The date of that statement was 6 February 1983 A.D. 

We are now in December 1983 A.D. The same manager is still at his post, and 
doing very well, thank you. 

2. A Pakistani accountant was sent with another person in accordance with Decree 
Number 310 for 1983 A.D. to list the debts which the Maritime Shipping Adminis-— 
tration owed to foreign lines. This was done despite the fact that the Maritime 
Shipping Organization has native accountants who are university graduates. But 
with the Pakistani, the day will come when he will leave the country, and all 
that would be relegated to oblivion! 

These are some of the practices that occur in the Maritime Shipping Organization. 
We will-continue [our account of these practices] in the next issue.
