Summary (AI): A 1982 Washington Post article reports that former CIA agent Edwin P. Wilson, charged with exporting explosives, said in court filings that he had worked with the CIA while in Libya, which the agency denied. His lawyers argued he was useful to the government from abroad and had supplied information on Libya's nuclear efforts and on Letelier assassination fugitives.
• Wilson's bond was $20 million, and he was in federal custody at the time of the filing.
• Wilson claimed he gave the CIA information on Libyan efforts to acquire a nuclear bomb after his April 1980 indictment.
• Prosecutors reportedly categorized Wilson's information as worthless or designed to harm business competitors.
• The CIA spokesman again denied Wilson worked for the agency while in Libya.
• 1980-04: Wilson indicted in the United States
• 1981-07: Wilson reportedly gave information to prosecutors at a meeting in Rome on illegal aircraft parts shipments to Libya and on fugitives wanted in the 1976 Letelier assassination
