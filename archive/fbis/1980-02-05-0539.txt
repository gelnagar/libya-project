TUNISIAN EMBASSY IN LIBYA REPORTEDLY ATTACKED 4 FEB 
NCO41732 Paris AFP in English 1726 GMT 4 Feb 80 NC 


[Text] Paris, Feb 4, (AFP)--The Tunisian Bmbassy in Tripoli came under attack by 
demonstrators this morning at about the same time as a mob was ransacking the French 
embassy in the Libyan capital, Tunisian diplomats said here. 


Demonstrators apparently entered the Tunisian Bmbassy precinct as only one guard was 
on duty, all diplomatic personnel having been recalled. Tunisia is now expacting to recall 
its remaining consular staff from Libya, the same sources said. 


Embassy Not Damaged 
nc041812 Paris AFP in English 1832 gmt 4 Feb 80 NC 


[Excerpt] Paris, Feb 4, (AFP)--Tunisian diplomats hastily evacuated their embassy in 
Tripoli today in the face of a mob which has earlier sacked the French mission there, 
the Tunisian ambassador to France said. But Ambassador Hedi Mabrouk denied earlier 
reports that the Tripoli embassy had been sacked and burned, and said that diplomats 
there had been warned by Libyan police of the approach of the demonstrators. The 
diplomats had been advised to leave the building rapidly, and it had done so by the time 
the demonstrators arrived. No damage was caused to the embassy. 


FRENCH AMBASSADOR COMMENTS ON FRENCH EMBASSY INCIDENT 
IpD042306 Paris Domestic Service in French 1800 GmT 4 Feb 80 ID 


[Text] [Announcer] This morning it was every man for himself at the French Embassy in 
Libyae The building was suddenly attacked by demonstrators and the premises ransacked. 


These very violent incidents are, without any doubt, connected with the Qafsah events. 

The Libyans wanted to punish France for the military aid it gives to Tunisia. Anyway, 

that is the eplanation given by the ambassador himself, Charles Malo, who has taken 

refuge in his private residence where (Francoise Gaujour) telephoned him: [begin recording] 


[Malo] I assume that the demonstration was aimed at France's policy of freindship towards 
Tunisia which is resented here. Moreover, the demonstrators certainly had the Tunisian 
affair in their minds since they were shouting slogans such as "Popular Revolution in 
Tunisia!" "Popular Revolution in Tunis!" "Popular Revolution in Qabis!" "Popular 
Revolution in Safaqis!", and so on. The majority of the slogans related to the neighboring 
country of Tunisia rather than to France. 


{[Gaujour] What is the attitude of the population in Tripoli? 


[Malo] There is no hostility toward the French. [end recording] 


[Announcer] For the moment, the 2,000 French people living in Libya are not frightened 
of anything. All the same, a crisis headquarters--a crisis cell--is assembled at the 
Quai d'orsay at the moment. The French Government protests over what it calls an 
inadmissible and unspeakable act. And the Quai d'orsay adds that France will be able to 
draw conclusions from this affair regarding the level of French-Libyan relations.

LIBYA DECIDES NOT TO RAISE OIL PRICES 
NcO41930 Paris AFP in English 1920 GMT 4 Feb 80 NC 
[Text] London, Feb 4, (AFP)--Libya has decided not to raise its oil prices, going 
against the price increase trend started by Saudi Arabia one week ago and followed by 
almost all other members of the Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC). 
Libyan oil will stay at 34.72 dollars a barrel, But rather than charging a base price 
of 30 dollars a barrel plus an additional surcharge of 4,72 dollars per barrel, Libya 
will now charge a straight 34.72 dollars.
