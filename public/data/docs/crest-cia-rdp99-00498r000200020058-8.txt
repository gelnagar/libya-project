Summary (AI): A 1981 New York Times (AP) article reports the CIA acknowledged some employees, without its knowledge, supported former agent Edwin P. Wilson in dealings with Libyan leader Muammar al-Qaddafi. Wilson and Frank Terpil were indicted in 1980 for supplying Qaddafi with explosive timers, setting up a terrorist training school in Libya, and conspiring to assassinate the Libyan leader.
• CIA said the investigation found no official encouragement or involvement by the agency.
• Two employees were forced to resign; the CIA declined to name them, but law enforcement sources named Patry Loomis and William Weisenburger.
• The article is dated 27 August 1981 and the AP dateline is 26 August.
• 1980-04-25: Federal grand jury indicts Edwin P. Wilson and Frank Terpil on charges of illegally supplying Qaddafi with explosive timers, setting up a terrorist training school in Libya, and conspiring to assassinate the Libyan leader
• 1981-08-26: CIA acknowledges some employees supported Wilson in his dealings with Qaddafi without agency knowledge; two employees were forced to resign by Admiral Stansfield Turner
