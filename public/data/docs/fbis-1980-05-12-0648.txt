AL-QADHDHAFI ELABORATES ON PHYSICAL LIQUIDATION DOCTRINE 
LD120230 Tripoli Voice of the Arab Homeland in Arabic 2315 GMT 11 May 80 


[Excerpts] The brother leader of the revolution the day before yesterday met with the revo- 
lutionary committee of the oil and mineral engineering college at Al-Fatih University. The 
meeting was attended by Lt Col Mustafa al-Kharrubi and Maj al-Khuwaylid al-Humaydi; Ahmad 
Jibril, secretary general of the PFLP--General Command; Mohamed Abdul Aziz, secretary general 
of the POLISARIO Front; and Bashir Mustafa, assistant secretary general of the front. 


The leader talked about the revolutionary (?basic) doctrines which were adopted by the third 
meeting of the revolutionary committees. He affirmed that every revolutionary must under- 
stand and absorb these revolutionary doctrines because they form new revolutionary (?concepts) 
affirming that physical liquidation is the last stage in the controversial revolutionary 
struggle to put a final end to it. If the economic, political and social processes to stop 
the activities of the opposition forces have not done this, then physical liquidation becomes 
imperative, and this is the last stage in the controversial revolutionary struggle for a 
final decision, 


The leader affirmed that we cannot say the revolution has succeeded when the counter- 
revolutiormary forces still maintain their strength and weapons. We can say the revolution 
has triumphed when revolutionary forces are able to strip the counterrevolutionary forces 
of their weapons. Therefore, physical liquidation is not a necessity in the stage of revo- 
lutionary transformation (?in the encounter), but it occurs in the end. 


(?It is necessary) to deprive the counterrevolutionary class of their political, economic 

and social weapons. When the workers staged the revolution on 1 September 1978, they seized 
the factories, contracting establishments and all centers of production. They overthrew 

the business class, the contractors and agents. [sentence indistinct] From that day the 
workers have become free and these weapons are in the hands of the revolutionary forces, 

in the hands of the popular masses. The weapons of the businessmen were the factory, capital 
and contract licenses. All the economic potentials which they held were dangerous weapons 
through which they were able to enslave us. The workers are not free when they are employees; 
and, indeed, they are slaves to the businessman regardless of whether this employer is a 
government or an individual. 


A worker or a free country which practices democracy does not exist as long as the worker 
still remains an employee to any quarter. The worker is not free as an employee. 


The leader explained another revolutionary doctrine adopted by the third meeting of the 
revolutionary committees, and it is this: The defensive and security organs [words indis- 
tinct] remain essential until the people uphold power over them even if these organs do not 
belong to the society of the masses, until such time when a popular substitute replaces 
them and end their existence automatically. [passage indistinct]

hold power. Therefore, the (?police forces) and secret police in Libya after the emergence

and in fact, today they are being used in the service of the masses, There is no longer

LIBYA DEMANDS COMPENSATION FOR WORLD WAR II LOSSES 
LD112130 Tripoli JANA in English 1758 GMT 11 May 80 


[Text] Tslali, May 11, JAMAHIRIYAH NEWS AGENCY--The foreign secretary, Dr. 'Ali 'Abd 
as-Salam at-Turayki, affirmed in a statement today that the Jamahiriyah has complete right 
to be compensated for the heavy losses it incurred during the Second World War and later 
when its territory was used as a minefield and a launching point for international terrorism 
to serve no interests of its own, 


Dr at-Turayki stressed also that the Jamahiriyah had put this subject before regional and 
international organisations. Those included the Islamic conference, foreign ministers 
conference, the summit meeting of the nonaligned states, the UN General Assembly and the 

UN Environmental Programme (UNEP), which issued resolutions condemning the imperialist 
countries for the remnants of its wars, especially mines, and held them responsible for the 
ensuing moral and material damages. Dr at-Turayki added: ‘Those organizations have called 
upon such countries to submit immediately all relevant facts concerning the location of mines 
to the countries that have incurred the damages. Furthermore, international resolutions 
have also called for compensations to be paid in such circumstances, UNEP has entrusted 
its executive director to prepare a reportonthe damages incurred upon the Jamahiriyah 
peoples and others,.' 


In conclusion, the foreign secretary asserted that the Foreign Secretariat in the Jamahiriyah 
has gathered all the relevant documents in preparation for their subsequent presentation 

to the countries concerned and implementation of the resolution passed by the above-mentioned 
organizations,

TA091155 Jerusalem Domestic Service in Hebrew 1105 GMT 9 May 80 


[Interview with Interior Minister and Chairman of the Israeli Ministerial Committee for the 
Autonomy Affairs Dr Yosef Burg by Razi Bargqalti; time and place not given--recorded ] 


[Text] [Question] Dr Burg, I believe that new bad tidings have come from Cairo, that the 
working teams will not meet either? 


[Answer] In any case, this is the report. If these are [had] tidings--time will be the 
judge. I was surprised by the first version--that the ministerial committee will not meet 
and that only the working teams will meet--because that was somewhat illogical. If there 

is an embargo [on the talks of] the ministers, it is hard to conceive that the working teams 
would be able to operate without contimuous contact with the ministers. Moreover, this is 

a question of atmosphere. If that is indeed the case, then the report we have just received 
that the work of the ministerial delegations and the working teams--formed by senior officials 
to discuss issues--are both suspended, then things are clear and the atmosphere is clear. 
However, they are clear but not good. 


[Question] You are speaking about a clear atmosphere. Does it mean that President as-Sadat 
decided that the talks have failed? 


[Answer] I do not know. In any case, he did not tell me about it. However, he has 
apparently decided to exert some pressure. What is interesting about it is that the date 
today is 9 May, and, besides the sabbaths and holidays, there are about 2 weeks left 

until 26 May. Therefore, it is very strange that precisely before that date--instead of 
making a supreme effort to reach some sort of understanding--he made such a step. However, 
I do not know. Perhaps the conclusions of the Accadia talks--we began to discuss the prin- 
ciples of Israel's security--perhaps As-Sadat did not like that conclusion. I really do not 
know. 


[Question] Dr Burg, did you gain the impression or did you receive any information that 
As-Sadat's decision was coordinated with the United States? 


[Answer] I think the contrary would be true. I have no such information. However, I have 
gained the impression that, if the report is true that even the delegation members who 
stayed in Herzliyya this week and returned to Egypt on Thursday were also surprised at 
As-Sadat's remarks, then this holds true as far as the Americans are concerned. 


[Question] That is to say, according to you, the Egyptian prime minister and foreign 
minister were also surprised by As-Sadat'!s decision? 


[Answer] This is according to reports, and this is my impression based on what I heard 
from the Americans. This afternoon I will probably receive a call from Mr Linowitz. 
Then I will be wiser. 


V. 12 May 80 


[Question] Sir, how does it always happen that President as-Sadat steals the show? 


[Answer] I do not think that he steals the show. I use a different terminology regarding 
the president, if you will excuse me for it. I think that he made what he thinks is a 

right move. Certainly, he did not take this step without considering the impression. 
However, I think he was wrong, because on the eve of 26 May he is the one postponing and not 
us. Therefore, I think that this issue does not positively affect his status in the Free 
World. 


[Question] Nevertheless, one cannot ignore the fact that the progress made in the talks was 
very minimal. In fact, the main issues are still being disputed. How does one get out of 
this situation? 


[Answer] First of all, I do not know what people thought when they conceived that all the 
issues could be concluded within 1 year. I think that complicated issues take a long time. 
One cannot overcome 30 years of wars in 12 months of negotiations. In our case, there are 
other elements active in the area: there is the PLO terror; there is a European initiative, 
Which is definitely detrimental, in my opinion. Therefore, I do not think it is fair. I 
can only reiterate my statements at the Accadi~ Hotel this week; those who watch a marathon 
and those who run the marathon have diiferent criteria regarding the pace and the progress 
made by the racer. 


{Question] Finally, perhaps we will connect this issue to another issue in today*®s headlines. 
I am referring to the proposal or desire expressed by *Ezer Weizman that the National

[Answer] I heard ite=pardon me--I heard it from you; I heard it on the radio; I did not

such remarks from the defense minister. If the defense minister advocates such things then 
there is a forum called the Cabinet where this issue should be raised or at the Ministerial 
Committee for Security Affairs. It is not my practice to argue with a friend with whom I 
sit together over something he did or did not say over the radio.

MA'tARIV Interview 
TAO090853 Tel Aviv MA'ARIV in Hebrew 9 May 80 pp 13, 14 


{Interview with Israeli Defense Minister 'Ezer Weizman by Dov Goldstein--time and place 
not given] 


[Excerpts] [Question] Will you serve as minister of defense or ac¢gept another portfolio 
in a government headed by the Alignment? 


[Answer] If the political organization in whose name I am elected to the Knesset joins 
a coalition with the Alignment, and the Alignment prime minister, if there is one, asks 
me to join his government, I will. If the political organization to which I belong 
remains in the opposition, I too will be in the opposition. 


[Question] Ih such an event, will you serve in a government headed only by Shim'on Peres? 


[Answer] Certainly not. If Yitzhaq Rabin heads the government and circumstances make 
us coalition partners, I will serve in Rabin's government. Despite all the differences, 
we have a joint military background and this certainly counts for a lot. 


[Question] You have known about the existence of the "National Guidance Committee" for 
a long time. What is your attitude toward it, its composition, discussions and influence 
over what is going on? 


[Answer] I have said this before. We are in a difficult and extended struggle for 

Eretz Yisratel. The Arabs who face us believe in the rightness of their path. They 

want a Palestinian state. They do not hide this desire. They are not saying that they 
wish to negotiate with us for the establishment of the autonomy. They are not saying 

that they wish to negotiate with us on conditions for the annexation of the territories 

to Israel. What do they wish to negotiate with us about--about the conditions for the 
establishment of a Palestinian state, and we cannot negotiate this with them. However, 
does this imply that we will not discuss any issue with the political leadership of 

the Arabs in the territories? If we want to persuade the Arabs to cooperate on the 
autonomy--elect a council and be elected to it--I want to see an acceptable political 

body that does not call for violence or civil rebellion with which we can negotiate and 
reach a dialog. If we reach agreement with the Egyptians on the autonomy, then the 
Palestinians will have to implement it, and there must be a body with whom we will talk. 

If the National Guidance Council does not preach physical violence and does not claim that 
a Palestinian state should be established instead of Israel it can be the political body that 
will serve as a partner in talks and negotiations on who will organize the elections, how 
the self-administration will be composed, who will establish a strong police force and how. 


At Camp David Prime Minisver Menahem Begin promised PresZdent Jimmy Carter that we 

would grant the inhabitants of the territories freedom of political activity. We stressed 
the difference between political freedom and the "liberty" to advocate civil rebellion 

and advocatte violence. Therefore, if the National Guidance ommittee meets this condition, 
it can be a partner to the negotiations. It would be good to have a recognized leadership 
there. If it does not meet the condition, I will recommend that it be outlawed. 


V. 12 May 80 N 7 ISRAEL 


[Question] What will happen if, despite all the difficulties, we achieve an agreement 
with Egypt on the autonomy, and the Palestinians in Judaea and Samaria do not agree t 
cooperate and refuse to conduct negotiations on the implementation of the autonomy? 


[Answer] In such a events, there will be two courses open: a continuation of the present 
situation with the military government, or Israel can unilaterally implement the agree- 
ment with Egyot. For example: If the Egyptian-Israeli agreement provides for the 
evacuation of tne military government from Arab population centers, we will do this. If 
it calls for the IDF's redeployment in one form or another, we will do it. 


[Question] Which of the two paths appeals to you? 


[Answer] The second. If we reach agreement with Egypt, I will recommend that Israel begin 
to implement it unilaterally, even without the cooperation of the Palestinians and even 
if they do not wich to discuss the autonomy with us. 


[Question] And if, as a result of this, Nabulus, Hebron or Ramallah become PLO centers 
from which terrorists squads attack Israel? 


[Answer] Then we will return in full force and totally abolish the autonomy. 


[Question] Do you not fear the possibility that a harsh policy in the territories will 
cause civil rebellion or, at least, severe bloodshed that will destroy the chance of 
achieving an agreement on the autonomy and even harm peace with Egypt? 


[Answer] I fear yarious possibilities and developments. When I decide on a military 
action of any kind, not just in Judaea and Samaria, the consideration of how it will affect 
the peace with Egypt is something I take into account along with other considerations. 
Today Z had lunch with General 'Ali at the Accadia Hotel, and we were served by a waiter 
called Mustafa from Tulkarm. The general and he conversed, in Arabic, of course, and I 

saw this as a manifestation of a reality with which we all have to live now. It is not 
that Mustafa from Tulkarm has to be a waiter serving lunch to General 'Ali and me. But 
this reality of Arabs from the territories working in Israel and Israelis walking around 
the territories, and the two economic systems being influenced by each other and the weaving 
of this delicate tapestry of coexistence--this is the reality in our region, and this is 
how it will remain without the establishment of a Palestinian state. 


Of course I fear the development of a civil rebellion or serious bloodshed. Therefore, I 

do not want to use firm measures outside those places, such as Hebron, where this is 
necessary. This is not a "festival of punishment." Whoever proposes that on "this festive 
occasion" I settle other accounts and expel, for example, Bassam ash-Shak*ah because now, in 
the present atmosphere, it can be done easily--I say to them: No, with a capital N. We will 
not behave in this manner. This is exactly what the PLO wants us to do. More firmness, 

more expulsions, more explosions, more bloodshed and mutual attacks and the whole peace proesss 
will be washed away. This week the Egyptians said to me: "The murderers in Hebron were 

not only out to get at you, even though it was your blood that was spilled. They also 

meant us. They oppose peace. They hate Egypt no less than they hate you." There is no 
reason for us to do what these murderers want.

[Answer] Certainly. It was a mistake and remains a mistake. The government has to decide

settlements was right. The thing that disturbs me about the government on the settlement 
issue is not only the "music" but also the "tone," We talk too much about settlements 
instead of deciding on what is important to do, what we can do, and then do it. 


[Question] This week, when your colleague and friend, Egyptian Defense Minister General 
'Ali submitted to the Egyptian plan for the autonomy security issue (especially the clause 
that the IDF will have to have the permission of the Arab autonomy institutions in order 
to act) the media in Israel reported that the Israeli ministers were stunned and shocked, 
Were you stunned and shocked? 


[Answer] The word stunned is not acceptable, I can understand battle shock after a heavy 
shelling. After such a shock one cannot function, We did function, We even functioned 
properly. It is true that I was surprised, but this is the nature of negotiations. It 
starts up high, say with 100 points, in order to compromise at the end of the process on 
50 points. Is it only the Egyptians that act in this way? In many cases we also began 
from high negotiating positions in order to achieve a positive result, At Camp David 

the Egyptians submitted an initial document...we started to pack our bags. We did not 
pack, and we did not go, and agreement was reached. 


General 'Ali said what he said. Okay, we did not dance a jig. In a private talk I was 
very explicit with him: "I am telling you as a friend that you have made a mistake, 

both in the presentation of this issue and in its content. Why did you do this?" He 
made various interpretations and gave various explanations. He is a very wise and 
experienced man, Twice, in 1948 and in 1967, he was injured in the wars with Israel, 

He is a polite man and has a good understanding of our security problems. But he is not 
a member of the Israeli Government and not even a disciple of Jabotinsky. He has 
positions, and we have to contest him and his positions, It is a fact that the Egyptians 
have withdrawn their inflexible proposal, the one that "shocked us", and even now the gap 
between our conceptions is still great, 


For example, the Egyptians make a very interesting contention. General 'Ali said to me: 
"You say that Israel needs to establish settlements for security reasons. Let us look 
at which settlements have been set up for security reasons and which have not, Those

should be abolished or moved elsewhere. It is written in Camp David that IDF forces 
will move from their present positions and deploy in defined regions, The same should 
apply to the settlements," 


[Question] What is the Israeli answer to this contention? 
[Answer] That Israel will be solely responsible for security and nothing else is conceivable,

[Answer] In fact, except for one time in Alexandria, I did not participate in the Israeli

criticized and praised. For a while I wanted to detach myself from the negotiations with 
Egypt and be active on issues to which I am directly responsible as defense minister: The 
withdrawal to the Al-fArish-Ra's Muhammad line, the relationship with the Egyptian defense 
establishment, the normalization and so on,

The autonomy discussions have been going on for 11 months, but nothing has moved. I am 
not being critical. I very much respect Dr Burg and enjoy my talks and meetings with 
him. However, since the discussions have presently reached the security subject--a 
subject I deal with anyway in my capacity as defense minister--I thought it would be

[Question] Do you think it is possible? 


[Answer] Yes; otherwise I would not have joined. I definitely believe that things can 
be moved, If these negotiations fail, I do not want to be left with the feeling that I

nothing about it. 


[Question] Do you favor the plan "Gaza first?"

[Question] During the prime minister's visit to the United States, you called for

[Answer] Grateful? This is not the exact word. I appreciate his act. I did not

[Question] And do you still favor early elections? 


[Answer] I have not retracted my statements. We should examine the means for changing

However, the people are walking around with their heads bowed. One should examine why 
the national mood is so low instead of criticizing the people. If we reach the date 
scheduled for the elections with this kind of spirit, our situation will not be very 
good. 


Two paths lie ahead of us: to immediately formulate a plan for action in the 18 months 
left until the elections and firmly implement it, or to return the mandate to the people 
and ask them to decide on the rulers, The third way--another plastering, another 
"malfunctioning," another half-move, is self-defeating and should be ruled out,
