After visiting New York the foreign minister stopped over in West Germany, Italy 
and Yugoslavia. Nothing is known of his talks with West German and Italian

a move to change the venue for the next meeting of the non-aligned nations from

In Tehran, back from his visit abroad, Velayati tried hard to prove that his 
failure to stop the Baghdad meeting was inevitable, and that this did not repre- 
sent a setback for the Iranian regime's foreign diplomacy. In any case Iran 
will not attend the meeting; thus the mullahs are losing a valuable platform. 


For his part Moadikhah was unable to make much of a showing with members of his 
mission sent abroad to publicise the importance of Iran's revolution to the 
Third World nations. These activists were barred from most Islamic countries, 
and as a result have so far visited India, Rumania and West Germany. India also 
rejected a request to call for the moving of the non-aligned meet from Baghdad.

Perhaps the regime's biggest diplomatic setback was a move by Algeria and Libya 
to influence Syria to resume its peace initiative between Iran and Iraq. Reli- 
able sources report that Libya has been urged by the Algerian leaders to tone

The Algerian leaders have also let it be known that they would be ready to 
mediate with Velayati over the war if asked to do so by both Iran and Iraq and 
basic conditions for discussions were properly agreed beforehand. It is known 
that Algiers is pretty exasperated with the leaders in Tehran and has been 
unenthusiastic about having closer ties than at present. 


Meanwhile, the foreign ministers of Gulf Cooperation Council states have again 
appealed to Iran to come to the negotiating table in a bid to end the war. At 
the same time they pledged their full support to Bahrain and other member states 
in the event of further attempts by Iran to undermine then. 


There were signs that the Iraqi forces were renewing their activity along parts 
of the war front as a preamble to what some Baghdad reports hint will be an 
attempt to reverse some of the gains made recently by Iran. The Iraqis claimed 
advances in the Bostan area, where Iran made ground late last year in a fierce 
attack which led to very heavy losses. New crack units are said to be going into 
action at other parts of the front. 


Tehran has denied the Iraqis have made any advances, saying their attacks were 
repulsed.

SAUDI ARABIA 


GREECE SEEKS NEW OIL PACT 
Beirut THE ARAB WORLD WEEKLY in English No 655, 13 Feb 82 pp 17-18 


[Text] The Greek government is expected to sign, this week, an oil agreement 
with the Saudi crude oil company Petrolin, according to Greek Deputy Minister 
of Commerce John Papspyrou. In a statement to the Saudi English-language daily 
ARAB NEWS, Mr Papspyrou said that the quantity and price are still to be nego- 
tiated, specifying that the agreement will hold through 1982. 


According to the deputy commerce minister, the total quantity of oil that Greece 
is seeking will be reduced as compared to the annual import of 2.5 million tonnes 
during the last three years. Mr Papspyrou explained that this reduction of oil 
imports from Saudi Arabia will only be valid for 1982. He added that this policy 
comes under a conservation policy designed to cut back imports from all sources 
to only five or 5.5 million tonnes. He underlined that last year, Greek oil 
imports reached eight million tonnes. 


In further statements Mr Papspyrou said that the ne Greek government, elected 
in October 1981, attempts to reduce Greece's trade balance deficit by cutting 
back oil imports to a minimum. "The government has decided to use more oil from 
its three million tonnes reserves which is blocking foreign exchange," he added. 


Supplies would be reduced with greater quantities cut back from the other sources, 
mainly Iraq, Libya, Tunisia, and the Soviet Union. Mr Papspyrou said that 
preference was given to Saudi Arabia because of its good quality oil which is 
suitable for refined products. 


He underlined that a considerable amount of the refined products are export- 
oriented. 


Trade: Saudi Arabia is the fifth largest client of the Greek market. It imports 
large quantities of cement, refined products, aluminum, marble, olives, tomato 
paste, steel and iron products. Elsewhere, it should be noted that more than 
10,000 Greek nationals are currently working in the kingdom, mainly in the vari- 
ous development projects being undertaken in the different parts of Saudi Arabia.
