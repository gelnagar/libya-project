Shelving the Differences 


In his first post-election interview with TIME magazine, President-elect Ronald 
Reagan named Jordan as an alternate choice and expressed “his belief *hat Jordan 
is the key to a compromise to avert war between Muslims who believe that the way 
to enter paradise is to fight Christians or Jews," 


President Reagan appeared optimistic in his discourse about a compromise with the 
help of the United States. However, his naming of Jordan as the key to a 
compromise was not new...for President Carter had the door to Camp David negotia- 
tions open to Jordan for as long as 2 years, and King Husayn did not respond. 

But Reagan's naming of Jordan towards the beginning of his rule does not facilitate 
the task of the Jordanian monarch to call for a summit meeting or to cause 
decisions to be issued for the following reasons: 





First, the Arab Position--Syria affirmed its request for a postponement of the 
summit meeting. It was reaffirmed by Libya and Eden. While Algeria takes this 
stand impliedly, Saudi Arabia uses all it has from status to support the Jordanian 
position which calls for its convening. 


For this reason, there is more than one Arab official who believes that the 
probabilities for the postponement of the summit meeting are stronger than the 
probabilities for convening it. While the Iraqi-Iranian war continues to escalate, 
Iraq's allies continue to be adamant in their position and the Arab countries which 
are on Iran's side raise their voices calling for the cessation of the war. 


Second, the International Position--The Iraqi Foreign Minister, Mr Sa'doun Hamadi, 
had called his colleagues at the seven-member committee of foreign ministers, which 
was meeting in Amman to prepare for the summit, to shelve their differences and
