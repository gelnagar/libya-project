Summary (AI): A 1981 New York Times article, reprinted in a CIA reading-room file, reports that the U.S. State Department confirmed Americans were servicing Libyan Air Force planes and serving as crew on charter flights ferrying troops to Chad. The article concerns Libyan foreign military activity and recruitment of Americans, with no places in Tripoli named.
• Americans were recruited and paid by a Swiss company not controlled by Wilson, per the article's account of the Libyan recruitment.
• Justice Department said recruiting American pilots and technicians would not appear to violate federal law, though officials said enlisting in a foreign military is illegal.
• Libyan troops had been in virtual occupation of parts of Chad since the previous year.
• State Department said it would be willing to give material assistance to the OAU to replace Libyan troops in Chad with African peacekeepers.
• 1981-11-02: State Department spokesman Dean Fischer confirms Americans serviced Libyan Air Force planes (including C-130 transports and CH-47 helicopters) and served as charter crew ferrying troops and equipment; the department calls the involvement reprehensible.
