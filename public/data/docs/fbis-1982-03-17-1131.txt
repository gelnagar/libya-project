LIBYA

JANA COMMENTS ON PLANNED U.S. MANEUVERS IN SIDRA 


LD162020 Tripcli JANA in English 1953 GMT 16 Mar 82 


[Text] Tripoli, 16 Mar (JANA) -- In an urgent item from Washington, the French news agency 
reported tonight that the American secretary for the navy, John Lehman, announced today, 
Tuesday, that the American Sixth Fleet in the Mediterranean would hold manoeuvres in the 
Gulf of Sidra on the Libyan coast during the next six months. 


JANA's political editor has commented on this statement by the American secretary of the 
navy and said: "If the American minister's announcement is confirmed, though we do not 
believe this, it has no explanation justifying it except that America has decided to invade 
Libya. In this case, Libya must get ready to confront a big state -- and this means a third 
world war is imminent, for which the American administration is held responsible. For an 
aggression on the Gulf of Sidra is an aggression on Libyan territories and on the integrity 
of Libyan soil.

JANA EDITOR REJECTS MUBARAK'S NEWSWEEK REMARKS 


LD162158 Tripoli JANA in English 1755 GMT 16 Mar 82 


[Text] Tripoli, 16 Mar (JANA) -- In a recent statement to the U.S. magazine NEWSWEEK, 
President Husni Mubarak stressed the importance of resuming relations between Egypt 
and the other Arab countries. He said Egypt was part of the Arab homeland with which 
it has enjoyed good relations for hundreds of years and that the Egyptian peace treaty 
with Israel is not inconsistent with Egypt's relations with the other Arab countries. 


Our political editor commented on the statement saying: We agree with President Mubarak 
regarding the need for Egypt to return to the Arab fold but not in the way Egypt has 
taken. Egypt's return becomes more impelling when it burns everything presented by the 
traitor As-Sadat as capitulationist documents which sanction dealings with the Zionist enemy. 


We strongly believe in Egypt as being part of the Arab nation with which it has had 
historic ties. The Arab nation severed its relation with Egypt because As-Sadat's Egypt 
committed an act of apostasy when it recognised an alien entity planted in its heart. It 
had, thus, given the green light to the Israelis to expel the Palestinians from their homeland. 


We do not accept President Mubarak's statement that the Camp David accords are not 
inconsistent with Egyptian-Arab relations because we believe the capitulationist treaty to 
be absolutely contradictory to Egypt's relations with the Arab nation. This is a [word 
indistinct] which the Egyptian president cannot neglect in discussing the question of 
Egypt's return to the Arab fold." 


SUDANESE OPPOSITION URGES INCREASED STRUGGLE 


LD170956 Tripoli JANA in English 0855 GMT 17 Mar 82 


[Text] London, 17 Mar (JANA) -- Mr Zayn al-'Abidin, the provisional leader of the Executive 
Committee of the Sudanese Democratic Party, called upon the Sudanese people to escalate the 
struggle in order to topple the agent regime of the country. 


He told a press conference held in London on Monday that the party includes more than 
6,000 committee sections within Sudan in addition to the members of the party abroad 
who are endeavouring to overthrow the rule of stooge Numayri. Numayri is presently 
stirring religious and tribal conflicts leading the country towards chaos and is being 
used as an agent to carry out U.S. and Arab reaction schemes in the region. 


Concerning the economic situation, Mr Zayn al-'Abidin said that Sudan's debts have surpassed 
the 4 billion dollar figure and that inflation is at 100 percent rate despite aid from 

other countries. He added that his party has warned these governments that the aid is 
looked [as received] by members of the regime and their aides instead of being invested 

in projects to help the Sudanese people's struggle to support its national economy. 


On Sudanese foreign relations, Mr Zayn al-'Abidin said Sudan's relations with neighbouring 
countries are critical after the regime's alliance with imperialism and Arab reaction. 

The U.S. Government is backing this agent regime to help it get rid of some of its 

crisis and consolidate its repressive force. 


On the internal situation, the leader of the Sudanese opposition said that his country 
is going through a state of unrest never witnessed. Railway workers and judiciary [as 
received] who constitute the majority of the working force staged a strike which was 
violently thwarted by the authorities. A large numberof union members were also arrested. 


Most professional and skilled workers fled Sudan to protect their dignity or seek imployment 
abroad, most of whom were teachers, engineers and doctors. As for the armed forces, he said 
the Sudanese Army is practically finished as the hired ruling authority is backed by America. 


He concluded by affirming that the regime is near collapse as the Sudanese people are 
determined to topple stooge Numayri.
