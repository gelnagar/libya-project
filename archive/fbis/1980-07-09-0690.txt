LIBYA

LE MONDE CITES DISSIDENT ON ANTI-AL-QADHDHAFI CAMPAIGN 
LDO40855 Paris LE MONDE in French 28 Jun 80 p 4 


[Michel Deure dispatch: "Libyan National Democratic Movement Wants to Extend 
Oppositionist Action"] 


[Text] Tunis--The "Libyan National Democratic Movement" whose objective is to overthrow 
Colonel al-Qadhdhafi's regime intends to emerge from the anonymity it has preserved since 
its formation 3 years ago. The composition of its politburo will be announced 
immediately after the Congress it is going to hold this summer in a European capital, 
Executive Bureau Foreign Relations Chief (Fadel Messaoudi) told us while on a brief 

visit to Tunis. Only the names of members living in Libya will remain secret. 


The movement, which essentially comprises intellectuals and "moderate" politicians most 
of whom live abroad, has apparently succeeded in organizing cells inside Libya where 

it also enjoys active support within the army. The principal task of its congress will 
be to revise the principles of its charter for "a national and democratic regime based 
on the supremacy of law, respect for civil liberties and the people's effective 
participation in running the country...through constitutional and modern institutions." 
However, according to (Messaoudi) this congress will, above all, provide an opportunity 
to draw up new plans for sabotage aimed at stepping up the atmosphere of insecurity in 
Libya and disturbing the climate still further. "Thirteen members of revolutionary 
committees were killed in Tubruq at the end of March following clashes with the 
population," he specified. "We also set fire to three oil wells. Our action will be 
more particularly directed at oil companies, especially U.S. oil companies, because we 
think that it is ultimately these companies which constitute Al-Qadhdhafi'ts main support 
and we want to show them that other forces exist in Libya." 


(Messaoudi) was more discreet about methods of combating the commando groups appointed 
by Tripoli to kill Libyan oppositionists living abroad. "We will be increasingly vigilant 
in watching all those we might suspect of belonging to Al-Qadhdhafits green brigades," 
he said. "We have noted the goodwill shown by the French, Swiss, British, German and 
U.S. governments in ensuring Libyans! safety and we have on occasions given them and 
Arab governments information about guspects. We have no intention of countering 
violence with violence in foreign countries and resorting to terrorism...except 
perhaps in Italy where the green brigade killers find too many facilities, and too 
mich complicity and indulgence. Moreover, we have told the Italian Government 

that we will possibly be forced not only to defend ourselves but also to retaliate 
for attacks." 


(Messaoudi) reported that in the past week repression has been stepped up still further 
in Libya, mainly against intellectuals and businessmen. Since April at least 600 people 
have apparently been arrested and four members of his movement--lawyers (Ameur Deghaief), 
(Hassen Sghaier) and (° Mohamed Hemmi) and engineer (Mohamed Bannoun)--have been 

executed without trial after being tortured. Among Libyans assassinated in foreign 
countries since the beginning of the year only two, (Mahmoud Nafaa) and (Mohamed 
Mustapha Ramadan), shot in London, were close to the National Democratic Movement.
