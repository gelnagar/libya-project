TRIPOLI TALKS BOOST ITALIAN-LIBYAN ECONOMIC TIES 
LD061543 Rome IL POPOLO in Italian 26 Oct 79 pp 1, 17 LD 


[Gianfranco Rossi report: "Closer Cooperation Between Italy and Libya"] 


(Text ] Tripoli--Having overcome the unpleasant episode of the Italian sailors held by 
the Libyan authorities (who were released in Tripoli yesterday) a new and broader chapter 
of economic cooperation has opened between Italy and Libya. [paragraph continues ] 


V. 7 Nov 79 : 9 NORTH AFRICA 


The talks which [Italian Foreign Minister] Malfatti has had in Tripoli with his counter- 
part At-Turayki (and also with Libyan No 2 Jallud yesterday) ended with a series of 
agreements containing all the provisions for joint projects to be carried out in organic 
form over a long period in an atmosphere which is now decidedly calmer and more 
characterized by mutual trust. Agreement has even been reached--and ‘his is a great 
innovation--on the sensitive issue of fishing. There are now in principle no obstacles 
to the formation of a joint company for the exploitation of fisheries, since the Libyans 
have withdrawn any prohibition on the distribution of the fish, agreeing that it should 
be distributed on the basis of respective shareholdings. Of course, for the operating 
phase--which could start virtually immediately--as far as the Italian side is concerned, 
the decision rests with the fishing fleet's private owners. 


The oil supplies situation is also good. Libya is reorganizing its own marketing on the 
basis of OPEC's latest decisions, but it has confirmed its promise to export an 
additional amount of 20 million tons to Italy over 4 years, in other words, between 1980 
and 1983. The "normal" quota of 12.5 million tons per year remains unchanged. 


However the results--certainly very encouraging--of the two days of talks Malfatti has 
had in Tripoli (at the same time as talks between members of the It2lian-Libyan committee 
for economic cooperation) do not end there. For instance, it has also been possible to 
solve the final problems relating to the agreement reached between Libya and the Pirelli- 
SIRTI-CEAT consortium for the supply of coaxial cables. This agreemen. has produced a 
contract worth $0.5 billion. Other contracts worth $600 million to $700 million are 
planned for the current year in the infrastructures sector. Joint ventures are also 
being studied in the building and hotel sectors both in Libya and third countries. 


Finally, Italy and Libya are cooperating in exploiting energy sources other than oil 
(solar, geothermal and nuclear). 


These form a substantial package of agreements which revive relations between Rome 
and Tripoli on the economic plane. From this viewpont Malfatti's mission to Libya has 
undoubtedly been a success. 


On the political plane--this was to be expected--some differences remain. The long 

talks which Italian Foreign Minister Malfatti had yesterday with Jallud were bound to 
confirm their divergent positions on the Middle East. For Libya the Camp David agreements 
"do not lead to peace" between the Arab world as a whole and the State of Israel. 


Italy, however, still regards these agreements as "an initial step toward an all-embracing 
solution" based on three fundamental principles: nonacquisition of territories by force, 
the right of Middle East states to live within secure and guaranteed borders, and the 
right of the Palestinians to realize their legitimate desire for a homeland. 


As regards southern Lebanon, Italy and Libya express the same concerns and the hope 
that that beleaguered country will return to "independence, sovereignty and territorial 
integrity." The role of the nonalined countries was also mentioned during the meeting 
between Malfatti and Jallud. The foreign minister said that Italy regards their role 
as extremely important for the maintenance of peace in the world.

AT-TURAYKI RECEIVES MALAGASY FOREIGN MINISTER 
LD061954 Tripoli JANA in English 1632 GMT 6 Nov 79 LD 


(Text] Tripoli, November 6, JAMAHIRIYAH NEWS AGENCY--The Foreign Secretary, Dr. 'Ali 
‘Abd al-Salah at-Turayki, received at the Foreign Secretariat this morning Mr. Remi 
Christian Richard, Madagascan minister of foreign affairs, who is visiting the socialist 
people's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah at present. During the meeting Mr. Richard told the 
foreign secretary his country's absolute rejection of holding the meetings of the coe 
ordination committee for the liberation of Africa is Cairo as that contradicts with the 
aims of the committee is seeking to achieve for resisting colonialism in the African 
Continent. The necessary steps were also taken to hold the meeting of the special 
committee of Reunion Island formed by the African summit conference to discuss the French 
occupation to this island and to present it before the U.N. special committee on the 
ending of colonialism, as well as discussing the issue of the four islands surrounding 
Madagascar which the African summit had decided to restore to Madagascar as they are part 
and parcel of its territories. 


Bilateral relations between the Jamahiriyah and Madagascar, as well as bilateral cooperation, 
were discussed in addition to taking the necessary steps for implementing bilateral agreee 


ments signed by the two countries in all fields, the African issues and the international 
issues of mutual concern.
