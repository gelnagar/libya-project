Cairo AL-AKHBAR in Arabic 30 May 79 pp 1, 4& 


[Article: "The Libyan National Movement in Europe Publishes a Second 
Newspaper To Reveal al-Qadhdhafi's Crimes and To Call for His

[Text] AL-AKHBAR has received a new newspaper, AL-JIHAD that is 
published by the Libyan National Movement in Europe. Its motto, "The 
best holy war is a word of truth spoken against a tyrannical ruler,” 
appears beneath the masthead. 


This is the second newspaper published by the Libyan National Movement 
for the purpose of freeing the Libyan people from the petty tyrant, 
Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi. The first newspaper, SAWT LIBYA [The Voice 

of Libya] had adopted a verse from the Koran as its motto: "Permission 
to take up arms is hereby given to those who are attacked, because 
they have been wronged" [The Pilgrimage:39]. On Wednesday May 23 
AL-AKHBAR had published a review of this newspaper which revealed the 
brutal massacres that al-Qadhdhafi had launched against the Libyan 


people. 


The first issue of the new newspaper, AL-JIHAD has appeared. Its 
editorial stated that its purpose was "to overthrow the despotic regime 
that is oppressing the homeland and the citizen" while the country 
suffers under the burden of foreign debts to the East. [It stated 
that] its national objective was to overthrow the reign of terror, 
oppression and corruption in Libya so that free nationalists can take 
part in establishing a genuine democratic regime in which the people's 
opinion would play an effective and a decisive role. [The full text 
of the editorial is cited below]. 


AL-JTIHAD states that Libya is now suffering from a declining economy 
that is sinking under the burden of foreign debts to the East and to 
the West. This situation is a natural result of the absence of an 
expenditures policy that is in balance with the state's general

revenues. Fopular resentment [aginst the government] is acute and the 
unrest among the Libyan masses is unprecedented. The regime is 
beleagured by this rancor and unrest because of the arsSitrary 
confiscations that al-Qadhdhafi carried out and is still carrying out 
under the slogan of the socialism of conquest and deprivation. His 
repime is beleagured by this unprecedented unrest and rancor because of 
its inability to provide and to meet the daily needs of the people, 
because of the absence of stability and because the citizen feels that the 
stute is playing the role of "We, the Arabs” and that it is spending 
Libyan funds without any oversight and disobeying God. 


AL—JIHAD stated that al-Qadhdhafi had introduced into Libya ways of 
torture that have hitherto been unknown. In some cases usage of these 
methods has caused the disappearance of the bodies of some prisoners. 
Among those victims are Lt Col al-Majiri, Cpt Muhammad al-Tumi and 
Cpt ‘Abd-al-Majid Husayni. 


The newspaper then stated that al-Qadhdhafi had abolished the laws of 
the state and had thereby created a state of chaos to ensure that his 
speeched and his directives would became the laws that citizens would 
have to obey. Then he dismissed more than 100 judges who dared to 
protest his action of repealing the laws of the land. 


Then al-Qadhdhafi interfered in the sentences of his puppet courts. In 
spite of their harshness and in spite of the fact that they do not 
follow any civil or military law, he revoked all these sentences and 
turned a 15-year sentence into a capital sentence; a 10-year sentence 
into a life sentence; and a 3-year sentence into a 10-year sentence. 
[In addition], he ordered the arrest of all those who had been 
acquitted by the courts. 


AL-JTHAD stated that al-Qadhdhafi had made bribery, corruption and 
favoritism a slogan for his government. Major projects and contracts 
were given only to his relatives and his aides who have responsible 
positions in his regime. A person who accepts bribes is considered 
honest because he has contacts, whereas an honest person remains at 

m loss inside the country and abroad and his honesty goes unrecognized. 
Al-Qadhdhafi is also wasting the wealth of the country on his trips, 
his dreams, his delusions and his efforts to glorify himself and his 
"Green Book." AL-JIHAD revealed serious incidents that took place in 
the Libyan army. It stated that al-Qadhdhafi had paralyzed the armed 
forces by placing his relatives, in spite of their ignorance, in command 
positions of militias and armies. The newspaper stated that he 
appointed police officers who were his relatives as provincial 
governors and commanders of entire military zones. Such [appointees 
include] Mas'ud "Abd-al-Hafiz, commander of the southern area and 
Ashkal, commander of the central region. Al-Qadhdhafi [also] retired 
or returned to civilian life all qualified officers whom he does not 
trust.

"It is for this reason that the Libyan National Movement thought of

of strengthening, furthering and guiding towards the achievement of 
national goals. 


"AL-JIHAD will make every effort to become a free forum and a true,

emotions so that these can became our non-depletable mentors and

"AL-JIHAD is naturally not the first activity of the Libyan National 
Movement. Its members, like other nationalists had the honor of 
exposing the client Fascist regime. They indicated and pointed out

and, accordingly, to know what they are rejecting. This was done 
through meetings, leaflets, pamphlets and occasionally through 
articles in some newspapers and magazines that are published in the 
Arab world. 


"Thus AL-JIHAD is an attempt which we hope will gradually become 

stronger so that the reign of terror, opression and deviation in Libya 
may be overthrown. It is an attempt to participate with free 
nationalists in the establishment of a genuine democratic regime in which 
the people's opinion would be effective and their word would be decisive."

[Text] Col al-Qadhdhafi's letter, which has been called "the scornful 
blow” letter aroused the interest of political commentators and those 
who are interested in the developments of the Baghdad Conference Front. 
The colonel had sent the Arab kings and heads of state, except ai-Sadat 
and Sultan Qabus, a form letter that was made public by the Libyan 
News Agency. The letter criticized Arab silence on the Israeli 
agression against Lebanon and the resistance. The colonel expressed 
his surprise at this silence, saying “It were as though the Arab 
nation had accepted the nction that its role in life was to receive 
scornful blows without taking action or without reacting to protect 
its dignity." 


At. the outset it was thought that the purpose of the letter was to 
provoke Syria and eventually Iraq because Syrian troops were in fact 

in Lebanon. Anyone who wants to provoke Syria is always asking this 
question, "Why doesn't the Syrian army respond to the Israeli aggression?” 
But those who are asking this question are not thinking of the agression 
or of the response to it as much as they are thinking of their hostility 
to Syria. 


It has been said that the colonel was trying to respond to the 
accusations Iraq was making on Syria's behalf that he was not meeting 
the commitments to support Syria that he had made at Arab Summit 
conferences. The most recent conferences vere the Baghdad Summit 
Conference and the Conference of the Ministers of Foreign Affairs and 
Finance. The Iraqi magazine AL—-RASID had attacked al-Qadhdhafi in its 
20 May 1979 issue because he had not paid Syria's subsidy share and 
had, instead, sent 10 million dollars in a suitcase to Syrian officials 
who refused to accept the funds: (AL-HAWADITH 1 June 1979). This was 
not the first time for the Iraqi publication AL-RASID to attack the

colonel. On 4& March 1979 it attacked his position on the Kurds and 
accused him of harboring the Syrian-Iragi unity ill will for domestic 
considerations and as a resuit of foreign recommendations. The importance 
of AL-RASID lies in the fact that it is not a periodical publication 

and that it appears on important occasions. The first critical remarks 
about Iraqi communists appeared on its pages. That marked the beginning 
of the fierce battle which ended with the liquidation of the communist 
“ally” in the army and the government and even the emergence of an 

Iraqi communist party committed to the principles of al-Ba'th. 


Al-Qadhdhafi had provoked Iraqi authorities when he was interviewed by 
French radio. Al-Qadhdhafi explained his theory of nationalities 
during that interview and announced that the Kurds were a separate 
nationality from the Arabs and that they were living on land joined 
with that of the Arabs. Therefore, [he claimed], they had the right to 
determine their destiny. [Al-Qedhdhafi said that the Kurds] vere 

like the Irish nationalists but that the Circassians, however, did 

not. belong to a nation and that for this reason they had to observe 
their duties and their rights in accordance with the laws of the land 
to which they belong. By contrast, as Arab logicians would argue, 

the Kurds are not obliged to abide by the laws of the land where 

they live. 


And so it was that some observers construed al-Qadhdhafi's disapproval 
of Arab silence regarding the aggression on Lebanon as part of a 
campaign to provoke the Syrians and the Iraqis. But the fact that Libyan 
and friendly media had stepped up their critical comments and had 
applied them to cover all the Arab countries, and especially those 

that participated in the Baghdad Ccaference, and the fact that 

South Yemen had responded quickly and had suggested that a summit 
conference devoted to the situation in Lebanon be convened made most 
observers believe that the purpose of all this went beyond that of 
embarrassing the Eastern Front. 


It is known that Col al-Qadhdhafi rejects the policy of summit 
conferences out of a commitment to the slogan that was coined by the 
late ‘Abd-al-Nasir who called them a “cover for reactionaries” and 

a “deflation of the mass movement.” After the first Baghdad Conference 
the colonel announced that al-—Jamahiri (Libya) would support any 
uprising in Egypt, Morocco or Yemen. [He made this announcement] in 

a speech he delivered at the Benghazi Festival on 9 March 1979. His 
minister of foreign affairs--who is called the secretary of foreign 
affairs after the abolition of the government--'Ali ‘Abd-al-Salam al- 
Turayki claimed in a statement to the Italian newspaper, IL MESSAGERO, 
that the Gulf states would follow in Iran's footsteps. He said that 
the Libyan position had remained unchanged even after an Arab consensus 
had been reached at the Baghdad Conference and the world had recognized 
Saudi Arabia's role in opposing the treaty. Libya's position remained 
unchanged even after Morocco broke off relations with Egypt and King 
Hassan chaired the Islamic Conference. Al-Qadhdhafi went to Algeria

The question still remains: why is the colonel trying to get himself 
out of the framework of the Baghdad Conference? the easy explanation 
lies in discussions about a colonialist conspiracy and a U.S.-Soviet 
plan to break up the front that is rejecting the Israeli-Egyptian treaty. 
But because this is mere speculation or idle accusations, commentatars 
are calling attention to several factors that are making President al- 
Qadhdhafi's position inside the country critical and, consequentiy, 
requiring action on his part. This is in accordance with the theory of 
a space satellite which must stay in orbit or fail. 


The domestic situation is critical because of two factors: the ruling 
group is being liquidated; and there is an overall defeat on all the 
foreign fronts. 


The liquidations began with the removal of the members of the revolutionary 
Council. Al-Majrif was killed the marning after he had criticized 
conditions in Libya to British Journalists. Al-Muhbciyshi and al-Huni 
have escaped to Egypt. ‘Awad Hamzah, Bashir Hawadi and Muhammad Najm

Bakr Yunis remains in the government. Yunis's mandate to revolutionize 
the army has been curtailed, and he has been relieved of his duties 

of supervising intelligence. Naturally, the centers of power of the 
fallen members had to be liquidated, and the rest of the members had 
to be divested of any bases that they may relie on in the game of 
musical chairs which usually ends vith the survival of one chair for 
the leader. It was thus that the slogan of abolishing [government] 
organizations was proposed; this included the Revolutionary Council 
which has practically been liquidated. Jallud has been removed as 
government chief, and he has been nominally replaced by ‘Abd-al-‘Ati al- 
‘Ubaydi who has the title of secretary general of the i'eople's 
Congress. The army has been reduced to the level of a popular 

militia and a national guard, and power has theoretically been shifted 
to the masses. Actually, power has been shifted to the colonel's 
organizations whose formation was aided by Nasirist and Soviet 
expertise. 


Because of the government crisis that vas provoked by these liquidations, 
the bourgeois class hoped to take advantage of this situation and the 
bourgeoisie asked for some political freedams along democratic lines. 

But the regime som dealt this class a fatal blow by applying to [its 
members] the second volume of the "Green Book” which deals with economics. 
It proclaimed the slogan of “partners not hirelings;" "a home belongs 
to its occupant;” and “no individual ownership in production.” Such 
notions paralyzed the Libyan bourgeoisie who were forced to immigrate 
and to smuggle their funds abroad. For the first time Libyan opposition 
nevspapers appeared abroad. The quality of their printing and subject 
matter indicated that they were well supported by effective Libyan 
intellectuals and financiers. The Libyan bourgeoisie were discovering 
that the regime which they had supported to protect oil funds fram the 
greed of neighbors had turned against them to liquidate then.
