[Q] The presidency of the OAU chose Lagos for the site of an emergency meet- 
ing. What are the restrictions on the holding of this meeting which Morocco 
called for? 


[A] Morocco did not specifically urge this meeting. But it believes along 
with a number of African states, including Sierra Leone, Guinea, and Sudan, 
that holding this conference is the way to save the OAU from coming apart 

and from the dangers that threaten it. In an emergency summit meeting, it 

is possible to know the opinion of two-thirds of the members on whether the 
so-called "Saharan Republic" is an independent, sovereign state with the ele- 
ments or a state or not. This question which was raised at the Freetown 
summit in the summer of 1980 is still unresolved. 


[(Q] In case this emergency conference is not held, will a Tripoli summit be 
qualified to discuss the matter? 


[A] Not atall, for the coming apart of the OAU has become an element prevent- 
ing the conferring of legitimacy on any future meeting whether at the level 

of committees, cabinet, or summit. If the problem is not solved--I mean the 
admittance or nonadmittance of the so-called Saharan Republic to the OAU--it 
will be impossible for any regular summit to meet in Tripoli or another 
African capital.

Although the meeting of ministers otf foreign affairs of the countries of the Gulf 
Council ot Cooperation which was held in Riyadh was a regular and an ordinary 
meeting, it was clouded by a few unexpected questions. Among those questions was 
the emergence of the sudden dispute between two countries that are members of the 
council--Qatar and Bahrain--over sovereignty over the island of Hawar. To empha- 
size che fact that a Gulf model of action for dealing with disputes that develop 
between member countries does exist, the deliberations were concluded with the 
adoption of a "constitutional solution" to that dispute. An agreement was reached 
that the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia would play the role of umpire and observe the 
praiseworthy efforts that were taking place between Qatar and Bahrain to settle 
this dispute before it should grow and affect the entire Gulf community. This 
confirms the fact that joint Gulf action is distinguished from joint Arab action 
because it departs from the established manner of making decisions and it 
achieves solutions. 


Among the other subjects to which the ministers of foreign attairs devoted a prin- 
cipal portion of their talks was that of the extremely tense relations between 

the Kingdom ef Saudi Arabia and Libya. The council sharply criticized Libya's 
position and denounced the propaganda and political campaigns that Libya was 
waging against Saudi Arabia. The council had previously supported Iraq against 
Iran at the Bahrain meeting.
