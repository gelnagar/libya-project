JANA: HABRE FORCES ‘LIQUIDATE ‘ LEADING CHADIANS 


LD231236 Tripoli JANA in English 1001 GMT 25 Oct 82 


(Text) Paris, Muharram 6, Oct 23, Jamahiriyah News Agency -- Habre's forces attacked the 
area east of Sarh where the forces of the Democratic Revolutionary Council are centered. 


Habre's forces staged 3 unsuccessful attacks and retreated after losing more than 200 
soldiers, and a number of ammunition and food supplies vehicles. In retaliation for 
their losses, Habre's troops have resorted to the liquidation of leading personalities 
in southern Chad including Major General Kafir, commander of the armed forces during 
Goukouni Oueddei's rule, Major General Rodai and Major Mayliavu.

‘Arafat News Conference 
0W240215 Hong Kong AFP in English 0149 GMT 24 Oct 82 


[Text] Tunis, Oct. 24 (AFP) -- Paiestine Liberation Organization leader Yasir ‘Arafat 
had two hours of talks late Saturday with a non-aligned nation commission, and said 
afterward that he expected a non-aligned group to visit Beirut to discuss treatment of 
the Palestinians in Lebanon. 


Speaking at a news conference after the talk, Mr "Arafat said the non-aligned group 
would discuss "the dangerous acts committed by the Lebanese Army against the Palestinian 
camps in Lebanon, and against our allies." Mr ‘Arafat was referring to the Lebanese 
leftists of the National Movement and other leftist and Moslem groups. 


He did not say when the non-aligned delegation would visit Beirut. But, he said, the 
group would also "be informed directly of the crimes perpetrated against Palestinians 
and Lebanese patriots." He also said the talks with the non-aligned delegation touched 
upon the consequences of Israeli "aggression," both in the occupied territories and in 
Lebanon, “where this aggression is continuing even now." 


The non-aligned commission, headed by Cuban Foreign Minister Isidoro Malmierca, also 
discussed means "of helping (the Palestinians) to face the Israeli aggression and the 
war crimes committed by the Israeli army," he said. "The commission reaffirmed the 
position of determined support of the non-aligned nations for the struggle and the just 
cause of the Palestinian people," Mr ‘Arafat added, “including its right to return to 
its homeland, to self-determination and to the creation of an independent state on its 
national soil." 


Mr ‘Arafat was asked about the U.S. Government's refusal to receive a PLO representative 
with the Arab League group that met Friday with President Ronald Reagan. Mr ‘Arafat 
commented: "The American authorities demonstrate this attitude of refusal even though 
they often contacted me in the past when they had problems such as the one they had with 
our Iranian brothers." He was referring to the Iranian taking of hostages at the U.S. 
Embassy in Tehran in November 1979. "The United States do not respect their promises... 
{as received] it seems that the American administration is pursuing a well-established 
plan against the Palestinian people."

Mr ‘Arafat announced that the Palestinian National Council, a sort of Palestinian 
parliament, would convene next month. He did not give a specific time or place. But 
he said between 1,200 and 1,300 Palestinians would participate in the council, many of 
them from Israeli-occupied territories. "We hope that the Israeli authorities will not 
prevent them from leaving those territories, as happened in 1971." 


Committee Issues Statement 
LD251748 Tunis TAP in Arabic 1000 GMT 25 Oct 82 


{Text] Tunis, 25 Oct (TAP) -—- Members of the committee that was set up by the extra- 
ordinary meeting of the Nonaligned Movement's Coordinating Bureau on Palestine last July 
in Nicosia issued a statement in Tunis. The statement particularly said: 


The committee, set up by the extraordinary ministerial meeting of the Coordinating Bureau 
of the Nonaligned Movement on Palestine, held a meeting with Yasir ‘Arafat, chairman of 
the PLO Executive Committee, on 23 October in the Tunisian capital. 


The meeting was chaired by Cuban Minister of Foreign Relations Isidoro Malmierca and 
attended by Cypriot Minister of Foreign Affairs Nikos Rolandhis;: Indian Minister of 
External Affairs Narasimha Rao; Nicaraguan Minister of Foreign Affairs Miguel d'Escoto; 
Yugoslav Federal Secretary of Foreign Affairs Lazar Mojsov; Guyana High Commissioner in 
Britain C.L. Joseph; Senegalese Ambassador to Tunisia Ibra Deguene Ka; Sri Lankan 
Ambassador in Paris N. Balasubramaniam, who represented their countries’ foreign ministers, 
members of the committee, and Secretary of State at the Tunisian Ministry of Foreign 
Affairs Mahmoud Mestiri. 


The committee informed ‘Arafat on this occasion of the outcome of its visits to France, 
the Soviet Union and Poland and of its contacts with the United Nations General Assembly 
president, the UN secretary general and the chairman of the Permanent Committee for the 
Rights of the Palestinian People. 


The committee again stressed its solidarity with the Palestinian people and their sole 
legitimate representative, the PLO. 


The committee was made aware during its talks with ‘Arafat of the continuation of the 
genocide against the Palestinian people in Lebanon after the Israeli invasion and the 
urgency of a speedy and decisive move by the international community to end this genocide. 


After a thorough exchange of opinions with ‘Arafat and consultations, the committee 
expressed its concern about the danger prevalent in Lebanon. 


The committee agreed that its visit to Lebanon with the assistance and cooperation of 
the Lebanese Government would enable it to reduce the agonies of the Palestinian people 
in that country. 


FOREIGN MINISTER ON PLO'S ‘FREEDOM OF MOVEMENT: 
LD251846 Tunis TAP in English 1648 GMT 25 Oct 82 
E"Pool") item] 


[Text] Tunis, Oct. 25 (TAP) -- The presence in Tunisia of the PLO leadership, the out- 
come of the 12th Arab summit of Fes, the Arab plan for peaceful settlement of the 
Palestinian issue and President Reagan's plan for peace in the Middle East were the 
main subjects dealt with by Tunisia's Foreign Minister Beji Caid es-Sebsi in an inter- 
view with the magazine "AL-HAWADITH" published in London. 


V. 26 Oct 82 Q4 NORTH AFRICA 


Answering a question about the freedom of movement granted by Tunisia to the PLO and whether 
this freedom is equal to that enjoyed by the PLO in Beirut, the minister said he knew nothing 
about the situation of the PLO in Beirut and added: "If Palestinian leaders wanted to come 

to Tunisia, it is because they were convinced that they would be ensured propitious conditions 
for political action. 


The minister recalled in this context that Tunisia had housed, in the past, national liberation 
movements and organizations and stated "all the Palestinians are satisifed with their stay 
in Tunisia". 


As regards the statements of some Palestinians to U.S. television according to which their 
movements in Tunisia are limited, Mr Caid es-Sebsi said that in U.S. T.V. there are trends 
very favourable to Israel supported by the Zionist lobby and that Palestinians had chosen 
themselves the place of their stay, stressing that Palestinian leader Yasir ‘Arafat had pre- 
ferred the camp to the sumptuous residence Tunisia put at his disposal. 


To the question whether Tunisia will become the base of PLO action on political, military 
and economic plans, the minister answered that Tunisia's stand has always consisted in 
non-interference in PLO affairs and its role is to provide the PLO with necessary support 
at all levels. 


Speaking of the outcome of the last summit of Fes, Tunisia's minister said the Arabs adoped 
during this summit a peace plan including very precise items and recalled the setting up by 
this summit of a seven-member-committee entrusted with explaining the plan to presidents of 
the Security Council permanent member countries. 


As regards the fact that no comments were made in Fes about U.S. President Reagan's plan for 
peace in the Near-East, Mr Caid es-Sebsi stressed that this plan had been neither refused 
nor accepted pending more details about it. 


He said Reagan's plan was not the only one and there were actually other peace plans in- 
cluding the Franco-Egyptian plan and Soviet President Leonid Brezhnev's. But Arabs, he added, 
are only bound by the Arab peace plan of Fes which ensures minimum legitimate rights for 
Palestinians. 


Tunisia's minister who granted this interview in connection with his participation in the 
37th session of the U.N. General Assembly stressed the need to understand the implications 
of Reagan's plan in so far as it is submitted by the President of the greatest power which 
has most means of pressure on Israel. In conclusion, the minister pointed out the need to 
return to international legality which is the best surety, the best cover and the best 
platform to make the Palestinian cause triumph.

VISA REQUIREMENT FOR IRANIANS REINSTITUTED 


LD251304 Tunis Domestic Service in Arabic 1200 GMT 25 Oct 82 


[Text] The Iranian Government has abrogated the agreement concluded between the Tunisian 
Republic and the Islamic Republic of Iran in June 1969 on abolishing entry visas for the

this agreement and to reintroduce entry visas effective 15 October.
