Summary (AI): An FBIS translation of a Tripoli TV broadcast of Qadhdhafi's 11 June 1985 Evacuation Day address, in which he denounces the American presence at Wheelus and other former U.S. bases in Libya and warns about U.S. bombers and Egypt. The speech is a political rant, with few concrete site details beyond the names of former bases.
• The speech says Americans once believed the base would remain for 99 years, and says the revolution liberated it.
• Other former U.S. bases named include Bir (Sbamilat) for supplies, al-Watiyah for shooting ranges, Ras Dhanuf for shipping surveillance, and Tajurah for telecommunications and radar.
• Qadhdhafi says the forces evicted from the base were stationed in Egypt, and refers to B-52 bombers flying over the Egyptian desert.
• The broadcast was logged by FBIS London on 11 June 1985 and is a take of several Tripoli TV segments.
• 1985-06-11: Qadhdhafi gives an Evacuation Day address on Tripoli TV marking the departure of U.S. forces from the base in 1970.

THROWN CANS AT THE FACE OF A LIBYAN. BY GOD, I ONCE MET A LIBYAN 
WHO LIVED IN A SHANTY TOWN; HE WAS CRYING IN THE MIDDLE OF THE 
NIGHT. I ASKED HIM WHAT WAS WRONG? HE EXPLAINED THAT AN 
AMERICAN VEHICLE, CAME FROM AL-MALAH BASE FULL OF DRUNKEN 
AMERICAN SOLDIERS WHO THREW EMPTY CANS AT HIM. HE SAID THAT THE 
ONLY THING HE COULD DO WAS TO SAY: ALMIGHTY GOD, PLEASE GIVE

THEIR DRIVE, HE SAID. THEYT WERE FULLY DISDAINFUL. LOOK HOW THE

THE AMERICANS THOUGHT THE BASE WOULD REMAIN FOR 99 YEARS.

THE LEAST PRESTIGIOUS THING IN THE ARAB NATION ARE THESE RULERS.

THERE ARE FOUR OTHER BASES, AND YOU KNOW THEM ALL; THIS BASE 
USED TO RELY ON THEM. THERE WAS BIR (SBAMILAT) FOR SUPPLIES; 
AL-WATIYAH FOR SHOOTING PRACTICE RANGES; RAS DHANUF FOR SURVEILLANCE 
ON SHIPPING; AND THE OTHER BASE, TAJURAH, FOR TELECOMMUNICATIONS, 
RADAR AND ((WORD INDISTINCT)) SURVEILLANCE. TAJURAH WAS TEAMING 
WITH WIRES. PEOPLE COULD SEE THEM ALONG THE COASTAL HIGHWAY. 
ONE WAS UNABLE TO ENTER IT FOR SO MANY AERIALS. AMERICA WAS 
SETTLING DOWN PERMANENTLY -- IN RAS DHANUF, TAJURAH, (SBAMILAT), 
AL-WATYAH. MOREOVER, THE LAND, SEA AND AIR WERE OPEN IN THE 
TERMS OF THE TREATY FOR AMERICA. IT WAS DO AS YHOU PLEASE. 
THE AL-QARDHABIYYAH BASE. IT WAS THAT NAME WHEN I WAS A 
STUDENT IN SECONDARY SCHOOL. I WAS WALKING; I CAN ACROSS SOME 
TENTS AND SOLDIERS. I KNEW A FEW WORDS OF ENGLISH. I APPROACHED 
A SOLDIER. I REMEMBER HIS NAME. HE SAID HIS NAME WAS JIM. WHERE 
ARE YOU FROM JIM? HE SAID: I AM AMERICAN. WHAT ARE YOU DOING 
HERE? THE SAID: I (?COME) FROM AL-MALLAHAH BASE. AND WHAT ARE 
YOU DOING HERE IN THE AL-ZIYYANIYYAH, OR HUNAYSH, AREA? HE SAID: 
I WORK IN THE BASE. WHAT? HE SAID: I WORK AND I SURVEY. THEY 
WERE SURVEYING THE LAND: TO CONSTRUCT RUNWAYS ON IT. THEY WERE 
IN THE PROCESS OF TURNING IT INTO A BASE. BUT THEY WERE BEHIND 
IN ITS COMPLETION. THEY WERE EXPECTED TO COMPLETE IT BY THE END OF 
THE SIXTIES. 
THE REVOLUTION BROKE OUT. THEY LEFT, IT WAS THE SAME SPOT 
ON WHICH WE BUILT THE BASE. THE TRUTH IS THAT I REMEMBERED THAT 
THE AMERICANS WERE CARRYING OUT SOME SURVEYS AND I THOUGHT THE 
PLACE COULD BE SUITABLE FOR A BASE. ‘IT WAS INSPECTED AND WAS FOUND

WHAT I AM DRIVING AT IS THAT TODAY'S CELEBRATION, IN COMPLETE
