Summary (AI): A 1980 Jack Anderson column (Washington Post) alleges that U.S. former CIA-linked mercenaries Frank Terpil and Edwin Wilson ran a terrorist-training school for Qaddafi's security forces in an abandoned palace in Libya, with possible CIA awareness. The text is about Libyan security training and general Libyan regime activity, with no specific Tripoli site clearly named beyond an unnamed "abandoned palace".
• Terpil and Wilson were described as both former CIA agents, both wanted in the US on gun-running charges.
• Both men had reportedly been working on classified CIA concealed weapons systems at China Lake, California, before going to Libya.
• Terpil reportedly offered to smuggle a U.S. Redeye missile into Libya.
• Mercenaries reportedly supplied timers and booby-trap devices to Libya at inflated prices; one order was reportedly marked up from $6 to up to $250 per unit.
• 1976-08: Americans set up in a former winter palace of a Libyan royal, code-named 'Swanee', to train Qaddafi's security forces.
• 1976-09: Terpil and Wilson offered three CIA contract agents a deal to kill a Libyan 'traitor' and train Qaddafi's assassins; the agents declined.
• 1977-07: A lethal device killed three Libyan soldiers and wounded three others after an officer picked up a booby-trapped device from a truck.
