FORMER VIRGINIA RESIDENT CHARGED IN INDICTMENT WITH AIDING QADDAFI

Summary (AI): A 1981 Washington Post article reports a federal indictment of Douglas Schlacter, a former Virginia resident, for aiding Qaddafi's Libya through covert supply of personnel and explosives, including bomb-making and terrorist training. It names former CIA agents Edwin Wilson and Francis Terpil as central figures in the Libyan operation, which allegedly included a $1 million assassination contract and a bomb-making factory at the palace of the deposed King Idris near Tripoli.
• Indictment is 17 pages and was unsealed by Judge John H. Pratt.
• Schlacter allegedly managed Wilson's 2,400-acre farm in Fauquier County, Virginia, until August 1976.
• Bombs were allegedly disguised as ashtrays, lamps, clocks, coat hangers, boxes of tea and vases.
• Wilson and Terpil allegedly accepted a $1 million assassination contract from Qaddafi to silence a member of the Libyan Revolutionary Council.
• Schlacter allegedly traveled with former CIA ordnance specialist John Henry Harper and retired U.S. Special Forces soldiers to train Libyan commandos.
• 1976-04: Wilson and Terpil signed a contract with Libyan leaders to supply personnel, explosives and support services.
• 1976-10: Schlacter and Terpil allegedly asked Jerome Brower to supply explosives and manpower for clandestine pyrotechnic devices.
• 1977-07: Grand jury alleged Schlacter and two co-conspirators directed placement of Brower's explosives into several motor vehicles.
