LIBYA

COMMENTARY CRITICIZES U.S.-SAUDI ARMS DEAL 


LD211104 Tripoli Voice of Arab Homeland in Arabic 0115 GMT 21 Apr 81 
[Unattributed commentary ] 


[Excerpts] News media throughout the world have recently concentrated on reports that the 
United States is about to supply Saudi Arabia with strategic weapons, particularly AWACS 
aircraft. According to reports, U.S. Secretary of State Alexander Haig has said that if 
Congress rejects the government's proposals concerning the arms deal with the Saudi family, 
which includes deliveries of AWACS aircraft, this action will represent a serious setback 
in U.S.-Saudi relations. 


If we compare this report with reports about the efforts to increase military cooperation 
between (?the United States) and France and the visit of British Prime Minister Margaret 
Thatcher to a number of states in the region, and Saudi Arabia in particular, we notice 
that the news media coverage, the diplomatic efforts and the military moves all clearly 
indicate that there is an earnest desire among the imperialist and capitalist powers to 
(?dominate) the peoples of the area under the cover of political tricks. These tricks 

are centered at this stage on the fears expressed by Arab leaders concerning the so-called 
dangers that threaten the security and resources of the Gulf states. 


Most of these so-called imminent dangers to the Gulf area and the Arabian Peninsula and 
indeed the entire Arab area emanate from the U.S. military presence there. The extent of 
this presence will cause the area to become an arena for military conflict as it is at 
the present time an arena for political maneuvering. 


If we return to the statement of the U.S. secretary of state in a television interview in 
which he stressed the importance of supplying Saudi Arabia with AWACS aircraft we will see 
that this U.S. diplomatic view contains a military view point as well. The secretary of 
state in the Reagan administration, it is well known, is a military man who has taken up a 
political office. There is no doubt that he thinks that such an arms deal will be a mili- 
tary gain in the area for the United States. 


The United States places Israel, Israel's security and Israel's military superiority at 

the head of its strategic priorities in the area. for this reason the United States would 

not agree to supply Saudi Arabia with strategic weapons unless it was assured of making several 
gains, which include the following: Saudi Arabia will not be a danger to Israel; it will 
continue to maintain its hostility toward the Eastern Bloc; and it will protect U.S. 

interests and influence in the Gulf and the Arabian Peninsula. One must not forget that 

by supplying the Saudi ruling family with weapons the United States will force this kingdom 

to pump more and more crude oil to the United Stat.s. This will enable the United States 

to maintain its oil reserve while exhausting Arab oil reserves. 


Finally, a point worth mentioning is that West Europe is more dependent than the United 
States on oil from the Gulf. If there is disagreement between the United States and Europe 
over the means of a Western military presence, there is nevertheless complete agreement over 
the need to maintain this presence. If there is disagreement between Europe and the United 
States coucerning the Middle East, the two sides still agree fully on the need for Israel 

to remain powerful and secure. Therefore, the disagreement is over the means, and they are 
numerous. However, there is only one objective. So to whom will the U.S. AWACS aircraft 

be giving the early warning?
