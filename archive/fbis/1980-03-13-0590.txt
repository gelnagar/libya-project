DAVAR: LAND EXPROPRIATED IN JERUSALEM INVOLVES ARAB, JEWISH OWNERS 
TA121423 Tel Aviv DAVAR in Hebrew 12 Mar 80 p 1 TA 
{By Yosef Peritel] 


[Text] The government expropriated 4,400 dunams in northern Jerusalem yesterday. The 
order was signed by Finance Minister Yig'tal Hurvitz. The objective of the expropriation 
is to create continuity between the Neve Ya'taqov quarter and the French Hill quarter, both 
in the northern part of the capital, on the way to Ramallah. All the expropriated land 

is within the limits of Jerusalem on territory annexed to the state of Israel after the 
Six-Day War. 


Treasury officials estimate the expropriation will cost the government more than half 
a billion pounds in compensation to several hundred landowners in the area, and it is not 
Clear from which part of the state budget that sum will be taken. 


Sixty-eight percent of the expropriated land belong to Arabs, 30 percent to Jews, and 2 
percent is state land. Most of the area has no buildings and is rocky. After the expro- 
priation order is published in the govermment gazette, the chief government assessor will 
send the landowners statements on the amount of compensation, to enable them to appeal 
to the court should they wish to do so. 


The expropriation was made in accordance with Housing Ministry construction plans and 
on the recommendations of the treasury's advisory council on land expropriation. The 
expropriation was implemented after the Cabinet authorized Ministers Levi and Hurvitz 
to do so at last Sunday's Cabinet session. Plans to expropriate land in that area have 
been in the works at the Housing Ministry since the beginning of the seventies.

[Text] The possibility of advancing elections to the 10th Knesset received momentum recently

Support for early elections has been increasing recently and Finance Minister Yig'al Hurvitz 
said this to me last night: "I am in favor of early elections, things can no longer go on 
this way," The minister's associates added: "Yig'al's nerves are not made of steel and 
one has to cut the living flesh of the ninth Knesset." 


NRP Knesset member Yelmda Ben-Me'ir told a DAVAR correspondent in the Knesset last night: 
"If within the next 2 or 3 weeks it transpires that the finance minister's policy lacks full 
backing by the Likud and the coalition, I will propose to hold early elections. Religion 
Minister Aharon Abu-Hatzera also supported Ben Me'ir's view and told me that he would favor 
earlier elections. 


Labor Party Chairman Shim'on Peres refused to comment on today's expected meeting with the 
NRP, neither confirming nor denying it. 


The DAVAR correspondent has been told that contacts on the possibility of advancing the 
elections have been going on for a week with the NRP, especially after Dr Burg mentioned such 
a possibility, a remark that was strengthened the next day by an expression uttered by Deputy 
Prime Minister Simha Ehrlich. Ben-Me'ir said yesterday he was not taking part in contacts 
with the aim of dismantling the coalition and replacing the Cabinet by another coalition. 
"However, the subject of early elections is another matter." He recalled that he had 
supported earlier elections in the past. 


It has been reported, meanwhile, that apprehensions over the matter are increasing among the 
Likud partners, particularly in Herut. A senior associatevcof the prime minister told me 
last night: "There is indeed cause for concern, especially since Begin's authority in the 
Likud and the coalition has weakened." The same source also said that "everything depends 
on the NRP now. Qmly the NRP can decide the issue of whether the elections will indeed be 
advanced. He speculated that the prime minister might initiate an urgent meeting with NRP 
leaders on the subject, to dissaude them from soliciting earlier elections. 


The Labor Party Executive explains that the contacts with the NRP are meant to examine ways 
for forming a new Cabinet, not to discuss an alternative coalition based on the existing 
Knesset, nor to discuss the composition of the next Cabinet, should the Alinement return to 
power, or the distribution of portfolios.
