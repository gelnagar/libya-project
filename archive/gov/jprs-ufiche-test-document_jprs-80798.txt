Small But Significant Signs 


There are signs of stabilization visible in Al-Qubayyat, however. A florist 
has just opened a flower shop. "It may surprise you, but this event 
introduced a note of gaiety and a civilized air here. Look at the fields," 
continues Ilyas, "they are growing potatoes out there. That's a good sign. 
Wheat is a poor man's crop. We haven't attained the superfluous, but we 
have gone beyond the strictly necessary." These words conceal another 
phenomenon, Far from the capital, cut off from Tripoli by a nearby 
Palistinian camp, Al-Qubayyat is trying to attain a degree of self- 
sufficiency. I visited the towns factotum, Originally a peasant, he 

had learned to cut rock and had built his own house bit by bit over the 
years. Then he got a press and prepared the metal. He can repair 
anything. Machanic, blacksmith, farmer, builder, he supports his family 
while the women perform household tasks and take care of the animals. 

They all make their own bread here. 


A little higher up the hill there is an unfinished house overlooking the 
valley. It could be used as a television relay tower, Al-Qubayyat only 
receives broadcasts from Syria. "This is not good, even though the people 
are fortified against propaganda, The Beirut government has promised to 
remedy this problem." I went through the various floors up to the roof. 
On the way I noticed how clean the sparsely furnished rooms were. The 
bathrooms were as clean as those in the most modern apartment. 


Al-Qubayyat looks like a happy island far from the conflict. Beirut is 

far away; one cannot hear the constant bursts of machinegun fire or the 
noise of bombs exploding--at least one every night--but the people know the 
war is far from over. An elderly priest told me: "Leave Al-Quaayyat in 
oblivion; the devil's eyes know where to bring hell." But how can one 
forget Lebanon?

[Interview granted to the Qatari paper AR-RAYAH's chief editor, Nasir Muhammad 
al-'Uthman, in Doha by Moroccan Prime Minister Maati Bouabid--no date given] 


{Excerpts} [Question] What are the most important developments in the Western 
Saharan question on the domestic level and on the level of the OAU? 


[Answer] Regarding the developments of the Western Saharan question on the 
internal level, I assure you that, from the military point of view, Morocco is 
in control. The so-called Polisario, mercenaries being trained and supported 
by Algeria, is now in the Algerian territories. We are part of the desert and 
the desert is an inseparable part of Morocco. 


On the foreign level, the Saharan question is supported by law and history. We 
have always proclaimed that the desert regions are inseparable parts of Morocco. 
However, his majesty the king adopted an initiative during the recent OAU con- 
ference in Nairobi at the request of some Arab, African and European leaders. 

He accepted the idea that there should be self-determination and a referendum. 
This is the thing that our foes the Algerians, Libyans and other African states 
were demanding. But, amazingly, our Algerian and Libyan foes announced follow- 
ing the Nairobi conference that they would not accept the referendum which they 
themselves had requested. 


|Question] What is your expected stand on the African summit which will be 
held in the Libyan capital, bearing in mind that the recent Nairobi conference 
called for discussing the Saharan question in the Libyan summit? 


[Answer] Regarding the Tripoli meeting and whether the Africans will meet or 
whether a meeting will take place, following the wrong decision, his majesty 
sent several messages and envoys to all the leaders of the African states in 


order to draw to their attention the fact that the OAU secretary general's 
individual decision to accept the Saharan Republic in the OAU is likely to 
cause an explosion within theorganization. As you know, Morocco is one of the 
founders of the OAU and we are anxious to prevent its explosion, but if our 


foes are seeking to explode it we have to give warning. I cannot say that the 
orevanization has actually exploded by the organization is probably on its way 
to explosion.

literature faculty at the university here after clashes between opposing groups 
of students yesterday which left 32 wounded, some of them seriously. Police 
arrested 23 people. The trouble at the literature faculty of Tunis University 
came am?d strikes and agitation over the last few days in the wake of weeks 

of simmering tension. The Interior Ministry earlier said students had been 
hurt in fighting with bicycle chains, knives, iron bars and bottles filled 

with acid. Police were called at the request of the university authorities, 

-t said. Among groups active in the Tunisian student movement are an Islamic

COMMANDO GROUP ARRESTED--Tunis, April 7--Tunisian police have arrested six 
armed Tunisian youths who crossed into the country from Algeria after setting 
out from Libya, according to diplomatic sources here. The sources said that 
members of the group carried weapons and explosives. They entered Tunisia 
across the border with Algeria in Tunisia's south-west and were detained by 
police in Kasserine, in Central Tunisia, before they were able to carry out 
4 series of sabotage attacks they allegedly planned to commit, the sources 
said. Among those arrested were several former members of a commando group 
which attacked the Southern Tunisian town of Gafsa in January 1980. Evidence 
during the March 1980 trial of captured members of the earlier commando indi- 
cated they were trained and armed by Libya and had been given Algerian help to 
eross the frontier. Government officials declined to comment on the latest 
affair, which has not been reported in the national press. (AFP) [Text ]
