Summary (AI): A 1986 Philadelphia Inquirer article says a August 1986 U.S. disinformation campaign against Libya's Gadhafi, approved by President Reagan and John Poindexter, misled reporter John Walcott, whose Wall Street Journal story of Aug. 25 falsely suggested a second U.S. military attack. The article covers the press fallout, not specific Tripoli sites.
• Walcott's Aug. 25, 1986 Journal story was described as containing misleading information about a possible second US military attack on Gadhafi.
• The disinformation campaign was undertaken in August 1986 and was revealed by the Washington Post.
• Walcott's 1981 Newsweek story (Nov. 30, 1981) claimed a Libyan hit squad targeted Vice President Bush, Haig and Weinberger; FBI Director Webster later deemed it a likely phony.
• 1986-08-14: Reagan approved national security adviser John Poindexter's proposal to scare Gadhafi with disinformation that the US was about to move militarily against him (per leaked memo, reported by Washington Post)
• 1986-08-25: Wall Street Journal front-page story by John Walcott said the US might launch a second military attack on Gadhafi; based on misleading information
