Summary (AI): A 1980 New York Times article (reprinted in a CIA reading room file) reports Billy Carter's denial that he had copies of State Department cables about his dealings with the Libyan government, and a dispute with Justice Department investigator Joel Lisker over his foreign-agent registration and Libyan payments. It has no specific Tripoli locations.
• Billy Carter registered as an agent of the Government of Libya on July 14, 1980.
• Lisker said a $20,000 bank deposit on Dec. 31 was the first part of an eventual $220,000 Billy Carter received from the Libyans.
• The White House released texts of seven previously classified State Department cables about Billy Carter's September 1978 trip to Libya.
• The cables said the trip had gone well from the U.S. Embassy in Tripoli's point of view.
• Congressional committees were examining Justice Department documents about machine-gun sales to Libya and C-130 transport planes held back from Libya.
• 1980-07-31: Billy Carter denied having copies of State Department cables relating to his involvement with the Government of Libya.
• 1980-01-16: Justice Department investigator Joel Lisker interviewed Billy Carter about his possible role as an agent of Libya.
• 1980-07-14: Billy Carter registered as an agent of the Government of Libya.
