MINISTER OF DEFENSE ADVOCATES NUCLEAR NON-PROLIFERATION 
Cairo AKHBAR AL-YAWM in Arabic 29 Dec 79 pp 3, 15 


[Interview with Minister of Defense by AKHBAR AL-YAWM Political Editor: 
"Al-Qadhdhafi Is a Uranium Thief; Minister of Defense and War Production 
Says, ‘Keep Nuclear Destruction Away from Middle East;' Israel ‘Was an 
Army That Had a State’ and Is Now a ‘State with an Arny'™] 


{Text} Trucks carrying uranium were supposed to be en route to one of the 
African ports. A ship was awaiting them to transport the uranium to France. 
This is a process that has been repeated more than once in the past, and 
every time the shipment used to arrive safely to the nuclear reactors in 
France where the uranium was used tc generate nuclear power. This enabled 
France to become a member of the world's nuclear club. 


It is curious that the last shipment of uranium did not arrive in France. 
What is even more curious is that the shipment was lost on the land route 
before arriving at the African port. The trucks were found in sound condi- 
tion, but they were deserted and empty. 


This is a very serious incident. The French government, however, did not 
attach much importance to it. The news was printed on the front pages of 
the world's newspapers, and news agencies transmitted it. The news agen- 
cles stated that broad investigations were underway to attempt to unravel 
this baffling mystery from the beginning. 


In spite of its gravity, this incident had been preceded by a similar inci- 
dent that was more provocative and more curious. This was when a cargo 
ship was subjected to piracy, and large quantities of uranium were stolen 
from it. Later, it turned out that it was Israeli intelligence that had 
thought of and carried out the piracy. The Israelis were able to transport 
the uranium to the site of Israeli nuclear reactors. 


Some people thought that Israel had carried out the second theft on land, 
after having established the precedent by sea! 


Others, however, denied this and affirmed that it was the Libyan regime that 
had imitated the Israelis, wishing to compete with them in their most 
outstanding activity. 


31 











Libya neither denied nor confirmed the news. It was evident that the news 
had pleased it because it presented Col al-Qadhdhafi to the world in the 
image of a pirate who accosted caravans and burglarized the property of 
others. He was achieving the same success [in this activity] that the 
Israeli mentality had previously achieved. 


But this self-satisfaction did not last long. It was soon announced that 
the only role that al-Qadhdhafi had played was that of financing the piracy 
operation. 


Not only that, but it was also said that the burglary operation had been 
presented to al-Qadhdhafi with all its particular details and with the names 
of the international pirates and terrorists who were to carry it out. Al- 
Qadhdhafi did no more than approve the plan and give the order that it be 
financed and carried out. 


New information kept coming in. It was said that the sranium whose theft 
Libya had financed did not go to Libya and that it bzd gone instead to Paki- 
Stan. 


Pakistan has suddenly emerged as a nuclear state. It has ambitious projects 
with which it hopes to join the nuclear club and acquire an atomic bomb. 


Although the world in its entirety was interested in this news, Libya's par- 
ticipation in this operation and the fact that it financed the burglary 

may raise more than one question. We all recall that when Col al-Qadhdhafi 
first came to power in Tripoli, he announced that he was willing to purchase 
a nuclear bomb from any country that would sell him one. At that time we 
all laughed at this proposition. Today it is being said that al-Qadhdhafi 
is financing the theft of a quantity of uranium and that he is sending the 
uranium to Pakistan which has announced its first nuclear bomb. 


What Does This Plan Mean? 


This question has been raised by strategy experts in some majur capitals 

of the west. The question implies considerable anxiety and considerable ap- 
prehension. It is clear that Mu‘ammar al-OQadhdhafi is playing « dangerous 
game ani? that this game is not confined to the borders of his country but 

is rather extended inside the borders of numerous African countries. We 
have heard of the foolish military adventures that al-Qadhdhafi had under- 
taken; they all ended in failure. Al-Qadhdhafi made his soldiers, whom he 
had sent into Uganda and other countries, the laughing stock of military men 
everywhere. 


A head of state who engages in these foolish adventures can undertake any- 
thing. He can do whatever he pleases. He can undertake whatever he be- 
lieves will achieve his fancies even if that includes the des*ruction of his 
country and the destruction of those who had rejected him. 





The question that is raised by strategy experts defines the dangers that may 
arise from the possibility of having a head of state like al-Qadhdhafi ac- 
quire an atomic bomb. It is true that al-Qadhdhafi does not have this wea- 
pon, but he did nevertheless carry out the burglary and the gangster-like 
operation to supply Pakistan with quantities of uraniun. 


I took this concern to Gen Kamal Hasan ‘Ali the minister of defense and war 
production. I asked him to tell me his opinion. The minister of defense 
replied [as follows]: 


[Answer] Al-Qadhdhafi has become his own enemy. But there is no doubt 
that Libya has a nuclear reactor even though its capacity is very small. 
This has not prevented al-Qadhdhafi from trying to enlarge this capacity. 
He has also granted Pakistan 200 million dollars to help it produce a nu- 
clear bomb. It is known that al-Qadhdhafi's goal is to occupy North Chad. 
As I said al-Qadhdhafi has become his own personal enemy, and he no longer 
has any respect inside the country or abroad. 


Iraq Is Asking for Nuclear Reactors 


[Question] The mad man of Libya is not the only one who is dreaming of own- 
ing a nuclear bomb. Iraq has concluded an agreement with France to purchase 
a number of nuciear reactors. 


[Answer] The area is in danger in case the countries of the area decide to 
enter into a nuclear race. This danger threatens everybody and will not be 
confined to one country and not the other. It is in the public interest 
that the countries of the area stay away from the nuclear struggle, especi- 
ally since alli the countries are densely populated. Major preventive mea- 
sures must also be available, and these are not within easy reach of the 
countries that are thinking of utilizing nuclear weapons. Nuclear weapons 
are costly to produce, and their preventive measures are also expensive. 

I do not believe that the introduction of nuclear weapons into the area 
will be in anyone's interests.

figures for the armed forces showed an increase.
