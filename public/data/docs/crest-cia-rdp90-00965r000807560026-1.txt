Summary (AI): A New York Times article from October 1986 reports that the Reagan administration was accused of deceiving the press with disinformation about Libya and Colonel Qaddafi, including leaked false reports and an August 1986 policy memo. It concerns US policy and press handling rather than anything located in Tripoli.
• A mid-August State Department memo to the White House laid out a disinformation or deception campaign against Qaddafi, later endorsed by Poindexter and rewritten as a Presidential directive signed by Reagan.
• The disinformation goals were to increase Qaddafi's anxiety about his internal strength and US military power, deter new terrorism, and ultimately topple him.
• The French government refused a joint military campaign in Chad against Libyan forces after the Journal article.
• Officials said the article's front-page assertions were unattributed, while inside pages were more careful and attributed.
• 1986-08-14: National Security Planning Group meeting over which Reagan presided, where the Poindexter memo on the Qaddafi policy was considered
• 1986-04-14: American planes bombed Libya after evidence of Libyan involvement in a West Berlin discotheque attack that killed two Americans (2 killed)
• 1986-08-25: The Wall Street Journal published an article beginning 'The U.S. and Libya are on a collision course again'
