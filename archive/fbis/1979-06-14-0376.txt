[Excerpts] DAVAR writes that the performance the Knesset gave to the nation of 
Israel yesterday was not of a very high educational quality. Those who believe that 
it is possible and advisable to conduct political fights in a parliamentary style last 
heard with frustration the rich lexicon of epithets echoing through the assembly 
The public would do well not to regard the style of its representatives yesterday 
as a perfect example of relations between a man and his colleague, The paper adds that 
the saddening and even embarrassing riot that occurred in the Knesset yesterday is 
nothing but an expression of the social-political process occurrring under our eyes in 
the wake of the process of the polarization of the government's policy in the terri- 
tories. The process of cancelling the national consensus and of deepening the rift in 
the public is likely to lead to a split in the nation, 


Jerusalem POST says that conducting a political argument on a cheap level of personal 
attack is a sure way to undermine what is left of the efficiency of parliamentary 


rocedure,. Mr Sharon is acting diligently to speed up Israeli settlement in the 
territories and, by so doing, he is arousing a matter which is in dispute, but 


legitimate: The enthusiasm for one issue which he brings to his post arouses the 
fear that this will irreparably damage the political and social structure. Mr Begin 
may be deluded into believing that he has a political asset in a man of this nature. 
If he is, he is badly mistaken. Mr Sharon should be held on a tight rein rather than 
given carte blanche to behave crudely. 


HAMODLI'A writes that we are accustomed to saying that the Knesset reflects society as 

it is. If this is what our generation is, as reflected in the Knesset yesterday, there 
was nothing in this to compliment its representatives, who regard themselves as the face 
of the generation, The paper quotes the old saying: Israel; When they climb, they climb 
to the heavens, and when they descend, they descend to the depths. People of taste stand 
appalled before the revelation of the descent to the depths as it is reflected in the 
institution which represents society, 


'AL HAMIESHMAR says that there is no excuse or pardon for the vicious statements made 

by the minister of agriculture in the Knesset, even if he thinks--as is his right--that 
settlements like Elon More will bring redemption to the country. As long as the argu- 
ment is over opinions, everything is legitimate and everything is permitted. This is 
not the case with slanderous and vicious words of the sort used by the minister in his 
reply to the motions for the agenda. This is a violent style which, until not long ago, 
had no place in public. What happened in the Knesset yesterday is only a more serious 
expression of the verbal violence begun recently by Minister Sharon's colleagues in 
power. He was preceded by Deputy Defense Minister Mordekhay Tzipori and was followed 
by the prime minister, who made a violent verbal settlement of accounts with the 
opposition and who sat deaf and did not react, either during Tzipori's speech or when 
he heard Sharon's words, 


14 Jun 79 


In HATZOFE's view, Israel must determine firmly, right at the beginning of the negotia- 
tions over the autonomy, that the U.S, status is not one of an active, participating side, 
but that it represents an element taking part in the talks and, as needed, will supply 
its good offices. The U,S, role in the continuation of the negotiations on the question 
of the autonomy is as it was determined in the Camp David agreements.

[Text] Even after the storm in the Knesset and after he has had a chance to calm down,

DEFINITION OF U.S, STATUS IN AUTONOMY TALKS DEMANDED 


TA131156 Tel Aviv HA'ARETZ in Hebrew 13 Jun 79 ppl, 2 TA 


[By 'Uzi Benziman] 


[Excerpts] Full coordination of moves between Egypt and the United States was revealed 
in the round of autonomy talks in Alexandria and this, among other things, is the basis 
for Israel's demand for a definition of the status of the U.S, delegation as an active 
partner but not as a party to the negotiations, 


Ve 14 Jun 79 


The HA'ARETZ correspondent comments that at the round of talks in Alexandria it was pub- 
licly confirmed that the argument over the status of the United States may be more than 
theoretical--in principle--than actual. However, in Israel's view, the role of the U,S, 
delegation should be determined precisely. 


However, in internal talks in the Israeli delegation an opposite evaluation was heard, 
according to which the argument has an immediate, practical significance because there 
may be a situation in which the U,S, delegation will present a hard line--in the spirit 
of Egypt's positions--while the Cairo Government shows warmth toward Israel as long as 
the withdrawal from Sinai is in process, The Israeli delegation has tried to prevent a 
situation in which it will be confronted by a united position of the representatives of 
the United States and Egypt, with the U.S, delegation having the same status as that of 
Egypt. 


Israel regards the United States as a mediator and supplier of good offices and not as 
a full party. In the deliberations a U,S, trend toward speeding up the negotiations 
was noted, 

Opinion of U,S, Representative 


TA131626 Tel Aviv MA'tARIV in Hebrew 13 Jun 79 p 1 TA 


[By Yosef Harif] 


[Text] The U.S, representative to the autonomy talks, James Leonard, according to the 
impressions formed by the members of the Israeli delegation, is "not friendly"to use an 


understatement, and right from the beginning of the talks he began to show impatience. 
One of the delegation members even described some of his comments as "impudent." 


Thus, for example, everyone rejected Leonard!s idea to the effect that the delegation 
members should be forbidden to take notes during the talks. It was his opinion that the 
talks should not be formal, but friendly. And since they were not formal, there was no 
need to take notes. "What lay behind this approach--in the evaluation of several dele- 
gation members--was the belief that if there were no notes, the sides would be able to 
speak more freely and without fear of internal criticism in their countries, and it 
appears that Leonard meant Israel in particular." 


The Israeli delegation was opposed to this and its spokesmen said that it was their 
right to take notes as they wished, even if someone thought that there was no need for 
notes of the talks. The U,S, trend toward being the dominant element in the autonomy 
negotiations also stood out in secondary matters such as, for example, the proposal that 
the autonomy talks should have one authorized spokesman and, of course, an American one. 
Israel demanded that there be three spokesmen.

[From the Morning Newsreel] 


[Text] The talks in the joint Israeli-Egyptian military committee are progressing well, 
we heard from Kamal 'Ali. Yesterday the committee held a meeting in Al-'Arish with the 
intention of completing and summing up its work on the matter of the evacuation of 
Al-'Arish. No conclusions were achieved, although the meeting was held, according to 
our correspondent!s report, in a good, friendly atmosphere. Here is our military 
correspondent, Roni Daniyel, on the meetings and the differences of opinion. 


[Daniyel--live or recorded] Next Tuesday the joint committee will meet for a first dis- 
cussion in Beersheba and then, this is my evaluation, it will be possible to sign th« 
report of the joint conclusions on the first evacuation phase. Yesterday, in Al-'Arish, 
rapprochement was achieved by both sides, but there are still differences of approach 
which are preventing agreement. The two chairmen agreed not to publish the issues on 
which there is disagreement to permit a discussion in an atmosphere as relaxed as 
possible. I have learned that the issues which are preventing signature are mainly 
technical. This is what is causing the optimism that the matters will be settled at 

the next meeting. 


Thirteen days before the implementation of the next phase of the evacuation of Sinai it 
is not at all clear whether this will indeed take place. The military appendix to the 
peace agreement states that every 2 months Israel will evacuate a slice of Sinai. It 
has not yet been determined whether this is figured from the day of signing or from the 
day the treaty was ratified, and nothing has yet been agreed upon. One way or another, 
zone two of the evacuation, that is the one next to the o11 strip, will be evacuated. 
The IDF will fulfill this task even if it is in less than 2 weeks. In the area to be 
evacuated there are few civilians and few military camps. The evacuation process will 
be simple and will not present difficulties. 


It is possible that next week Israeli and Egyptian civilian teams will meet for a discus- 
sion on several questions which have remained open. Police personnel from both sides will 
meet and determine procedures for dealing with the problem of the smugglers and infil- 
trators from both sides who are ignoring the new border. It has been agreed that the 
Israeli electricity corporation will supply Al-'Arish with electricity in case of a 

hitch. The arrangements have not yet been concluded. The Syrian-African Rift Fever 

which is bothering both sides will be the issue for a meeting between health and veterinary 
elements. These meetings, as I said, have already been planned for next week. 


HA'ARETZ ARTICLE COMMENTS ON BURG-DAYAN DISPUTE 


TA131254 Tel Aviv HA*tARETZ in Hebrew 13 Jun 79 p 11 TA 


['uzi Benziman commentary: "Impudent Ministers"] 


[Text] The ministers of foreign affairs and the interior are impudent ministers. They 
exchange roles while scorning the public and harming state affairs. The personal rela- 
tions between them and the amount of cooperation between their ministries, as a result 
of the enmity exposed by their new roles, are a striking revelation of a petty war of 
the Jews at the expense of the interests of all. 


Ve. 14 Jun 79 N 7 


Because of the prime minister's Coalition considerations and the personal aims of the 
foreign minister, the minister of the interior has become Israel's main autonomy nego- 
tiator. Moshe Dayan did not want to assume the chairmanship of the Israeli negotiating 
team because he was opposed to the swollen composition of the Israeli representation 
(six ministers and another three advisers) and because he was angered by the restriction 
of his possible moves by the prime minister's constant supervision and the need for 
constant authorization by the full Cabinet. 


Begin did not plead much with Dayan to retract his opposition and gave the mission to 
Dr Yosef Burg. So the minister of the interior was taken away from a job in which he 
had, on the surface, become an expert over his 30 years of service, in order to begin 
to deal, at this advanced stage in his public career, with the most sensitive and pro- 
blematic issue confronting the State of Israel: the autonomy. 


It is unnecessary to expand on Dr Burg's skill in coping with the burden. The minister 
of the interior is, it is true, a man with a moderate temperament, a lover of verbal 
brilliance and a man with a broad education, but he has never dealt with political 
negotiations. It is unnecessary to say that he has never negotiated with an Arab ele- 
ment, except that this deficit has been compounded by something grave which is worth 
indicating. During the coming year Dr Burg will have to cut himself off from the 
affairs of his ministry and devote all his strength to the complicated autonomy nego- 
tiations. Even someone who has long been casting doubts on Dr Burg's devotion to the 
internal affairs in his charge cannot remain indifferent to the strange appearance of the 
situation in which the minister of the interior--even superficially--will cease dealing 
with his ministry!s affairs. 


These, gentlemen, are not minor affairs. Dr Burg, for example, is in charge of the 
police. The police is in charge of coping with organized crime and the curbing of 
violence and crime, How can Dr Burg deal with this when his interests will necessarily 
be directed toward Dr Butrus Ghali, Mr Mustafa Khalil and to the Egyptian positions in 
the autonomy negotiations? 


During the coming year Dr Burg will have to sit every 2 weeks at meetings with the 
Egyptian and U.S. delegations. Between meetings he will have to convene the committee 
of six to discuss the positions it must take toward the joint talks with the Americans 
and the Egyptians. Every couple of days he will have to report to Mr Menahem Begin 
about what is happening, since everyone knows that without the blessing of the prime 
minister the Israeli delegation will not take one new step. 


I have not mentioned the tension which the minister of the interior experienced before 
every session, over whether Moshe Dayan would appear. I have ignored the need imposed 
upon him to learn by heart all the clauses of the Camp David agreements and to get 
right down to the fundamental Israeli intentions in the formulations of the peace 
agreement touching on the autonomy. In brief, Dr Burg will be a very busy man over 
the next year and it cannot be assumed that he will be able to free himself to super- 
vise his ministry. 


The need to satisfy the NRP and the personal pleasure Dr Burg gets out of his new 
status as a figure which the international community will get to know more and more are 
apparently more important than his business as minister of the interior. Dr Burg has 
remained at the head of the ministerial negotiating team. The director general of his 
ministry, Hayim Kubersky, accompanies him to meetings (Burg claims that he is his chief 
adviser). 


14 Jun 79 e ISRAEL 


Commander Arye Ivtzan, the commander of the southern police district has also been 
harnessed to the new effort laid upon Dr Burg, The Ministry of the Interior spokesman, 

a nice boy called Yehezgel Lavi, briefs journalists on sensitive foreign affairs, To 
prevent exaggerated hitches, the minister of the interior has recruited Dan Patir, the 
prime minister's spokesman, so that he can maintain contact with the foreign press on the 
matter of the autonomy negotiations, 


At the other end, Moshe Dayan is playing the part of the rebuked soloist. His demands to 
be the star actor in the negotiations were not accepted and he declares "anger" at the 
minister of the interior, Up to now Dayan has not appeared at any meeting of the committee 
of six, It is true that he went to Alexandria yesterday, but he did so only after direct 
pressure was applied by the prime minister. Dayan is also instructing his ministry not 

to be generous in cooperating with the Ministry of the Interior in dealing with the 
organization of the negotiations. 


Since the Ministry of the Interior is in charge of this issue, Dr Burg and his aides are 
being forced to cope with problems and issues with which they have never been faced: the 
organization of international conferences, replying to journalists on a delicate political 
issue, the hospitality for a foreign statesman and so on, The Ministry of the Interior's 
cries for help are only being given a penny-pinching response by the Foreign Ministry. 


And so, on the most important political issue confronting Israel, it is not the foreign 
minister who is dealing with it. Dayan's involvement in the negotiations is purely 
formal and his actual participation is the end result of the amount of pressure the prime 
minister puts on him every two weeks, The minister of the interior is abandoning, even 
in practice, the issues of the quality of the environment, planning, the registration of 
the inhabitants, the police and all the other spheres over which he is in charge to be 
Israel's main negotiator on the main political issue it will be dealing with over the 
coming year.

naval bases along the coast, for possible strikes against Israel, What car you say today 
about the terrorist deployment in southern Lebanon in the wake of the IDF attacks in the 
area and our attempts to disrupt this deployment? 


[Answer] There is no doubt that we are now confronting a growing wave of terror in its 
many varieties since the signing of the peace agreement, 


Ve 14 Jun 79 ISRAEL 


There are attempts in all spheres: attempts via the UNIFIL, in which, as you pointed 
nut, they try to infiltrate UNIFIL, and the targets, by the way, are not only us, the

witnessed only this week--last week we saw the sinking of that Katyusha-carrying speed-

and are plotting against UNIFIL personnel, Recently, an officer from the Dutch unit 

was kidnaped and only released after a severe exchange of words between the UNIFIL 
command and the terrorists. Our correspondent points out that UN Secreta’y General

in the report he submitted to the Security Council for the debate on the extension of 
the force's mandate in southern Lebanon,
