LIBYA

LE MONDE REPORTS AL-QADHDHAFI PRESS CONFERENCE 


LD030957 Paris LE MONDE in French 2 Dec 80 p 7 


[Paul Balta report on press conference given by Libyan head of state Colonel al-Qadhdhafi 
in Tripoli 29 November: "We Are Supporting the Legitimate Ndjamena Government, Which 
Has Situation Under Control, Colonel al-Qadhdhafi States") 


[Text] Tripoli--The man who has been known as the "leader of the revolution" since the 
institutions (presicency, ministries, embassies and so forth) were abolished and replaced 
by "people's committees," received several journalists Saturday, 29 November, at the 
"command" headquarters--a collection of buildings in the Bab al-'Ziziyah district in 
Tripoli.

Throughout the press conference he stressed the "antagonism" between U.S. and European 
interests in Africa and the Near East. Indeed in his view "the U.S.-Israeli-Egyptian 
axis" will continue and will oppose West Europe's interests which are defended mainly 
by France and, secondly, by the FRG. "The danger of U.S. control over Europe will be 
greater with Reagan than with Carter and tensions will increase between Paris and 
Washington," he asserted. According to him, this has already started in Africa where 
the Americans are "trying to undermine France" in the French-speaking countries. 


Since the United States was the first to decect Libyan military aid to President Goukouni, 
Colonel al-Qadhdhafi counterattacked: "The Americans have made Hissein Habre their candi- 
date against the legitimate Chadian Government. If they succeed with him they will 

extend the rebellionof other French-speaking states, especially Niger, Gabon, the CAR 

and even the Ivory Coast and Senegal." When it was pointed out to him that several of 
those countries, which are friends of France, have bad relations with Libya, he replied 
without batting an eyelid: "These misunderstandings are the result of U.S. intrigues." 


Coming back to the Chadian affair, which is clearly dear to his heart, Colonel al-Qadhdhafi 
asserted that Egypt and Israel have supplied arms to Hissein Habre. "Some of those 
weapons," he explained, "have been seized by Goukouni's forces." "Nonetheless," he 

added "Habre only holds two or three cities including Abeche and Ati along a thin line 
running from north to east. His movement is in its death throes and he can only last a 
few days." 


Reliable witnesses report large consignments of troops and equipment heading toward 
southern Libya, where doctors--some of whom are foreign, notably Yugoslavs--and nurses 
have also been sent to treat Libyans with gunshot wounds. Men who had clearly taken part 
in military operations were apparently also admitted to hospitals, including Sebha 
hospital. A treaty of friendship and alliance between the two countries signed dis- 
creetly if not secretly 15 June could be used by Tripoli to justify military aid to 
President Goukouni Oueddei. 


Questioned on this subject Colonel al-Qadhdhafi stressed his country's “humanitarian 

aid." When it was pointed out to him that Libyan soldiers have been taken prisoner 

in Ndjamena, he replied, rather cleverly: "We are supporting the legitimate Chadian 
Government which isincontrol of the situation. We have given it the aid it requested. 
This aid has helped it to face the famine and shortages. The Libyan aircraft and vehicles 
which are in Chad are taking humanitarian aid to the disaster areas. Journalists can 
accompany these convoys to observe the mass and racist atrocities and massacres committed 
by Hissein Habre." 


[Question] Is Libya occupying the Aouzou Strip? 


The colonel jumped up from his chair and pointed to a map on which Aouzou is drawn inside 
the Libyan borders. Then he brought a Libyan school textbook printed in Italy in 

1965 containing a similar map. "Aouzou," he exclaimed, “is a Libyan oasis and its in- 
habitants would not understand if they were told that they are Chadians and not Libyans. 
I went there recently with President Goukouni. Things are fine between Chad and us; 

it is in Europe that people are saying that things are not going well." 


The Libyan president became more serious. "We do not accept encirclement by hostile 
regimes. We had a gentlemen's agreement with As-Sadat under the terms of which he 
promised not to intervene in Chad. However, he is helping Hissein Habre. If he 
persists in doing so I will be forced to give the go-ahead to the East Saharan libera- 
tion movement." In this connection he indicated that the Libyan Sahara sl.ould extend 
"as far as the 9th km [post]," which is apparently in Egyptian territory.

[Question] Some people accuse you of having instigated separatist movements in Niger, 
Mali and elsewhere. 


{Answer ] he Tuaregs are not a separatist movement. However, I would like to point 
out that they are Libyans. But since we are anxious about the stability of our borders, 
we respect the territorial integrity of the countries in which they live. 


[Question] However, some people say that you intend to encourage the creation of a 
Saharan state or, at the very least, to group the various nomadic peoples of the region 
into one big Saharan unit. Is that true? 


[Answer] Libya already has a great deal to do in its Sahara; the Saharans who live out- 
side the Libyan Sahara are free to determine their future. 


With regard to Polisario, which Colonel al-Qadhdhafi advised to form a united or feder- 
ated state with Mauritania when the conflict started, we pointed out to the "revolution 
guide" that Libyan officials recently suggested that Morocco and Algeria are "plotting" 

to find a solution at the expense of the Saharans and Mauritanians. The colonel re- 
torted: "Even if there were no plot, nobody will ever be able to defeat Polisario." 

After a pause he added: "We are in favor of an improvement in relations between Algiers 

and Rabat because the conflict is not between Algeria and Morocco but between Polisario 

and Hassan II. As far as I know Libya and Algeria are still backing the Polisario Front." 


[Question] It has also been said that you support separatist movements like the Spanish 
Basques, the Bretons, the Corsicans... 


[Answer] We are unionists. Therefore we cannot support separatists. The Basque country 
is on Spanish territory and is part of that territory. The situation in Corsica is 
different: It is an island in the Mediterranean. However, we do not have relations with 
the Corsicans. 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi returned to the question of relations with Paris and expressed the 
hope of visiting Paris after the presidential election. The atmosphere between the two 
capitals has improved since the French Embassy in Tripoli was sacked 4 February. None- 
theless the embassy buildings still have not been repaired despite the promise that they 
would be within 2? months. Is France, which was accused of being "neocolonialist" at 

the time, no longer so? Colonel al-Qadhdhafi only seemed to have heard the first part 
of the question. "Such a visit," he said, "is possible because France is sufficiently 


independent of the United States. This visit could, therefore, only strengthen the ties 
of friendship between Paris and Tripoli and increase our trade."

IDF ATTACKS TERRORIST POSITIONS NORTH OF SIDON 


TAQ30511 Jerusalem Domestic Service in Hebrew 0500 GMT 3 Dec 80 


[Text] Last night an IDF force attacked terrorist objectives north of Sidon. The IDF 
spokesman reported that several terrorists were killed and many were injured. None of 
our forces was injured. During the action the force encountered two terrorist vehicles 
and destroyed them. Our military correspondent, Shmu'el Tal, learned that after the 
vehicles were destroyed the terrorists began firing indiscriminately from their positions 
in the area. The force located the source of the fire and completed the mission despite 
the firing. It also shelled terrorist objectives in the area from naval ships. The 

IDF spokesman reported that the action was part of the deterrent actions the IDF is 
taking against the terrorists in the area to prevent them from organizing for attacks 

in Israel. The action took place about 70 km north of the border, not far from Ad-Damur. 
As stated, none of our forces was injured and the force returned safely to its base. 


FOREIGN MINISTER INTERVIEWED ON EEC INITIATIVE 


LD021347 Paris LE MONDE in French 2 Dec 80 p 5 
[Jacques Amalric interview with Foreign Minister Yitzhaq Shamir in Paris] 


[Excerpt] [Question] What do you think of the EEC diplomatic initiative on the Israeli- 
Arab corflict? 


[Answer] We have fundamental differences of opinion on this approach and we do not 
accept the principles put forward by the EEC at the Venice summit: withdrawal of our 
troops to the 1967 lines, self-determination for the Palestinian Arabs, partition of 
Jerusalem. The implementation of those measures would greatly weaken Israel. 


Our differences of opinion have become even more marked as a result of the recent events 
which have taken place in our region: the Iraqi-Iranian war and growing tension between 
Syria and Jordan. These two events, which have nothing to do with the Israeli-Arab con- 
flict, threaten peace in the region and world peace. They show that the Israeli-Arab 
conflict is not the only conflict in the region or the most important or most dangerous 
conflict. However, everybody knows that the Israeli- Arab conflict will be regarded as 
the most important at the Luxembourg summit. We do not understand that logic. 


[Question] What is your logic? 


[Answer] For us the main cause of the conflict between Iran and Iraq is Iraq's original 
conviction that it had military superiority over Iran. The same applies to the tension 

between Syria and Jordan; Syria now thinks it has military superiority, especially since 
Iraq is involved in a war. The lesson we learn from this is that you have to be strong, 
very strong, to survive in this region. Weakness is an invitation to aggression. I do 

not believe that the Europeans are aware of those facts because the idea of using mili- 

tary superiority to settle disputes does not occur to anybody in Europe. 


[Question] Do you feel that France is playing a leading role in the EEC initiative? 
[Answer] We are well aware that France is a very important member of the community and 


that the community stance is strongly influenced by France. That is no secret to 
anybody. 


[Question] Will you raise the problem of French arms supplies to Iraq in your talks 
with Giscard D'Estaing and Francois-Poncet?

[Answer] We may discuss arms supplies to states in the region. That is the most important 
factor in the feeling of military superiority which I mentioned with regard to Iraq and 
Syria. 


[Question] How do you intend to fight the EEC initiative? 


[Answer] By explaining our viewpoint; we know that we must seek peace and that that re- 
quires compromises. The implementation of the Camp David agreements has proved that we 
are prepared to make concessions and sacrifices. 


[Question] Have the negotiations on Palestinian autonomy not reached deadlock? 


[Answer] It is true that we are not making much progress in these negotiations. However, 
that is due to Egyptian vacillation and to the U.S. presidential election. Once those 
obstacles are removed we are sure we will reach an agreement. There is no other solution. 


[Question] And yet you are unable to find Palestinian interlocutors? 


[Answer] We must first reach agreement with Egypt and the United States. Then we hope 
that the Palestinians will decide to take part in the discussions. If an agreement is 
reached there would immediately be elections in the territories to appoint the Palestinian 
population's representatives. 


[Question} Are you not afraid that those with whom you are now refusing to negotiate will 
be elected? 


[Answer] Perhaps. That is a risk which must be taken. However, it is important to bear in 
mind that this autonomy solution is temporary, transitional. Three years after the 

autonomy agreement negotiations will start on the final status. Having said that, the 
Palestinian Arab people already have their state; 80 percent of Jordanian territory and all 
Jordan's population are Palestinian. 


[Question] What do you think of Mr Reagan's election? 


[Answer] The United States has been very active in the Camp David negotiations. We believe 
that the new U.S. administration will also join in this process. We are counting on getting 
the same understanding from it. 


[Question] On more or less understanding? 
{Answer} Not less. 


[Question] What do you think of the statement made by your government (Begin stated: 

"The French Government's anti-Israeli policy is indirectly fueling the anti-Semitic attacks 
in France.... It is impossible to separate anti-Zionism and the anti-Israeli policy from 
anti-Semitism.") which, following the attack in Rue Copernic, established a link between 
the French Near East policy and the growth of anti-Semitism in France? 


[Answer] I do not remember the exact wording of that statement. However, it is 
important to understand that we are very worried following this wave of anti-Semitic 
attacks. We are very sensitive about these attacks. We are affected by everything that 
happens to our brothers throughout the world, and that is why we are asking the Western 
countries to be very cautious in their relations with some organizations which practice 
terrorism. 


[Question] Do you believe there is a Jewish vote in France? 


[Answer] I do not meddle in France's internal affairs.

[Text] The presidents of the People's Republic of Angola, Comrade Jose Eduardo dos Santos, 
the People's Republic of Mozambique, Samora Moises Machel, the Republic of Cape Verde, 
Aristides Pereioa, and the Democratic Republic of Sao Tome and Principe, Manuel Pint:

At the end of the summit, Angolan Foreign Minister Comrade Paulo Jorge read the followin;

[Begin recording] Joint communique: The heads of state of Angola, Cape Verde, Mozambique 


and Sao Tome and Principle met in Luanda, capital of the People's Republic of Angola, on 

2 December 1980. The meeting was attended by their excellencies Jose Eduardo dos Santos, 
president of the MPLA-Labor Party and president of the People's Republic of Angola; 
Aristides Pereira, secretary general of PAIGC, president of the Frelimo Party and presi- 
dent of the People's Republic of Mozambique; and Manuel Pinto da Costa, president of the 
Movement for the Liberation of Sao Tome and Principe and president of the Democratic 
Republic of Sao Tome and Principe, who headed high-ranking party and government delegations. 


The summit discussed matters of common interest in the light of the need to preserve the 
ties of solidarity, unity and cohesion forged in the struggle against colonialism, imperi- 
alism and in the construction of a new society. 


In particular, the meeting analyzed the Guinea-Bissau coup d'etat and its effects on the 
life of the PAIGC and relations among our five countries. 


The summit observed that the coup d'etat had put in doubt both the survival of the PAIGC 
as a supranational party and the implementation of its project of uniting Guinea-Bissau 
and Cape Verde, as well as constituting a blow to the liberation struggles of our people. 


In accordance with the universally accepted principles governing relations among states, 
the summit reaffirmed noninterference in the internal affairs of Guinea-Bissau. 


In conformity with the principles which have always guided the actions of our parties and 
with the fraternity which united the peoples of the five countries in the struggle against 
Portuguese colonialism, the summit decided to send a delegation to Guinea-Bissau. 


The summit expressed its conviction that the physical well-being of and respect for the 
former militant comrade Luis Cabral, the deputy secretary general of PAIGC, and other 
leaders and militants who have been jailed, should be considered quickly in order to have 
them freed. 


The summit was held in an atmosphere of friendship, cordiality and concord. 

At the end of it the secretary general of PAIGC and the presidents of the Frelimo Party 
and the Movement for the Liberation of Sao Tome and Principe thanked the president of the 
MPLA-Labor Party and of the People's Republic of Angola for the warm and fraternal 


hospitality afforded them during their stay in the Angolan capital. 


[Signed] Luanda, 2 December 1980

CAPE VERDE 


RELATIONS WITH GUINEA-BISSAU BEING RECONSIDERED 


ABO11146 Paris AFP in French 1105 GMT 30 Nov 80 


[Text] Praia, 30 Nov (AFP)--Unless there are new developments, Cape Verde is getting 
ready to break with the past (?relations) with Guinea-Bissau, following the coup 

d'etat which occurred there on 14 November. It is said that the Cape Verdians are main- 
taining absolute silence in order not to enter into a polemic. However, it has been 
gleaned from private conversations within different circles that the general tendency 

in Praia is to consider that future relations between Bissau and Praia will be based 

on simple state-to-state links. 


Until now relations between the two countries had been maintained through the African 
Independence Party of Guinea-Bissau and Cape Verde (PAIGC). The PAIGC, which was 

founded in 1956, organized the war of independence in Guinea-Bissau (continental 

country comprising 800,000 inhabitants of African ethnic origin) and led that country 

and Cape Verde (archipelago comprising 300,000 mulatto inhabitants) to total independence 
in 1974 and 1975. It was in power in both countries, it possessed an army for both 
countries and it wanted to unite both countries organically, by stages. 


Since the assassination of its founder, Amilcar Cabral, in 1973 the PAIGC's secretary 
general has been Mr Aristides Pereira, current president of Cape Verde, and its deputy 
secretary general the deposed Guinea-Bissau president, Mr Luis Cabral. Today Cape 
Verdians consider that the PAIGC and its ideal of unity between the two countries were 
ruined irremediably on 14 November. 


The Cape Verde Government and Mr Aristides Pereira, in his double capacity as head 

of state and secretary general of the PAIGC, have condemned the coup d'etat twice. 

The Cape Verdian leaders are, however, careful not to allow themselves to interfere in 
the internal affairs of Guinea-Bissau or to return Mr Luis Cabral to power. It is 

said in government circles that the 14 November coup d'etat has created an irreversible 
state of affairs and that Guinea-Bissau is free to choose its path and Cape Verde 

its own. 


The Cape Verdian path will be determined by an enlarged conference of government officials 
which will be convened after the legislative elections on 7 December. The main aim 
of that meeting will be to determine the future of the PAIGC in Cape Verde. 


It is generally considered in Praia that the future Cape Verdian Party will adhere closely 
to Amilcar Cabral's precepts and the watchwords of the PAIGC, that is: national democracy, 
liberal economies, nonalignment. This last point is of particular importance for 

Cape Verde which is situated at the maritime and aerial crossroads of the Atlantic, 

500 kilometers off the West African shore and which has already rejected requests 

from both the East and West for the use of its former Portuguese bases of Port 

Mindelo and the Sal Airport. 


One can, however, tell sometimes, from talks with Cape Verdians, that there is a certain 
fear of the rebirth of some kind of cold war, in the present world context, which could 
place the archipelago in a difficult position. 


Particular attention will certainly be paid to external relat _cns to avoid, as a result 
of the Guinea-Bissau affair, any isolationist attempt contrary to the archipelago's 
interests as it is one of the countries which has been hardest hit by drought. 


According to diplomatic missions accredited to Cape Verde, that country has a good 
reputation because of the seriousness with which it implements its development 

projects. It has also established excellent relations with the former Portuguese 
colonies and countries belonging to the Economic Community of West African States. 


In regard to future relations with Guinea-Bissau it is said within the governing circles of 
Praia: We would like them to be good. All will depend on development in Guinea-Bissau.
