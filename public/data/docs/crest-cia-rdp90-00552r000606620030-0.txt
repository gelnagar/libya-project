Summary (AI): A 1981 New York Times article (reprinted in a CIA reading room file) reports that ten US Green Beret veterans and a serving master sergeant were recruited by Edwin P. Wilson to train terrorists for Libya's government in 1977. Wilson, a fugitive believed to be living in Tripoli, was indicted in 1980 for illegally exporting explosives to Libya.
• Ten men, nine retired Green Berets and one active-duty master sergeant, went to Libya to train terrorists.
• The participants were promised $4,500 a month plus bonuses.
• The CIA denied any involvement; the Army review ended inconclusively.
• Wilson was indicted in 1980 for illegally exporting explosives to Libya.
• 1976: According to investigators, Edwin P. Wilson closed a business deal with Colonel Qaddafi to sell expertise in intelligence, arms and explosives, including training of terrorists.
• 1977-07-21: Luke F. Thompson, a Special Forces master sergeant, received a phone call from a man identifying himself as Patry Loomis, starting the Libyan training operation.
• 1979-07: Justice Department grand jury questioning of many Green Berets produced no indictments.
• 1980: Edwin P. Wilson was indicted by a federal grand jury on charges of illegally exporting explosives to Libya.
