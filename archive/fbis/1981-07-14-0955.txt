LIBYA

JANA REPORTS EGYPTIAN-ISRAELI PLAN TO INVADE LIBYA 


LD140830 Tripoli JANA in English 1825 GMT 13 Jul 81 


[Text] Tripoli, 13 Jul (JANA)--The Libyan security authorities have obtained details of 

a plan prepared by Egypt for the invasion of Libya with Israeli aid. The plan was suggested 
for the first time at President Anwar as-Sadat's home at Giza during the second part of 
March, 1981. 


Present at his home then were the Israeli agriculture minister Ari'el Sharon and the Egyptian 
defence minister General ‘Abd al-Halim abu Ghazalah. 


During the meeting, President as-Sadat proposed the idea of joint Egyptian-Israeli military 
action against Libya with the intention of toppling the regime there. 


At the outset of the meeting, President as-Sadat asked for Sharon's views on the plan. But 
Sharon said he would like to study the topography of the Libyan-Egyptian border region 
before giving an opinion. Upon Sharon's answer, As-Sadat immediately ordered the provision 
of all facilities for him to visit the border region.' The official excuse for Sharon's visit 
would be to explore the possibilities of carrying out joint Egyptian-Israeli agricultural 
cooperation in the region. 


Two days later, a second meeting was held to continue discussions over the plan. The meeting. 
held at President as-Sadat's retreat at Qanater al-Khairiyyah, was attended by Sharon and 

the Israeli Air Force commander who was hastily summoned after the first meeting, and 
accompanied Sharon on his visit to the Egyptian-Libyan border region. The talks were also 
attended by the Egyptian vice president Husni Mubarak; the defence minister, General ‘Abd 
al-Halim Abu Ghazalah; the director of Egypt's secret intelligence Mohammad Sa'id al-Mahy; 
the director of military intelligence; and the deputy director of the Israeli secret 
intelligence service. 


During the meeting, the following points were made: Sharon said the terrain in the border 
region necessitated the carrying out of a special operation on four distinct axes: 


1. A lightning air-raid to destroy Libya's rocket bases and military build-up 
centers, irrelevant of whether these are human or equipment centres. This will 
rely on the photographs possessed by the Egyptians and Israelis which were taken 
by U.S. satellites. However, to ensure the success of the plan the main targets 
will be those nearest to Egyptian territory. 


2. The second axis of the design involves a land attack launched from the half-way 
mark between Libya and Egypt. The attacking Egyptian force will move in one unit 
from the Siwah Oasis. After passing through the Libyan border the unit will divide 
into two divisions, one to head for Al-Gaghboub and the other for Barga. 


3. The third axis of the plan is to use Libyans opposed to the revolution. Those 
factions will start from the inner Egyptian oasis and move southward towards Kufra. 


4, The fourth axis would move from the Sudan and station itself on the Libyan- 
Chadian border to prevent Libyan forces deployed in Chad from intervening and then 
annihilate them on their return. 


President as-Sadat expressed admiration for the plan and asked whether it could be implemented 
immediately.

The Israeli Air Force commander noted thata lightning air attack would require 35-40 F-16 
fighter jets because the attack would have to be launched from a remote air base so that 
Soviet intelligence could not record the operation at its inception. 


The Israeli Air Force commander suggested that the jets should commence their attack from 
air bases at Qana North, Wahat Dakhla, or Cairo West, an’ return to bases at Mersa Matrouh, 
Siwah and Marbout. 


Since Egypt did not have that type of aircraft, Israel would consider the use of her own 

jets in this operation, provided they were painted over with Egyptian marking and piloted 

by Israelis who are provided with Egyptian identities and speak with a fluent Egyptian accent. 
Furthermore, the Israelis demanded, the Egyptians would offer compensation for any damages 
incurred. 


Later, the director of Egyptian military intelligence and deputy director of Israeli military 
intelligence disclosed all available information on Libya's military capabilities and the 
Soviet military presence in Libya. 


Mahy, director of Egypt's secret intelligence, then discussed the subject of training 
Libyans. He said Libyan nationals were already being given military training in two villages 
in the Buhaira region, Bab al-Matamir, and Dilangat. 


Mahy said Egyptian intelligence had contacts inside Libya and was obtaining information from 
a Libyan living in London who had excellent contacts with a senior Libyan official. 


He said this official was recruited during a visit to Cairo and that he was furnishing 
Egyptian intelligence with highly important information. Mahy added that his network had 
further highly reliable contacts inside Libya. 


Sharon suggested that the plan should be put in action during the month of Ramadan this year 
and that the attack should be by air. The timing, he said, should coincide with the first 
appearance of dawn in the period between July 23 and 31, i.e. the period preceding president 
as-Sadat's visit to Washington. President as-Sadat would, thus, arrive in Washington with 

a strong stand which would enable him to obtain the assistance he requires. 


Husni Mubarak expressed objection to an operation of this size which would have the risk 
of Soviet intervention. He said American approval must be obtained prior to the plan's 
implementation by President as-Sadat personally during his proposed visit to the United 
States. 


According to information giving the details of this plan, the Libyan security authorities said 
that the meeting ended with the creation of a committee grouping the Egyptian defence minister, 
the Israeli Air Force commander, the director of the Egyptian intelligence service, the deputy- 
director service [as received] the deputy-director of the Israeli intelligence service, and 

the director of the Egyptian military intelligence service. 


This committee will prepare the final plan to be presented to President as-Sadat before 
July 15, 1981.
