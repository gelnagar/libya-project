Summary (AI): A 1981 New York Times article reports that Libyan student opponent Faisal Zagallai was shot in Fort Collins, Colorado, in October 1980 by a man the police said was an American mercenary, allegedly acting on Qaddafi's orders. The article describes the Libyan regime's attempts to kill exiled dissidents abroad.
• Zagallai was on a Libyan list of 24 students to be killed, according to an FBI warning about six months before the shooting.
• Zagallai's father was a critic of the old regime, a member of Parliament and Mayor of Tripoli under King Idris.
• Jana, the Libyan press agency, said four days after the shooting that the World Revolutionary Headquarters had acted against Zagallai.
• The article says about a dozen foes of Qaddafi were murdered in spring 1980 in Europe and the Middle East.
• Zagallai helped set up the Free Libyan Students Union, which the organization says had 2,000 members.
• 1980-10-14: Faisal Zagallai was shot in his apartment by a man posing as a corporate recruiter; he survived with a bullet through his skull that blinded one eye.
• 1981-04-22: FBI arrested Eugene A. Tafoya of Truth or Consequences, N.M., and charged him with attempting to kill Zagallai.
• 1981: Two weeks after Tafoya's arrest, the Reagan administration ordered the Libyan People's Bureau in Washington to close and expelled all 27 of its diplomats.
