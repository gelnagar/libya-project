LIBYA 


PAKISTANIS REPORTED RECEIVING GUERRILLA TRAINING IN DESERT 
Paris AFRICA AFP in English 9 Oct 81 p 17 


[Text] Islamabad, October 7--Pakistan has expressed "deep concern" to Libya 

over reports that thousands of Pakistani ex-servicemen recruited as security 

guards in Libya were being given "forced guerrilla training" to fight as mer- 
cenaries for Libyan leader Moamer Qadhdhafi. 


Press reports here said today that the Pakistanis were being trained in the 
Southern Sahara desert to take part in any "adventures Colonel Qadhdhafi might 
launch in Africa or the Middle East. 


Some 3,000 retired Pakistan Army personnel were employed by Libya through a Lon- 
don-based firm called Al-Murtaza Associates. Their contract, approved by the 
Pakistan Embassy in Berne, Switzerland, early this year specified that they 
would not be engaged in military or paramilitary duties but would be employed 

to protect Libyan vital installations only. 


Today's reports said the Libyan authorities had violated the contract and were 
training the Pakistanis to handle Soviet-supplied arms in desert conditions. 
The question was said to be affecting relations between Islamabad and Tripoli. 


Letters from the Pakistanis to their relatives said they had spent six months 

in military training and those who refused were tortured, Urdu daily NAWA I WAGT 
said. Previous reports indicated that some of them were trained for Libyan 
action in Chad. 


Other reports said that late last month more than 100 of the ex-servicemen were 
flown from Libya to Damascus. They protested the action by staging a sit-in 
at Damascus airport and demanded to see the Pakistani Ambassador to Syria. 


The reports said they feared they were being sent to fight against Israel in 
Lebanon and wanted to return to Pakistan. 


Official sources said that on President Zia-Ul Haq's direct instructions Pakis- 
tan's Foreign Secretary Riaz Piracha was in touch with Libyan and Syrian author- 
ities on the issue. Pakistan was still awaiting permission from Tripoli for a 
high-level ministerial delegation to visit the camps housing the Pakistani 
emigrants to verify their charges of maltreatment and hardship, the sources said. 
(A.F.P.)
