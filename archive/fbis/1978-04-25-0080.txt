[Text] At the invitation of the brother Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi, leader of the Great 
1 September Revolution and secretary general of the Generai People's Congress, Ahmed 
Sekou Toure, president of the Republic of Guinea, leading an important delegation, paid 
a visit to the Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah from 17 to 23 April 1978. 


President Ahmed Sekou Toure was accorded a warm official and popular welcome expressive 
of the feelings of fraternity and friendship that link the two sister peoples and countries, 
and the position which President Sekou Toure enjoys among the citizens of the Jamahiriyah. 


During the visit the two leaders reviewed in the course of their many meetings the 
bilateral relations between the two countries, and the African, Arab and international 
political situation. They also conducted official talks. Taking part in those talks 

on the Guinean side were Prime Minister Lansana Beavogui, President of the National 
Assembly Damantang Camara, Minister of Social Domain Saifoulaye Diallo, Justice Minister 
Lansane Diane, Minister of Information and Ideology Senainon Behanzin, Minister of 
Planning and Economic Cooperation N'faly Sangare, Minister of Banks and Securities 
Mamadou Toure, Director of the Banks and Currency Department [name indistinct], 
Arbassador at the Foreign Ministry (Ali Bangora), Guinean Ambassador to Libya Fode 
Soriba Camara, 


On the Libyan side: Secretary of Agriculture Muhammad 'Ali at-Tabu, Secretary of Trade 
Abu Bakr 'Ali ash-Sharif, Secretary of Nutrition Dr Umar Ahmad al-Maqsi, Secretary of 
Foreign Affairs 'Ali 'Abd as-Salam at-Turayki, Secretary of Planning Musa Abu Furaywah, 
Foreign Undersecretary 'Isa Ayad al-Ba'ba!', Director of the African Department at the 
Foreign Secretariat Salim al-Shuwayhidi, Director of the Treaties Department at the 
Foreign Secretariat Mahmud al-Bakkush, Ambassador to Guinea Hamid Ahmad al-Hudhayiri and 
a number of senior technicians. 


All the negotiations were conducted in an atmosphe.e of cordiality, fraternity and 
frankness, and dealt with various aspects. These negotiations were characterized by an 
identity of views by the two sides. 


In the sphere of bilateral relations, the two leaders praise the strong historic relations 
that link the Libyan Arab and Guinean peoples. They express full satisfaction with the 
development of joint solidarity and cooperation in struggle between the two states in all 
fields--a matter which was once more manifested in the signing of the minutes of an 
agreement on economic cooperation between them. This resulted from their realization 
that economics is one of the main supports for the continuation of their liberation 
struggle totally and effectively in order to realize their true independence, The two 
sides express their commitment to make every possible effort so that this cooperation 
will be an example to be followed. They reaffirm that this commitment manifests the 

two peoples' will to be liberated from all sorts of foreign domination and imperialist 
control, They reaffirm that the commitment of the two countries stems from the concept 
of African cooperation and originality.

In the African sphere, the two sides condemn the imperialist attempts that aim at estab- 
lishing alliances and dominant groupings among the countries of the continent. They denounce 
alliances leading to the division of the countries of the continent and call on the African 
States not to enter into such alliances. They call on the African countries to resist 

such groupings in accordance with the principles of nonalinement. 


The two sides reaffirm their total commitment to the principle of self-detcrmination for all 
peoples, and their total support for the sUruggle of che African liberation movements in 
Namibia, Zimbabwe and southern Africa against foreign occupation and racism. The two sides 
reaffirm at the same time their desire to participate in finding a just solution for the 
conflicts in the African Continent, particularly in the Arabian Sahara region and Eritrea. 


In this connection the Guinean side expresses its approval of the efforts made by Libya to 
make successful the meetings of reconciliation among brothers in Chad--meetings crowned with 
the convening of the Sabha conference on 24 February 1978--and the meetings of all parties 
on 27 March 1978 in Sabha and Benghazi for the realization of reconciliation in the Sudan. 


The two sides praise the success of the Jamahiriyah's efforts to stop the fighting among 
brothers in Somalia and Ethiopia. They reaffirm that the efforts made to bring about a 
reconciliation will not become futile unles«~ the colonialists interfere in the African issues; 
consequently, the colonialists should take their dirty hands off Africa. 


In the Arab sphere, the two sides call for the continuous development of fraternal relations 
and true cooperation between the African and Arab states. In addition to the common interest, 
this cooperation is of benefit to the peoples of the Third World, who are struggling for 
development and freedom. 


Regarding the Palestinian cause, the two sides assert their revolutionary and unconditional 
Support for the legitimate struggle of the Palestinian people for the return to their homeland 
and their right to mdependence and nationai dignity, in accordance to the will of this people, 
which alone has the right to determine its fate. 


In tne Islamic field, the two sides observe with deep regret that many peoples describe Islam 
as backward and contravening science and knowledge, generally speaking, as opposed to any 
democratic 2nd social progress. The two sides, determined to work to restore the dignity 

of Islam, stress that the faith of Islam is an international and progressive fact that is 
based on moral and spiritual values that call for respect of man and for the continuous 
Jevelopment of communities. Islam also calls for peace, freedom and solidarity in relations 
between peoples. The Koran emphatically calls for increasing social justice and the brother- 
hood of man, and for respecting the principles of democracy. It forbids man to do wrong 

to his brother. 


Tne two sides believe that if states were guided in their laws by the humane teachings 

f the Koran, they would be able to honor social and historic values and the rights of the 
individual and the community, a matter which basically contradicts the philosophical 
principle and the various practices of imperialism and old- and neo~-colonialism and exploiting 


capitalism. 


The two sides stress their resolve to support and back the Moslem peoples in the political 
and economic fields, to enable these peoples to play their roles in the Third World to 
the benefit of mankind.

In the international field the two sides affirm their adherence to the charter and principles 
of the United Nations and consider it necessary to introduce a change in the organization's 
charter and the basic stutute of the Security Council to conform with current developments 

in the world in accordance with the requirements and aspirations of the nations, 


The two sides call in particular for the abolition of the veto. 


During these talks brother Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi spoke about the principles of the Great 
1 September Revolution and its achievements, botn at home and abroad, represented in the 
emergence of the people's power, which had been achieved through conferences, and the 
people's committee, in accordance with part one of the Green Book. It was also represented 
in the need to solve the economic problem in the second part of the Green Book, a fact which 
was manifested in the achievements covering all aspects and successes of the Libyan Arat 
people against foreign monopolies It also affirmed the continuing cail of the people of 
the Jamahiriyah for the unity of the Arab nation, the African peoples and the peoples of 

the Third World, and for confrontation with for2ign interventions and facts, and for con- 
tinuing the support of peoples struggling for freedom. 


President Ahmed Sekou Toure expressed his conviction that the Jamahiriyah enjoys the 
appreciation and respect of all peoples and governments of the world for its revolutionary 
Sincerity and its great ability to confront all attempts aimed at encroaching on the helpless 
peoples in their struggle for progress. 


President Ahmed Sekou Toure acquainted brother Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi with the great 
changes that have taken place in the march of the Guinean revolution since September 1958. 
He explained to Colonel al-Qn‘i.inafi the great efforts which the Republic of Gu. nea 

is making to provid? prosperity, establish social justice and control the national resources, 
He also acquainted him with the efforts which the leadership and people of Guinea are making 
for Africa and for consolidating Arab-African solidarity. 


Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi expressed admiration at the progress achieved by the Guinean 
revolution in the fields of economic and cultural development, for the prosperity of the 
Guinean people. 


At the conclusion of the visit, President Ahmed Sekou Toure, president of the Republic of 
Guinea, expressed his profounc thanks to brother Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi, secretary general 
of the General Pe yple's Congress and leader of the Great 1 September Revolution, and to the 
Libyan Arab people ior the warm fraternal reception accorded to him and members of his 
accompanying delegation in the Jamahiriyah. 


President Ahmed Sekou Toure extended an official invitation to his brother Col Mu'ammar 
al-Qadhdhafi to visit the Republic of Guinea. The invitation was accepted with thanks and 
its date will be fixed later. 

Issued in Tripoli on 23 April 1978

calm in the country and confirm the conciliation agreement reached at the Sabha and Senrnazi
