Summary (AI): A New York Times article (18 December 1981) reports that President Reagan defended his administration's warnings about Libyan assassination teams, saying the threat was real and based on several sources. It describes the travel ban on Americans to Libya and an informer who came forward in Africa in November 1981.
• The White House banned travel by Americans to Libya and advised Americans living there to leave.
• Federal officials made composite sketches of five alleged terrorists, described in the article.
• The first indication of Libyan assassination plans came in early September 1981, shortly after the US jet fighter downing of Libyan planes.
• The informer was flown to the United States in late November and passed lie detector tests by the FBI, Secret Service and intelligence agencies.
• An American diplomat in Paris barely escaped an attack in November 1981 that officials thought involved Libya.
• 1981-11: An informer came forward in Africa claiming he had taken part in Libyan assassination team training and had first-hand information about teams heading to the United States.
