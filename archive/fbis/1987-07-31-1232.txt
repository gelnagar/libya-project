Hostages Reportedly Near Juba 
JN301750 Khartoum SUNA in English 
1730 GMT 30 Jul 87 


[Text] Khartoum, July 30, SUNA — An Equatoria Region 
official has denied that the three Americans and the British, who 
were recently kidnapped by the rebels in south Sudan, were 
working in relief organizations in the region. 


Dr Simon Kuwaja, the finance official in the region, has told 
(SUNA) that the abducted foreigners were working as teachers 
in the region and that their abduction was meant at terrorizing 
foreigners. He asserted that the four people are still near the 
provincial capital Juba because “it is impossible to transport 
them to distance [as received] area due to the intensive search 
campaign” carried out by the security forces there. 


The four people were said to be abducted by the rebel elements 
of Sudan People’s Liberation Army, SPLA, of John Garang, 
early this month from Juba. 


Radio SPLA Terms Al-Sadiq London Meeting ‘Failure’ 
EA310336 (Clandestine) Radio of the Sudanese 

People’s Liberation Army in English 

1300 GMT 30 Jul 87 


[Text] The leaders of the Ummah, DUP [Democratic Unionist 
Party}, and NIF [Islamic National Front], Mr Al-Sadiq, Dr 
Turabi, and Ahmad Mirghani, went to London last week to hold 
a closed secret meeting. They were accompanied by their closest 
political auxiliaries and senior retired Army officers including 
Lieutenant General, retired, Yusuf Hamid Yusuf and Major 
General, retired, Uthman Abdallah. 


A highly reliable source who was in this London meeting had sent 
details of the agenda of the meeting to Radio SPLA and appealed 
to Radio SPLA to expose it to the Sudanese people. A summary 
of the agenda is here presented to the Sudanese public as it was 
reported by our source: 


— The three parties to forget and put aside all their differences 
and merge together and form a government on national unity in 
order to preserve and (?protect) Arabism, Islam, and the Arabic 


language against dangers posed by non-Muslim and non-Arabs. 
There was no agreement nor resolution on this point. 


— The three parties to agree to dissolve the Council of Ministers 
and the Constituent Assembly and to declare a state of emer- 
gency all over the country, and appoint Al-Sadiq as caretaker 
prime minister with emergency powers until elections are held 
after one year. On this, the DUP and Ummah agreed on the 
necessity for a l-year state of emergency, but the parties dis- 
agreed on the other issues while the NIF opposed even the state 
of emergency. 


— To consider the feasibility of handing over the government to 
the Army for | year to bring law and order to the capital and to 
crush the SPLA, after which elections are to be held and the 
parties take over again. On this, the parties expressed reservation 
and feared that there was no guarantee that the Army would 
hand power back to the parties. No resolution was passed on this. 


— To review what Al-Sadigq called the inability and incompe- 
tence of the security organs to uncover SPLA formations inside 
the country. Al-Sadig bitterly complained that the SPLM-SPLA 
chairman and commander in chief is well informed of up-to-date 
and sensitive information in government institutions more than 
himself. On this, the Ummah and DUP agreed that the state 
emergency will strengthen the security organs to eliminate 
SPLM-SPLA supporters and sympathizers and silence any other 
forms of internal opposition. 


— To pool their ideas together to devise means and ways of 
procuring ammunitions to relieve the acute shortage in the Army 
and to modernize and strengthen the Army with modern arms. 


— To request Egypt to intervene in the civil war. On this there 
was unanimity although the request has not been made yet. End 
of agenda. 


Generally our source said the meeting was a failure, and that 
Al-Sadiq in particular left very gloomy and depressed. In essence, 
there is no government in Khartoum and nobody or party knows 
or has a clear vision as to what must be done or of what will 
happen to the Sudan. Our source revealed to us that the three 
parties are at a loss, and the leaders and some seniors are in a 
panic about their own security and property. They felt defeat by 
problems and problems [sentence as heard]. 


‘Major’ Southern Party Rejects Emergency Laws 
JN301758 Khartoum SUNA in English 
1725 GMT 30 Jul 87 


[Text] Khartoum, July 30, SUNA — A major southern Sudan 
party has dismissed as unacceptable the recent declaration of 
emergency all over the country, and described as unconvincing 
the government’s reasons behind the declaration. 


The Southern Sudan Political Association SSPA said in a 
statement issued here yesterday Wednesday that the deteriorat- 
ing economic and security situation was an outcome of the 
government policies. 


The SSPA said the governemnt should change its polcies, abro- 
gate the Septemebr 1983 laws and put into effect the provisions 
of Koka-Dam accord signed in 1986 by the Sudan People’s

embraces virtually all the Sudanese parties and trade unions. 


On the other hand the Sudanese Medical Association rejected in

Students Demonstrate in Kassala 30 Jul 
JN301922 Khartoum SUNA in Arabic 1730 GMT 30 Jul 87 


[Text] Kassala, Sudan, 30 Jul (SUNA) — This morning, the 
students of Kassala continued demonstrations that they began 
yesterday. The police confronted the demonstrators and dis- 
persed them. 


Following the demonstrations, Isma‘il Ya‘qub, deputy governor 
of the Eastern Region, issued a statement asking the students to 
be patient, alert, and to abide by the law. He also asked them to 
act in a manner befitting to one of the pillars of the April uprising, 
in solidarity with popular and official endeavors. Ya‘qub also 
declared that these endeavors seek to obliterate the worn-out 
policies of the May regime from which the Sudanese people still 
suffer. 


Egyptian Mediation Ends Clashes With Ethiopia 
JN300848 Abu Dhabi AL-ITTIHAD in Arabic 
28 Jul 87 pp 1, 18 


[Excerpt] Khartoum, Cairo — Al-Ittihad and agencies — A 
Sudanese military source has told Al-Ittihad that clashes along 
the Sudanese-Ethiopian border have now stopped completely due 
to Egyptian mediation between Khartoum and Addis Ababa. 
The source added that the Ethiopian authorities had released a 
number of Sudanese civilian detainees who were accused of being 
Eritrean rebels. 


Another Sudanese military source told Al-Ittihad that Colonel 
John Garang’s rebel forces are fully cooperating with Ethiopian 
forces to launch an attack on the region of Akobo. This implies 
an expansion of Ethiopian prorebel activity. 


The military source added that Ethiopian aggression against the 
eastern border recurs from time to time on the pretext of pursuing 
Eritrean rebels. The source noted that Sudan’s forces are pre- 
pared to deal with the situation on all fronts, and denied reports 
on the arrival of any military aid from Arab nations to Sudan. 
[passage omitted]

‘Responsible’ Sources Cited 
JN300840 Al-Sharigah AL-KHALIJ in Arabic 
28 Jul 87 pl 


[Text] Khartoum — Al-Khalij learned from responsible eco- 
nomic sources in Khartoum yesterday that the IMF delegation 
still insists that the Sudanese pound be devalued. The sources 
also stated that the IMF also has its own views concerning 
Sudan’s 4-year economic reform plan. 


These sources additionally reiterated that Sudan’s delegation for 
its part is sticking to its policy of subsidizing commodities, 
reducing public spending, and preserving the public sector due to 
the private sector’s inability to invest in losing projects such as 
the railway system. 


On another level, Dr Bashir ‘Umar, minister of finance and 
economy, commented yesterday that the two sides will today 
begin negotiating Sudan’s economic policies and strategies fol- 
lowing 2 days of discussing future economic scenarios for Sudan. 
These scenarios were based on Sudan’s achieving an annual 
5-percent growth rate while reducing both inflation and reliance 
on foreign aid.

Delegation to OAU Summit Returns 30 Jul 
JN301900 Khartoum SUNA in English 
1735 GMT 30 Jul 87 


[Text] Khartoum, July 30, SUNA — The Sudanese delegation 
to the OAU 23rd summit has returned to the country today. 


The delegation, headed by Foreign Minister M. Tawfiq Ahmad 
had objected over the inclusion of south Sudan conflict in the 
report of the outgoing chairman of the OAU (Organization of 
African Unity). 


We have objected to the inclusion of this issue in the report 
because it (the south Sudan question) is an internal matter, the 
minister said in a press statement following his arrival today. 


The OAU Constitution has stipulated that the OAU should 
neither study nor look into internal affairs of the member states, 
the minister argued. 


The minister, however, denied any contact between the del- 
egation and the rebels hosted by Ethiopia. 


He further reiuted reports that Sudan had presented documents 
incriminating Ethiopia, to the OAU, over its backing of the 
rebels. 


It is understood that reports circulated here, prior to the conven- 
ing of the summit, talked about documents showing “the 
involvement of foreign elements in the south Sudan conflict”.

[Text] Khartoum, July 30, SUNA — Egyptian State Minister at 
Foreign Ministry Butrus Ghali announced that the African 
summit held recently in the Ethiopian capital, has approved 
holding another summit next Sept. in Addis Ababa to discuss the 
economic crisis in the continent. 


Ghali, who stopped over at Khartoum airport on his way to Cairo 
from Addis Ababa, said the African summit has reviewed the 
question of Namibia, racial segregation in South Africa besides 
the Chadian-Libyan dispute. 


The Egyptian minister reaffirmed that Sudan and Egypt will 
back the African unity, settle the conflict between African 
countries in addition to boosting the Non-Alignment Movement. 


Al-Baz on Sadan, Ethiopia 
NC301945 Cairo MENA in English 1835 GMT 30 Jul 87 


[Text] Khartoum, July 30 (MENA) — Dr Usamah al-Baz, 
Egyptian first foreign under secretary, and head of the Egyptian 
President’s Office for Political Affairs, said here today he 


believes points of agreement between Egypt, Sudan and Ethiopia 
are far more than points of disagreement. 


In a statement during his stopover at Khartoum airport on his 
way to Cairo, Al-Baz said he believes the elements of difference 
are temporary and bound to disappear. The three countries, 
therefore should look forward to a better future in their relations, 
particularly Egypt and the Sudan, he added. 


Al-Baz had taken part in the Egyptian delegation headed by 
President Husni Mubarak to the meetings of the African summit 
which wound up in Addis Ababa yesterday.
