LIBYA

Al-Qadhdhafi Sends Letter to UN on Chad, Lebanon 
LD1 22321 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 2000 GMT 12 Sep 83 


[Text] Tripoli, 12 Sept (JANA) — The brother leader of the 
revolution has sent a letter to the president of the Security 
Council. Here is the text: 


It has become clear that two places, one in the Arab homeland 
and one in Africa, have become more explosive than at any time 
before — a matter that threatens the peace and security of the 
Arab homeland in the first instance, and the security of the 
Socialist People’s Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah and Africa in the 
second. If we compare the conditions of the two countries — 
Chad and Lebanon — in the past and what is taking place in 
them today we find that their peace and security is threatened 
today more than at any time in the past. The situation in Lebanon 
was reasonable despite the civil war which raged there. Most of 
its areas were more secure because the struggle in Lebanon was 
partial and factional, and a limited affair that made controlling 
it possible through factional understanding [as received]. But 
today, the situation in Lebanon has become so explosive as to 
render peace and security almost impossible in this Arab country. 
It is clear that the internationalization of the struggle and the 
entry of external parties under the name of peace-keeping forces 
and on the pretext of restoring peace and security has aggravated 
the situation. 


These parties, which are represented in the French, 
American,and other forces that came in the name of peace, are 
the ones which are threatening peace and which have 
transformed the Lebanese struggle from one of factional and 
localized struggle into a struggle in which the multinational 
forces are playing the role of supporting one Lebanese faction 
against another for imperialist and crusader aims. As for Chad, 
the civil war is between Chadian groups which were fighting for 
power. This struggle did not, however, embrace all the Chadian 
territory — a matter that made peace and security relatively 
prevalent. Indeed, the Chadian parties reached a national recon- 
ciliation in Lagos in 1979. None of them opted out of it except 
the rebel Hissein Habre, the ruler of Ndjamina today. If we look 
at the situation in Chad, it is clear that it is liable to erupt at any 
moment. If this situation ever explodes, the peace and security of 
Africa in general, and of Chad and the Jamahiriyah in particular, 
would be threatened. It is clear that U.S. intervention, which 
emerged under the pretext of achieving security in Chad, is 
responsible for the grave aggravation of conditions there because 
the American and French intervention has made the Chad issue 
an international one, a matter that might push other big powers 
to intervene. 


American and French planes, warships, and weapons are the ones 
which are destroying Lebanese towns and contributing to the 
slaughter of its Muslim inhabitants. They are the ones which are 
taking part in strikes against Muslims, who destroy their homes 
in northern Chad. Intervention in this manner has made the war 
enter a new, crusader, stage which, we believed, civilization and 
humanity had long ago passed.
