LE MONDE: AIR FORCE FIRES ON FRENCH PATROL PLANE 


LD271301 Paris LE MONDE in French 27 Mar 80 pp 1, 7 LD 


{[J.I. report: "Franco-Libyan Air Incident in the Mediterranean" ] 


[Text] On Sunday, 23 March two Libyan Air Force Mirage-III planes opened fire on a French 
Breguet-Atlantic marine patrol plane on a mission in the Mediterranean. The air incident 
took place 20 nautical miles (around 36 km) off the Libyan coast, in other words outside 
territorial waters, according to information given by French sources. 


After the Qafsah affair last January, which prompted France to supply the Tunisian army 
with several Transall transport aircraft and Puma helicopters while three French Navy 
warships temporarily patrolled the Gulf of Gabes, Libya reacted energetically against 
France. 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi's government immediately denounced what he called the "French in- 
vasion of Tunisia." Libyan demonstrators attacked the French Embassy in Tripoli and the 
French Consulate in Banghazi on 4 February. Since then the quarrel has died down. 
President al-Qadhdhafi indicated that his country was not pursuing its grievances against 
France while Paris submitted demands for compensation for the sacking of its offices. 


Since then it has been revealed that a French cargo ship L'Astor had been held since 
October 1979 after being chartered to transport arms. 


The air incident which took place at the end of last week on Sunday, 23 March was 
between two Libyan Mirage-III aircraft and a French Breguet-Atlantic patrolling outside 
territorial waters which are limited to 12 nautical miles (around 22 km). As is known 
France has since 1969 supplied Tripoli with 110 Mirage-III and Mirage-I aircraft and 38 
Mirage F-1 interception aircraft and that it trained the crews. 


Although we do not know at present whether the Libyan fighter planes first made the customary 
international challenges, one of the Mirages made a series of passes over the Breguet- 
Atlantic, firing warning shots. None of the warning shots hit the French plane, 


The Quai d'Orsay is making no official comment on this incident, but it does not seem 
that the French authorities have made any protest so far.

The Breguet -Atlantic was apparently on a conventional surveillance mission in the 
eastern Mediterranean. These marine patrol flights are routine but, since the Qafsah 
affair, they have tended to be more frequent off the Libyan and Tunisian coasts, 


Last week these missions consisted notably of obtaining intelligence on the movements 
of the Soviet fleet in the Gulf of Gabes, where it h ;: traditional mooring grounds in the 
shallows. U.S. warships were also in the vicinity.

[Text] Paris, March 27 (AFP)--Libya has admitted that two of its air force Mirage- 
III's should not have gone after a French military plane on Sunday, an authoritative 
source at the French foreign office said today. 
The unarmed Breguet-Atlantic plane was making reconnaissance flight in international 
airspace off the Libyan coast when the two Mirages reportedly harassed it. No shots 


were fired and the French pilot was able to return to base, 


France immediately protested about the incident, but Libya!s admission of a "mistake" 
was seen here as closing the incident. 


Relations between the two countries, soured after violent anti-French demonstrations in 
Libya over the Qafsah affair, had been improving in recent weeks,

SIX FRG GEOLOGISTS DETAINED NEAR EGYPTIAN BORDER 
NC271608 Cairo DPA in Arabic 1445 GMT 27 Mar 80 NC 


[Text] Cairo, 27 March=-DPA learned today from FRG sources that six West German geologists 
were arrested at the border triangle among Egypt, Sudan.and Libya 3 weeks ago and that 
Libya is still detaining them. These sources said that the geologists, who are from 

West Berlin Free University, were doing research that could raise enormous political 

furor, in cooperation with the German research society and the Egyptian scientific 

research academy. 


The geologists had been in the area since early March and were provided with tents and 
two cars, The group included an Egyptian driver and a mechanic as well as a Sudanese 
geologist, 


On 3 March the group camped on the Sudanese border, Some of them made a trip that was 
said to have taken them to Libyan territory, where Libyan soldiers captured them. It was 
reported that an Egyptian was killed in the incident. These sources said that the Libyan 
authorities promised to release the detainees shortly when it is confirmed that they are 
not spies. 


The FRG Embassy here and the academic service exchange office said that they had no infor- 
mation on the activities of the research group on the border triangle. Reports are 
conflicting about what the Germans were doing. 


Certain reports say that they were doing topographic research while others said they 
were prospecting for water resources; still others said they were searching for uranium 
and tantalum, The Cairo press so far has not reported the incident despite its great 
interest in any events related to Libya. The names of those who participated in the 
operation have not been disclosed, 


JALLUD OPENS FIFTH CONFERENCE AGAINST OIL MONOPOLIES 


LD271556 Tripoli JANA in English 1420 GMT 27 Mar 80 LD 


{Text] Tripoli, March 27, JAMAHIRIYAH NEWS AGENCY--Staff Major 'Abd as-Salam Jallud 
yesterday inaugurated, at the People's Hall in Tripoli, the fifth conference against oil 
monopolies which is supervised by the Union of Petroleum, Petrochemicals and Metallurgy 
in the Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah,

Staff Major Jallud started dy welcoming the members of the conference in the Jamahiriyah. 
He said: "The holding of this conference in Libya indicates the appreciation of the 
forces of progress and virtue which struggle and fight in order to establish their 
identity. For the Libyan Arad people, it is a token of appreciation and esteem from your 
peoples to ours." He went on to say that this meeting will entrust the Libyans, men and 
women, with a great responsibility which will make them more determined to confront and 
fight the capitalist monopolies. 


"The Libyan Arab people, which led the revolution of oil producing countries and of the 
possessors of resources, are proud to see this conference against the monopolies held 
on their land, an honour which gives them great moral and political support." 


Staff Major Jallud pointed out that the Libyan Arab people, after triggering off their 
great revolution in 1969, have realized that monopolies are the most dangerous bases. 
Consequently, and while struggling in order to expel the British and American bases, 
they started their action to abolish the monopolistic ones represented by the oil 
monopolies. 


"The Libyan Arab people who are preparing to celebrate the anniversary of the evacuation 

of the British bases, consider this meeting against monopolies in international encourage- 
ment and support which reflects the esteem held for this small people which has achieved 
great things. We thank the conferees who travelled thousands of miles to stress unity 

in the struggle against the monopolies and show that political freedom lies in the realiza- 
tion of cultural, economic and social freedom." 


He pointed out that the Libyan people's oil revenues were the smallest in the world before 
the outbreak of the grand al-Fatah revolution. This led this people to struggle against 
the oil monopolies in 1970, a few months after the outbreak of the revolution. And then, 
he said, Third Jorld countries, which possess oil and mineral resources did not react to 
these monopolies. 


He said that Libyan people depended very much on oil even to win their bread and it was 
very likely for them to loose both. But at the end, they accepted the challenge, and 
demanded the return of the sums robbed by oil companies since 1961. He added that the 
economists learned about the principle of backdating in settling accounts, through the 
Libyans and the economic dictionary. The word 'backdating! sounded very strange to the 
companies which had neither been penalized nor forced to settle accounts for the past 
ten years since the battle of pricing had started. 


Staff Major Jallud went on to say: "Then we were not aiming at a particular price but 
wanted to correct certain economic concepts. For example, it was the capitalist countries 
and companies which used %o determine the prices of raw material from the Third World 
countries. So we corrected this concept and these countries are now able to price their 
materials, not only oil but other resources as well. 


In his inaugural speech, Staff Major Jallud said that the Libyan Arab people have also 
fought a battle to control their oil production. Before the revolution Libya used 
to export 4,600,000 barrels of oil per day--a figure which shows a usurpation not only 
of the Libyan wealth, but of that of the world. He pointed out that the Libyan people 
are using the most up-to-date technology and scientific formulae to control production.

He remarked that the varied battles fought by the Libyan Arab people to control their 
oil resources by recovering the robbed sums or the so-called arrears of accounts from 
the companies and preventing them from burning the gas which was to be reinjected in 
the wells, in addition to the pricing of the oil of Libyan companies and the battles of 
nationalization which were not easy but necessary to achieve the Libyan's full freedom. 


He pointed out that such battles are over neither in Libya nor in the world. More battles 
are still to come, he said, to eliminate the monopolies of technology and to change Libya 
from a country possessing and exporting rescurces to one industrializing them. This 
battle is the most dangerous, he stressed, because the monopolistic capitalist companies 
and countries are not likely to accept this change easily. 


"We are self-confident and the experiences and victories we scored in previous battles 
give us faith in our ability to win in these." 


Staff Major Jallud pointed out that the peoples cf the developing countries are now 
experiencing a political awakening which will lead them to reinforce their independence 
and give it its real meaning, explaining that the political independence will remain 
irrelevant unless supported by the social, economic and cultural independence. 


The U.S.A., he said, all the colonialist countries in its satellite and all the monopolies 
are trying to delay the peoples movement to reinforce and consolidate their freedom. They 
successfully disguised their attempts so that the real meaning of freedom became a mere 
form without content. Colonialism and its capitalist monopolies, he added, are intended 
to limit the independence of peoples to the superficial facad2 of political independence. 


The battle with neo-colonialism, he continued, is one of the most serious and peoples 
should accept that. He stressed that victory is for the peoples who will win the fight 
once and for all, the interests of freedom in its real meaning. 


Staff Major Jallud added: "The United States said she is living an economic crisis in 
addition to inflation and deficit in payments, but, we on the other hand, see the U.S. 
war budget increasing as more sums are allocated for the prompt military intervention 

and other forces assigned to defend the Gulf." 


Then he wondered: "Prompt intervention against who?", and answered by saying "It is 
against the peoples who might try to take over their own resources by force." 


Staff Major Jallud then referred to the false claims of Western countries which attribute 
their crisis to the oil prices. He described such allegations as untrue and invalid be- 
cause the huge increase in their military spending and in their budgets to manufacture 
more means of destruction and killing and form prompt intervention forces of utmost 
mobility were biusically forced to defeat and drive to submissior. the peoples of the 
developing countries endeavouring to consolidate their independence. 


The peoples who believe in their right to lead free and decent life will not be threatened 
by these colonialist policies, simply because they are ready to die for their cause. 


Staff Major Jallud elaborated that this conference has special importance because it is 
for peoples, not goverments, Those who travelled thousands of miles to come to the 
Jamahiriyah are fully aware of its importance because it represents the peoples who 
suffered from imperialist monopolies concerning the scientific understanding of socialism.
