MEMORANDUM FOR THE RECORD 
SUBJECT: Working Group Meeting on Libya, 30 December 1981 

1. The meeting was held in the White House Situation Room and chaired by 
Ray Tantner of the NSC Staff. (Admiral Nance dropped in for about 15 minutes 
but made no comments.) The purpose was to discuss the contents of an 
interagency paper on “Soviet Responses to US Military Actions Against Libya" 
that will be the first item on the Agenda of the Libyan Task Force Meeting 
scheduled for 11 January (agenda attached). 

2. Since most participants in the meeting did not have copies of all the 
contributions to the paper, the discussion simply went through the outline of 
the paper identifying. points of possible disagreement and additional questions 
that need to be addressed. There was no significant disagreement on the key 
judgment of the paper -- that the USSR will not use its own military forces to 
try to counter US military action against Libya but will subsequently take 
advantage of such an attack to promote its own diplomatic and propaganda 
objectives and increase its access to Libyan military facilities. The 
potential disagreement over the question of "prepositioning" of equipment in 
Libya for use by Soviet forces disappeared; in the final analysis the only 
point State/PM wants to make is that non-Libyan pilots (Soviet, Cuban, Syrian, 
or whatever) could be used to fly some of the excess planes in the Libyan 
inventory, which would raise the potential military costs to the US somewhat 
and increase the odds that non-Libyans would be casualties of any attack. 
Other areas of apparent conflict also disappeared. 

3. NFAC was asked to provide to Howard Ticher at State by noon on 
31 December three bits of factual information: 

-- The latest specific information we have on the presence and functions 
of Cuban personnel in Libya. 

-- Details on past Soviet approaches to the Libyans for access to Libyan 
military facilities and Libyan responses (DIA came up blank). 

-~ A few examples of earlier situations in which Soviet military 
personnel serving as advisors in Third World countries had been killed 
in military attacks by other countries. 8;
