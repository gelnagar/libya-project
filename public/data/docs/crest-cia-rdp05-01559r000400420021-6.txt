Iranian Envoy Briefs Al-Qadhdhafi on Gulf, War 
LD231729. Tehran IRNA in English 1703 GMT 23 Jul 87 

[Text] Tripoli, July 23, IRNA — Libyan leader Colonel Mu‘am- 
mar al-Qadhdhafi reiterated his country’s solidarity with the 
Islamic revolution. “Libya is always beside the Islamic Republic 
and Muslim nation of Iran,” he added. 

Col. Al-Qadhdhafi made the remarks in a meeting with Iranian 
Ambassador in Tripoli Mohammad Kazem Khonsari here last 
night. 

Hojjat ol-Eslam Khonsari briefed the Libyan leader on the latest 
developments, including the success of Muslim combatants of 
Iran in the war fronts. 

Khonsari termed the current tension in the Persian Gulf jointly 
plotted by Arab reactionaries and U.S. imperialism, as an 
attempt to save the Iraqi regime from sure downfall and prevent 
Iran’s certain victory in the imposed war. “The Islamic Republic 
is ready to thwart the plot,” he stressed. 

In response, the Libyan leader lauded the Islamic revolution, 
declaring his country’s strong support for the Islamic Republic’s 
righteous stance in the Iraqi-imposed war. Libya’s position is 
unchangeable, he emphasised. 

Pointing to the Persian Gulf developments, Col. al-Qadhdhafi 
said presence of the U.S. fleet and other foreign forces in the 
Persian Gulf waters is hostile. The Islamic Republic’s resistance 
against coordinated pressures recently imposed by the U.S. and 
her allies is a sacred defence and is the Iranian nation’s right. 

“We will be beside our Iranian brethren by all our means in case 
of any invasion on the part of the U.S.,” he stressed.

[Text] Tripoli, July 23 (TANJUG) — When Libyan leader Col. 
Al-Qadhdhafi, in agreement with local leaders, abolished Tripoli 
as the Libyan capital early this year, many thought the new 
Capital would be the desert town of Hun at the Al-Jufrah Oasis, 
700 kilometers from Tripoli. 

Hun has not been proclaimed the new capital so far, but all 
government institutions, the governments and ministries have 
been instructed to move to the desert. 

The Army staff has moved but not the ministers. Only their 
emmisaries, some furniture and some seals. [sentence as received]

to sign documents which they bring with them from Tripoli. 

The revolutionary committees’ weekly 4/-Jamahiriyah sharply 
criticized the Hun phenomenon in its latest issue. In conclusion 
the weekly says no-one, except the military staff, has fully

government buildings in the city and ban the ministers from 
entering. In this way Libyans would no longer have to make their

Despite the criticism and call for the government to leave Tripoli, 
Al-Jufrah has little chance of becoming the capital since its
