LIBYA

WIDE ATTENDANCE AT ISLAMIC CONFERENCE REPORTED 
International Groups Attend Conference 
Tripoli AL-FAJR AL-JADID in Arabic 15 Aug 82 p 4 


[Text] Dr Muhammad Ahmad al-Sharif inaugurated the Second Conference for the 
Islamic Call which is being held in Tripoli for 6 days. Over 150 Islamic 
societies and agencies from all over the world are taking part in this con- 
ference. In addition, a large number of Muslim thinkers and scholars are also 
participating. Dr al-Sharif delivered the inaugural address during which he empha- 
sized the importance of that conference and enumerated the activities that 
Islamic societies have been engaged in ever since their establishment after the 
first conference. A law was issued then by the Revolutionary Command Council 
establishing the Islamic Call Society as a private organization for the public's 
benefit. The society would have access to the Holy War Fund from which it could 
draw to spend on God's cause and to spread Islam all over the earth. 


The society was thus able to achieve the objectives that the first conference had 
set for the Islamic call. It became a universal organization for all Muslims 
wherever they may be. Through it Muslims would offer opinions and assistance and 
through it and through them, they would receive support and assistance. This 
society thus became quite unlike some organizations that work in the area of the 
Islamic calling. These organizations had set themselves up as guardians of Islam 
and Muslims in a manner similar to the institutions of the church. This violates 
the spirit of Islam which is a purely monotheistic religion in which a person 
communicates directly with his creator without guardianship or intercession and 
without clergymen. 


In his address Dr Muhammad Ahmad al-Sharif said that che Second Conference of the 
Islamic Call comes years after this society was established and that it is a new 
turning point in the history of the Islamic call. 


Dr al-Sharif indicated that the purpose of this large Islamic gathering was to 
consider the difficulties surrounding our activities and to make plans for the 
future ot the call to ensure its progress and success despite all the problems 
and challenges we are facing in this age. This is the age during which the 
Islamic Revolution became a reality drawing people to it. Before the outbreak of 
the Great First of September Revolution, an Islamic Revolution had been the dream 
that Muslims everywhere harbored in their hearts.

He went on to add, "Hence come the three focal points around which the debate at 
this conference will take place. These are: the Islamic call in our contemporary 
world; Muslims' problems in this age; and Islam and the challenges of the age. 
The presence of this large number of societies and agencies that are active in 
the area of Islamic calling and [Islamic] scholarship and the presence of this 
number of professors and scholars will enable this conference to come to a 
scholarly and a practical approach to the questions of the Islamic call. The 
conference will be able to make this approach more effective and more capable of 
achieving our objectives." 


Several of the brothers who were taking part in the conference made numerous 
speeches during which they referred to the role that the Islamic Call Society is 
playing to support Islam and Muslims all over the world. 


International Delegations Arrive 
Tripoli AL-FAJR AL-JADID in Arabic 15 Aug 82 p 4 


[Text] Qasim Yatim, minister of labor in the Republic of Mauritius arrived yes- 
terday evening in Tripoli to take part in the Second Conference for the Islamic 
Call. 


In a statement he made to the representative of the al-Jamahiriyah News Agency 
the minister of labor praised the development of bilateral relations that exist 
on all levels between al-Jamahiriyah and Mauritius. 


In a statement [he made] he criticized imperialist, U.S. plans against the 
nations of the African continent. 


He said that the U.S. administration was behind the fact that some African presi- 
dents did not attend the African Summit Conference in Tripoli. 


He strongly conde~ned the barbaric Zionist invasion of Lebanon. 


He said that the aim of the Zionist, racist invasion was to exterminate the 
Lebanese and Palestinian peoples. 


The delegations of the Islamic Mission Society in Zimbabwe also arrived yesterday 
evening in Tripoli. The president of the Islamic societies in Belgium and a rep- 
resentative of the societies in Brussels arrived yesterday evening also. 


Brother Mohamed al-Din, minister of transport in Sri Lanka and the delegation 
accompanying him also arrived at Tripoli International Airport. 


Brother ‘Abdallah al-Wasif, dean of the College of Education in Tunisia also 
arrived to take part in the activities of the conference. Islamic delegations 
from Australia, Malaysia, Japan and South Korea also arrived in Tripoli yesterday 
evening. 


The delegation of the Islamic Call Societies in the Netherlands and the Nigerian 
Islamic delegation also arrived in Tripoli.

ACTIONS, PRACTICES OF GOVERNMENT OFFICIALS ATTACKED 
Tripoli AL-ZAHF AL-AKHDAR in Arabic 26 Jul 82 p 9 
[Article: "A Government and a Black Market" |] 


[Text] When AL-ZAHF AL-AKHDAR raises any question and reveals through it a slew 
of irresponsible practices that cut across several popular channels--or at least 
this is what the newspaper should do--it does so without harboring any feelings 
toward one official in particular and it does so without challenging him. It is 
the cause of the revolution and only the revolution that brings us together [in 
this discussion] and it is also the cause of the revolution that separates us. 


Many secretaries of People's Committees had not met with any of the people who 
had exposed the errors they had committed in office. None of them has any kind of 
feeling [for those officials] that would define some of the courses that this 
criticism will take. The purpose behind this revolutionary exposure is to make a 
comprehensive change in that kind of conduct which the Revolution had eliminated. 
However, this conduct has returned to society by means of a mental attitude found 
in some people who have found their way into positions of responsibility. But the 
old disposition to dominate is still eating away at this mental attitude. 


Some people have tried to make our minds bourgeois; they wanted to transform our 
attitudes into mere masses of greed, plunder and avarice. 


With conditions being what they are, some weak souls tried to steal the people's 
money and to use it to turn themselves into a private new bourgeoisie. A policy 
of dumping goods in the marketplace has contributed to the corruption of tastes 
and stimulated in greedy minds desires for corrupting society. These people thus 
wot their hands on the people's wealth and began padding their bank accounts 
abroad at the expense of the [average] citizen who found himself forced with 
extremely low-quality merchandise. Examples of such merchandise are those clothes 
that were purchased specifically to corrupt the public's taste and to take away 
the people's money without giving them any collective benefits. The least 
punishment for those who imported such materials may be to have them stabbed to 
death with needles in the Green Square. 


Others who shoulder the responsibility for implementation--only implementation-- 
are now legislating and making laws and demands in the name of the people's autho- 
rity. They issue decrees that do not take into account decrees issued by the 
People's Congresses. They scuttle projects, and they create projects. They

dispense millions to some foreign companies as they see fit, and they refuse to 
spend a few dirhams on activities that spread human thought. They follow 
Mussolini's practices in restraining | human] thought. 


Still a third group seems less bold. [People in this group] restrict themselves 
to forming one or several committees through which they make decisions on matters 
not covered by the resolutions of the General People's Congress. The Wage Law, 
‘the Social Security Law, the Real Estate Bank Law and other laws that hurt the 
masses may be evidence of that. 


Others attach importance to the feelings of ministers, the behavior of ministers 
and the receptions for ministers. They attach importance to the soirees of 
ministers where [guests] drown in pools of alcohol, perfume and sweet-smelling 
BMW's. 


We said that when this newspaper exposes such practices, its motivation for doing 
so is not hostility toward those people. The newspaper has no quarrel with those 
people except over the questiagn of the revolution which they are neglecting in 
this manner, with this reactionary method and conduct. If we leave them alone, 
they will destroy the ambitions of the masses and the revolutionary gains they 
have achieved. 


When people who are entrusted and charged with carrying out the resolutions of 
People's Congresses make a decision without involving the masses, that decision 
is one that establishes their mastery and confirms a reactionary mentality. Such 
a decision mocks the masses' resolutions and heeds only the worn-out, inherited 
mental attitudes of those who made it. If these ghosts [from the past] try to 
mislead people and pass their program under slogans and terms that they think 
will titillate the emotions of the masses, such an attempt would be a presump- 
tuous mockery. It would be a misguided attempt that will not be easily bought by 
the masses. Although the masses may try to overlook such matters, that does not 
mean that they do not know what is going on. All practices have now been 
disclosed and exposed to the masses. 


Many secretaries of some People's Committees have turned into legislators and 
contractors in the name of the public interest. Many have turned into brokers in 
the name of preserving the economy. And in the name of the exigencies of the 
phase many have turned into swindlers. 


This wage law could have been issued by the reactionary al-Bakkush cabinet. The 
Social Security Law could have been issued by the parliament of 'Uthman al-Sayd, 
that most erudite gentleman. A_ third [example is that] People's Committee 
secretary who acts as though he were the cashier of Mahmud al-Muntasir and 
compensates some international companies that distorted our projects. A fourth 
divides and distributes Libya's land so that very small farms do not exceed 60 or 
80 hectares. It were as though he were the surveyor of al-Kashik's or Ibn Sasi's 
land. 


A fifth changes work hours: he adds hours [here], and cancels hours [there]. He 
compensates workers, denies workers compensation and suspends workers as though 
he were a director in General Motors or the Ford Corporation.

Another People's Committee secretary issues a decree raising the annual alloca- 
tion for Libyans traveling abroad to 750 Libyan dinars, considering that our 
country is an oil country and Libyans must appear to have the same standard that 
those dollar-laden turbaned Arabs have. However, this increase was soon elimi- 
nated, and allocations were reduced to less than half [that figure] when the 
secretary of the General People's Committee discovered that the 300 Libyan dinars 
are more than what is enough for travel. That sum became a burden on the 
country's budget, particularly when it was learned that those who had traveled 
outside the country in 1981 were only 98,917 persons. They cost the state budget 
74,187,750 dinars. This is in addition to over 4 million dinars in paper currency 
[they took with them] since each citizen is entitled to take 80 dinars in local 
currency on every trip [abroad]. In addition, we can add to this sum almost 80 
million dollars that were purchased from the black market. 


This is what has happened to the country's treasury in 1 year. This is more than 
100 million dinars! 


When j|that person] got knowledge of this matter, he made a decisive and 
unequivocal decision to reduce the sum to less than half [what it was] provided 
that part of the total reductions in these payments be used to compensate the 
Esso Corporation and to pay the cost of private, expeditious and secret missions 
where austerity is such that scores of luxurious cars are prepared, scores of 
private suites [are reserved], servants [are provided], and a collection of 
suitcases | purchased and] loaded with tons of clothing, perfumes and other goods 
that may be foynd in the streets of London, Paris and Rome! 


Those who left al-Jamahiriyah for tourist purposes or to visit someone in the 
period between 1 January and the end of May of this year were 51,457 male and 
female citizens. Most of them went to Italy, Greece, London and Tunisia. They 
took with them 15,437,100 Libyan dinars. 


Besides, they took with them more than twice that sum in hard currency for which 
they paid three times the exchange rate, that is, 45 million dinars. In addition, 
a comparable sum of money or more in Libyan currency was smuggled out of the 
country in secret compartments of suitcases, underneath chairs, in children's 
toys, in underwear and God knows where else! 


Although the people's treasury has taken in more than 50 million dinars from its 
principal source of revenue which is policed by the secretariat, double this 
amount was taken out of the budget underhandedly by their excellencies. This is 
torbidden for the toilers who were simply delighted with the reduction since the 
one responsible for this diabolic decision did open many doors for them. Among 
these doors are [the following]: 


The alleyways of al-Suwayhili Square where the private dollar changers realize 
unimaginable prices in the Public Street Exchange and as for French francs coming 
trom there, don't ask! 


--Outlets for smuggling Libyan dinars to a fraternal country where the Libyan 
dinar is sold dirt cheap. Libyan dinars are sold abroad to obtain currency that 
can be spent locally, that is Libyan dinars are exchanged for the currency of 
that fraternal country, or they are sold to purchase another currency such as the 
franc or the Deutschemark.

There are other black markets there [in that fraternal country] that are more 
popular than the ones we have because of the economic policies that are being 
followed there. The policy of a citizen's allocations for that scene amounts to 
50 Libyan dinars per year. 


--Currency exchange: Here currency is exchanged on the basis of trust. This means 
that a sale is conducted outside the boundaries of the law. A Libyan citizen 
spends some money in one country, and when a citizen of that country comes to 
Libya, he can get [from that Libyan citizen] the Libyan currency he wants so he 
can spend as much as he wishes. The evidence for this can be seen in the 
quantities of merchandise [that one sees| at the points of departure. If one were 
to ask one of those travelers how much money he had brought into Libya, the 
traveler would say that he was visiting. 


We have said nothing about the currency and our Arab Libyan brothers who work in 
our people's embassies. They know how to receive the secretaries, just as they 
know how to receive poor people! 


The decision then is a loss for the Libyan dinar on the one hand and for the citi- 
zen on the other. This citizen finds himself at a loss between patronizing the 
black market and smuggling dinars or selling his integrity and his dignity. In 
all casés the Libyan citizen is anxious and disturbed. Instead of traveling with 
a feeling of confidence and happiness, he is followed by hardship and worry even 
after his vacation is over. In this he is quite unlike those who travel on 
official business and who spend their days resting, enjoying themselves and 
familiarizing themselves with fashion firms. [They stay in] furnished apartments 
[and travel in] different kinds of luxurious cars whose chauffeurs are very very 
good. They stay away from the white markets and the eyes of [curious people]. 


Arab Libyans can thus hecome quite equal. 


We do not really want to make Libyans equal. Equality [implies] good and optimis- 
tic intentions, but it is totally objectionable. The Wage Law's rejection of the 
mental attitude of equality among the people who live here [is due to the fact 
that there must be differences between] Hamidah and Muhammad, and there has to be 
a poor minister. Otherwise how will those of rank in government preserve their 
positions? 


Equality means the fall of reactionaries and exposure of their defeatist inten- 
tions. It also means the removal of all deceptive masks to show those reac- 
tionaries as they really are. 


What if Arab Libyans were to prevent themselves from traveling [abroad]? What if 
they were to give themselves a break from black markets and the temptations of 
Arab reactionaries who set up their deceptive wiles on every street to entrap 
them? 


What if this 300 Libyan dinar [allowance] were finally abolished? We would gain 
currency in the country and the dignity of every Libyan. 


If His Excellency the Prime Minister tries to issue a law, an order or a decree, 
let it be one that is appropriate to the conditions of life outside the country.

himself, all the members of his cabinet and all those poor people who travel on 
arduous and difficult missions to Europe. This particularly applies to those who

the neighborhoods of Abu Salim and al-Nabatiyyah. It applies to those who travel 
to negotiate with the companies that will distribute the newspaper, AL-ZAHF

God has indeed created all kinds of things!
