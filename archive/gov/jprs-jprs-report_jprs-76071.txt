Syria has been striving for a solution to the problem by peaceful means. 
Syrian President Hafiz al-Asad had interfered several times openly and in 
secret to prevent the outbreak of fighting between Morocco and Algeria out 
of solicitude for the unity of the Arabs; for the fact that they would not 
fight with each other; and so that the peoples of the Arab Maghreb would 
focus their attention on the primary question of the Arabs: the question 
of Palestine. But Syria's solicitude has always clashed with the policy 

of the two countries, Morocco and Algeria. This policy has been stated 
repeatedly by officials of both countries and may be summarized [as 
follows}: the question of the Sahara is a question of life and death for 
the two countries. Every time Syria would try to find a solution to the 
crisis with one of the parties, it would be turned over to a second party, 
and the second party would turn it over to a third party that was unac- 
ceptable to the first party, and so on. During its efforts to solve the 
problem, Syria was always trying to maintain its good relations with the 
two countries, especially since the Moroccan army had fought [with Syria] 
in October and Algeria had offered much to Syria." This was how a Syrian 
official summarized Syria's position on the question of the Western Sahara. 


But there was an important development when the Egyptian president visited 
Jerusalem and the Resistance and Opposition Front was formed. Algeria and 
Libya joined the front, but Morocco did not. Afterwards, Syrian-Moroccan 
relations deteriorated noticeably when Damascus was notified about the 
role played by Morocco in arranging the Egyptian president's visit to 
israel. But these relations were relaxed again especially after the 
Moroccan monarch condemned the Camp David Accords and the separate peace 
treaty. 














The parties of the Resistance Front were able to avoid the subject of the 
Sahara during the last three summit meetings in the past 2 years. But 

the Front's fourth summit, which was recently held in Tripoli, happened at 
a time when Arab differences were sharp and the role of the front was almost 
obstructed. Hence came the Syrian resolution to strengthen the front and 
to develop its activities. Each one of the parties in Tripoli had a number 
of embarrassing requests to make to his other allies. In order to avoid 
the failure of the summit, and consequently, the fall of the front, an 
agreement was reached that decisions would be made by majority vote and 
that the minority was to be bound by the decision of the majority. Some 
important decisions were thus made. Among those decisions was one that 
made it impermissible to equate the Soviet Union with the United States, a 
course which some Arab parties were advocating. A majority decision was 
also reached requiring that the Republic of the Sahara under the leadership 
of the Polisario be recognized. Morocco quickly asked Syria for clarifi- 
cations, and Syria notified Morocco that it was committed to the resolu- 
tions of the front; that the step to recognize the Sahara had merely been 
one of the resolutions of the front; and that Syria was also committed to 
its good relations with Morocco. Rabat then summoned its ambassador to 
Damascus for consultations, saying that he would return to [his post] soon 
after he succeeds in convincing his government about the soundness of the 
Syrian position. 


At the same time the Puolisario Front sent a delegation to Damascus, and 
Mr Faruq al-Shar', Syrian minister of state for foreign affairs explained 
the Syrian position to this delegation.

MOSLEM BROTHERHOOD ASSASSINATES COMMUNIST--The Regional Committee of the 
Syrian Communist Party in the governorate of Aleppo announced yesterday 
that a group of people from the destructive Moslem Brotherhood group had 
assassinated Comrade ‘Umar ‘Awwas (Abu Salam), the well-knowwn staff person 
and fighter. In a statement issued yesterday the Syrian Communist Party 
blamed the criminal, reactionary Israeli gangs for the crime of assassina- 
ting the late 'Awwad. The statement added that Comrade 'Awwad “had devoted 
his life to support the questions of the Syrian people and the Syrian 
working class." The statement affirmed that "Criminal and terrorist acts 
will not dissuade the Syrian Communist Party from its movement to struggle 
for the preservation of Syria's progressive political course in the face 

of capitulatory conspiracies that seek to liquidate our national questions 
and hurt the liberation and progress movement. [Text] [Aden 14 OCTOBER
