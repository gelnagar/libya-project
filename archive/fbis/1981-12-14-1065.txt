LIBYA

COMMUNIQUE DENIES AMERICANS IN LIBYA THREATENED 


LD111900 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1806 GMT 11 Dec 81 


[Text] Tripoli, 11 Dec (JANA) -- The People's Committee of the People's Bureau for 
Foreign Liaison today released a communique denying the American administration's 
allegation that the security situation for American citizens in Libya has deteriorated. 
The following is the text of the communique: 


The American administration announced that the security situation in the Jamahiriyah for 
U.S. citizens has deteriorated and that given the danger threatening Americans and the 
increasing risks for them, the American Government has urged them to leave the 
Jamahiriyah as soon as possible. It also made American passports invalid for travel to 
the Jamahiiiyah. 


14 Dec 81 Q2 NORTH AFRICA 


The People's Bureau for Foreign Liaison denies the allegation contained in the U.S. 
communique and emphasizes that nothing in the Jamahiriyah disturbs the security situation 
for foreigners, including American citizens. It also stresses that there is not truth to 
claims that these and other foreign citizens are in danger. 


While welcoming all foreigners, including American citizens, who work in the country and 
praising their role in economic development, the Jamahiriyah points out to all of them 
that their freedom to stay, to visit and to leave is guaranteed and that what they do in 
this regard is entirely up to them. 


On the other hand, the Jamahiriyah sees no reason to poison the atmosphere of the 
excellent relations betreen it and the Americans working in the country. It emphasizes 
that it will continue to look after the security, freedom and safety of all Americans 
residing in the Jamahiriyah for any purpose and also stresses its eagerness to cooperate 
with them whether they choose to stay or opt to leave. 


JANA: U.S. OIL WORKERS INTEND TO STAY IN LIBYA 


LD140852 Tripoli JANA in English 1805 GMT 12 Dec 81 


[Text] Washington, 12 Dec (JAMAHIRIYAH NEWS AGENCY) -- Independent oil analysts and American 
oil company spokesman agreed today that President Reagan's call on American workers to 
leave Libya will not harm the Libyan oil industry. 


The Voice of America radio this morning quoted a top American oil company executive as 
saying: "We should be aware that there are other non-American companies operating in the 
country and that many non-Americans working for U.S. companies in Libya prefer to stay 
there. Libya can maintain its level of currerit oil production without the assistance of 
American technicians. 


A spokesman for "Marathon" oil company also backed the above viewpoint, saying Libya will 
not be affected by the Reagan decision. 


In Washington, the managing director of "Conoco" oil company's affiliate in Libya is 
carrying out its activities there normally with no changes. 


Our correspondent in New York said the directors of some American oil companies with 
affiliates in Libya said in a State Department meeting reported by the "C.B.S." news 
network that there is no threat to the safety of their employees in Libya and that the 
American administration's declarations on the subject are incorrect. They were quoted 

as saying that the Jamahiriyah is eager that these companies continue their activities 

in Libya normally. They told administration officials that American companies will suffer 
as a consequence of Reagan's decision. They said they are certain these companies do not 
wish to withdraw from Libya because their interests there cannot be sacrificed. 


The C.B.S. news programme further included a series of interviews carried out in London 
and Rome with a number of Americans working in Libya who are either on holiday or on 
business in these capitals and who unanimously declared that they are not planning to 
leave Libya as long as Libya continues to grant them leave to remain. They categorically 
said there was no harassment or anything else distrubing their stay in Libya. 


One worker said he has spent six years working happily amongst Libyans. Another U.S. 
specialist added that he had never felt his safety at risk despite the U.S. stances against 
Libya. C.B.S. said that all the employees interviewed stated that they will be returning 
to Libya once their leave or business are concluded and that none is willing to comply with 
Reagan's request.

The CBS correspondent in Bussels said that all U.S. companies working with NATO have also 
categorically objected to the U.S. policy towards Libya and expressed their determination 
to continue normal relations with it. 


In London, UP quoted reliable sources from "Occidental Petroleum", the largest oil company 
in Libya, that the compauy will not advise its employees in Libya to depart. 


In view of press and T.V. interviews with a large number of officials from U.S. oil companies 
reports from Washington indicate that U.S. employees in Libya have decided to stay in Libya 
where they live safely unharassed by anybody. 


Commenting on this from Washington the KUWAITI NEWS AGENCY said all this clearly refutes 
Reagan's allegations regarding the threatened safety of the U.S. citizens in Libya.

AZ-ZAQ'AR ON U.S. BOYCOTT, OPEC CONFERENCE 


GF121131 Manama WAKH in Arabic 0800 GMT 12 Dec 81 


[Text] Abu Dhabi, 12 Dec (WAKH) -- Libyan Petroleum Secretary ‘Abd as-Salam az-Zaq'ar said 
Libya does not care about its boycott by U.S. companies despite the serious situation that 
may result. He declared: "We have not compelled anyone to leave Libya. Libya shoulders 
its responsibilities alone." 


Speaking at a press conference in Abu Dhabi today, Az-Zaq'ar welcomed any Arab or European 
companies that wish to replace the departing U.S. companies. 


Referring to the seriousness of the situation arising from the departure of U.S. companies 
fromLibya, Az-Zaq'ar said: "The situation is not as simple as it appears for there will 
be problems. However, this will not stop us from making efforts. The Jamahiriyah will 
operate its installations and fields by itself, or at least with its friends, who are 
many.” 


Az-Zaq'ar asserted: "Libya believes that the OPEC conference did not succeed." He stressed 
that his country rejects the prices based on differentials and said such a system naturally 
will affect developing countries which will be harmed as result of a reduction in aid given 
to them. 


V. 14 Dec 81 Q 4 NORTH AFRICA 


He said the price issue depends on the oil market and on technical studies and methods. 
He declared: "When we call for just prices this is because we feel we are being preyed 
upon and subjected to pressures from all directions." 


As for OPEC bearing the damages that Libya may suffer as a result of the withdrawal of 
the U.S. companies, the Libyan petroleum secretary said that OPEC should shoulder some 
of the responsibilities. He said: "All OPEC members are liable to meet the same situa- 
tion Libya is facing." 


The Libyan petroleum secretary said that the U.S. hostile stand toward Libya is due to 
Libya's opposition to the anti-Arab policy of the United States. 


The Libyan petroleum secretary said his country will firmly confront the United States 
in theGulf of Sidra even if the color of the Gulf turns red. He pointed out that France 
and Germany support Libya against the U.S. boycott. The Libyan minister said: "We 
believe that should a certain country go astray, other countries will not make the same 
mistake. In that sense, France and Germany will not stand up for the United States 
instance." 


Az-Zaq' ar called on European countries to avoid following in the footsteps of the United 
States, saying: "Europe should be wise enough not to exert pressure on Libya because the 
European countries know the power of the Libyan Arab people." 


The Libyan minister denied that his country's refusal of Tunisia's OAPEC membership had anything 
to do with the continental shelf problem between the two countries. He explained that 

Libya was not prepared for the conference and Libya's stand on Tunisia'’s membership has 

nothing to do with the continental shelf -- despite the fact that Libya was aware of 

Tunisia's application for membership. However, as was mentioned, there are certain 

economic considerations. He also said that his country was surprised by the conference 
adjournment. 


On the supply-and-demand situation inthe international oil market, the Libyan petroleum 
secretary said he expects the demand for OPEC oil to improve during the second half of 
1982 after the series of reductions the members applied during the organization's two 
recent conferences take effect. He expressed optimism concerning increased demand for 
oil next year and said his country will study the situation in the international oil 
market and then decide what to do on prices or production. 


He refrained from elaborating on measures his country will take after the return of U.S. 
companies, asserting that his country has experts in addition to the assistance it 
receives from brothers and friends. 


The Libyan minister said there is no basis for U.S. allegations that Libya wants to kill 
the U.S. President. He said: "We ask the United States to provide any evidence of these 
allegations. 


The Libyan petroleum secretary, who attended the 62d OPEC ministerial conference, warned 
that "Libya will take the measures it deems necessary to protect itself if the United 
States persists in its hostility toward Libya and tries to push it into a corner." He 
said: "It would be wise for the United States not to exert pressure on the Libyan people 
because they know their way very well and enjoy the assistance of all peoples struggling 
for the sake of freedom, including the American people."

ENVOY ASKS UN TO INVESTIGATE U.S. CLAIMS 


NC110943 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 0600 GMT 11 Dec 81 


[Text] The Jamahiriyah UN mission charge d'affaires met yesterday with the Security 
Council president and informed him of the U.S. threats against the Jamahiriyah and 
the premeditated intentions against the Libyan Arab people these threats involve. 


The mission's charge d'affairs emphasized to the Security Council president the 
Jamahiriyah's rejection of all U.S. allegations and claims involving attempts to 
assassinate the U.S. President and some of his aides. He asked in this respect that 
these claims and accusations be investigated, affirming that the Jamahiriyah challenges 
Reagan's administration to present evidence of proof substantiating its false allega- 
tions agains the Jamahiriyah. 


The JANA correspondent has reported that the Jamahiriyah UN mission charge d'affaires 
told the Security Council president that the Jamahiriyah insists on demanding that a 
factfinding committee be formed. He also asked the council to authorize the se retary 
general or his representative to contact the two countires to investigate the accusations 
being leveled against the Jamahiriyah. 


JANA CITES U.S. JOURNALIST'S ALLEGATIONS 


LD121948 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1821 GMT 12 Dec 81 


[Text] Tripoli, 12 Dec (JANA) -- Over the last 2 days, U.S. television broadcast 

a commentary by American journalist and commentator Jack Anderson, who is known for 
his links with the CIA, in which he made false allegations and fabrications over an 
alleged Libyan assassination squad sent to assassinate President Reagan and some of 
his aides. Jack Anderson tried to convey an illusion to his viewers and to convince 
them his claims were true. He presented photographs and names of the members of this 
alleged squad and mentioned their nationalities. 


And, in an arrogant manner, Jack Anderson claimed that the U.S. security authorities 

knew these details and confirmed the existence of this squad. However, he failure of 

the U.S. authorities to produce any evidence or proof corroborating their allegations 
stresses the relevancy of the requests made by Libya to the United Nations and the fact 
that Libya underlined to the Security Council the need for an international investigation 
that will expose to the U.S. public and particularly to world public opinion the fallacy 
of the Reagan administration claims. 


Through hints on the existance of imaginery names of the members of the alleged Libyan 
assassination squad, the U.S. administration tries to mislead U.S. and international 
public opinion. And the simple publicizing of imaginary names and-alleged photographs 
such as the ones presented by Jack Anderson is further material evidence to the lies and 
fallacies of the U.S. allegations. 


The names presented by the American journalist do not exist. They are a figment of the 
U.S. administration's imagination and even more so since the allegations in question 
tendentiously say that some of these names are Iranian, Palestinian and East German. 


To expose these allegations, it suffices here to point out that world opinion knows 
quite well the hostile U.S. positions toward the Palestinian, Iranian and East German 
peoples. 


Hence, we reaffirm that in the face of the failure of the Reagan administration to 
produce any evidence and its continuous blunderings, the Jamahiriyah will continue 
emphatically to ask for an international investigation over the U.S. allegations.
