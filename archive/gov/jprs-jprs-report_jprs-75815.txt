It Exists and History Is the Witness 


Last year, a team of professors from the college of engineering at Cairo 
University and MIT made a trip to the western desert area. Although all 
these professors were specialists in irrigation engineering, they were 
interested in visiting the old tombs and temples in the area. Why? Because 
the drawings on the walls of the temples confirm the existence of an old 
Egyptian civilization dating back thousands of years and this civilization 
could not have existed without water and the Nile is about 300 kilometers 
from the area. Was their water transported? This also is impossible. 

The only logical supposition is that there are large sources of underground 
water used by those who built this civilization. History also relates 

that Cambyse and his army of more than 50,000 Soldiers were in this area 
more than 4 straight days and this large number of men could not have 
survived 4 days without there being underground water sources. History 
confirms to us that the western desert contains a large reservoir of under- 
ground water which we have not yet laid hands on. This is confirmed by 

Dr ‘Abd-al-Wahhab ‘Amir, professor of irrigation engineering at Cairo 
University, who says that there is a strong belief that there is a 

huge reservoir of potable water located at Egypt's border with Libya and 
Sudan at the outer and inner oases, al-Farfarah and al-Bahriyah; the 
existence of this reservoir is confirmed.
