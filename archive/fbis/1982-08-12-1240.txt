LIBYA

COOPERATION AGREEMENT SIGNED WITH UGANDA 11 AUG 


LD111534 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1400 GMT 11 Aug 82 


[Text] Tripoli, 11 Aug (JANA) -- The brother secretary of the People's Committee of 
the People's Bureau for Foreign Liaison and Ugandan Prime Minister Otema Alimadi signed 
the minutes of a bilateral agreement on cooperation here this morning. The minutes 
included the revival of all economic estabishments and cultural and technical coopera- 
tion between the Jamahiriyah and Uganda and bolstering and developing aspects of 
cooperation at various levels in a manner that realizes the aspirations of the two 
peoples. 


UGANDAN PRIME MINISTER, OTHERS DEPART TRIPOLI 


LD111336 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 11 Aug 82 


[Text] Ugandan Prime Minister Otema Alimadi left Tripoli this morning after attending 
the great African conference in the Libyan capital. 


V. 12 Aug 82 Q2 NORTH AFRICA 


In a statement to a JANA correspondent at Tripoli International Airport, he said his visit 
to the Jamahiriyah has achieved positive results that will greatly bolster relations 
between the two countries in various fields. He affirmed the Ugandan people's anxiousness 
to develop and strengthen their relations with the Jamahiriyah people on all levels. 


In conclusion, he expressed regret at the basence of some African countries from the 
great African summit conference -- a matter which wrongs the African people and African 
unity. 


Mohamed Khouna Ould Haydala, chairman of the Military Committee of National Salvation and 
head of state of Mauritania, left Tripoli last night after attending the great African 
summit conference. Didier Ratsiraka, head of government in the Republic of Madagascar, 
and the foreign minister of the Seychelles also left Tripoli yesterday. 


All were seen off by Brother Staff Maj ‘Abd as-Salam Ahmad Jallud at the Tripoli Inter- 
national Airport. 


DELEGATES ARRIVE FOR ISLAMIC VOCATION CONFERENCE 


LD111340 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 11 Aug 82 


[Text] Delegations to the second Islamic vocation conference to be held 14 to 19 August 
in the Jamahiriyah have begun to arrive in Tripoli. During the past 2 days, a large 
number of delegations representing Islamic societies and Islamic thinkers from throughout 
the world have arrived; about 450 will attend. 


The conference will discuss several issues of importance to Islamic vocation and to 
Muslims worldwide; including Islamic vocation and our world today, Islamic vocation and 
Muslim problems in the present age and Islam and the challenges of the age. Several 
studies will be submitted to the conference, dealing with various aspects of Islamic 
thought and Islamic vocation and with finding solutions provided by Islam for the various 
problems of our present age.
