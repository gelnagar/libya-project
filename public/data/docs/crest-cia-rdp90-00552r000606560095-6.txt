Summary (AI): A Los Angeles Times article (reprinted in a CIA reading room file) describes a US plot in which about 20 tons of C4 explosive was shipped to Libya in 1977 for a terrorist training center and arsenal in Tripoli. The center was reportedly set up in the servants' quarters of the former King Idris's Tripoli palace and staffed by former US Green Berets and bomb experts.
• Shipment of 7,500 pounds of C4 was escorted by two Louisiana state troopers to the Texas border, the first leg of a 2,000-mile trip to a desert bunker east of Los Angeles.
• The dealer assembled about $300,000 worth of private stocks of military surplus explosive available in the US and Canada.
• Bombs were camouflaged as ashtrays, lamps, flower vases, alarm clocks, televisions, briefcases and tea kettles.
• The Tripoli center was staffed by American ex-Green Berets, retired US government bomb experts and at least two civilian employees from China Lake Naval Weapons Center.
• Authorities said at least 30 Americans were recruited to train Libyan soldiers and foreign terrorists in covert operations, sabotage and assassination.
• 1977: About 20 tons of C4 explosive, bought by Pomona, California dealer Jerome S. Brower, was secretly shipped to a Libyan arsenal, airlifted from Houston aboard a chartered DC-8 in boxes marked as drilling mud.
• 1978: A terrorist training center in Tripoli was set up and staffed by American ex-Green Berets and retired US government bomb experts.
• 1977-summer: Bombs made by American instructors killed several Libyans and injured three American technicians/instructors.
