LIBYAN CW PLANT AT RABTA
https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp90g01353r001200180001-3
https://archive.org/details/cia-readingroom-document-cia-rdp90g01353r001200180001-3

Summary (AI): CIA memos from late 1988 assess that the Rabta chemical plant (Technology Center near Rabta, southwest of Tripoli) was built for chemical warfare agent production rather than pharmaceuticals. The site is heavily guarded by Libyan soldiers, is protected by several SAM and antiaircraft units, and is administratively linked to the Tajura Nuclear Research Center.
• Precursor chemicals at Rabta could be used for pesticides and dyestuffs but not pharmaceuticals, which Libya claimed was the plant's purpose.
• The plant's corrosion-resistant equipment and precursors indicate production of mustard and sarin agents, per CIA analysis.
• Specially coded badges are reportedly required for access to the Rabta site.
• Air defence at Rabta reportedly includes SA-8, SA-6, SA-3 and SA-2 units plus antiaircraft guns.
• The Rabta Technology Center's metal fabrication section may support surface-to-surface missile work and armored vehicle repair.
• Analysts believe Qadhafi might give chemical agents to an Arab country facing an immediate threat, but Tripoli would likely retain control over their use.
• 1988-11-17: Lieutenant General Colin L. Powell raised questions about Rabta at a meeting with the Director of Central Intelligence.

MEMORANDUM FOR: Director of Central Intelligence 

VIA: Deputy Director of Central Intelligence 
FROM: Richard J. Kerr 

Deputy Director for Intelligence 
SUBJECT: Rabta Chemical Warfare (CW) Production 

Facility in Libya 

1. Action: None. In reference to the questions raised by 

Lieutenant General Colin L. Powell at your meeting on November 17, 1988, 
the following information is provided: 

a. We believe it unlikely that the chemical warfare . 
production facility located at the Technology Center near Rabta 
was intended for a purpose other than chemical warfare (CW) agent 
production. The types of precursor chemicals and equipment at the 
site--when viewed in total--can indicate only CW agent, i.e., 
mastard and sarin, production. Any one of the precursor 
chemicals, by themselves, or the equipment on its own could be 
used to argue the point that the facility has a legitimate 
purpose. The precursor chemicals present at the plant could be 
used legitimately to manufacture pesticides and dyestuffs, but not 
pharmaceuticals, which the Libyans contend is its true purpose. 
However, we believe that the combination of the chemical warfare. 
agent precursor chemicals present and the corrosion resistant 
equipment are indicative of CW agent production. In addition, the 
Rabta facility is over-designed to serve as a pharmaceutical 
plant. 

b. Another strong indication of CW agent production is the 
security at the site which far exceeds that required for a 
commercial chemical plant. The site is guarded by Libyan 
soldiers, and specially coded badges are reportedly required for 

access. In terms of air defense, this is probably now the most 

heavily defended site in Libya. 
there are SA-8, SA-6, SA-3 and SA-2 units, 
antiaircraft artillery guns located nearby. In addition, the site 
is supported by SA-5s at Al' Azizyah.

c. Qadhafi traditionally has acquired more military equipment 
than his armed forces can absorb or use mainly because he views 
Libya as an arsenal to supply the Arab militaries in the next war

modern, sophisticated arsenal. We also believe that Qadhafi might 
sell or give chemical agents/munitions to an Arab country facing 
an immediate security threat if he believed doing so would greatly 
enhance Libyan influence with that government. It is unlikely, 
but still possible, that Qadhafi would supply a terrorist group 
with chemical agents because we believe he would view the risk of 
association with a chemical terrorist attack, and the danger of 2 
severe retaliation, as too great. Moreover, we believe Qadhafi is :
