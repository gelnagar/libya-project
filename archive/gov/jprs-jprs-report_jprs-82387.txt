AGRICULTURAL PRODUCTION TARGETS UP TO 1985 DETAILED 
Tripoli AL-FAJR AL-JADID in Arabic 20 Sep 82 p 5 
(Article: "Mismanagement Obstructs Self-Sufficiency/ 


[Text/ An urgent question is raised as to whether the Jamahiriyah has achieved 
self-sufficiency tin the production of wheat, barley, meats and other foods. 
Despite the bitterness of having to raise such a question, it is a legitimate 
question to raise for several important and serious reasons: 


First, the Jamahiriyah as a people knows well that it is a society targeted 
from all sides and that imperialism will not hesitate to apply various kinds 
of political, economic and military pressures on it. 


Second, confronting the terrorist United States objectively requires produc- 
tion bases at the level of the confrontation to counter the use of economic 
pressures. 


Third, attaining the level of self-sufficiency is something tied to the new 
revolutionary transformation strategy because raising production to maximum 
levels is being done under the banner of the new ideological theories of the 
great 1 September revolution--the revolution of partners, not hirelings. 


Fourth, there is no independence for people who get their food from abroad. 


From the above, the question of self-sufficiency seems legitimate, especdally 
since 13 years have passed since the great revolution. All the development 
programs abounding in the development plans since the revolution's inception 
assert time and again that we will achieve self-sufficiency immediately. But 
our aspirations, it seems, have been no more than programs that may or may not 
be realized. On the conclusion of the preceding 5-year plan, namely, the 
1976-80 plan, we found out that we had not achieved self-sufficiency in wheat, 
barley or other strategic commodities. We have been surprised to find that 
self-sufficiency has not even been achieved in the production of honey and 
that there is a large shortage of this commodity, which has compelled the Pub- 
lic Markets Company to import it from abroad. We find this even though the 
Agriculture and Agricultural Reclamation Secretariat repeatedly declared that 
self-sufficiency in this commodity would be attained by 1980 and that there 
would be a surplus to export! 














The observer of the revolutionary media in the Jamahiriyah finds that for 13 
years the thousands upon thousands of farms, reclaimed hectares of land and 
food plants (that were to be built in succession through the various phases 
of production) have not been built. The Libyan Arab citizen remembers well 
the documentary films shown by the television day after day and pertaining to 
mammoth agricultural production projects. Yet, the bitter and weighty ques- 
tions raised are: 


Where is self-sufficiency in wheat and barley? Where are the food commodities 
in which self-sufficiency was to be achieved as has been asserted? How long 
will we continue to import these essential goods that drain our economy and 
whose continued importation creates agap in the structure of a society of 
revolutionary confrontation and defiance? 


The answer to these questions lies in the following: 
Mismanagement in the production units, 

Poor marketing and distribution. 

Poor supervision and follow-up. 


This is the real problem preventing the Jamahiriyah from achieving self- 
sufficiency. The scientific production reports say that the wheat, barley and 
vegetables produced by the farms are enough to meet the needs of the Jamah- 
irivah and even exceed these needs. But poor distribution, management and 
marketing is what is obstructing the attainment of our self-sufficiency goals. 


This requires the exercise of a form of guided sternness in this regard. The 
situation has no mercy and hunger has no mercy. 


Within the framework of explaining the revolutionary programs being imple- 
mented to achieve the goal of self-sufficiency, the following is a brief re- 
view of some aspects of the new revolutionary 1981-85 5-year plan. Let us 
start with the programs and the projects. 


The 1981-85 Plan 

Programs and Projects 

Agricultural reclamation and land development work in the 1981-85 plan is 
characterized by focusing on well-studied programs that seek to realize 
agricultural work targets in thespheres by underlining the methods and means 
capable of achieving these targets in addition to proposing solutions for 


tackling and dealing with the expected problems in projects of the 1976-80 
transformation plan.

First, the land reclamation and development program: 


This program seeks to complete the existing land reclamation and development 
projects through the reclamation and development of 69,089 hectares of

land reclamation and development projects in the areas of Sahl al-—Jifarah, 
al-Jabal al-Akhdar, al-Sulul a]—Khudr, Fezzan, al-Kafrah and al-Sarir, with a

Besides completing the existing programs, there is a number of new projects,

Third {mo second--as published/, the Grain Cultivation Program: 


One of belief in the importance of cultivating and producing grains in the 
Jamahiriyah and of increasing the production rates of grains to reach self- 
sufficiency in the shortest time possible, a decree was issued in April 1980 
by the General People's Committee to establish the Public Grain Production 
Authority. This authority seeks to develop che national economy by enhancing 
grain production to achieve self-sufficiency. The authority is the main agency 
concerned with implementing the transformation plan in this sphere. It issues 
the grain production projects, conducts studies and research to develop new 
areas fit for the cultivation of grains and exerts efforts to supply and 
utilize all of the requirements for production, such as agricultural equipment 
and machinery, seeds, pesticides and means of transportation, and to gather 

and prepare data concerning grain production and the local markets‘ grain needs. 


During the years of the plan, the grain cultivation program has three main 
goals, namely: 


1. Raise total wheat production from nearly 19,500 tons in 1980 to nearly 
428,800 tons in 1985 to meet 75.4 percent of the local demand. 


2. Raise total barley production from nearly 71,500 tons in 1980 to nearly 
105,600 tons in 1985 to meet all of the local demand. 


3. Cultivate 120,000 hectares of dry land farms with (al-maydak) annually 
for use in feeding 240,000 head of sheep. 


The lands cultivated with grains, both the lands irrigated regularly and lands

In the Sphere of Animal Production Projects: 


Cattle-Breeding Projects: 


This project seeks to produce fresh milk throughout the year and to help 
achieve self-sufficiency in dairy and meat supplies. 


The project's accomplishments since its foundation are summed up as follows: 


Completion of a cattle farm, which is now in full operation. Other farms 
are under construction. 


The number of cattle bred at the project is 16,955. 
Milk production in 1981 amounted to 26.8 million liters. 
Milk production in the first 6 months of 1982 amounted to 13.7 million liters. 


The project's average annual sale of calves and cows is 5,000 head, the equiva- 
lent of 1,000 tons of meat. 


Sheep-Breeding Project: 


The project seeks to continue the experiments and research on raising the 
meat production of local strains of sheep by raising the percentage of twins 
they produce and increasing the weight of lambs at birth and on weaning 
through interbreeding with improved foreign strains and natural birth in the 
local strain. 


Enhancing the productivity of sheep through hereditary improvement of the local 
strain through selection amd exclusion. 


Regulating and modifying the methods of breeding to achieve a vertical increase 
from sheep, such as applying the system of triple births every 2 years to pro- 
duce a larger number of lambs and selecting superior-quality rams and dis- 
tributing them to breeders to raise the productivity of their herds. 


Poultry-Breeding Project: 


This project seeks to supply white poultry meat for the table, to achieve self- 
sufficiency in poultry products, to supply 200 eggs per citizen annually and 

to raise the proportion of poultry meat to other meats so that poultry meat will 
meet 60 percent of the Jamahiriyah's meat needs by 1985. 


The following accomplishments have been scored in the project since its founda- 
tion: Completion of 6 fattening plants with a capacity of 1.6 million birds 
annually, a turkey farm with a capacity of 40,000 birds annually and 2
