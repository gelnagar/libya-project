[Text] The bloody scenes in Tripoli are not new. They go back at least 7 
years and therefore coincide with Beirut and the rest of the Lebanese 
cities and regions, some of which have been subjected to hostile Israeli 
campaigns and others of which have been subjectéd to things more intense 
and worse, that is, internecine fighting, slaughters among people of the 
Same group or people who stand in the same trench or those who occupy the 
same position and so on to include people of every affiliation possible 


over the last 8 years. 


However, what has happened and what is now happening in Tripoli, particu- 
larly since the beginning of the new year, exceeds all imagination in terms 
of killing, suffering and destruction and exceeds that which Beirut was 
exposed to after the Israeli invasion in terms of blood, rubble and echoes 
of grief in its streets and districts which came from the houses in which 
children, women and old people are piled. 


If the tragedy of Beirut is associated with "steadfastness" in the face of 
the invading, occupying Israelis, something which lightened the burden and 
helped Beirut bear its wound with patience and dignity, then the tragedy of 
Tripoli is associated with "negligence" of certain families who insist that 
the capital of northern Lebanon remain a bloody field and a place open to 


hatred and the settling of scores with various heavy weapons which kill 
people, most of them children, innocents, active businessmen, laborers and 


those who are forced to leave their homes seeking food for a family shielded 
from society but exposed to artillery and gunfire. 


Things took place in Tripoli in a few hours which did not take place in 

some European cities during World War II, and this is not a random distor- 
tion but a fact and a reality attested to by the streets of the city and 
demonstrated by the cameras which were able to move about among the violent 
battles which, in the beginning, took two axes, the Ba'l Muhsin axis and 

the Bab al-Tabanah axis, then began to develop and expand so that no quarter 
or passageway in Tripoli and its outskirts was outside the reach of gunfire. 


As the regions of Ba'l Muhsin and Bab al-Tabanah became well-known as the 
two battle fronts and for having confrontation lines between them, so other 


46 








common streets, quarters and squares gained notoriety for being explosive 
locations though they were originally innocent of the game of bloodshed. 
And so to the black list were added the names of places unknown except to 
Tripolians, such as ‘Aqabat al-Zahid, 'Aqabat al-Mufti, Tal'at al-Khuri, 
Tal'at al-Rifa'iyah, and Khan al-Sabun in addition to the names of major 
locations known by the people of northern Lebanon and northern Syria such 
as al-Tal Square, Sha'ir "Azmi, Abi Samra‘, al-Mina' Road, al-Mayyitayn 
Road, Abu-"Ali Boulevard, Cedar Street, Army Street, Syria Street and 
American Square. 


The names of these places became prominent on the list of the war because 
they had become, intentionally or accidentally, the targets of missiles and 
rockets exchanged between "brothers in arms" who are experts in the game of 
firing weapons but who had missed the target intentionally or accidentally. 
The result is that small buildings fall on their residents and the dead and 
injured fall in the streets and at the entrances to houses and behind 


windows. 


One night last week the city of Tripoli was engulfed in a real hell. The 
electricity was off due to the lack of fuel which was nearly impossible to 
transport to the generating plants because of the fighting. Heat was 
limited because of the exhaustion of combustibles such as gas, heating oil 
and benzene and the result was that food had to be eaten sparingly and 
those who did not have provisions lost the necessary nutrition because 
shops and businesses were closed and fruit and vegetable sellers had dis- 
appeared because they had fled with their wagons and displays and the 


citrus and other harvests were sitting and rotting. 


With this situation Tripoli was struck by storms the likes of which had not 
been seen for years. The streets were flooded with rain water and the city 
was hit with north and east winds carrying icy snows to the nearby moun- 
tains and hills. Thousands of families were huddled in the dark but it 

was not long before the city's skies lit up with the fire of artillery and 
missiles and the houses started to be hit with rockets from top to bottom. 
Men, women and children tried to defend themselves in the dark, groping for 
safe passage to sanctuary on the ground floor or in the basement. Those 
standing behind the artillery and the machineguns knew where they were and 
where some of their enemies were so they began to storm the houses one by 
one. One can imagine the condition of the residents of the buildings 

which were subjected to storming by grenades and rpg's. 


The miserable night was followed by a more miserable day. The people of 
Tripoli were kept from sleeping during the night and moving about during the 
day. Some of those who risked moving about because they had to receive 
their share. The scenes were terrible. In the middle of the streets and 

on the sidewalks were dead who could not even be approached for identifica- 
tion. There were injured bleeding to death to whom no voices were raised 
and whom no ambulances approached. There were no telephones working to 
inform someone of where the injured were. Then occasionally a car wouid 
pass and some would try to stop it only to discover that it was already 


carrying two dead bodies in the trunk. 





These are only some of the things afflicting Tripoli. It is as if it is a 
movie script aboi a city not found in Lebanon or as if the director of the 
movie and the write. of the script wanted to strip the city from Lebanon 
after eliminating it and killing it physically and morally. But the script 
is real, written with blood and gunpowder and the city nicknamed the 
"fragrant city" has become a hell trying to cry to the universe for help 
but its voice is strangled and the tragic psychological situation explodes 
again between families. During recent days nervous breakdowns have 
appeared and it has become a common occurrence for people to hear a scream 
in their neighbor's house or in the street coming from a woman who has lost 
one of her sons or from another woman who has lost all hope in escaping 


the turmoil. 


During the times in which the artillery was quiet and replaced by rifle 

fire groups of mothers and fathers would head for the house of (former) 
Prime Minister Rashid Karami to ask him to do something. One day Prime 
Minister Karami was chairing a meeting of the coordination committee which 
was held to deal with the situation in the city when the areas of the fight- 
ing erupted violently and rockets and missiles fell from all directions 
around Karami's house. The discussion stopped and all present turned to 

the gentleman and in his eyes was one question, "What can be done and who 

is able to stop the ‘blood bath' in Tripoli?" 


With all that is known about Karami, his strong nerves, his calmness, 
patience and ability to bear hardships and surprises, perhaps what he 
should have done at that time was advise those around him to go to a safe 
place in the house. However, this advice was not forthcoming. Instead, 
he picked up the phone and called the commander of the deterrent forces in 
the north, General Sulayman al-'Isa, and asked him to do something to stop 
the random bombing which was killing innocent people and forcing families 
in the area to flee and disperse in any direction where they imagined that 


they would be safe. 


The commander of the deterrent forces promised that he would try to honor 
the request but the situation continued at its previous level. Some of 
those present said to Prime Minister Karami, "We must get in touch with 
President Hafiz al-Asad and Abu-'Ammar and any other Arab leader who can 
decisively affect the battle front which is including undeclared targets 
and has become a disgrace to any Tripolian and any other Lebanese citizen." 


Because of this all groups in Tripoli have raised a unified voice calling 
for the evacuation from the city and its outskirts of the deterrent forces 
and all armed organizations, Lebanese and non-Lebanese, and the handing 
over of security to the legitimate government, i.e., the army and the 
interna] security forces. They have done this with the knowledge that 
achieving this will be a difficult matter during the current phase for a 
number of internal and external reasons. This is what (former) Prime 
Minister Amin al-Hafiz implied in his request for any legitimate force 
which is capable of imposing security in Tripoli even if it is the multi- 
national force.

occasion Dr Amin al-Hafiz claimed that there was a need for the multi- 
national forces to enter Tripoli. Many segments of Tripoli backed him in 
this request. The people of the city say that legitimate security in 
Tripoli is a Lebanese responsibility to be taken on by the internal security 
forces who currently have the roads blocked in front of them and are sur- 
rounded in their centers under the threat of being bombed. And so it is 
with some groups of the army who are surrounded and prevented from inter- 
vening under penalty of destruction. Among the most recent victims in 
Tripoli was a Lebanese officer named Niqula Sawwan who was killed in the 
street and whose body remained there for some time before an ambulance was 
able to take it to a military hospital. 


Security in Tripoli is also a Syrian responsibility to be undertaken by 
the deterrent forces who are clashing with a number of local organizations 
the foremost being the "24 October Movement" and the "Popular Resistance." 
These organizations call for the deterrent forces to be relieved of their 
security mission in Tripoli and to withdraw to outside the city so that the 
legitimate Lebanese authorities can assume their mandatory powers and their 
responsibilities. 


But with this reality Prime Minister Karami faces a delicate situation. On 
the one hand he realizes the importance of the Syrian role in Tripoli 
specifically and in Lebanon generally. On the other hand he cannot ignore 
the positions of the local organizations who are operating in a different 
direction. Despite this, Karami confronted the two difficult matters and 
headed for Damascus last week to meet with President Hafiz al-Asad and 
inform him of the reasons that the fuse of the situation in Tripoli remains 
lit despite all the efforts which have been exerted and despite all the 
measures which have been agreed upon and announced, the most recent being 
the decisions taken at a meeting attended by a high-level delegation headed 
by Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister 'Abd-al-Halim Khaddam and 


Defense Minister General Mustafa Talas. 


The odd aspect of the matter is that the dangerous decline which Tripoli 
has witnessed during the last week came after the visit of the Syrian 
delegation to the city and after the measures whose implementation was 
agreed upon. Another Syrian delegation was scheduled to arrive in the 
northern capital to examine the reasons for the developments which have 
taken place. However, the weather conditions along with the security 
conditions prevented the delegation's arrival so Prime Minister Karami 
was forced to travel to Damascus. 


Observers said that Karami exerted efforts with numerous quarters in order 
to ensure a period of security during which he could leave the city on a 
specific route. All of the city's entrances and exits were closed and it 
was as if Tripoli were sentenced to live in an isolated prison cell sur- 
rounded with fire while inside it volcanoes of violence were erupting and 


blood was being spilled with impunity.

A large segment of Tripolians do not agree on the existence of a conspiracy

Also, the secret and sometimes public struggle between Syria and the PLO
