Summary (AI): A 1982 CIA memo on U.S. imports of Libyan crude oil shows imports falling from 704 thousand barrels/day in 1977 to about 134 thousand in late 1981 and probably under 100,000 b/d by early 1982. It discusses substitute suppliers and Libya's options for selling crude in Europe. Nothing here concerns Tripoli itself.
• Libyan crude oil imports to the U.S. were 704 (thousand b/d presumed) in 1977, 638 in 1978, 642 in 1979, 546 in 1980.
• Libyan imports fell to 154 in September 1981, 136 in October, 134 in November and 122 in December.
• Libyan imports probably fell under 100,000 b/d by early 1982, less than 5 percent of total U.S. imports.
• The U.S. could easily replace Libyan crude with similar-quality crude from Nigeria, Algeria, or the North Sea.
• Omani crude is not of the same quality as Libyan crude and cannot replace Libyan supplies.
