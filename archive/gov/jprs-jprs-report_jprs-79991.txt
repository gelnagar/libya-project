Military Towns 


It's only fair to note that experience thus far has proven that the military 
threat presented by the Saudis is less than that from Libya, Morocco or 
Algeria. In all past wars, except the Sinai Campaign in 1956, the Saudis par- 
ticipated actively withexpeditionary forces (in 1948 with three units under 
Egyptian command; in 1967 with a division fighting in Jordan; in 1973 with a 
reinforced division in Syria). In all cases the Saudis experienced military 
defeat. 


The Saudi army today numbers 40,000. It is under the minister of defense and 
aviation with a general staff comprised of four branches--intelligence, man- 
power, operations and training and logistics. Most of the military units are 
located in the military town of Tabuq, only 200 kilometers frum Eilat. The 
town merited a once-over this week by the Israeli Air Force. Another portion 
of the Saudi military units is stationed in the south of the oil kingdom, its 
purpose, among others, to defend the borders with the Republic of South Yemen 
and Yemen. Yet another section of military units are permanently positioned 
in eastern Saudi Arabia, their responsibility being to defend the oil fields 
and the borders with Iraq.

camps with their modest barracks). The soldiers in these Saudi towns work
