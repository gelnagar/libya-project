Summary (AI): A 1981 London Daily Telegraph article, reporting on a New York Times investigation, says Western pilots and mechanics were recruited through a London office on Knox Street to fly and maintain Libya's air force aircraft. It names Wilson, a former CIA figure who had moved to Tripoli, and a London firm called Brilhurst. Tripoli is mentioned only as Wilson's base, not as a site of specific events.
• Brilhurst was a London service company that handled recruitment and paid travel expenses and salaries for pilots working in Libya.
• Two Wilson-linked recruiting companies were named: OST (a Swiss corporation) and Western Recruitment Inc.
• Wilson was wanted in the United States on a federal indictment for illegally shipping explosives to Libya to help train terrorists.
• Libya's C-130 Hercules fleet and American-built CH-47 Chinook helicopters were acquired from the United States before relations broke down.
• 1980: Libyan air force lost two Russian-supplied fighters in a dogfight over the Gulf of Sidra against two American fighters from the carrier Nimitz (reported as occurring 'last summer'; article is from 1981, so likely summer 1981).
