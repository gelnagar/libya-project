Summary (AI): A Washington Post article from 8 December 1981 reports that President Reagan said the US had evidence Libya sent assassins to kill senior US officials, and that the administration weighed economic options against Libya, including an oil boycott. It mentions a Heidelberg ambush on Gen. Kroesen and about 1,500 US citizens remaining in Libya.
• Reagan said the US had evidence Libya sent assassination teams to the United States; he did not publicly produce it.
• Reagan convened a National Security Council meeting where economic options against Libya were discussed.
• Options included an embargo of Libyan oil, which would require British, French, Italian and Japanese cooperation; chances of such agreement were judged slight.
• British cooperation alone could temporarily close Libyan oil fields by withdrawing American and British technicians.
• About 1,500 US citizens, mostly oil workers and their dependents, remained in Libya.
• Six US oil companies operated in Libya; the State Department had advised US citizens to leave Libya the previous summer.
• 1981-09: Ambush attack with rocket-propelled grenades and gunfire damaged the armor-plated car of Gen. Frederick J. Kroesen, commander of US Army forces in Europe; he was not seriously injured. Linked to West German terrorists.
