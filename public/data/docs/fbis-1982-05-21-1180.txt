LIBERATION FRONT OFFICIAL INTERVIEWED ON AIMS 


LD021700 London ASH-SHARO AL-AWSAT in Arabic 20 May 82 p 3 


[Unnamed ASH-SHARQ AL-AWSAT correspondent interview with former Libyan prime minister 
and oppositionist 'Abd al-Hamid al-Bakkush in Khartoum -- date not given 


[Excerpts] ‘Abd al-Hamid al-Bakkush, the Libyan lawyer who once was in power, is now 
secretary general of the Front for the Liberation of Libya which is led by Bashir ar- 
Rabiti, chairman of the Federal National Assembly during the Egyptian-Libyan-Syrian unity. 
"Abd al-Hamid al-Bakkush occupied several ministerial posts and became Libyan prime minister 
during the King as-Sanusi era. Later he became his country's ambassador in Paris, 

and on his return to Tripoli after Al-Qadhdhafi's coup in September 1969 he was arrested 
several times. 


ASH-SHARQ AL-AWSAT interviewed him in Khartoum during a visit to Sudan, where he was 
attending the exhibition on Libyan sabotage in Africa. The interview follows: 


[Question] What are the objectives of the Front for the Liberation of Libya? 

[Answer] Its immediate objective is to remove Al-Qadhdhafi's regime, which is 
oppressing the Libyan people, and to unite the Libyan people in order to establish a 
democratic regime in which there will be no room for autocratic rule. A regime that is 
based on a constitution and institutions that protect freedom and enable the people to 
elect their rulers and lay down laws in a civilized manner. 

[Question] Does this mean you are in favor of a multiparty system? 

[Answer] Yes, wecall for political parties. 

[Question] How will you achieve these objectives? 

[Answer] First, we must bring the Libyans living abroad together and organize them for 


resistance at home and enable them to confront the colonel's regime. We believe our 
basic task is to organize, bring together and help resistance inside Libya.

We have our means and contacts with the resistance inside the country. We do not claim 
we have become big or a force that can change the regime now, but we are sure of standing 
fast until victory. There is great resistance at home and there is public grumbling, but 
there is a lack of organization. We do not need to convince the public of the need for 
Al-Qadhdhafi's regime to be removed, for Al-Qadhdhafi's bloody system has convinced the 
people of that. 


[Question] How do you evaluate Ai-Qadhdhafi's position internally? 


[Answer] Al-Qadhdhafi has made himself repulsive to the people, and he is therefore helping 
us to achieve our objectives as early as possible. He hangs innocent people without trial 
and makes unpopular decisions, such as abolishing free enterprise, changing the currency, 
halving the wages of workers and employees and abolishing private ownership. His decisions 
stem from an idea that has dominated his thinking, namely, that he is above the people, 

and so he does not care whether the public is pleased or angry. 


[Question] What is Libya's current international position? 


[Answer] On the regional level nobody trusts him, and Libya's name has become synonymous 
with everything evil. Some rulers appease him either out of greed for his money or fear 

of his terror. This state of affairs applies to Africa and the Arab area. Even the Soviets 
themselves do not trust him, but they use him in their operations in Africa, first in 

order to finance such operations and, second, to reflect their views, which they cannot 
express directly to the peoples. 


[Question] What kind of relationship is there between the Front for the Liberation of Libya 
and the Front for Libyan Salvation? 


[Answer] There is no relationship between the two organizations, and we differ from them. 
[Question] What are your differences? 

[Answer] There are in fact neither differences nor agreement. In the final analysis any 
Libyan working against Al-Qadhdhafi's regime is, in our view, performing a sacred national 
duty. We have not considered uniting with them. Their method is different from ours and 
diversity could enrich the Libyan opposition movement. Our action stems from Cairo, and we 
have offices in Morocco, Sudan and Somalia. But we could move our headquarters to Khartoum.
