Summary (AI): A 1982 Washington Post article reports that Douglas Schlachter, an aide to former CIA agents Edwin Wilson and Francis Terpil, pleaded guilty to conspiring to supply explosives and train commandos for Libyan leader Qaddafi. Wilson was reported living in Tripoli, and Terpil was a fugitive.
• Schlachter faces a maximum of seven years in prison or $110,000 in fines, or both; no sentencing date set.
• Wilson and Terpil were indicted in April 1980 and again in August 1981 with Schlachter over a 1976 contract to supply explosives, high-technology weapons and training to the Libyan government.
• Eleven deputy US marshals were posted inside the courtroom during the plea hearing.
• The CIA said in January 1982 it could find no documentary evidence of an official relationship between Schlachter and current or former agency officials.
• 1982-02-12: Douglas Schlachter pleaded guilty in US District Court to conspiring with Wilson and Terpil to supply explosives and train commandos for Libya, and to violating federal explosives shipment laws
