Summary (AI): A 1980 Washington Post article (reproduced in CIA reading room) reports U.S. intelligence and Justice Department findings that Billy Carter, the president's brother, received $220,000 from the Libyan government, and congressional calls for investigation. It concerns Libya's foreign influence efforts in the United States rather than places inside Libya.
• Billy Carter received $20,000 in January and $200,000 in April 1980 from the Libyan government, according to the article's account.
• Billy Carter was forced to register as a foreign agent after the payments were publicly disclosed.
• Libyan diplomats were expelled from the U.S. in early May 1980 over an alleged intimidation campaign against opponents of Qaddafi.
• Libyan Foreign Minister Ali Turyaki said at a July press conference in Nicosia that Libya established relations with the president's brother to maintain contact with the American people.
• 1980-06-11: Billy Carter acknowledged receiving the $220,000 from the Libyans at the Justice Department and later met National Security Adviser Zbigniew Brzezinski.
• 1980-04: Qaddafi publicly called for the assassination of opponents of his revolution living abroad, according to the article.
