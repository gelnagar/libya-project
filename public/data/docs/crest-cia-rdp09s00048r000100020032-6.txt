Summary (AI): A 1982 Chicago Tribune article reports a federal indictment of a Northbrook, Illinois firm (Tencom), two of its officers, and a Libyan Air Force colonel for illegally exporting aircraft parts and equipment to Libya in violation of U.S. export laws. The colonel allegedly flew a Libyan government Jetstar, used by top officials including Qaddafi, to the U.S. and West Germany to place orders.
• Tencom was founded in 1978 by Nedim Sulyak, a native of Turkey.
• The indictment has 30 counts and charges violations of federal export laws.
• Since 1978 the U.S. has banned shipments of such equipment to Libya, according to the article.
• Tencom reportedly sent 60 shipments of parts worth $2.5 million, including parts for C-130 military cargo planes and L-100 aircraft.
• The federal government began investigating Tencom after receiving an anonymous letter with a packing slip for a parts shipment.
• 1982-07: Federal indictment of Tencom, two officers and a Libyan Air Force colonel on 30 counts of violating federal export laws in a sales scheme to Libya.
