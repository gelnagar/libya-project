LIBYA

JUSTICE SECRETARY DOES NOT WANT WAR WITH EGYPT 


LD180856 Kuwait KUNA in Arabic 0730 GMT 18 Oct 81 


[Text] Kuwait, 18 Oct -- Today it was reported here that Libyan Secretary of Justice 
Muhammad Abu Al-Qasim Az-Zuwayy said that his country does not wish to enter into war with 
its two neighbors Egypt and Sudan. He blamed the Sudanese Government for escalating 
tension with Libya. 


The Kuwait paper AL-WATAN quoted Az-Zuwayy as saying the aim behind this escalation by Sudan, 
which increased after the assassination of Egyptian President Anwar as-Sadat, was to 
reinstate Chadian Defense Minister Hissein Habre, who was defeated by the forces of 
President Guokouni Ouddei with large Libyan military support at the end of last year in 
Ndjamena. 


He said the Libyan forces in Chad drove out pro-Harre forces from three villages along the 
Sudanese border at the beginning of this month in the wake of an assault by the Libyan 
forces backed by fighter aircraft. 


Earlier this month Sudan accused Libya of strafing Sudanese villages on the border with Chad. 
Sudanese President Ja'far Numayri also accused Libya of making preparations to invade this 
country. President Numayri expresed his readiness to make Sudan a base for what he described 
as joint punitive actions against Libya by the United States, Egypt and Sudan and he called 
on America to speed up the supply of arms. 


Az-Zuwayy said the only dispute between Khartoum and Tripoli is over the question of 
Habre. He added that Libya does not wish to wage a war with Egypt. He said decisions 
have been made by the Libyan people's congresses to "prevent any confrontation between 
the Libyan and Egyptian armies." 


However, he explained that the only condition for reconciliation with Egypt is for it to 
abandon the Camp David accords and the peace treaty with Israel. He called on the Arab 
countries to "place all their resources at Egypt's disposal because it is not proper for 

the Arab nation to remain a bystander to America's avid assault on Egypt in order to make it 
follow the Camp David line, and we must avidly launch ourselves on Egypt to restore it 

to its Arab nation.

JANA EDITOR COMMENTS ON ARTICLE IN CAIRO PAPER 


LD181558 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1450 GMT 18 Oct 81 


[Text] Tripoli, 18 Oct (JANA) -- In an article disseminated today by MENA, Cairo's 
AL-JUMHURIYAH affirmed Egypt's adherence to the Arab right in Palestine and to the 
realization of the legitimate rights of the Palestinian people to self-determination. 
The paper added that Egypt believes that every party must assume responsibility for its 
cause and that Egypt's role will only be to help the Palestinian people retrieve their 
legitimate rights. 


Commenting on this, JANA's political editor points out that these statements by 
AL-JUMHURIYAH help to restore confidence in Egypt's return to the Arab fold and its 
assumption of its pan-Arab role of shouldering the burden of the struggle for Palestine 
and other causes. The paper's comments, he adds, have great political significance 
indicating a new line being pursued in Egypt following As-Sadat's death. This line has 
begun to crystallize in a manner which calls for satisfaction and reaffirms Egypt's Arab 
character and the ability of the Egyptian people to continue to struggle in the front 
trenches for the cause of Palestine, the Palestinians’ right to self-determination and 
the Arab nation's commitment to support that right through the continuation of the 
struggle to affirm it in a practical manner. 


The paper's clear signs, following As-Sadat's death, affirm that his political line was 
wrong, particularly as it caused Egypt to lose its leading role. These signs also indi- 
cate another fact, a vital fact: The Palestine cause concerns neither As-Sadat, America 
nor the other imperialist countries. It concerns only the Palestinians who have the right 
to decide on it, as affirmed by the Egyptian paper AL-JUMHURIYAH. 


In the face of this new indication, which affirms the Palestinian people's rights, we 
believe the Egyptian people, as they shoulder responsibility for change and clean-up, 
affirm beyond all doubt that they had nothing to do with the traitorous agreements con- 
cluded by traitor As-Sadat which constitute a departure from all values and laws. 


TRIPOLI NOT SURE ABOUT EGYPT'S FUTURE LINE 


LD182030 Trivoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1512 GMT 18 Oct 81 


(Unattributed commentary ] 


[Excerpts] Brothers: God's peace, mercy and blessings be with you. The follower of 
events in Egypt, since the death of the symbol of treason As-Sadat on 6 October and until 
this very moment, notes the speed with which events are taking place and that what is 
taking place in Egypt is extremely grave and demands an objective pause to analyze their 
dimensions and the threat they pose to the Egyptian and Arab peoples and probably to inter- 
national peace and security in general. 


If the assassination of As-Sadat by fire, in execution of the sentence passed on him by 
the Arab People's Court on 19 November 1979 and his fall before the eyes of the world, 
constitutes a bold heroic act manifesting the will of the Arab masses both inside and 
outside Egypt, the events taking place now do not call for reassurance about Egypt's 
present and future in the days following the death of the traitor. This is because the 
death and fall of As-Sadat do not mean the death and fall of all As-Sadats, nor do they 
mean that treason was buried with As-Sadat. 


For the As-Sadatites both inside and outside Egypt are still alive and there are still 
other Arab As-Sadatities in fraternal Arab countries who are perhaps more rancorous and 
more serious agents and more ready to commit acts of treason and treachery than he. 
Herein lies the danger.

This is a fact which the revolutionary forces in the Arab homeland must fully understand. 
They must not become overoptimistic about the assassination and fall of As-Sadat. 


If the revolutionary and progressive forces in the Arab homeland have expressed optimism 
about any radical changes that will take place following the death of As-Sadat in the 
interest of the progress and goodwill of the Egyptian people and the entire Arab nation 
out of their belief that treason and the documents of capitulation which As-Sadat signed 
have been buried with him, that the stable David process has died with As-Sadat, that 
the so-called autonomy talks have ended once and for all and that the Palestinian people 
in the occupied territory and the Palestinian refugees in southern Lebanon will realize 
their hopes and aspirations to return to their homes after the death of As-Sadat, who 
was an obstacle to this and who plotted with the enemy against them, then reality shows 
most certainly that matters will become more intricate than ever before, that the situa- 
tion can explode at any moment and that the area might witness a war between the forces 
of good and the forces of evil -- a war in which the forces of good will certainly triumph 
as established historical facts have proven over hundreds of thousands of years. 


When the revolution forces asked their masses in the Arab homeland to consider the state- 
ment issued by the Egyptian Government following the death of As-Sadat as a mere state- 
ment demanded by circumstances and when they asked us not to anticipate events for the new 
Egyptian leadership who could still realize the good and aspirations pinned on it for 
bringing Egypt back into the fold of the Arab nation by means of a bold national and 
pan-Arab resolution which the Egyptian leadership would take in response to the calls of 
the Arab masses and their wish to see Egypt return to them after their long wait -- when 
the revolution forces ask for this, they were right. 


However, this does not mean that the revolution forces confine themselves to watching 
events, recording them and rejecting them without taking any action against the enemy and 
his agents with a view to preventing them from realizing their imperialist ambitions of 
imposing their full control over the Egyptian people and further isolating Egypt in a 
worse manner than ever before. 


It was out of their love for Egypt and the Egyptian people and out of their eagerness to 
preserve Egypt as a free, sovereign and independent country as it was in the days of the 
heroic leader Jamal ‘Abd al-Nasir, a fortress for the free and revolutionary, not 
dominated by a Begin or a Reagan trying to turn it into a base for aggression against its 
sister Arab countries and other sister countries in Africa and Asia, that the revolution 
forces strongly warned against the consequences of continuing the march along the road of 
stable David, the road of treason, humiliation and disgrace. They reflected the con- 
science of the Arab nation when they affirmed that anyone who tries to follow in the steps 
of the defunct As-Sadat or who tries to betray the nation will meet an end like that of 
As-Sadat -- indeed, a worse end. 


The Arab masses might be able to forgive the new Egyptian leadership and members of the 
Egyptian Government for trying to go along with As-Sadat and to march with him on the 
road of treason, capitulation and submission to the enemy. 


These men might have pretexts that would justify their mistakes. Even if they had nothing 
to justify their errors against the Egyptian masses, all their errors are forgiven now 
that As-Sadat has been killed. His death should prompt them to reconsider all the nega- 
tive aspects of Egyptian policy both internal and external -- that policy which has 

harmed Egypt and the Arab nation alike.

They must begin a new white chapter of a new national and pan-Arab progressive unionist 
era in which Egypt turns from being an agent regime and a base for aggression against the 
Arab nation into a free revolutionary regime which rejects subservience and agentry and 
an advance Arab trench against the enemies, reactionaries and agents. 


We and all our brothers in the Arab homeland ask: In whose interest are these open con- 
flicts now raging in Egyptian towns in which hundreds of Egypt's sons have fallen; in 
whose interest are the consequences of these battles; in whose interests are the most 
barbaric and unjust detention in prisons and dark cells and persecution by As-Sadat of

U.S.-Egyptian-Sudanese maneuvers which will take place next month along the Egyptian- 
Sudanese-Libyan border, the free men and revolutionaries of the Arab nation have no 
alternative but to stand by the fraternal Egyptian people whom we pray to God to save 
them from all the tribulations and disasters which fate holds in store. 


Though standing by the Egyptian people and their living forces is important at the 
present time, what is more important is that the free men of the Arab nation and its 
revolutionaries should keep their fingers on the triggers in preparation to fight a war 
which America and its reactionary agents may impose on them and which they would fight 
not out of love for war but in defense of right, honor and dignity, for without these, 
life can have no value.

clear and final stand toward the new Egyptian leadership, the Arabs in general and the

attempt to revive the stable David accords, which are considered buried with the traitor, 
who signed them. Our primary consideration is to rob Arab reaction which was in league 
with As-Sadat before his death of the opportunity it might deem favorable for restoring 
relations with the Egyptian regime as one of the alternatives for the stable David accords. 


The Egyptian leadership must rise to the level of national responsibility and historical 
responsibility and thus place the sublime interests of Arab Egypt above all other con-

History will not forgive any agent or traitor just as it did with As-Sadat, who has
