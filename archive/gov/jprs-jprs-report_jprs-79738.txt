LIBYA

ALGERIAN REPORTER DESCRIBES QADHDAFI'S LIBYA 
Algiers AL-SHA'B in Arabic 21 Sep 81 p 5 


[Article by Bashir Hamadi: "The Libyan Jamahiriyah: First September Revolution 
In Its Twelfth Year; Popular Committees Everywhere" ] 


[Text] Writing about the Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah from the standpoint of the 
organization of society is delightfully simple, for the experiment upon which the 
Jamahiriyah embarked (which is represented by its name) as of March 1977 is 
different from existing experiments in the various Eastern and Western countries. 
It may be truly said that it is a return to the era of the “city state" in princi- 
ple, that is, direct democracy and rule of the people and by the people. It is 
different from the standpoint of methods and particulars, and there is no room here 
for an academic comparison. We are concerned here not so much with comparison as 
with learning the facts and through them evaluating the revolution and its slogans. 
The fact is always the test of each theory and slogan. 


However, can one offer a true picture of the situation of a country from a first 
visit which is limited to the capital, which usually is like an open flower to each 
visitor when behind it, in most countries, hundreds of wilted blossoms are hidden?! 


When our plane landed at Tripoli International Airport after midnight on 2 Septem- 
ber, Tripoli seemed to us a radiant pearl or a beautifully dressed bride on its 
twelfth anniversary. 


The road from the airport to the city is like our Jaysh al-Tahrir Street. One side 
takes you to the city, and the other side carries you from it to the airport, while 
light-green trees align it, adding beauty and shade. 


The first housing complex whose modern buildings greet you is the architecture of a 
"former hut quarter" whose buildings have risen on the rubble of the huts which 
represent the belt of misery which surrounds large cities, especially capitals. 


The bleary-eyed, one-eyed and even the blind can see the new bracelet of contruction 
rebirth. The city of Tripoli embraces the sea and meets the sand. New construction 
predominates. In all of its quarters buildings have risen and are still going up 


on the rubble of old housing.

Its large major streets, such as Shari’ al-Fatih and al-al-Magrif (on which are 
located the airlines of the various countries, including Air Algerie), ‘Umr al- 
Mukhtar and others, empty into its Green Square, which is the gathering place of 

the masses on every holiday and special occasion. This square embraces the popular 
market, Suq al-Mushir, which in architecture and layout is similar to our casbah, 
where foreign goods are sold on the sidewalk near the entrance. It is a picture 
similar to the entrance to the Tariq al-Sahil casbah, with its eastern end differing 
from the western end. On the end toward Benghazi, sand and dirt mix on the pavement. 
Both sides are lined with the green of camphor and olive trees, as far as the 
Tajura’ tractor factory which Col Qadhdhafi dedicated on the occasion of the tenth 
anniversary of the Fatih, mingling with the blue waters of the sea. 





Going west one passes through upper-class neighborhoods with villas in various 
forms, tree-shaded squares and flowers. Distributed among them are large markets 
such as the Hay al-Andalus collective market and al-Thalatha' collective market, 
which are similar to the Algerian bazaars. Here you can find any domestic or 
foreign commodity, especially the latter, at very reasonable prices. 


These markets and small establishments provide stiff competition for the private 
sector, which no longer can stand up to them and is gradually being ruined so that 
it is now on the verge of being wiped out in all fields. 


Everything Is Green! 


We find that everything is green: the Green Book, green signs, the Green Square. 
All windows and doors of homes and stores or businesses are green. Why the 
concentration on this color? 


Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi said in his first meeting with the revolutionary commit- 
tees in Benghazi that all of the colors used by traditional revolutionary movements 
in the world symbolize not life and hope, but destruction and ruin. The color green 
is the color of life, hope and the future, of which people everywhere dream. Green 
is the symbol of life everywhere in the world. Even paradise is symbolized by the 
color green. Green is the color of growing plants, the color of growth and not of 
growing old. All permanent and temporary slogans are written in this color. 
Speaking of slogans, the Green Book in its three parts ("Solving the Problem of 
Democracy," "Solving the Economic Problem," and "Social Support") is about to be 
transcribed in its entirety over the Arches of Victory and on the tablets and slabs 
designated for that, and in residential or non-residential buildings. 


The most important slogans which you encounter wherever you go are “Partners, Not 
Competitors," "Power, Wealth and Weapons Among the People," “The Fatih Forever," 
"Committees Everywhere," and hundreds of other slogans. 


Speaking of committees, what is meant? Are these the Popular Committees, which are 
a basic pillar of the organization of society according to the Green Book, or the 
revolutionary committees which are not mentioned in the book? 


We posed this question to one of the members of the Office of Revolutionary 
Committees, as it is written on the door. Since the Third Gathering of Revolution- 
ary Committees, in February 1980, it has been called the Office for Liaison with 
the Revolutionary Committees, headed by Staff Maj ‘Abd-al-Salam Ahmad Julud, but 
the old name is still in use.

This liaison office is located in the middle of a large garden, like the Palace of 
the People (Qasr al-Sha'b) at home, similar to the shrine of al-Aqsa Mosque. Facing 
it are fountains which attract tourists, especially after sunset when multicolored 
lights are lit under the water. 


Revolutionary Committees 


In the building of the Office of Liaison with Revolutionary Committees I met with 
Brother ‘Umar in a large, grandiose office. We had a long coversation about the 
revolutionary committees, the need to form them, their place in the organizational 
pyramid of society, the functions which they handle and other subpoints about the 
central topics. The gist of the discussion was that the revolutionary committees, 
which were formed in late 1977, are a tool of the popular revolution and are a 
Stage. The theory of the authority of the people and its application do not 
require the existence of revolutionary committees, They are not based in theory but 
are made necessary by the social, political, economic and cultural conditions handed 
down from a regime opposed to the authority of the py.ople. The presence of these 
committees is required by the nature of the transition period. The existence of 
these committees is linked to achieving the authorily of the people. Whenever that 
is achieved there is no need for the revolutionary :ommittees to continue if the 
goal for which they were formed is achieved. 


I asked Brother ‘Umar: "Aren't these committees evolving over the years into 
apparatus exercising authority in lieu of the people? Aren't huge offices like 
this (referring to the office of my conversation partner) one of the temptations to 
move away from the masses gradually in body and spirit?" 


His facial expression and the tone of his voice changed, and he said, choosing his 
words carefully: "You know very well that the revolutionary committees have no 
authority and no right to practice any kind of authority. The revolutionary 
committees were designed to have no authority so that they would not turn into a 
party or group setting up a dictatorship over the masses, thus becoming an obstacle 
to the authority of the people rather than a tool for them." 


The revolutionary committees are an evangelistic tool of the new organization of 
society, spurring the people on to exercise their authority without controlling 
them or exercising authority for them. One can understand their role in society 
through the functions which they are performing, represented by: 


1. Encouraging the masses to exercise authority, through the presence of these 
committees at People's Congresses when they meet and as the masses are climbing to 
the Popular Committees and the leadership committees, for the purpose of making 
things clear to the masses so that they may reach a sound decision; investigating 
attempts to dupe or deceive the masses; explaining the true dimensions of subjects 
being discussed by the masses at their conferences so that they will gain complete 
knowledge of their responsibilities; and working to have the People's Congresses 
choose revolutionary leadership elements. 


2. Establishing the authority of the people, which is the result to which the 
support functions of the revolutionary committees are leading.

3. Exercising revolutionary supervision by revealing practices hostile to them 
and revealing those elements to the masses so that suitable decisions can be made 
concerning them. The revolutionary committees do not make a decision concerning 
anyone unless acting in place of the people. 


4. Actuating People's Congresses so that the masses will exercise their right to 
authority. The masses need someone to get them started; they do not change position 
overnight. They do not change from absolute negativism to absolute positivism 
quickly. New things are not acceptable easily unless they are proved useful and 
preferable to the old. 


5. Providing guidance to the popular committees and the secretariats of the 
congress to prevent a relapse in the people's authority because of incorrect 
practices, especially in the transitional stage, since mistakes of individuals 

are charged to the people‘s exercise of authority, and some use that as justifica- 
tion for discrediting the people and their authority. 


For fear that the revolutionary committees would enter into a struggle with the 
leadership and popular committees, it was felt best to have them provide this 
guidance openly. 


6. Protecting, defending and guarding the Fatih Revolution by opposing reactionary 
groups who hate the revolution; opposing opportunists and hidden power seekers 
with revolutionary slogans who are more dangerous to the revolution than its con- 
spicuous enemies; and also opposing authoritative practices and attempts to usurp 
the people's power, at the same time working to communicate the words and theses 

of the revolution to all of the masses by scientific evidence, effective persuasion 
and practical pursuits. 


Committees Everywhere 


"No democracy without People's Congresses and committees everywhere" is a slogan 
found everywhere, as are such committees. The committees in “committees every- 
where" are the Popular Committees, which are considered the basic support of the 
permanent structure. They are not temporary or part of a stage, as are the 
revolutionary committees. These committees are chosen by the masses in the basic 
People's Congresses to take the place of the government administration. Thus the 
institutions of society operate through these committees. Consequently, admini- 
stration is by the people and supervision is also performed by the people, embodying 
the slogan of democracy: the people controlling themselves. 


The formation of these committees differs from the formation of the revolutionary 
committees. They are chosen by the masses and are found on different levels, that 
is, in every location, from a committee springing from the simplest gathering of 
citizens in a neighborhood to a committee emanating from the General Congress. 


First, there are Popular Committees on the neighborhood or local level; then there 
are Popular Committees on the level of municipal subdivisions. Next there are 
specific pupular Committees which are chosen on the municipal level during the 
meeting of the masses of each basic People's Congress to choose their member of 
each specific committee, such as a member of the Popular Committee on justice, 
education and health, farming and others. These Popular Committees in education

and other sectors are composed of the group of members chosen by the masses in the 
basic People’s Congresses. 


The structure is graduated in this way up to the General People's Congress, which 
is a general gathering point for the People's Congresses and Popular Committees, 
trade unions and vocational syndicates to formulate the decisions of the People's 
Congresses and choose the secretaries of justice, planning, agriculture and 
industry; that is, corresponding to the ministers. 


No Leniency Toward Enemies of Revolution 


The question which must occur to any reader is: Is the experiment proceeding 
without problems, or does it have enemies who are trying to obstruct it and destroy 
it if they can? 


From the beginning we have said that there is no sector or, in other words, 
organization, which satisfies all members of society, and any organization builds 
on the debris of the one preceding it if it wants to bring about radical change. 


Each organization has those who benefit from it, and the Libyan revolution is now 
building a new society of the masses on the rubble of the kingdom, which had its 
basic support from the bourgeoisie and the feudal system. These groups lost their 
privileges, and although they have seemed content, some of them have been biding 
their time, waiting for their opportunity to attack the revolution and return to 
the former system. However, methods of dealing with hostile groups are swift and 
violent. Thus the supports of the former regime are quickly being destroyed. The 
revolutionary committees are investigating every trick in the commercial field, 

and popular trials of several businessmen, contractors and others in this field have 
been held. They are also investigating incorrect practices such as patronage and 
bribery, and many examples have been offered to the masses, from high army officers 
to civilians in various posts. In its dealings with those hostile to it, the 
Libyan revolution is going beyond this. For example, the declaration of the third 
gathering of revolutionary committees in February 1980 states: "Bodily elimination 
of the enemy is the final stage in the agenda of the revolutionary struggle to 
decide it once and for all, if operations of economic, political and social dis- 
armament have not ended the activities of hostile forces." 


The declaration also announced the bodily elimination of the enemies of the 
revolution abroad, and the fourth conference, which was held in March 1981, approved 
the decision of the revolutionary committees to continue bodily elimination of the 
enemies of the authority of the people at home and abroad. 


Can there still be a relapse? The possibility is remote, especially since the 
forces of the revolution are growing every day, and the material supports of the 
new society are growing every day. 


Gleanings From Tripoli 
One of the greatest accomplishments of which any revolution can boast is the 


creation of a new people. The Libyan experiment has begun to bear fruit in this 
field, and that can be provided by giving examples: 











When we arrived at Tripoli International Airport after midnight we found two young 
men waiting for their family arriving on the same flight. Upon learning why we had 
come, they left their family members to another and acquainted us with various 
matters. They did not leave until we had joined the participating delegation, 
after spending more than 2 hours with us. 


An Algerian you_h who came with us aboard the same plane as a tourist could not 
find a vacancy in any hotel. He came to the steamer Toledo, and they, considering 
him as a guest of the Jamahiriyah (as are all guests) invited him to share their 
lodgings. 


If you asked for directions from one of the young people he would listen carefully 
to you, give you directions and often would take you there. 


The police in Tripoli are few, and traffic signs and lights are scarce. Conse- 
quently, the traffic system is subject to the good nature of the motorists more 
than anything else. 


Celebrations in the Jamahiriyah last for days, and a number of Arab and non-Arab 
troupes of artists participate in them. I noted that at the cockfights the Euro- 
pean:, perhaps more than the Arabs, favor the Lebanese birds. Speaking of Lebanon, 
I met the Lebanese troupe ot popular artists who accompany "Fayruz" (Turquoise) 
with a mixture of drums and the electric guitar. 


If the masses used to seem as if they "had not risen" to the level of Qadhdhafi's 
thought, they are gradually approaching it. However, the information media, which 
are supposed to be in the forward trenches, are more remote, so that one feels that 
the winds of the revolution have not yet reached broadcasting. 


Visual broadcasting, or television, as we call it, not only lags behind the 
procession of the revolution but also is backward from the standpoint of production 
techniques and programming. However, the day of progress is approaching, according 
to the citizens and the workers in the television field. 


Children are children, and their behavior is similar no matter how different the 
governmental structure. One of their favorite hobbies is selling items which they 
have bought from society at double the price. The day is coming when they announce 
their union to the world and organize to defend young sidewalk salesmen. 


Setting up a meeting with any military body ordinarily is more difficult then 
setting up a meeting with a civilian body, but in the Jamahiriyah it seems that 
things are different. Agreement on a meeting with the artists and writers league 
was more difficult than agreement with a military college. 


On the door of a recording studio in the television building this phrase is written: 
"Announcers and program hosts are forbidden to appear on screen in neckties." We 


have exactly the opposite!!
