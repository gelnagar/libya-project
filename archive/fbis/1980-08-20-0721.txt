MORROCCO 
MAURITANIA WARNED AGAINST COOPERATING WITH POLISARIO 
1D181325 Madrid EL PAIS in Spanish 9 Aug 80 p 2 


[Domingo del Pino dispatch: "Morocco Harshly Reproaches Mauritania for Its Cooperation 
With Polisario”] 


[Text] Rabat--Morocco has apparently warned Mauritania that it is not prepared to allow 
that country to deviate from neutrality in the Saharan conflict and has consequently 
demanded that Mauritanian territory cease to be used as a base for attacks by the Polisario. 


This stance on the part of Rabat was conveyed to Mauritanian head of government Khouna 
Ould Haydala on Wednesday, during a brief visit to Nouakchott by Moroccan Foreign Minister 
M'hamed Boucetta. 


0 Aug 80 NORTH AFRICA 


It is believed that the Moroccan authorities and other Western countries have confirmed 
the Polisario's use of the port of La Guera in order to land en route for Tindouf the 
Spanish and Portuguese fishermen kidnapped last May and June. It is also maintained in 
Rabat that the Polisario has recently used the neighboring country's territory to attack 
various localities in the former Tiris el Garbia. 


Although the Mauritanian reaction is not yet known, President Ould Haydala received a 
Polisario representative on the same day as Boucetta. However, it is doubtful whether 
Mauritania can maintain in practice the neutrality which its government publicly claims 
to adopt and which the Moroccans are demanding. 


The sympathies both of the Mauritanian Government and of the people toward the Polisario 
are well known. Both also appear to prefer to cooperate with Algeria rather than with 
Morocco, 


Any change of stance by Mauritania would damage considerably the Moroccan stance, now 

that there is talk again of secret Rabat-Polisario contacts. A Moroccan delegation headed 
by Gen Ahmed Dlimi has apparently met with Polisario representatives in Geneva. Although 
the king's personal adviser, Ahmed Reda Guedira, joined in the discussions at a late 
stage, he did not chair them, as on previous occasions. 


It is difficult to tell whether this could mean that the military wing has gained a 
certain advantage over the civilian wing--in particular, the liberal civilian wing headed 
by Reda Guedira--in the 'Alawite sovereign's favor. What is certain is that some 
diplomatic circles in Rabat believe that these secret contacts now have Algerian President 
Chadli Bendjedid's approval.
