The following appeared in the BOSTON GLOBE: “Former CIA agente are also training 

Col Qadhdhafi's terrorists.” There are “about 50 Americans who are collaborating 
with the Libyan Government in the military sphere." Among these are former members 
of the CIA, “green berets” discharged from the army, Vietnam veterans specialized in 
sophisticated weapons sectors and others. “Personnel already belonging to the armed 
forces and special unite and American firme are uniting to furnish products and 

skill to whoever can pay the price.” And further on: “A growing gang of nercenar ies 
is ready to eel) its services to the highest bidder.” 


All this is true. And we could continue from one day to the next with glaring head- 
lines: “Pilots of the Italian Air Force in Libyan aircraft are massacring the 
people of Chad,” or, “Australian tank drivers are sterring Qadhdhafi's tanks against 
the Moroccan Infantry,” or else, “Canadian and English saboteurs are destroying 
military installations in southern Sudan.” 


The truth is that, thanks to Qadhdhafi and Castro and therefore Moscow, “foreign” 
mercenaries and legionnaires are again having a field day. It is tho mass return of 
the “dogs of war.” It is the “newly rich” season of the mercenaries, of “inter- 
national” gangs, of combatants ready to risk the hide of anyone else and their own 
for money--under any flag. 


There is a saying, moreover centuries old, an image which arose at the time of the 
gerfalcons and serpentines, the culverins and cortans, the bombards and first guns 
with iron staves: “When there are guns, they fire by themselves." However, that is 
not true: someone must fire them. Libya is now attracting mercenaries. The coffers 
of Tripoli's colonel are overflowing and generous. Just as an example, in the past 
three years, not less than 60 Italian pilots have arrived in Libya. Mercenaries? 
Mercenaries with monthly pay of 4 million lire, 300 million life insurance, and 

extra pay for “operational flights” at 200,000 lire per flight hour. By quitting 

the Icoiian Air Force, they gave up an average monthly stipend of 750 to 780,000 lire.

Of the million people whe live in cities and villages, not counting the elderly, 
children and women, not much remains to build up an army. A modern army. Therefore, 
as this is being written, Libya has 2,600 tanks, including T062's and T-72's; more 
than 3,000 armored tracked vehicles; about 3,000 antitank missiles; and 300 missiles 
of the GAM type. That is not all: it has not less than 1,200 artillery pieces of 
every Caliber and about 170 fighter planes, including Mig-23's and Mig-25's and an 
unknown number of Tue22's. That is not all: it has four diesel submarines, motor 
Patrol vessels, vedettes. Just counting the number of armored vehicles, Libya 
could muster 10 tank brigades and 5 heavy~equipment divisions. Commanding officers, 
tank=-crew leaders and crews would add up to at least 70,000 men. And these Col 
Qadhdhafi does not have. Soe? Bo we have the “advisers"=--Boviets, Cubans, Bast

Legion,” mercenary formations of every nationality~--from youths enlisted in Niger 

to South Americans, from Italians to the French, from Americans to the Belgians, 
Canadians and Australians. 


Speaking of Qadhdhafi, it is not wise to underrate hie mental processes on the basis 
of his obvious faults. Bedouin astuteness is always reflected in his insane- 
appearing eyes. One thing, at least one thing, he has understood: everything (or 
almost) in the West, but not only in the West, can be obtained with money. He has 
spent about $20 billion for arms, since he assumed power over the desert. He needs 
an army. But his intuition, his sixth sense tells him that it is better to have 
mercenaries, legionnaires (mercenaries surely loyal to his dollars and indifferent 
to any ideology, ready to fight, kill, operate weapons and teach their operation to 
his people), even Soviet “advisers,” if necessary. In the desert colonel's opinion, 
there is something more credible, convincing and authentic in the mercenary, foreign 
legionnaire, “dog of war,” the man who fights in his mercenary forces for money, 
than in the Soviet emissary, the Marxist preacher, the Kremlin “adviser.” Rapport 
with his “international” battalions ig more clearcut, more understandable. 


The possibility that Libya might be converted into a base of operations for the 

USSR in North Africa and Central Africa is undoubtedly becoming one of the major 
concerns of military leaders in America and Western Burope. But Qadhdhafi's actions, 
initiatives, undertakings do not leave the Kremlin at all at ease. Libya's 
insertion into the strategic contest in the Mediterranean and African areas is an 
unknown for everyone, except those who are unaware of unknowns: mercenaries. And 
in Libya the mercenaries are truly having a field day. 


Even in Australia, through newspaper ads and appeals, Qadhdhafi enlists men willing 
to serve in his international battalions. In Sidney, a weekly newspaper is actually 
published in English and Arabic--the SAOUT EL-MOUGHTARES--entirely devoted to 
notices on enlistment, pay and an award for demobilization (several million /Tire7) 
for volunteers willing to serve "Tripoli's madman.”
