LE CONTINENT: How do you view the future of the multiparty system in Tunisia? 


Hmida Enneifer: It is still rather difficult to see how things will develop, 
since the country has been living under a strong and authoritarian regime for 

25 years. Nonetheless, I think the prime minister, Mohamed Mzali, is sincere. 

But the question which must be answered now is whether he has a team determined 

to work in the same direction as him. That is not clear. Moreover, the situation 
must be taken into account. The authorities in countries like Algeria or Libya 
are not likely to view this experiment favorably. They must fear some contagion 
because this open approach has been adopted under pressure from the population. 


Nonetheless, the Tunisian experiment is still at the stage of an adventure. We 
must wait and see. What is certain is that the government now has its back to 


the wall. It has no alternative. The Destourian Socialist Party supporters are 
no longer represented even in the university. This is a sign of change.
