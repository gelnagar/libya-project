LIBYA

JANA REPORTS ON CLOSING SESSION OF GENERAL PEOPLE'S CONGRESS 
LD210655Y Tripoli JANA in English 1424 GMT 20 Dec 78 LD 


[Text] Tripoli, Dec 20, JAMAHIRIYAH NEWS AGENCY--The General People's Congress held its 
closing session today at 10 a.m. in the people's hall in Tripoli. 


At the start of the session, the secretary-general of the General People's Congress 
announced the return of Brigadier Abu Bakr Yunis from a short visit to Algiers to enquire 
about the health of President Houari Boumedienne. 


"He is conveying to you the greetings of the Algerian people and the Revolutionary 
Command Council." 


"Abu Ammar is also back after a short visit to Algeria," said the secretary-general of the 
congress. Then he gave the floor to the secretary of the People's Congress in Sidney, 
Australia. The latter started by greeting the Libyan Arab people and stressed that all 
the traditional ruling systems had collapsed following the establishment of the people's 
power. 


He commended the third international theory which offered final solutions to the social 
economic and political problems of man. 


He affirmed that the masses of Sidney Basic People's Congress, being a part of the armed 
people, were ready to carry arms to defend the Jamahiriyah's revolutionary pillar. 


He explained that this defence was not confined to the land but included the defence of 
Arabism, Islam and the whole of humanity, "because the Great lst September [Revolution] 
is not a regional revolution but that of the masses everywhere."

Jallud Address 
LD210658Y Tripoli JANA in English 1639 GMT 20 Dec 78 LD 


{Text] Tripoli, Dec 20, JAMAHIRIYAH NEWS AGENCY--Staff-Major 'Abd as-Salam Jallud pointed 
out that although the Libyan Arab people was not among the founders of the non-aligned 
movement, yet it shoulders the responsibility of its continuation, for imperialism and 
reactionism have imagined that by the disappearance of its founders, they could put an 

end to it. 


"This", he said in a speech delivered at the General People's Congress morning session, 
"gives greater responsibility to the Libyan Arab people to support human freedom and to 
bring nearer the day of peace as well as to disperse the looming shadow of war so that 

humanity could live peacefully." 


Referring to the 1977 recommendations especially on achieving Arab unity by all available 
means and on continuing the Arab revolution, Staff-Major Jallud stressed that revolution 
was the answer to man's liberation, Arab unity and the liberation of Palestine. 


Staff-Major Jallud pointed out "among the important resolutions passed by the congresses 
were defining our policy under the motto 'we befriend those who befriend us and fight 
those who fight us," and increasing our alliance with the socialist group particularly 
with the Soviet Union." 


"The previous resolutions are still operative and binding," he stressed, pointing out 
that, "the world gives much concern to our revolutionary and political movements, there- 
fore the revolutionary command considered it appropriate to confirm to the world that 
these matters were *ndorsed by the people's congresses resolutions and are by nature not 
limited to any period of time but are there to remain." He went on to say: "If we are 
to play a major role in the Arab nation, the Islamic world and the non-aligned countries, 
we should be strong on this land. We chose to achieve this strength through realizing 
the people's power, the people's wealth, factories, achievements and social changes. We 
have certainly been able to play a more important part for the interest of our nation, 
Islam and the whole of humanity." 


Staff-Major Jallud stressed the statement made yesterday by the leader of the revolution, 
indicating that the activities of the congress had been affected by the health condition 
of the great struggler Houari Boumediene ard the circumstances surrounding the Algerian 
people. "For, as the leader of the revolution said, anything that affects Algeria is 
(?bound to) affect us and anything that concerns Algeria is bound to concern us.... 
Similarly, what happens in the Jamahiriyah also concerns Algeria, this, in view of the 
circumstances of apostasy, treason and capitulation now experienced by the (?Arab nation),"

[Text] Tripoli, Dec 20, JAMAHIRIYAH NEWS AGENCY--Staff-Major 'Abd as-Salam Jallud reed 
out the following cable sent by the General People's Congress to the Algerian Council of 
the Revolution at the final session of the congress this morning. 


The General People's Congress which includes people's congresses and committees as well 
as vocational unions and associations, currently holding this year's ordinary meeting, 
wishes to express its deep sorrow at the illness of the great militant, President Houari 
Boumedienne, and prays to God for his recovery so that he could take up his militant 
role soon,

The Great lst of September Revolution has been the eternal and fateful ally of the Arab

[Text] The following are decisions of the people's congresses after being drafted by the 
General People's Congress: 


In the name of God: decisions and recommendations of the people's congresses in the course 
of their third ordinary meetings from 21 October to 20 November 1978 and drawn up by the 
general meeting of the people's congresses, the people!s committees, unions, federations 
and vocational leagues, the General People's Congress in the course of its fourth ordinary 
session for 1978. 


First: Review of the decisions and recommendations of the people's congresses in the course 
of their ordinary session during 1977, drafted by the General People's Congress in the 
course of its third ordinary session of 1977. 


The basic people's congresses reviewed their decision and the recommendations and endorsed 
them. They showed their satisfaction with the executive measures adopted. They asked 
for the speeding up of the implementation of what has not yet been fulfilled. 


Second: Domestic policy:

1--To continue to implement the transformation [tahawwul] budget and the administrative 
budget for 1978. The people's congresses record their satisfaction with what was 
achieved in the transformation plan along the path of progress and prosperity. They 
ask for the speeding up of the implementation of what has not yet been fulfilled of 
projects identified in appendix No l. 


2--The transformation budget and the administrative budget for 1979. 


(A) It proves the allocation of the administrative budget for the fiscal year 1979, 
identified in appendix No 2. 

(B) The people's congresses reviewed the draft transformation budget for 1979 and 
added new projects identified as appendix No 3. 


They approved the allocation of the budget and the new projects taking into consideration 
the following basic factors: 


(1) The available financial resources which can be allocated for expenditure on 
the transformation process in various social and economic aspects. 

(2) The various aspects of the executive resources including those aspects connected 
with the study of and preparation for the implementation of activity as well as other 
aspects concerning implementation and supervision. 

(3) The giving of priority to the implementation of the schemes approved in the 
plan which was allocated by the people's congresses in their final form for 1977. 

(4) The exclusion of new repetitive schemes. 

(5) The exclusion of schemes which cannot be implemented due to technical reasons. 

(6) The giving of priority to the productive schemes and then the essential 
Services schemes. 


3--The general people's committees in the municipalities. The people's congresses 
decided to set up general people's committees in the municipalities in accordance to 
the provisions of the rules in the (?memorandum) and appendix No 4, 


4.~-On the matter of economic crimes: 


The people's congresses decided to pass a law on economic crimes to protect public funds 
and to guard public office against exploitation and (?manipulation) and to delegate the 
(?justice secretariat) to draw it in accordance with the basis laid down by the people!s 
congresses appendix No 5. 


5--Consideration of conditions for naturalization, and conditions for the withdrawal and 
the cancellation of citizenship. 


The people's congresses decided to define the conditions for naturalization and conditions 
for withdrawing and canceling the citizenship in accordance with the following rules: 


(A) The following are conditions for naturalization: 


(1) A Libyan origin; 

(2) A marriage by an Arab to a Libyan woman provided they reside in Libya; 

(3) Being an Arab and residing in Libya; 

(4) Being a son of a Libyan woman who married a non-Libyan provided they reside in Libya; 
(5) Being an Arab woman married to a Libyan provided they reside in Libya; 

(6) Being a foreign woman who married a Libyan provided they reside in Libya; 

(7) Ulama; 

(8) The surrender of former citizenship.

(B) Conditions for withdrawal of citizenship from any Libyan person of non-Libyan 
origin during the first 10 years since the person obtained the Libyan citizenship 
under the following conditions: 


(1) If the Libyan citizenship was obtained by false statements or by concealing 
some important facts; 

(2) If judicially convicted, proving the person's unloyalty to the homeland; 

(3) If convicted for an immoral conviction or crime; 

(4) If the person committed actions (?unbecoming) of a citizen and harmful to 
Jamahiriyah's interests. 


(C) Conditions of cancellation of citizenship: 


(1) Being a traitor; 

(2) Being a person who does not return after the completion of one's mission or 
studies, or being recalled; 

(3) Being a person who avoided military service; 

(4) Being a political refugee; 

(5) Being a person who flees abroad, or who smuggles his moneys abroad, or 
invested his money with the enemies. 

(6) Being a person who takes up a faith other than the Islamic faith; 

(7) Being a person who left the country following the outbreak of the revolution 
and who did not return; 

(8) Being a Libyan who takes up another nationality; 

(9) Being a Libyan who stays 10 years abroad without permission, excluding the 
immigrants who came as a result of Italian colonialism; 

(10) In addition to the condition already in force at present. [as heard]
