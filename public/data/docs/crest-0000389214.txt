Summary (AI): A January 1986 CIA talking-points memo for the DDI assesses Qadhafi's domestic standing after the US naval confrontation, saying his support is limited to a hardcore of young radicals, many middle-class Libyans fear economic collapse from US sanctions, and he is not in immediate danger of being ousted. It gives no specific Tripoli sites.
• Qadhafi reportedly sent messages via Malta to the Italian Prime Minister offering to call off attacks in West European countries in return for a guarantee that the US would not attack Libya.
• Qadhafi's willingness to accept Algerian preconditions, including discussing a Libyan withdrawal from Chad and the Aozou strip, is seen as a sign he feels US pressure.
• Qadhafi's tightening of travel restrictions for Libyans and militarization of schools were assessed to aggravate grievances fueling opposition.
• The memo assesses that a successful US strike causing few casualties might encourage activity toward Qadhafi's removal, while an unsuccessful strike or one causing civilian casualties would strengthen him.
• 1986-01: US naval maneuvers and confrontation with Qadhafi; Libyan public reaction assessed as mixed, with fears of US sanctions and economic collapse within two years
