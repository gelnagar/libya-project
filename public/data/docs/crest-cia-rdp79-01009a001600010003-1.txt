Summary (AI): A 1956 CIA geographic intelligence report on land mines left from World War II and later clearing work in Egypt and Libya. It covers Libyan minefields in Tripolitania and Cyrenaica, including Tripoli-area fields, and notes that Royal Engineers in Tripoli kept mine records and that oil companies began clearing mines in 1955.
• Tripolitania: two known mine-clearing attempts, in 1949 (Royal Engineers) and a later Tripolitanian Police Force program supervised by a former British Army mine expert.
• Oil companies swept access tracks and survey lanes in 12 concessions in 1956; clearing teams of 3 to 19 men cleared about 15 feet wide.
• Mined areas listed for Tripolitania include Bani Walid-area fields, Nofilia airstrips, Sedada, Sirte and Piaide, mostly from World War II.
• Mapping uses the Libya Zone Grid; the report's listings are mostly for Tripolitania, with little for Cyrenaica.
• 1949: Royal Engineers began clearing mines but stopped almost immediately after two British soldiers died. (2 killed)
• 1955: The first comprehensive effort to search and remove mines in the interior of Libya began under major oil companies.
