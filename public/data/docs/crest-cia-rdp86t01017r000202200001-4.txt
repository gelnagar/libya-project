Summary (AI): A April 1986 CIA Directorate of Intelligence memo assesses how Qadhafi would likely respond to a US military strike, including how attacks on his residences or on the Jamahiriyah Guard would be perceived. It warns that radicals could push him to expand terrorist attacks on US interests and that Libyan People's Bureau personnel might act on their own.
• Memo dated 11 April 1986, prepared by the Maghreb Branch, Arab-Israeli Division, Office of Near East and South Asian Analysis (NESA M#86-20052C).
• Assesses that Qadhafi would see a strike on his residences as a bid for his removal and that a strike on the Jamahiriyah Guard, his coup protection force, would likely reduce Libya's inclination to target US officials in Libya.
• Says US officials avoided targeting Qadhafi directly to avoid accusations of attempting to assassinate him.
• Links the crisis to a US press allegation that Washington had 'proof' of Libyan complicity in the West Berlin discotheque attack.

LIBYA: Oadhafi's Likely Response to a US Military Strike 

Summary 

In our view, Qadhafi remains determined to try to 
use the current crisis with Washington as a means of 
inflicting a dramatic political defeat on the US and 
establishing himself as the Third World's most 
prominent revolutionary leader. By so doing, he also 
wants to encourage the radical trend inside Libya, 
thereby consolidating his domestic revolution, 25X1

Oadhafi's concern about US military intentions almost 
certainly has increased since the US press alleged last week that 
Washington has "proof" of Libyan complicity in the West Berlin 
discotheque attack. Nevertheless, we believe that he is prepared 
for an escalation of the confrontation. We believe he would halt 
his aggressive challenge to US interests only if he believed 
Washington was willing to go so far as an invasion. 25X11 

Attacking Oadhafi's residences would convince him that 
Washington's objective in the current crisis is his removal. He 
would be personally shaken by such an attack, and might 
temporarily put terrorist planning on hold until he perceives 
that no additional action is forthcoming. On the other hand, a 
US strike against only facilities housing the Jamahiriyah Guard, 
Oadhafi's coup protection force, probably would reduce Libya's 
inclination to target US officials here. In our view, Qadhafi 
would regard such an attack as part of Washington's broader 
campaign to subvert his regime, At the same time, he would
