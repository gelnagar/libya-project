Summary (AI): A 1981 Boston Globe Magazine article (reprinted in CIA reading room) describes former CIA agents Edwin Wilson and Francis Terpil running a scheme that supplied explosives and terrorist training to Muammar Khadafy's Libya from 1976 to about 1979. Part of the work took place at a munitions workshop in the basement of servants' quarters at a former royal palace about twenty miles south of the seaside hotel in Tripoli.
• Explosives were camouflaged as lamps, candles, briefcases and ashtrays in a basement workshop.
• Twenty-five thousand pounds of explosives and a Redeye missile were allegedly contracted for shipment without State Department approval.
• Operation alleged to have run from 1976 through at least late 1979.
• Other Americans were said to be still supporting the Libyan government in 1981 (more than a dozen, perhaps as many as fifty).
• 1976: Edwin Wilson and Francis Terpil recruit Americans and begin supplying explosives and terrorist training to the Libyan government; the operation is alleged to have run through at least late 1979.
• 1976-08-13: Terpil, Wilson, Brower and others meet at Intertechnology, Inc. and allegedly agree to ship explosives to Libya without State Department approval.
• 1976: Brower's California company ships the first load of explosives to Tripoli, packed in drums labeled 'Industrial Solvent.'
