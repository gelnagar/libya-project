CURRENCY REFORM, AMENDMENT OF DEFENSE TAX ANNOUNCED 
Beirut THE ARAB WORLD WEEKLY in English 10 Jan 81 pp 23-24 
{Text} President Jaafar Numeiri has issued a decree exempting some industrial 


and consumer items and agricultural commodities from the five percent defence 
tax. 


Issued on October 11, 1979, the decree is part of Sudan's recent major campaign 
to promote increased agricultural production and to stimulate local industry. 


In the agricultural area, the following commodities are exempted: 


1. Agricultural equipment except that used in horticulture, gardens and 
sports playgrounds; 


2. Harvesters stipulated in article 84-25 of the Customs Tariff List; 

3}. Farm tractors stipulated in article 87(1) of the Customs Tariff List; 
4, Fertilizers; 

5. Insecticides for agricultural purposes and not for retail sale; 

6. Seeds; 

7, Jute. 


Also exempted from the defence tax are all industrial raw materials approved 
by the Ministry of Industry. Exceptions are tobacco and other raw materials 
for cigarette manufacture, and raw ingredients used in beer and wine production. 


Other commodities exempted from the tax include skimmed milk, bicycles, 
medicines, dates, printed materials and imports specifically exempted from 
the tax by agreement with the Sudanese government.

TUNIS AIR is to expand its regular fight 
service to new destinations in the Arab 
world, following the decision to relocate the 
headquarters of the Arab League in the 
Tunisian capital. The Tunisian national flag 
carrier already flies to Algiers, Casablanca, 
Tripoli, Cairo, Kuwait, Jeddah, Abu Dhabi 
and Damascus, and new routes to Baghdad 
and Khartoum are to be opened during the 
period from 1982-1986. 

Because of Tunisia’s relatively small size 
and limited population, Tunis Air is not 
contemplating long-distance transatlantic 
flights. Instead, according to Managing 
Director Ismail Khelil it will reinforce its 
European service, particularly to France, 
and develop new routes to Scandinavia. 
Another priority, following the example of 
Air Algeria and Royal Air Maroc, will be to 
open routes to sub-Saharan Africa.

its ambitious expansion programme, Tunis
