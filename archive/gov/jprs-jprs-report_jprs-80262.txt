FUTURE PRICE STRUCTURE DISCUSSED 
London AL-MAJALLAH in Arabic No 91, 7-13 Nov 81 pp 45-46 


[Article by al-Sirr Sayyid Ahmad: "OPEC Agreement to Unify Prices Has Restored 
Stability to Oil Prices. . . and Saudi Oil Has Become the Basis!"] 


[Text] "The third time is sure," is an Arab proverb, and perhaps this proverb 
applies beyond the Arab world, so that in the case of France it is Mitterrand, who 
lost in two previous attempts to sit in the Elysee palace as president and won the 
third time. And here now the proverb fits last week's successful OPEC meeting after 
the failure of two previous meetings to unify prices. 


The almost isolated Intercontinental Hotel which stands at the edge of the city of 
Geneva was the scene of the three meetings, which were crowned with the recent 
agreement. This agreement stipulates the unification of prices with the lowest 
limit around $34 for light oil; though surcharges may be added for excellent varie- 
ties, such as a sulfur-free petroleum, or due to nearness to consumer markets, these 
price hikes may not exceed $4, Likewise, a price freeze was agreed upon until the 
end of 1982. 


According to this agreement, Saudi Arabia raised the price of its oil from $32 to 
$34, and the rest of the oil-producing countries lowered their prices, especially 
the African countries, i.e., Nigeria, Libya and Algeria, who sell their oil at the 
highest prices, up to $40 a barrel. The end of the conference was followed by the 
announcement by Shaykh Ahmad Zaki Yamani that the Saudis will reduce their pro- 
duction by 750,000 barrels to about 8.5 million barrels. 


It was understood that the agreement includes a raising of the price of Saudi oil 
side by side with a reduction in production provided that the rest of the oil- 
producing countries agree to a unified price. Shaykh Yamani, Saudi Minister of Oil 
and Mineral Wealth, insists that the resolutions pertaining to production are a 
private matter of the national authorities, but the unification of prices induces 
the production of large quantities of non-commercial oil, since it weakens the 
opportunity for competition due to the existence of other types that are of better 
quality and are sold according to a uniform price structure.

arrived at for two reasons: First, with the production of oil there is an emanation

The important thing to note in all this is that a real decline in prices occured,

$1.50, so that the price of the Nigerial barrel would be $34.50, while its official

after the reduction, and opened the possibility of competition to gain customers,
