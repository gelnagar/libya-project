LIBYA

OFFICIAL'S COMMENT ON U.S. DECISION CITED 


JNO70930 Doha QNA in Arabic 0910 GMT 7 May 81 


[Text] Abu Dhabi, 7 May (QNA)--In a Washington dispatch today the Abu Dhabi paper 

AL-WAHDAH cites Mr ‘Ali al-Hudayri, secretary of the Libyan People's Bureau in Washington, 
as saying that his country will respond to the U.S. decision to expel the Libyan diplomatic 
mission from Washington. He pointed out that suspension of oil is only one of various 
weapons with which the United States or any other country can be confronted. He added 

that closure of the U.S. Embassy in Tripoli is a possibility. 


Al-Hudayri affirmed that U.S. interests in Libya surpass Libyan interests in Washington. 
On the U.S. administration's accusing Libya of supporting terrorism, he said that this 
administration's stands are based on unsound foundations, since it cannot distinguish 
between terrorism, which we denounce, and the liberation movements, which we support. 


lwelve percent of the U.S. oil supply is imported from Libya. 


AL-QABAS INTERVIEWS JALLUD ON CHAD, OTHER TOPICS 


GF061150 Kuwait AL-QABAS in Arabic 5 May 81 p 20 


{Interview granted to correspondent Salih Al-Khuraybi by Staff Maj ‘Abd as-Salam Jallud 
in Tripoli--no date given] 


[Excerpts] [Question] The dominant impression outside Libya is that the Libyan revolution's stands 
are controversial, Libyan policy is embarrassing and Libya does not adhere to inter- 
national norms. What is your reply? 


[Answer] The Libyan revolution is massive. Therefore, it is natural that in its march 
it collides with traditional regimes because it has chosen alliance with the masses, not 
with regimes. As for adherence to international norms, we, as revolutionaries, have 
better morals than other politicians because adherence is a basic principle in revolu- 
tionary thinking. 


We have even outstripped Marxist thinking cf the 19th century--which is triggering most 
of the world's revolutions nowadays--and have proposed the masses' thinking instead. 


We, in Libya, differ from traditional regimes and traditional revolutions because we under- 
Stand peoples better and peoples also understand us better. As for adherence to norms and 
commitment to principle, we are more principled and more adherent than others. 


We, as a revolutionary power, believe we are the power of the future. I do not believe 
any thinker or philosopher can say--at least theoretically--that democracy is better 
than replacement of the ruler by the people. 


[Question] Western parties make another charge: Libya is trying to enflame Africa. 
[Answer] To do what? 

[Question] To enflame Africa and spread revolution in it. Egypt, Sudan and Morocco are 
charging you with that. Niger and Mali are talking about Libyan plots to trigger sedition 


in them. Ghana, Gambia and Senegal are charging you with direct interference in their 
internal affairs and with forming a Libyan force for this purpose. 


[Answer] As for Sudan and Egypt, this charge is no more than mere blackmail to receive 
arms and aid from the United States. This trick has succeeded to a great extent, 
particularly after Reagan's recent statements and the formation of the Rapid Deployment 
Force to confront revolutions in Africa. The Jamahiriyah may make that charge against 
Egypt and Sudan.

An example of this is when the Jamahiriyah intervened in Chad at the request of the 
legitimate government and quelled the insurgency there. It called for cooperation among 
African countries to strengthen peace. We sent cables to that effect to all the leaders. 
Numayri replied: "No, the war has started," and sent us a cable to this effect. 


The principal threat the regimes of those countries face comes from their peoples and 
not from us. We do not export revolution. Nevertheless, genuine thinking and rule by 
the people have become widespread--from Nicaragua to Iran. 


[Question] Yet these countries are talking about direct Libyan intervention in their affairs 
and about Libyan financing of seditious movements in them. 


[Answer] What if I tell you that the opposite is true, that we have helped the regimes 
of those countries to survive and have provided personal security for their leaders? 
Examples are what we did for Numayri when his regime was subject to a coup attempt 

and the economic and military aid we offered Niger after Kountche's coup. As for other 
countries, we aided them, supplied them with oil, opened our borders to thousands of 
laborers, concluded many agreements with them, built schools, mosques, houses for them, 
helped them establish agricultural networks and other similar kinds of aid. Our inter- 
vention in Chad to extinguish the fire raging there and to return life to normal should 
have been met with gratitude from the countries mentioned because the Chad war was likely 
to expand and spread. Which African country is free from opponents and rebels? If the 
rebellion in Chad had succeeded, it would be a serious case for Africa. 


[Question] Major, you talk about extinguishing the Chad fire and returning life to normal 
in Ndjamena. Does this not mean the Libyan forces should withdraw before they become 
occupying forces? 


[Answer] In fact, we have declared our intention to withdraw. We look forward to the 
day when foreign threats to that afflicted country end and when the legitimate govera- 
ment will confirm itself. However, so long as foreign threats exist and the legitimate 
government will confirm itself, establish a national army and enforce security, our 
responsibility remains. 


Libya is not an expansionist country, nor is it an imperialist power. Rather, it is a 
liberation power having one goal: to end the rebellion and see the Chadian people able 
to exercise their own will. When that is achiewed, we will not linger 1 day longer in 
Chad. 


[Question] Major, you are accused of trying to introduce nuclear weapons into the region 
in collusion with some parties like Pakistan. What is your reply? 


[Answer] We have neither the intension nor the resources to produce a nuclear bomb. 

This kind of uproar and deception are practiced against us by imperialist circles. 
Nevertheless, Libya will not allow itself to lag behind the technology of the year 2000 
or loiter in absorbing new energy sources, whether solar or nuclear. We will try to use 
energy to desalinate seawater, for example, and provide water to reclaim deserts, 
particularly since our energy resources will run out one day. Hence, we will be forced 
to resort to nuclear energy one day. Some circles do not want us to enter this area. 
They want us to remain backward, despite the fact that they know well that we will use 
energy for peaceful purposes and that Libya is among the countries that have signed the 
Nuclear Nonproliferation Agreement. Yet they insist on making an uproar. 


[Question] Brother ‘Abd as-Salam, Libya has offered air support to protect Lebanese air- 
space. How serious is this offer, and will Libya be able to bear the accompanying 
escalation? 


[Answer] For us, it is an issue of bearing responsibility. Ultimately, no one can prevent 
us from dying in defense of our honor. Guarantees on this issue are another issue, but 

no one can prevent us from dying while resisting. Our offer to Lebanon is pending. In 
this issue, geography does not have any significance.

We alone have stood with the Lebanese people in helping them to preserve their national 
unity and the Palestinian resistance. No one except the Libyan foreign minister was at 

the scene of the fighting for 58 days and was exposed to death 14 times. I was in Lebanon, 
not on my own behalf, but on behalf of the Libyan revolution. However, other foreign 
ministers remained in Damascus. We were sending arms despite the siege on Sidon and Tyre. 
You know the story of the ship that was attacked in Sidon. We are presently offering 
advanced weapons to the Palestinian revolution and other weapons to the Lebanese National 
Movement. 


[Question] As for the efforts to achieve a merger between Syria and Libya, it has been 
said that the reason Libya called for this merger is its desire to see Syrian troops sta- 
tioned on the Libyan-Egyptian border. Were you considering this when merger talks began? 


[Answer] Never. We are able to defend our land. Our people are armed, and our army has 
advanced weapons. This army has achieved a major victory in Chad. It has been fighting 
in an area stretching for thousands of kilometers. It has succeeded in turning a regular 
war into a guerrilla war, which is the most difficult kind of war. For Libya, a merger 

is something sacred, and we are trying to achieve it while we are strong and without having 
to resort to maneuvers. We proposed the motto "Merger for combat to help Syria." Syria's 
position at that stage of the merger negotiations was similar to the situation when it 
merged with Egypt in 1958. Our merger with Syria is a combat merger to aid Syria. It is 
a merger through which the two countries and peoples will mobilize their energies and turn 
steadfastness into resistance. It will lead the Arab uprising and will mark the beginning 
of the strategic attack against imperialism, which has returned to the Arab nation in the 
form of the Rapid Deployment Force and foreign bases in Egypt, Somalia and Sudan. 


[Question] How far has the merger progressed? 


[Answer] The issue of the merger is serious. We do not want it to be superficial or dema- 
gogic. Rather, we want it to be ideologically firm--a unity of the masses. Currently, there 
are two ideologically contradictory regimes in Syria and Libya. There must be a firm basis 
for the merger. Syria has offered proposals for the merger, and we have offered ours. We 
have decided to form a committee to study the proposals. We want it to be a real and firm 
unity. The merger has been announced and is considered a finished subject. Nevertheless, 
the unity issue is very serious and needs guarantees. Therefore, there is a need to answer 
all questions so it will be a genuine unity. For example, the Syrian regime is republican, 
and our regime is a popular one. Economic differences and other real issues need to be 
examined. We are determined to surmount all obstacles, since the resolution on unity has 
been ratified by the Libyan people's congresses and by the Syrian leadership and People's 
Assembly. 


[Question] Regarding Mauritania, is it true that Libya warned Mauritania of the coup 
beforehand, enabling the Mauritanian government to foil the coup? 


[Answer] Yes, our intelligence service is competent. We knew of the coup before it took 
place. 


[Question] Did you relay this information to Mauritania? 
[Answer] Yes, our intelligence service is competent. There are sizeable Muslim minorities 


in all African countries. These minorities--as well as the nationalist forces, students and 
workers--supply us with information. 


[Question] Let us go back to the time of Haydala's coup against Ould Salek. Did Haydala 
enjoy any Libyan support in his coup? 


[Answer] No, but we invited Ould Daddah twice to visit us. We advised him to withdraw 
from the war because the Mauritanian Army felt that it was being led into a war merely to 
satisfy the wishes of the king of Morocco, a war from which it had nothing to gain.

Therefore, we advised Mokhtar Ould Daddah to withdraw from the war. If Morocco had refused 
to accept the withdrawal, Libya and Algeria would have been prepared to come to 
Mauritania'’s aid--particularly as the war was impeding national development. 


[Question] Was Ould Salek's coup against Ould Daddah motivated only by his belief in 
the need to withdraw from the war? 


[Answer] I believe so. The first thing he did when he came to power was to withdraw from 
the war. 


[Question] With regard to U.S.-Libyan relations, what was the U.S. reply to Libya's 
invitation to reopen the U.S. Embassy in Tripoli? 


[Answer] We have not invited the Americans to reopen their embassy in Libya. 

[Question] There is a statement by Colonel Al-Qadhdhafi on this matter. 

[Answer] The purpose was to demonstrate that the United States is persisting in its 
hostility toward the Libyan people. We are not eager to resume relations with the United 
States. 

[Question] How would you describe the present state of Libyan-U.S. relations? 


[Answer] Relations of hostility on the part of one side--the U.S. side. 


[Question] U.S. Secretary of State Alexander Haig has accused Libya of helping the Soviet 
Union to become a Mediterranean power. What is your view? 


[Answer] I do not believe Haig is so ignorant that he does not know that Libya is neutral. 
However, the U.S. pressures to which we are being subjected are strong and do not help 
Libya to remain neutral. 


[Question] Does this mean that these pressures could push Libya into becoming completely

[Answer] Of course. We are not a people who can be pressured. As Churchill said: I am 
prepared to have the devil as an ally. 


[Question] And the devil is the Soviet Union? 

[Answer] Or the United States. 

[Question] It has been said that Alexander Haig has ordered an investigation of the charge 
that some Americans have sold military expertise to Libya. He says 50 Americans are in 
Libya helping your government acquire military technology. Do you deny or confirm this? 
[Answer] Address this question to Alexander Haig. It is he, not I, who is investigating. 
[Question] What about the Libyan opposition? 

[Answer] What do you mean by opposition in Libya? Opposition against whom? Anyone who 
is opposiug us is opposing the people. There is nothing called opposition in Libya. Our 
system is a people's system. The opposition is a group o° opportunists. Some of them have 
stolen money or borrowed large sums from a bank and then fled the country. Some former 
Libyan businessmen are now working abroad. They visit the people's bureaus and consider 


them to be theirs. 


[Question] How would you describe Libya's relations withthe Soviet Union?

[Answer] Relations of friendship in the true sense of the word. 


{Question] Do you not fear that the presence of Soviet experts and Libya’s reliance on 
Soviet arms will lead to a repetition of what happened in Egypt? 


[Answer] What happened in Egypt? 


[Question] Egypt found itself hostage to the Soviet Union's will. 
[Answer] This will not happen in Libya. Moreover, we have diverse arms sources. We 
buy from all sources. 


[Question] How does Libya view the Reagan Administration and the effect this new 
administration will have on the Middle East crisis? 


[Answer] Libya's views are not affected by the changes that take place in the White 
House. We believe there is continuity in U.S. policy. 


[Question] It has been said that the establishment of a Palestinian state is imminent. 


[Answer] Libya defined its stand on the Palestinian state at the conference of the 
Steadfastness and Confrontation Front. We rejected the state. We said it is a new 
product of Camp David. We believe that Palestine is for the Palestinians, and the Jews 
who used to live in Palestine have a right to stay. We offer this to the world as the 
ideal solution. 


KLIBI SAID TO ASK ARABS TO SEND FORCES TO LEBANON 


LD061722 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabi 1230 GMT 6 May 81 


[Text] The brother leader yesterday received Chedli Klibi, secretary general of the 
Arab League. The secretary general explained to the brother leader that he has sent a 
cable to Arab kings and presidents in which he called on the Arab League states to 
implement the joint defense and economic cooperation treaty among the Arab countries and 
its military supplement signed on 13 April 1950 and to dispatch Arab land and air forces 
and air defense to defend Lebanon and the Palestinian revolution. 


The secretary general said that the Arab League is now awaiting replies from Syria and 
Lebanon on adopting the necessary measures to implement the provisions of the treaty 
to ensure the freedom of Lebanon and the protection of the Palestinian revolution.
