Kus ait AL-TALI'AH in Arabic 24 Dec 80 pp 27-34 
[Article: “Democracy in the Arab World") 


[Text] Committees are being formed. Resolutions and declarations are being pub- 
lished. Scholars from all parts of the Arab world call upon one another, corres- 
pond, debate. The motive for all this activity, the issue on everyone's mind: 
democracy. 


If democracy as everyone insists is government by the people, how could this goal 

be achieved’? By resorting to one-party rule’? By adopting a multi-party system’ 

By abolishing all parties? Many questions come to one's mind; but before specula- 
ting on the issue one must be a scholar. Every party involved in this debate cl ims 
to possess this attribute. 


The issue of democracy was certainly on the minds of all who took part in the semi- 
nar “Democracy in the Arab World,” which was held on 12-15 November at the I'lal 
al-Fasi Hall, located in a quiet suburb of Rabat, Morocco. 


The group which organized the seminar--the Association of Intellectuals--was formed 
shortly after Anwar al-Sadat's 1977 visit to Jerusalem. The association--consisting 
rimarily of young educated Moroccans--is sustained by membership dues and contribu-

[Text] Damascus, 25 Feb (JANA)=<<The In ernational Union of Arab Trade Unions and 
the World Peace Council has called for che abolition of the military alliance 
existing between the signatories of the Camp David accords and the American rapid 
deployment force which undoubtedly cone ituted a spearhead for aggression against 
the Arab people and the peoples of Africa and Asia. 


In 4 communique issued in both Damascus and Helsinki at the end of the union 
delegation's visit to the council's headqurters in Helsinki, the two sides pledged 
to launch an effective campaign calling for the dismantlement of the American 
military bases established in Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Somalia, Bahrain, Oman, Sudan, 
Kenya and Diego Garcia. 


The two sides also expressed their deep concern about the participation of the 
French, British, and other of America's allies, military fleets in the manoeuvres 
carried out by the American fleet in the Arab Gulf region and the Indian Ocean. 


The two sides condemned the increasing French military presence in Africa using 
the Chad question as a pretext to threaten the security and sovereignty of many 
African countries. In relation to this, the two sides warn of threats directed 
by the imperialist and reactionary forces against the socialist people's Libyan 
Arab Jamahiriyah and of their attempts to undermine the African peoples’ unity. 


The two sides reiterated their strong condemnation of the Camp David and Washington 
accords and stressed their opposition to the so called Jordanian option and to any 
other licuidationist plan which does not guarantee the r alication of the inalienable 
rights of the Palestinian people to return to their homeland, to exercise their 

right to self-determination and to establish their independent state over the national 
soil. 


The two sides strongly denounced the threats practised by imperialism and the 
reactionary forces against the Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah, particularly the Egyptian 
military buildup on its borders, and also against the Democratic Yemen. 


The two sides indicated that the main objective of all these political and military 
plans was to reinforce the exploitative practices of oil monopolies and other 
multinational companies. The two sides also affirmed their total support for all

JAMAHIRIYAH STUDENTS HOLD CONGRESS 
Paris BULLETIN QUOTIDIEN DE L'AGENCE JAMAHIRIYA-PRESSE in French 8 Feb 81 pp 9-11 


[Jamahiriya News Agency report on 8 February (3 Rabii al Thani) from Benghazi: 
"Fifth Congress of Jamahiriyah Students") 


[Text] The leader of the Great Revolution of 1 September took part in some of the 
proceedings of the 5th congress of Jamahiriyah students which started yesterday 
evening at Gar Younes University, its theme being "Students, instruments of the 
revolution,” “instruments of unity and progress." 


Also taking part in this congress are student delegations from friendly countries, 
the secretary for trade union, association and union affairs from the People's 
General Congress, and the secretary of the Popular High Committee of Gar Younes 
University. 


In his speech during the opening meeting, the secretary of the Student Congress of 
Car Younes University started out by welcoming the delegations, organizations and 
fellow student unions taking part in the congress during which resolutions from 
the grass roots student congresses will be reviewed. 


The secretary of the student congress said that every day he was more convinced 
that congresses and popular committees, and unions, trade unions and professional 
associations are the only means through which the masses can have control over 
their lives, cnd that without that there would be nothing more than trickery and 
demogoguery. 


He also said that the student masses have continuously taken splendid revolutionary 
initiatives, and that they are in the forefront of the movement of the masses to 
overcome obstacles and destroy dictatorial power at the grass roots level. He 

went on to say that students are quite freely and unrestrainedly deciding to move 
from the stage of idelness and conservatism to that of production and awakening. 

They are deciding as well to take up arms and to transform their educational insti- 
tutions into military barracks to hasten the time when there is an armed population 
and to move to a counterattack mode. They are also deciding to revolutionize their 
programs in order to abolish the traditional systems which prevail in education, 

and this is so they can better further the revolutionary transformation of the masses. 


The secretary of the student congress also asserted that the student masses are 
worthy of the trust placed in them by the Great Revolution of 1 September. They 
have gone far beyond all the systems of representation which are such a hoax for

student masses elsewhere in the world, and they are iasuing an implicit challenge 
to Arab and other atudentsa. They wanted to have the experience of being atudent 
masses in a Jamahiriyah inepired by the Green Book, which has reviewed all human 
experience with an eye to democracy, and in order to be finally done with all 
forms of supervision, representation, delegation of authority, and takeover. 


Speaking in his turn, the steering committee secretary of the Jamahiriyah General 
Student Union explained the attitudes of the student masees and their refusal to 

be traitors and slaves. The demonstrations in 1958 and 1961, which were led by 
Mu'ammar Qadhdhafi, then a student, were already an expression of the students re- 
,2cting the oppression and dictatorship which characterized the system of govern- 
ment in place at the time. They were motivated by a nationalist sentiment which 
supported the revolutionaries in Algeria and everywhere in Arab areas and in Africa. 
They took a stand favoring unity and against separatism, contrary to the leanings 
of the tottering monarchical regime, which was tied to colonialism and fixed on re- 
gionalism, thereby attempting to save itself and to isolate the Arab people of 
Libya from the other children of the Arab nation. 


The demonstrations in January and June of 1964 were a continuation of the demonstra- 
tions led by Mu'ammar Qadhdhafi as a student in 1958 and 1961 and were premonitory 
signs of the revolution.

ELECTRICITY PROJECTS MOVE AHEAD 
Paris BULLETIN QUOTIDIEN DE L'AGENCE JAMAHIRIYA-PRESSE in French 9 Feb 81 pp 10-11 


[Jamahiriya News Agency report on 8 February (3 Rabii al Akher) from Tripoli: 
"Developments in Electricity") 


[Text] Work is currently underway on the construction of six power plants at El 
Khams, El Zouitia, Zouara, El Aril, El Sarir, and Sebha, to have a total capacity 
of 40,810 megawatts. 


There is also work underway to increase capacity at the Tobruk, Derna and northern 
Benghazi plants by 360 megawatts according to General Popular Committee for electri- 
city sources. Work is currently in full swing to provide the 220-volt branch Line 
network with 1734 km of extension so that it can cover 780 km of the Tarhouna and 

El Khmas regions. 


Work on this project will probably be completed during this year as probably will 
be the 790-km El Khams-Muserata line and the 100-km Chakchouk Nalout line. The 
line joining West Tripoli and Hira is 42 km long. 


The one from El Khams, from the transformer station at El Khams, goes 32 km to the 
east and west of Tripoli. 


Electricity Secretariat sources say that twelve 200-kw transformer stations are 
being built at Zamzam, Syrte Ajdabia, El Tamini, Al Barika, El Saraj, Ain Zara, 
Nalout, Al Kara Bouli, Mrada, El Khams, and Muserata. They are scheduled to be 
completed in 1981 or 1982. 


The same sources add that preparations are in full swing tc reach the last stage in 
the construction of 1329 km of branchline networks to have 66-kw capacity, 335 km in 
the Nalout and Ghadames regions, 110 km in Tobruk and El Bardia, 22 km in Syrte 

Ras Lanouf, 18 km in Bani Walid Abou Grin, 130 km in Abi-Alkir El Chhouiref, the 
Wadi Nina/Houn line of 350 km and the Bani Walid Mezda line of 260 km. 


Work is also underway on the construction of nine power plants to provide 66 kw 

to Naout, Sinanoun, Darj, Ghadamis, Ghadamis Airport, Sultan Benjouad Ras Lanouf, 
and the east part of Syrte. Work is likewise underway on the construction of four 
plants at El Khams, Znara, Milita and El Zouitinia for sea water desalinization with 
a capacity for putting out 181 cubic meters a day, and on the expansion of the Derna 
plant.

MODEL VILLAGE PROJECT 
Paris BULLETIN QUOTIDIEN DE L'AGENCE JAMAHIRIYA PRESSE in French 5 Feb 81 pp 5-6 


{Jamahiriya NewsAgency report on 5 February (30 Rabial Awal): "Construction of 
a Model Village as Part of the Maknousea Agricultural Project") 


[Text] The General Popular Committee for Housing is going to sign a contract with 
an international company specializing in construction to build a model village pro- 
ject at Maknoussa. The project includes 200 residential units, a dispensary, a 
primary school with 12 classrooms, a market, a mosque, a gas station, an administra- 
tive complex, a bus station, a piped water system, sewers, a network of roads, and 
water reservoirs. 


This process has gotten underway on the authorization of the General Popular Committee 
and in accordance with the recommendations and resolutions of the Popular Congresses. 


It has been learned from General Popular Committee for Housing sources that the vil- 
lage is for the agricultural project workers who are part of the grain production 
project in the region. 


It should be noted that the implementation of the Maknoussa agricultural project, 
whose 2,400-hectare area is divided into 40-hectare fields, started in 1978. 


In 1979 and 1980 the project managed to produce 12,178 tons of wheat, which is equiva- 
lent to 5.1 per hectare. This yield is considered one of the highest in the Sahara's 
agricultural regions. An area of nearly 1,800 hectares is slated for summer crops.
