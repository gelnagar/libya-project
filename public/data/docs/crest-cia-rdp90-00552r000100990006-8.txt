Summary (AI): A 1981 Washington Post column (reproduced in a CIA reading-room copy) describes Billy Carter's dealings with Libya, including a reported $220,000 "loan" from Libyans, alleged efforts to get embargoed C130 transport planes released, and ties to ex-CIA agents Frank Terpil and Edwin Wilson. It is about U.S. political and intelligence matters with only indirect bearing on Libya itself.
• Qaddafi reportedly believed Billy Carter could persuade the president to release eight C130 transport planes embargoed over Libyan support for terrorists.
• Billy Carter was reported to have received a $220,000 'loan' from the Libyans, an advance on expected commissions from an oil deal.
• Wilson allegedly discussed having Billy killed by planting explosives in an oil painting to be given to him.
• Terpil reportedly arranged a similar commission deal for himself in 1976.
• 1978-09: Billy Carter first visited Libya with Georgia associates, ostensibly to promote commercial ties; intelligence sources said Qaddafi had targeted him as a White House influence-peddler.
• 1979-09: Terpil reportedly invited Billy Carter to the 10th-anniversary celebration of Qaddafi's coup; reportedly the deal giving Billy a $220,000 'loan' was struck then.
• 1980-01-16: Billy Carter told a Justice Department official he had met Terpil on the 1979 trip; he later recanted this statement.
