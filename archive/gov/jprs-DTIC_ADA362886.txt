Jordan,  Iraq  Reportedly  Demand  Halt  to  U.S. 
Contacts 

45000105  Cairo  AL-SHA’B  in  Arabic  26  Feb  90 p  1 

[Text]  Egyptian  political  circles  have  mentioned  to  AL- 
SHA’B  that  an  agreement  was  concluded  in  the  closed 
session  of  Arab  Cooperation  Council  country  leaders 
held  in  Amman  last  Saturday  commissioning  Jordan  to 
call  for  holding  an  emergency  Arab  summit  to  discuss 
developments  on  the  Palestinian  issue  and  the  current 
international  changes.  The  sources  indicated  that  during 
the  session  Iraqi  President  Saddam  Husayn  and  Jorda¬ 
nian  monarch  King  Husayn  demanded  the  cessation  of 
contacts  with  the  United  States  because  of  the  ambiguity 
of  the  American  position  on  Jewish  immigration  to  the 
occupied  territories.  At  the  same  time,  President 
Mubarak  and  Yemeni  President  ’Ali  ’Abdallah  Salih 
asserted  the  necessity  of  continuing  these  contacts  until 
it  is  possible  to  change  the  American  position. 

The  features  of  the  Arab  plan  to  condemn  and  prohibit 
dealings  with  any  countiy  or  international  economic 
institution  giving  economic  and  financial  aid  to  Israel  for 
Jewish  settlement  in  the  occupied  territories  or  for 
building  new  settlements  were  confirmed. 

Libyan  Minister  Comments  on  Possible  Cairo 
Embassy 

900A0344B  Cairo  AKHIR  SA  ’AH 
in  Arabic  14  Feb  90  p  9 

[Interview  with  Jadallah  ’Azzuz  al-Talhi,  the  People’s 
External  Liaison  and  International  Cooperation  Bureau 
secretary;  date  and  place  not  specified] 

[Excerpt]  [Passage  omitted]  [AKHIR  SA’AH]  The  issue 
of  an  embassy  still  raises  the  question,  “How  can  rela¬ 
tions  be  established  between  the  two  countries  without 
an  embassy?” 

[Al-Talhi]  This  question  appears  to  attract  the  attention 
of  many  people  since  it  has  been  raised  more  than  once. 
The  first  time  was  when  Egyptian  journalists  accompa¬ 
nying  President  Mubarak  on  his  visit  to  Surt  asked 
Colonel  al-Qadhdhafi.  He  answered  clearly  and  frankly 
that  in  the  end  the  question  of  an  embassy  will  be  a 
formality,  in  view  of  the  relations  and  blood  ties  that 
unquestionably  go  beyond  the  symbol  which  an  embassy 
represents  of  two  separate  entities.  The  situation  is 
different  between  Egypt  and  Libya. 

[AKHIR  SA’AH]  But  do  you  agree  that  establishing  an 
embassy  is  more  than  a  “formality”  since  it  is  an 
important  means  of  linking  the  two  countries  and  is  also 
a  channel  through  which  relations  between  the  two 
countries  pass? 

[Al-Talhi]  It  was  decided  at  the  Tubruq  meeting  to  open 
offices  for  the  two  countries;  however,  we  know  that  the 
ties  between  the  countries  and  the  Arab  peoples  of  Libya 


and  Egypt  make  the  issue  of  an  embassy  less  Important 
than  might  be  suggested  by  the  raising  of  this  question.  I 
know  that  the  presence  of  embassies  at  this  time,  when 
the  ease  of  communications  has  made  the  world  a  global 
village,  no  longer  plays  the  influential  role  it  once  did  in 
shaping  relations  between  countries,  particularly  when 
historic  and  geographical  ties  exist,  as  is  the  case  with 
Egypt  and  Libya. 

[AKHIR  SA’AH]  What  is  Libya’s  view  of  President 
Mubarak’s  chairmanship  of  the  OAU  [Organization  of 
African  Unity],  particularly  in  light  of  his  role  in  solving 
many  African  issues? 

[Al-Talhi]  I  believe  that  President  Mubarak’s  chairman¬ 
ship  is  due  to  Egypt’s  influence  in  Africa  and  that  he  has 
an  important  role  to  play  in  settling  many  existing 
disputes  and  those  that  may  erupt.  We  appreciate  Pres¬ 
ident  Mubarak’s  efforts  to  solve  the  dispute  between  our 
brothers  in  Mauritania  and  Senegal,  for  example,  as  well 
as  his  efforts  concerning  relations  between  Libya  and 
Chad  and  many  other  African  issues. 

[AKHIR  SA’AH]  The  Arabs  have  called  for  an  urgent 
summit  to  discuss  the  dangers  of  Soviet  Jewish  emigra¬ 
tion  to  Israel  and  the  changes  in  Eastern  Europe. 

[Al-Talhi]  I  would  like  to  say  that  this  matter  has 
assumed  grave  proportions,  particularly  because  the 
number  of  Soviet  Jews  who  have  begun  to  arrive  has 
grown  so  that  it  is  has  gone  beyond  what  we  could  call 
emigration  to  an  attempt  to  colonize  at  the  expense  of 
another  people.  We  must  be  aware  that,  unfortunately, 
this  has  always  been  one  of  the  U.S.  stipulations  for 
detente  and  rapprochement.  Unfortunately,  with  its  sup¬ 
port  for  the  Zionist  entity,  the  United  States  is  creating 
this  assimilation  crisis.  With  the  financial  support  and 
weapons  it  provides,  these  emigrants  will  be  converted 
into  a  military  force  that  will  help  increase  the  Zionist 
threat.  Not  to  mention  the  fact  that  by  deciding  last  year 
not  to  enable  Soviet  emigrants  to  go  to  the  United  States, 
it  clearly  intended  to  compel  them  to  go  to  occupied 
Palestine.  This  is  a  U.S.  conspiracy  in  its  continued 
aggression  against  the  Arabs  by  all  means,  including 
exerting  pressure  on  the  Soviet  Union  to  allow  Jewish 
emigration.  The  matter  must  be  viewed  in  this  context.  I 
am  not  saying  that  the  Soviets  do  not  bear  some  respon¬ 
sibility;  however,  the  United  States  imposed,  planned, 
and  worked  for  this.  In  the  context  of  this  understanding, 
we  welcome  any  and  all  Arab  meetings  to  study  the 
threats  surrounding  the  Arab  nation. 

[AKHIR  SA’AH]  Do  you  agree  that  the  activity  of  the 
Arab  Maghrib  Union  is  somewhat  slow,  perhaps 
reflecting  a  difference  in  political  and  economic  visions 
among  the  members? 

[Al-Talhi]  No,  I  do  not  agree.  My  proof  is  the  practical 
steps  that  have  been  taken  to  make  the  Marrakech 
Agreement  concrete  in  the  near  future.  Do  not  forget  that 
it  has  only  been  a  year  since  the  Arab  Maghrib  Union 
was  founded;  nonetheless,  in  this  period,  the  treaty  has 
been  ratified,  and  all  the  agencies  specified  in  the  treaty

cause  for  satisfaction. 

[AKHIR  SA’AH]  Have  the  problems  between  Libya  and

conducted  recently  by  the  prestigious  London-based
