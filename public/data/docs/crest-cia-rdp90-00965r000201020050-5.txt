Summary (AI): A Washington Post article (11 March 1982) reports the Reagan administration banned further imports of Libyan oil and accused Qaddafi of plotting to kill Americans in Sudan. It describes the embargo's rationale, its limited economic impact, and the US and allied positions on Libya.
• The presidential order prohibits imports of Libyan crude oil and requires validating licenses for all US exports to Libya except food, agricultural products, medicine and medical supplies.
• US oil imports from Libya had already fallen to about 150,000 barrels daily, about one-fourth of their former peak and about 3 percent of US energy imports.
• Officials said about 350 Americans remained in Libya, down from more than 2,000 the previous year.
• Major allied users of Libyan oil are Greece, Turkey and Italy, and possibly West Germany; allied support was verbal rather than substantive.
• Officials said Qaddafi was also supporting radical governments in Central America and the Caribbean, including Nicaragua, without providing evidence.
• 1982-03-10: Reagan administration formally bans further imports of Libyan oil; accuses Qaddafi of plotting to kill Americans in Sudan
