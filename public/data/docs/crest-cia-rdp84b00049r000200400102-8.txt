SUBJECT: NSC Decisions on Libya 

1. We learned yesterday afternoon, in discussions with NSC staffers, 
that there is some disagreement about precisely what was decided at the 
25 February NSC meeting on Libya. As a result, issuance and implementation of 
the NSDD are being held up. A draft NSDD probably will be coming over for 
your approval or dissent. 

2. There is no disagreement on cessation of oi] imports or controls on 
items on the "national security" list. There are however unresolved details 
with regard to controls over oi] and gas technology and equipment. 

3. The principal question is whether controls will be applied to oil- 
related items on which there are contracts already in hand. Treasury and 
Commerce say no; State says yes. (State's position is that controls should 
apply to everything except goods already licensed; since oil-related goods 
have not to date required licenses, the effect of State's position is to 
cancel existing contracts.) 

4. There also remains some disagreement on whether the Allies should be 
asked to implement similar measures. Although al] concerned agree this would 
be fruitless, some lawyers apparently are arguing that such a request to the 
Allies is required under the provisions of the Export Administration Act.
