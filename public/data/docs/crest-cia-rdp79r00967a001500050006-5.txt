Summary (AI): A 1972 CIA Office of National Estimates memo assesses a possible Israeli raid on Libya after Qadhafi's support for fedayeen and the Munich attack. It covers likely targets (fedayeen training bases, the Nasser air base, formerly El Adem), the risks to about 3,000 US nationals and US oil interests, and concludes the decision would be a close call.
• The memo says there are about 3,000 US nationals in Libya.
• US oil-asset inflows to the US were $552 million in 1970 and $410 million in 1971.
• Libya had not received F-5 aircraft promised to King Idris before the revolution.
• An Israeli raid on fedayeen training bases would need one aerial refuelling; more distant targets would need two.
• 1972-09: Attack on Israeli athletes at the Munich Olympics, praised by the Libyan government, which gave the fedayeen killed a hero's funeral in Tripoli.

2. In the light of these facts, it is clear that

6. An Israeli attack or raid, even of small dimension, 
on Libya could have very serious consequences for the US. 
The Libyan regime is, of course, highly emotional on all 
matters having to do with Israel. Qadhafi and his col- 
leagues are also deeply resentful of the US relationship 
with Israel, and they are angered by the continuing 
refusal of the US to provide a handful of F-5 aircraft 
that were promised to King Idris before the revolution. 

An Israeli attack on Libya utilizing US-provided F-4 
aircraft certainly would inflame Libyan emotions and might 
lead to violent or drastic reactions. There are 3000 or 

so US nationals in Libya, and mob action against foreigners, 
with or without government approval, might well occur. 
Anti-US actions by the government, from the expulsion of 
the small Embassy up to and including nationalization of 
some or even all US oil interests in Libya, would be pos- 
sible under such circumstances. Oil nationalization, even 

if it resulted in total stoppage of Libyan production for
