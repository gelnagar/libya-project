Summary (AI): A 1981 magazine interview with former CIA agent Edwin P. Wilson, who was living in Libya and running an import-export business there while facing U.S. charges. He denies supplying explosives, Redeye missiles, or training terrorists, and says he never met Qaddafi. The piece describes Libyan military training and the Libyan pilot and mechanic contracts.
• Wilson's import-export company was called OSI-SA and was based in Tripoli; he also worked with a Swiss-based firm, SCF MO.
• Wilson said that over five years his operations employed between 150 and 200 people, about half of them American.
• Wilson said the contracts for Libyan pilot and mechanic training ended retroactive to Oct. 1 because the Libyans could fly and maintain their own planes.
• A federal four-count indictment handed down in Washington in 1980 charged Wilson and Terpil with supplying explosives to Qaddafi's government and conspiring to assassinate a Libyan dissident in Egypt.
• Wilson said the timers he supplied to Libya were legal and were for training purposes, with booby-trap timers.
• 1980: Wilson left Libya once in 1980 to go to Malta to expand his business operations.
• 1981: Wilson travelled to Rome for a meeting with the Justice Department and the FBI about his case.
