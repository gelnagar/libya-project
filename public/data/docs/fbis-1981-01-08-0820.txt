LIBYA

PEOPLE'S CONGRESS ADOPTS POLICY RESOLUTIONS 


LDC72200 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1615 GMT 7 Jan 81 


[Text of resolutions of the third session of the Libyan popular conferences of the General 
People's Congress read by the assistant secretary of the General People's Congress] 


[Excerpt] Tripoli 7 Jan (JANA)--Resolutions of the people's conferences in their regular 
third session for 1980, formulated by the general assembly of the people's conferences, 
people's committees, unions, trade unions and professional associations, endorsed by the 
General People's Congress at its sixth regular session from 3-7 January 1981: 


I. Review of the resolutions and recommendations of the people's congresses at their third 
regular session for 1979 which had been endorsed by the General Assembly of the people's 
conference, people's committees, unions, trade unions and professional associations [that 
is], the General People's Congress from 1-6 January 1980. 


The basic people's conferences reviewed their resolutions and recommendations and expressed 
satisfaction over what has been implemented. They stressed implementing what has not been 
done, particularly the resolutions related to the role of the revolutionary practitioner 
and revolutionary agrarian guide as well as the agricultural line and employing teachers. 


II. Internal policy: 


1. Follow up implementation of the budget of transformation [tahawwul] and the adminis- 
trative budget for 1980. 


A. The basic people's congresses express their satisfaction over the completed pro- 
jects and eliminating obstacles and causes of delay. 


B. The people's congresses approved what has been overspent on projects during 1980. 

The people's congresses discussed the 1981-85 plan for economic and social transfor- 
mation according to the best scientific and technical standards and in light of the great 
projects completed in the various economic and social sectors of the 1976-80 plan. 
Concerned about attaining a strategy for the objectives of the 1981-85 plan, the people's 
congresses stress the following: 


i. The necessity of turning Libyan Arab society into a productive socialist one, 
to be carried out in accordance with the third universal theory detailed in the 
Green Book in its three volumes. 


ii. Increasing production efficiency and exploiting all possible capabilities, 
efforts and potentialities in creating efficient production forces. 


iii. Technical education and training should be given special importance in order 
to successfully carry out the transformation plan and to enable the citizen to 
take part in building and developing the country. 


iv. Paying attention and concentrating on bringing about the agrarian revolution 
with the aim of attaining self-sufficiency in crops and basic farm products. 


v. Entering the domain of industrialization, especially carrying out strategic 
heavy industrial projects with greater strides so as to form a firm, productive 
economic base. 


vi. Paying attention to scientific research, promoting technical sciences and 
adopting advanced scientific methods in implementing programs and transformation 
programs. The budget for this purpose, estimated at 18.5 billion dinars, was 
approved for the years 1981-85.

[Presumably point 2] In order to achieve all this the necessary legislation will be issued 
and the general people's committee will be assigned the task of adopting all necessary 
measures to implement the programs and transformation projects leading up to the plan's 
targets in accordance with the specific timetables. It will also deal with the problems 
and difficulties arising during plan implementation in order to realize the total trans- 
formation of all social and economic spheres, particularly the following: 


A. Exempting contractors building strategic and transformation projects from taxes 
and levies. 


B. Stressing the responsibility of the Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah 
citizens for implementing the transformation plan by means of imposing penalties on whom- 
ever hinders the implementation of any project, even if he is in charge of an establish- 
ment implementing a project. 


C. Reorganizing national establishments and contracting bodies so that they can play 
a bigger and more effective role in implementing transformation plan projects. 


D. Prohibiting all agent contracts of any type in building construction, including 
all persons whether they are real or supposed. 


3. The people's conferences discussed the draft budget for the transformation plan for 
1981 and decided to endorse the draft budget within the limit of 3 billion dinars, in 
accordance with annex 2, provided the following are taken into consideration: 


A. Giving priority to implementing projects transferred from 1980 and which have not 
been delayed, particularly productive projects and essential public services. 


B. Prog. omming the implementation of projects which were not included in the first 
year--1981--bud set, in accordance with available funds during the remainder of the plan 
while giving priority to strategic and productive projects. 


C. They decided to endorse the administrative budget for 1981 (annex 3), totalling 
1.05 billion dinars. 


4. Revision of the general housing policy and establishment of a housing bank. The basic 
people's congresses decided the following: 


A. To draw up a housing policy in which the direct role of society is to make 
available free dwellings for those who cannot pay, building houses annexed to public 
projects and the construction of new towns and communities. 

B. Establishment of a housing savings and investment bank which will lend money to 
citizens and other business related to housing. It will be the basic instrument in making 
available a house for the citizen, who must play a direct role in financing the con- 
struction of a dwelling and its consequences.

AFP REPORTS ANNOUNCEMENT OF NUCLEAR PROGRAM 


NCO71755 Paris AFP in English 1748 GMT 7 Jan 81 


[Text] Paris, 7 Jan (AFP)--The prospect of an "Islamic bomb" loomed larger today when 
Libya announced it was launching a full-scale nuclear energy program. 


Libya is to set up its own atomic energy secretariat and build a nuclear research centre, 
officials in Tripoli said. 


Libya's flirting with nuclear energy goes back to 1973 when it set up an atomic energy 
commission with the aim, observers believe, of helping Pakistan develop an "Islamic 
bomb" financed by oil revenues. 


But it was not until 1978 that Libya first took steps to develop its own nuclear program. 
After unsuccessful contacts with France, Canada, Sweden and West Germany, Tripoli finally 
signed an agreement with the Soviet Union in October 1978 for the provision of a 300- 
megawatt nuclear power station. Construction of the station has not yet started, observers 
believe, but the Soviet Union is to provide Libya with a 10 megawatt research reactor 

some time this year. 


In July 1980 Libya also signed an agreement with the International Atomic Energy Agency 
(IAEA) which is responsible for monitoring nuclear installations. 


Meanwhile, Libyan technicians have been sent to Argentina to learn about uranium extraction 
techniques. Experts doubt whether Libyan territory holds much uranium ore, but observers 
here noted that announcement of the nuclear expansion program comes a day after Libya's 
merger with Chad following the civil war in that country. There had been reports of 
uranium deposits in northern Chad, just to the south of Libya, but experts do not believe 
there are important deposits there. 


But for the time Libya and Islamic countries lack nuclear fuel. Tripoli bought 300 tons 
of uranium from Niger in 1979. According to well-informed sources the uranium was then 
shipped to Pakistan.

AL-'UBAYDI EXPLAINS NEW SYSTEM OF PEOPLE'S BUREAUS 


LD081344 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 8 Jan 81 


[Text] Brother ‘Abd al-‘Ati al-'Ubaydi, secretary of the People's Committee of the 
People's Bureau for Foreign Liaison, met this morning with members of the diplomatic 
corps accredited to the Jamahiriyah, following the abolition of the traditional form of 
the foreign secretariat, the transformation of political contacts into people's contacts 
in implementation of the people's democracy which is now being practiced in the 
Jamahiriyah and the marches on the embassies of the Jamahiriyah abroad and their trans- 
formation into people's bureaus. 


In a speech he delivered on the occasion, brother al-'Ubaydi stressed that the Libyan 
Arab people, since the outbreak of the revolution, have asserted the people's authority 
and the role of the people's committees everywhere. They have also upheld the principle 
of the people's democracy in practice not just words. He pointed out that the 

jamahiri [masses] regime which the Libyan Arab people have established through their 
people's congresses, their people's committees, their federations and their vocational 
associations is based on the fact that the people hold authority through the people's 
congresses, the people's committees, and the federations and vocational associations. 
He said that the people's committee for foreign liaison will provide the means for 
contacting the peoples of the world through the people's bureaus abroad and through 

the embassies of the states accredited to the Jamahiriyah. 


He emphasized that the Jamahiriyah is seeking to consolidate friendly relations with 
all the countries and peoples of the world, explaining that changing the Jamahiriyah 
embassies into people's bureaus is the ideal way to achieve this goal. He also 
affirmed the concern of the Jamahiriayh about its relations with sisterly and friendly 
peoples and about consolidating and further promoting such relations.
