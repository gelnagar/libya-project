Summary (AI): A 1982 New York Times article (reproduced in a CIA reading room file) reports that the House Intelligence Committee would hold closed hearings on the CIA's internal inquiry into its links to former agents Edwin P. Wilson and Frank Terpil, who sold their services to Libya's Qaddafi. It describes the 1976-77 inspector general probe and the dismissal of two mid-level employees. No Tripoli sites are named.
• The CIA inspector general's inquiry was conducted in 1976 and 1977 to determine whether officials helped establish a terrorist training project in Libya.
• Wilson and Terpil were indicted in 1980 on charges of illegally shipping explosives to Libya.
• The Justice Department said the two men reached an agreement with Qaddafi in 1976 to sell expertise in intelligence and military matters to help train terrorists.
• The committee's inquiry also examined recruitment of Army Special Forces veterans and Wilson's association with a secret Navy intelligence unit called Task Force 157.
• 1977: Admiral Turner dismissed two middle-level CIA employees after the inspector general found they had helped Wilson establish a terrorist training operation in Libya.
