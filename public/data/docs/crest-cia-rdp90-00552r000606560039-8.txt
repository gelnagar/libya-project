Summary (AI): A 1981 New York Times article reports on the trial in Fort Collins, Colorado, of a former Green Beret charged with shooting Libyan dissident Faisal Zagallai in October 1980. The defendant claimed he was on a CIA mission, and the prosecution suggested he was hired to kill Zagallai for criticizing Qaddafi. This is about Libyan dissidents abroad, not events in Tripoli.
• Tafoya, 47, a former explosives expert in the Army Special Services, said he was carrying a CIA message when he went to Zagallai's apartment posing as a job interviewer.
• Prosecution maintained Tafoya may have been hired to kill Zagallai over his outspoken anti-Qaddafi criticism.
• The jury had nine women and three men and would be sequestered during deliberations.
• Tafoya's account changed: he first said he was sent to rough up Zagallai, later said he was sent by the CIA to deliver a message to stop anti-Qaddafi statements.
• 1980-10-14: Faisal Zagallai, a Libyan graduate student and critic of Muammar el-Qaddafi, was shot; he recovered except for the loss of sight in one eye.
