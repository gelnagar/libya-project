Summary (AI): A December 1981 White House memo, with a Top Secret attachment, tasks U.S. officials to study Soviet responses to possible U.S. military and economic actions against Libya under NSDD 16. It describes Soviet-Libyan military ties, Warsaw Pact and Cuban advisors, and possible Soviet reactions, with no places in Tripoli named.
• National Security Decision Directive Number 16 of December 10, 1981 lists the President's economic and security decisions for Libya.
• Item 8 of the NSDD requires the Secretaries of State and Defense, the National Security Adviser and the DCI to report on steps to deter Soviet military involvement.
• Terms of reference were produced by an interagency process culminating in a Libyan Task Force meeting on December 15, 1981.
• Study topics include Warsaw Pact and Cuban advisors in Libya, Soviet anchorage or base attempts, Libyan overbuilding of equipment, and possible Soviet reactions to a U.S. oil cutoff or export embargo.
• Inputs on Soviet reactions were due to the White House by close of business December 23, 1981.

SUBJECT: National Security Decision Directive Number 16 
for Libya 

National Security Decision Directive Number 16 of December 10, 
1981 lists the President's economic and security decisions for 
Libya. 

Item 8 of the NSDD states that: 

The Secretaries of State and Defense as 
well as the Assistant to the President for National 
Security Affairs and the Director of Central Intelligence 
shall report to the National Security Council on diplomatic 
and military steps required to deter Soviet military 
involvement in light of Presidential decisions. 

At Tab A are the Terms of Reference for the study of Soviet 
Responses to U.S. Military Actions Against Libya. These Terms 
of Reference are the product of an interagency process, culmin- 
ating in a meeting of the Libyan Task Force on December 15, 1981. 

It is requested that your inputs on Soviet reactions be 
sent to me by COB December 23, 1981.
