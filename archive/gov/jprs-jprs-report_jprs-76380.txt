Since that date, Tapline's installations had been working to provide the 
Lebanese government services of supplying crude oil and importing oil pro- 
ducts. More than once, the American company tried to close down the line 
which supplies Saudi oil to Jordan and Lebanon and liquidate its interests 
in order to avoid annual losses of at least $35 million, but the Saudi 
government opposed that. 


Although Tapline, by virtue of agreements concluded with the Lebanese gov- 
ernment, is obligated to supply the crude oil requirements of the Medreco 
Refinery in al-Zahrani, which refines 17,000 barrels a day, it is not ob- 
ligated to supply the Tripoli Refinery in the north with 33,000 barrels a 
day of Saudi oil. The latter, through its output, covers 70 percent of 
Lebanese consumption. The agreement both parties concluded on is a tempor- 
ary one and concerns each transaction separately. 


fefore the end of last year, the Aramco Company, which is in charge of pro-

would be compelled to stop supplying the Tripoli Refinery with oil at the

Lebanon tried to reach agreement with Iraq over the resumption of pumping 
to Tripoli, and the prime minister, Dr Salim al-Huss, discussed the issue 
with the president of Iraq, Saddam Husayn. Detailed discussions then oc- 
curred between Lebanese and L[raqi officials. However, they did not pro- 
duce results, for numerous reasons, some to do with politics and security, 
some related to the Lebanese war, and others related to the Middle East 
crisis as a whole, from which it has not yet been possible to separate the 
Lebanese crisis.
