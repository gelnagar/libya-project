Summary (AI): A 1987 Los Angeles Times article reports that State Department counterterrorism chief L. Paul Bremer said Libya had stepped up terrorist planning, and that Libyan-backed terrorism dropped after the April 1986 U.S. bombing of Tripoli and Benghazi. The piece is mainly about foreign policy and terrorism, with little on Tripoli itself.
• Bremer said Libya had stepped up terrorist planning in the previous six to eight weeks, possibly setting the stage for a new escalation of attacks.
• Bremer named Libya, Syria and Iran as the gravest state supporters of terrorism on the State Department list; he said Cuba and South Yemen had done very little recently.
• Libyan-backed terrorism declined sharply after the April 1986 U.S. bombing of Tripoli and Benghazi, and Libya was especially quiet in May, June and July 1986.
• Intelligence picked up from about the beginning of August 1986 indicated Libyans were preparing a terrorist attack, reportedly on a Pan Am airliner in September.
• Administration officials confirmed in October 1986 that Poindexter had signed an August 1986 memo outlining a deception program to keep Kadafi off balance.
• 1986-04: U.S. bombing of two Libyan cities, Tripoli and Benghazi, after which Libyan-backed terrorism declined sharply, according to Bremer
