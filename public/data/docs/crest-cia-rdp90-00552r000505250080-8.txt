Summary (AI): A Washington Post article (28 February 1982) by Bob Woodward reports the CIA concluded an assassination attempt was made on Qaddafi in December 1981, probably carried out by army officers without foreign support. It also notes Qaddafi's public reappearance on 5 January in Tripoli and his denial of hit squads sent to kill US officials.
• CIA concluded an assassination attempt against Qaddafi occurred about two months before the article, probably carried out by one or more officers who had just returned from the Libyan invasion of Chad.
• Qaddafi's driver was reportedly wounded in the shooting.
• An unconfirmed report said Qaddafi had lost confidence in his security forces and replaced them with outsiders, in some cases Syrians.
• Qaddafi denied on 6 December 1981 in an ABC interview that hit squads had been sent to assassinate President Reagan.
• A State Department report in early January said Qaddafi had gone to Moscow for treatment; other US sources doubted this.
• 1981-12-19: Reported assassination attempt on Qaddafi while he was visiting a military headquarters area; initial reports said eastern army commander Col. Khalifa Khadir was shot and killed by Qaddafi's guards, and Qaddafi was probably nicked by a bullet in the jaw.
