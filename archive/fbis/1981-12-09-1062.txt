ARAFAT STILL COMMITTED TO CEASE-FIRE IN SOUTH 
PMO081637 Beirut AS-SAFIR in Arabic 4 Dec 81 p 3 
[Unattributed report] 


Excerpts] An expanded meeting of the joint leadership of the Lebanese National 
Movement and the Palestinian revolution was held at noon yesterday at the headquarters 
9f§ the Central Political Council. The meeting was attended by PLO Executive Committee 


s 


thairman Yasir ‘Arafat. 


The participants discussed the situation in the south. ‘Arafat reaffirmed "the 
Palestinian resistance's adherence to its decision in relation to the cease-fire 
and to refraining from carrying out operations from Lebanese territory in order not 
to give Israel a pretext for taking military action against the south.” 


The participants also assessed the security situation in the light of the steps taken 
and measures carried out under the supervision of the higher security committee and in 
coordination with the security bureau of the National Movement. They expressed satis- 
faction with "the atmosphere now prevailing in western Beirut," and called for the 
"extension of these measures to cover the north, south and Al-Biqa'." 

Sources close to the meeting expect a tripartite Syrian-Palestinian-Lebanese meeting 
to be held to discuss recent developments and consolidate the nationalist front to 
vent any repercussions from developments in the Arab region.

GF021710 Manama CULF DAILY NEWS in English 2 Dec 81 p 10

That description comes from the man behind the launch, Information Minister Tariq

DECLINE IN CRUDE OIL PRODUCTION REPORTED 


GF081634 Manama WAKH in Arabic 1220 GMT 8 Dec 81 


[Text] Abu Dhabi, 8 Dec (WAKH) -- Crude oil production in Abu Dhabi during the first half 

of 1981 was approximately 216 million barrels. This figure is 13.7 percent lower than 
production figures for the same period in 1980, when production reached 251 million barrels. 
This decline can be traced to the UAE's policy to reduce production to protect the oilfields. 


In Dubayy, oil production during the first half of 1981 was about 65 million barrels. 
This has been reported in a report issued by the Central Bank. It points out that crude 
oil exports during the first 6 months of 1981 amounted to about 209 million barrels from 
Abu Dhabi and 62 million barrels from Dubayy. 


Forty percent of the total oil production from Abu Dhabi and Dubayy was exported to Japan. 
It was followed by France, the Netherlands, the United States, the FRG, Spain and Great 
Britain.
