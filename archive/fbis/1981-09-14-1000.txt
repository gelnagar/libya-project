LIBYA

TURKISH WORKERS DEMONSTRATE AGAINST U.S. 12 SEP 


LD121520 Tripoli JANA in English 1430 GMT 12 Sep 81 


[Text ] Tripoli, 12 Sep (JANA)--Turkish workers in Benghazi today demonstrated 
in protest against the recent U.S. aggression against the Jamahiriyah's 
people. The demonstrators traversed the city's main streets and ended in front of the 
Turkish Consulate, where demonstrators burned the American flag as an expression of 
solidarity between the Turkish and Libyan Arab people. 


The demonstrators also carried placards condemning the U.S. attack and calling for the 
quick withdrawal of American bases from the Mediterranean region. The following statement 


was also issued at the end of the march: 


"The Turkish community in Benghazi condemns the U.S. aggression against the Jamahiriyah's 
people and the vile conspiracies directed against the Great Al-Fatah Revolution's leader." 


STATEMENT ON TERRITORIAL DISPUTE WITH MALTA 


LD132032 Tripoli JANA in English 1716 GMT 13 Sep 81 


["Foreign Liaison Bureau Statement on Continental Shelf Demarcation"--JANA headline] 


[Text] Tripoli, 13 Sep (JANA)--The Jamahiriyah's Foreign Liaison Bureau issued a state- 
ment regarding the agreement signed between Libya and Malta on May 23, 1976, referring 
the demarcation of the continental shelf shared between both bountries, to the Interna- 
tional Court of Justice, so that agreement may be reached without difficulty on the 
delineation of the region.

The statement said the country's basic people's congress ratified this agreement, which 
was later endorsed by the General People's Congress on April lst, 1981. ‘the basic 
people's congresses also called for the prohibition of all drilling operations in 
disputed waters until the International Court of Justice issues its views on the matter. 


The statement said the Foreign Liaison Bureau has prepared a ratified document regarding 
this matter in accordance with international custom. It pointed out, however, that 
Malta only claims to have ratified such a document. 


The Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah has made known its desire on all levels 
and in all world bodies to finalise the exchange of the ratification documents whether 
in Malta or the Jamahiriyah. The personal representative of the United Nations 
secretary general and the Government of Malta have been informed of the Jamahiriyah's 
desire. 


The Foreign Liaison Bureau's statement said Malta has persistently placed obstacles for 
the exchange of the ratification documents. It has called for the amendment of the 
Libyan document as it refers to the decisions made by the people's congresses. It has 
further called for the notification of the international court's register of this 
special agreement. These two conditions are legally unacceptable. 


The statement added that in spite of the artificial furors persistently raised by Malta 
on all levels, the Jamahiriyah, pursuing a policy in harmony with international law and 
calling for the exchange of the ratified documents, sent a delegation to Valetta in the 
period March 23-27, 1981 for the exchange of these documents. Yet the Republic of Malta 
refused the exchange of documents on the grounds that the ratified agreement refers to 
the basic people's congresses decisions. 

A Foreign Liaison Bureau delegation carried out a second visit to Valetta for the same 
purpose on July 24, 1981. But the Republic of Malta once again refused to finalise the 
legal proceedings for the exchange of the ratified documents. 


The Foreign Liaison Bureau statement said the Government of Malta has persistently sought 
to drag the legal exchange of ratified documents into political argument and controversy 
dictated by domestic and foreign circumstances that are of no relation to the legal 
exchange. The statement added that the Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah, 
noting these facts, once again reaffirms its readiness to carry out the legal exchange 

of the ratified documents. The Government of Malta shall bear the consequences of any 
delay or the fabrication of any positions or conditions leading to the continued delay 

in the legal exchange of the ratified documents as constituted.
