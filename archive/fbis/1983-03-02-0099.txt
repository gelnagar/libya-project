LIBYA

MOROCCAN MOTIVES IN SAHARA QUESTIONED 


LDO10155 Tripoli Voice of Arab Homeland in Arabic 2115 GMT 28 Feb 83 
{Unattributed commentary] 


[Excerpts] It is not a falsehood nor an exaggeration when we frankly say that Moroccan 
King Hassan II is the main and direct cause of the state of Arab collapse and ruin. 

The Moroccan king is the one who is directly responsible for including the Arab area in 
the U.S. sphere of influence. He is the one who paved the way for the Zionist death 
train and who caused the bloody tragedy that has fallen on the Lebanese and Palestinian 
people. He is the architect of the plans of liquidation and capitulation and the one 
who arranged the Fes summit of. shame and treason. He is the U.S. key that led to the 
fall of Egypt into the Camp David enclosure. 


This regime is also being used today as a tool to liquidate the revolutionary eruption 
which was manifested by the revolution of the Saharan people against Spanish colonialism 
in the Saguia El Hamra and Oued Ed Dahab The Sararan people nearly succeeded in reaping 
the fruit of their revolution but the Moroccan regime quickly robbed them of victory 

by occupying the country with e excuse that it is part of Moroccan soil. If this 
claim was true, where was this regime when the Saharan war of liberation was in full 
swing? Why did it not make this claim when Spanish colonialism ruled the Saguia El 
Hamra and Oued Ed Dahab? 


V. 2 Mar 83 Q 3 NORTH AFRECA 


The issue here is not one of ownership but of the existence of a truly liberating 
revolution on this land that is hostile to colonialist influence and decaying reaction 
and that is calling for progress and unity on the basis of militant pan-Arab liberating 
foundations. This revolution was able to change the course of life and history in that 
area through the growth of its revolutionary and liberating tide in the Arab Maghreb, 
with the goal to destroy all U.S. imperialist bridgeheads and hireling reactionary 
states, headed by the Moroccan royalist regime. 


One question remains to be answered: Where are both the overt and clandestine opposition 
in Morocco? Where is it in the face of these lunatic, foolhardy, and reckless policies 
that Hassan II follows? If Moroccan opposition dares not even to say no in the face of 
this regime -- and it is a constitutional wordin any event -- it is better that it 
dissolves itself and goes underground to become the clandestine opposition and demolish 
the structure of this regime by means of popular revolution. This is the basic and 
decisive solution to all the national Moroccan problems and their pan-Arab dimensions.
