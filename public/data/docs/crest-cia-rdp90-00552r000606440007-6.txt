Summary (AI): A New York Times article (1 November 1981, datelined Washington, 31 October) reports that Libya relied on American and British pilots and technicians, arranged by former CIA agent Edwin P. Wilson, to fly and maintain its Air Force aircraft during the intervention in Chad. Wilson was reportedly a fugitive living in Tripoli.
• Libyan Air Force reportedly had about 20 CH-47 Chinook helicopters.
• Wilson reportedly supervised a 'clean room' used for assembly of conventional and nuclear weapons, per American intelligence officials; Libya reportedly did not possess nuclear weapons.
• Wilson was indicted in 1980 by a Federal grand jury on charges of illegally shipping explosives to Libya and reindicted in October 1981.
• Libya's C-130 Hercules cargo planes were flown and maintained by British pilots and mechanics per the article.
• Justice Department officials said recruiting Americans for Libyan forces may not violate federal law because the recruits were paid by a Swiss company controlled by Wilson.
• 1980: Libya's intervention in Chad began late the previous year (1980), resupplied by Western-recruited pilots and technicians.
• 1976: Wilson left US government employ in 1976; he and Frank E. Terpil signed a contract to sell their expertise to Libya.
