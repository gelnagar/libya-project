LIBYA

AL-QADHDHAFI REPORTEDLY SEEKS DIALOGUE WITH U.S. 


AU261443 Rome ANSA in English 0800 GMT 26 Oct 81 


[Text] Milan, 26 Oct (ANSA) -- Libyan leader, Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi, is seeking to 
re-open dialogue with the United States Government through probes "still going on" in 
Italy, Yugoslavia, and Algeria, according to Italian television journalist Sandro 
Paternostro. 


Writing in yesterday's edition of the Milan daily, IL GIORNO, Paternostro, who was 
anchorman in Tripoli for an interview with Al-Qadhdhafi for Italy's state radio-TV 
network, Rai, on Friday also reported that the Libyan leader is keen to be invited on 
an official visit to Italy. According to Paternostro, "despite that he said on television, 
through Italy's diplomatic channels too, Al-Qadhdhafi is trying to reopen relations 
with the United States." Describing some of Al-Qadhdhafi's declarations on the United 
States and the situation in the Mediterranean as "disconcerting" and "stupefying," 
Paternostro asserted, however, that there is a "key for reading them." If the Libyan 
leader had not repeated the "ritual phrases of polemic against American imperialism," 
he said, "you can be sure that extremists and fanatical young Libyans would sooner or 
later be shouting about treason in the streets." In fact, the journalist wrote, "I can 
say with no fear of denial that probes are still under way" in Italy, Yugoslavia, and 
Algeria for an improvement of relations with the United States. 


He would like to reopen dialogue with Washington, Paternostro claimed, "but to think it 
would be easy for him to throw the Soviet and East German advisers out of Libyan now, 

as As-Sadat did from Egypt nine years ago, is puerile." Paternostro also offered a 
rebuttal of a common image offered by the Wesern media of the Libyan leader as a dangerous 
dictator. 


"Al-Qadhdhafi is in no way a bloody »r paranoid tyrant. He is not Hitler, Stalin, or the 
great inquisitor, Torquemada," the journalist wrote. 


"He is a libyan An-Nasir, with all the qualities and defects of the charismatic and 
controversial Egyptian colonel." 


In other points, Paternostro described Al-Qadhdhafi's relations with the Italian ambassador 
in Tripoli, Alessandro Quaroni, as “open and positive." "Al-Qadhdhafi is keen, and 

very keen too, to be invited to Italy by President Sandro Pertini," added Paternostro. 

"The problem is, however, that he colonel's aides have let it be known that Al-Qadhdhafi... 
would like to address the crowds in a public square." According to Paternostro, "it seems 
that Al-Qadhdhafi's wishes have contributed to Pertini's relatively frigid attitude." 


"But Al-Qadhdhafi, on the other hand, is convinced that Reagan told Pertini: "Sandro, 
if you're my friend, don't invite Mu'ammar."

AB270842 Nouakchott Domestic Service in French 0700 GMT 27 Oct 81 


[Text] Dr ‘Amr Ahmad al-Maqsi, secretary to the Libyan People's Committee in charge of 
light industries, yesterday gave a verbal message from Colonel al-Qadhdhafi to the 

head of state. The message concerned the present Arab situation and the role which 
Mauritania and Libya must play. He delivered the message to the head of state during an 
audience at which cooperation between Mauritania and Libya was discussed. Also present 
were the minister for mines and energy, Mohamed el Moktar Ould Zamel, the Mauritanian 
ambassador to Tripoli and the secretary to the Libyan People's Committee in Nou ckchott. 

It will be recalled that Dr ‘Amr Ahmad al-Maqsi is heading his country's delegation to the 
meeting of the Maurtanian-Libyan joint cooperation commission which has just ended in 
Nouakchott. 


JANA CITES REPORT ON MUBARAK COUNCIL MEMBER 


LD261240 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1105 GMT 26 Oct 81 


[Text] Tripoli, 26 Oct (JANA) -- Kuwaiti paper AL-WATAN said that President Husni Mubarak 
of Egypt has formed a consultative council comprising three former prime ministers: 
"Abd al-'Aziz Hijazi, Mamduh Salim and Mustafa Khalil. 


The fact that ‘Abd al-'Aziz Hijazi has joined this council as an effective member is regarded 
as confirmation of a major tendency of the regime to benefit from a man that adopted positive 
attitudes toward Egypt and the Arabs, reflecting his loyalty and dedication to the interest 
of his people and nation. As a matter of fact, ‘Abd al-'Aziz Hijazi tried to put As-Sadat 

in his place and to prevent him from making the Egyptian economy an easy prey to foreign 
monopolist companies and the creeping parasite class. 


Hijazi also entered into a struggle with As-Sadat during which he tried to make him abandon 
the implementation of the open-door policy that harmed the Egyptian economy. Hijazi 

also holds well-known positions: He withstood the trends toward selling Egypt off to the 
West. 


Undoubtedly, there is a great deal of satisfaction about this right choice by the Egyptian 
side, making ‘Abd al-'Aziz Hijazi an effective member of this council.
