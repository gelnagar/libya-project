Summary (AI): A 1985 Washington Post article reports U.S. intelligence claims that dissident Libyan officers made two assassination or coup attempts on Qaddafi in early and late March 1985, followed by executions of dozens of officers. It also describes the regime's reliance on revolutionary committees, the East German-built security force, and economic strain from the U.S. embargo.
• Libyan armed forces reportedly numbered 73,000 members.
• Two Tripoli University students were publicly hanged the previous year (1984), fueling dissent; two participants in those executions were later found slain.
• Qaddafi reportedly turned to East Germans to help create his personal security force after an earlier assassination attempt; guards are believed to come from outside the military and be rotated regularly.
• Libya was producing about a third of its OPEC quota of 1.1 million barrels per day due to lack of U.S.-built spare parts, according to diplomats in Tripoli.
• The article reports a U.S. State Department statement that Libya's position on improving relations depended on reversal of support for international terrorism.
• 1985-03: First violent coup attempt by conservative officers against Qaddafi; reportedly as many as 15 officers executed in retaliation.
• 1985-03: Second reported assault on a convoy thought to carry Qaddafi; at least 60 more officers reportedly executed. (60 killed)
