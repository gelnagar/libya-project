Summary (AI): A Newsweek article (7 April 1986, reproduced in a CIA reading-room copy) describes Gaddafi's rhetoric after the US Gulf of Sidra operation, reported Libyan plans for new terrorist attacks on Americans, and the US decision-making behind Operation Prairie Fire. Kaddafi spoke to a crowd at the Bab al-Aziziya barracks in Tripoli.
• Libyan patrol boats were sunk; the fleet's best guess was 150 Libyans killed, though the fleet never determined the exact figure.
• Kaddafi claimed his missiles downed three American warplanes; US had no casualties per Newsweek.
• Newsweek reported CIA reports that Libyan agents had 35 American targets under surveillance overseas.
• Reagan ordered American residents to leave Libya and imposed a trade embargo earlier in 1986 after the Rome and Vienna attacks that killed 15 air travelers.
• Operation Prairie Fire planning began in early February 1986; the decision was made at a White House meeting on 14 March 1986.
• 1986-04: Gaddafi speech at Bab al-Aziziya barracks in Tripoli boasting of the 'line of death' repelling the US Sixth Fleet; Libyan soldiers slaughtered a cow painted with 'Reakn'.
• 1986-03-24: US Sixth Fleet operation in the Gulf of Sidra: Libyan SAM-5 missiles fired at US planes, US forces silenced the air-defence battery and sank at least two Libyan patrol boats. (150 killed)
