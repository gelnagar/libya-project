Summary (AI): A 1981 article summarizing Seymour Hersh's New York Times Magazine report says former CIA agents Edwin Wilson and Frank Terpil helped Gaddafi's Libya obtain explosives, timers, and training for terrorism. It mentions a Libyan training program with a laboratory near Tripoli for making assassination bombs disguised as ashtrays.
• Gaddafi reportedly wanted thousands of timers to set off explosives, ostensibly to clear Israeli mines from the 1973 October War, though no such mines were found in Libyan waters or territory.
• American Electronic Laboratories, Inc. of Falls Church, Va., agreed to build prototypes for Gaddafi's order.
• About 40,000 lbs of the high explosive RDX were flown to Libya in 55-gallon drums marked industrial solvent.
• Terpil provided arms, explosives and torture devices under a $3.2 million contract with Idi Amin's Uganda government.
• Mulcahy feared Gaddafi might arm a Redeye missile with a nuclear warhead.
• 1981-06: Reagan Administration ordered Libya's diplomatic mission in Washington to close and leave the US, per the article's account of the previous month.
