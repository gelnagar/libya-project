Summary (AI): A New York Times article (30 April 1984) reports US plans to consult allies to pressure Qaddafi after the London Libyan Embassy shooting of a British police constable and the diplomatic break with Britain. It covers US sanctions, Qaddafi's alleged assassination attempts, Libyan involvement in Chad and Nicaragua, and the oil revenue drop; the document is not about Tripoli itself.
• Libya had about 5,000 troops in Chad occupying the northern half of the country, per the article.
• Libya's oil export revenues reportedly fell from $22 billion in 1980 to $10 billion in 1983.
• Italy had about 20,000 workers in Libya and Britain about 8,000, per an unnamed official.
• A CIA plan for covert action against Qaddafi was reportedly drafted in 1981 and dropped after opposition from the House intelligence committee.
• 1984-04: Shooting of a British police constable by someone inside the Libyan Embassy during demonstrations by Libyan exiles; Britain severed diplomatic ties with Libya the following Sunday (1 killed)
• 1981: United States expelled all Libyan diplomats, barred Libyan oil imports and ordered Americans to leave Libya
• 1980: American Embassy in Tripoli was burned down
