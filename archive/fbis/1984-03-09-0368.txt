LIBYA

fi 8 March Speech on Syrian Anniversary 
LD082138 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1730 GMT 
8 Mar 84 


[Speech by Colonel Mu‘ammar al-Qadhdhafi at celebration in 
People’s Hall, Tripoli, on 8 March to mark 21st anniversary of 
Syria’s 8 March revolution — live] 


[Excerpts] We are meeting here today to commemorate the 
anniversary of the 9 March revolution, which destroyed the 
forces of secession in Syria, and which was the revolutionary 
reaction to secession which hit the most cherished aspirations of 
the Arab nation on that black Thursday, 28 September, 1961. 


From Tripoli we extend our greetings to Damascus, Syria, the 
pulsating heart of Arabism. We extend our congratulations on 
this day to our steadfast Brother Hafiz al-Asad. We extend our 
greetings and feelings of closeness to the soldiers of the heroic 
Syrian Army who stand fast on the lines of confrontation at 
occupied Golan and the Al-Biga’ where the battles of Arab 
nationalism, the honorable battles of Arab nationalism, are 
taking place. 


We must continue the task of the 8 March revolution. It is the 
task of restoring the unity that was severed by force. But the 
secessionist force that severed the unity was destroyed on 8 
March. The work must continue, and the struggle, the march, 
and the revolution must continue until unity is restored between 
Egypt and Syria — and not only between Syria and Egypt but 
also between Syria, Egypt, Libya and the other countries of the 
Arab nation. [applause and chants] 


Today we celebrate an anniversary which is not a regional 
anniversary. It is a revolutionary movement staged in Syria to 
strike at forces that plotted against the unity of the Arab nation. 
This occasion, when all businesses in the Syrian and Libyan 
regions are closed, is not a regional occasion and it is not a Syrian 
occasion. It is a pan-Arab occasion. [applause] 


Brothers, we must not content ourselves with remembering the 8 
March anniversary. We must achieve the objectives of 8 March 
which are: Destruction of the secession and dismantling of the 
borders between the countries of the Arab nation. It means the 
destruction of the regionalism in which we live now. 


We in Syria, Egypt, and Libya as well as the other countries of 
the Arab nation — particularly in Syria — are called upon to 
realize unity for which the 8 March revolution was staged. 
[applause and chants] 


For the sake of history, I must say that work for unity has never 
stopped. Syria has not contented itself with the destruction of 
secession on 8 March but went further to achieve unity. Another 
attempt was made to unify Syria, Egypt and Iraq after 8 March. 
But the secessionist and fascist forces plotted against it and 
aborted this unity in 1964, even before it was born. But unionist 
forces in Syria continued to work for the restoration of unity to 
the shattered homeland.

A few months before ‘Abd an-Nasir died, attempts were made 
to go from Tripoli to Damascus and declare a federation of Arab 
republics [UAR], or something similar. This was proposed by 
‘Abd an-Nasir when we, and the leaders of the Syrian region and 
the Egyptian region met in Libya after the revolution. [applause] 


But ‘Abd an-Nasir’s death and the plotting of the reactionary 
and agent forces against Egypt obstructed this great work. 


After that, Syria marched on the road of unity and the UAR was 
created among the Syrian, Egyptian, and Libyan regions. On Ist 
September, a referendum was made on this unity among the 
peoples of the three regions. The millions rallied in columns to 
say yes to the UAR. 


But the vanquished As-Sadat, the agent of Zionism, plotted 
against the UAR, violated the UAR constitution, and dishonored 
the oath he had taken before the people’s assembly of the three 
countries. 


Where are the millions in Syria, Egypt, and Libya who rallied on 
1 September and said yes to the UAR. Where are these masses 
now? Why do not they demand the revival of this federation? 
Why do not they defend this federation, which was created by 
the will of the masses? Why are there no constitutional courts 
held in Egypt, Syria, and Libya to try those who violated the 
UAR constitution? 


I ask the masses in Egypt, Libya, and Syria to defend this 
federation, which is being sought to bury. The UAR was made 
by the will of the masses. As-Sadat betrayed it and tried to bury 
it. He himself was buried but the UAR must not be buried. 
Consequently, I now ask the Egyptian people themselves and the 
men of law in Egypt to raise constitutional cases in the courts — 
that is if there are any courts in Egypt, if there is any constitution 
in Egypt, if there is any respect for the people’s will in Egypt. 


I know what I am saying constitutes some challenge to the regime 
in Egypt and a provocation to the Egyptiai people and army at 
one and the same time. However if there are such institutions, 
they should try the former and latter regimes in Egypt for 
violating the UAR constitution. 


When we in the front of steadfastness and confrontation met in 
Tripoli after the betrayal of the defunct and buried as-sadat, we 
did not expell Egypt from the UAR, but as-sadat himself was 
expelled from the UAR council. Constitutionally, legally, and in 
popular and Arab terms, Egypt is still a member of the UAR. 
We declare now that we adhere to the UAR constitution and we 
will implement everything that the UAR constitution provides 
for in respect of Egypt and the internal events in Egypt. This is 
because the UAR constitution gives UAR members the right to 
intervene in the internal affairs of any other member country that 
deviates from the course of the federation, harms the UAR, 
violates the UAR constitution, and plots against the UAR. 


The UAR constitution gives UAR member countries — even to 
one of the three countries forming the UAR — the right to act. 
Should the other countires be unable to act, then the constitution 
gives that country the right to intervene even in the internal 
affairs to safeguard the unity of the UAR. This is a legal and
