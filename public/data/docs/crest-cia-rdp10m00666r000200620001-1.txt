Summary (AI): A April 12, 1986 White House memo transmits NSDD 224, which directs U.S. pre-emptive air strikes on terrorism-related targets inside Libya by dawn April 15, 1986, after the West Berlin bombing of April 5, 1986 was linked to Libya. The directive says strikes should avoid Libyan economic targets and civilians, and keeps two carrier battle groups in the Mediterranean.
• NSDD 224 is dated April 12, 1986 and is addressed to the Vice President, Secretary of State, Secretary of Defense, DCI and Chairman of the Joint Chiefs.
• The directive says initial strikes should not include Libyan economic targets; planning for later economic targets should proceed if needed.
• Two aircraft carrier battle groups were to remain in the Mediterranean until otherwise directed.
• A Special Emissary was to be sent to London, Paris, Bonn, Rome, Madrid and Ottawa.
• Document is partly sanitized; several paragraphs are withheld.
• 1986-04-05: Bombing in West Berlin, which the directive says has been directly linked to the Libyan People's Bureau in East Berlin and the Libyan Government in Tripoli.
• 1986-04-15: Deadline set by NSDD 224 for U.S. pre-emptive air strikes on selected terrorist-related targets inside Libya.

Compelling evidence implicates Libya in numerous terrorist 
attacks over an extended period of time. Most recently the 
bombing in West Berlin on April 5, 1986 has been directly linked 
to the Libyan People's Bureau in East Berlin and the Libyan 
Government in Tripoli. We have further evidence that the Libyans 
have targetted U.S. citizens and interests for indiscriminate 
terrorist attacks aimed at killing the maximum number of innocent 

men, women, and children. 25X11

The United States has pursued every public, diplomatic, and 
economic opportunity to persuade the Libyan leadership to stop 
these acts of aggression. Despite these measures, Libyan-directed 
and supported terrorist attacks continue to kill innocent Americans, 
and additional attacks are being planned for imminent execution. 

The United States must, therefore, take more forceful measures in 
self-defense to stop these attacks and to deter Libya and other 
states from supporting and directing international terrorisn. We 
must demonstrate to the Libyan leadership that such criminal 
attacks cannot be conducted without consequence. (TS) 

Because the evidence of Libyan complicity in the Berlin attack is 
so compelling and the information on future attacks is so threaten- 
ing, I hereby direct that the United States take appropriate 
pre-emptive action, as permitted under international law, not 

later than dawn Tuesday, April 15, 1986. The objective of our 
action is to inflict serious damage to the infrastructure that 
Qadhafi uses to provide direction and support for global terrorism, 
while limiting the damage to those elements opposed to Qadhafi 

and his policies. Accordingly, the following actions recommended 
by the NSPG are directed: 

-- U.S. military units will move immediately to undertake air 
strikes at selected terrorist-related targets inside Libya.

Chiefs of Staff will ensure that all possible measures are 
taken to preserve the security of these activities and 
reduce the possibility of disclosing our intentions to Libya 
or the Soviet Union. The Secretary of Defense, in 
coordination with the Secretary of State and the Director of
