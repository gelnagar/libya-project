LIBYA

AZ-ZAHF AL-AKHDAR CRITICIZES MOROCCAN REGIME 


LD232300 Tripoli JANA in English 1843 GMT 23 Aug 82 
{Quotation marks as received] 


[Text] Tripoli, Dhulqidah 4, August 23 -- Jamahiriyah News Agen~ -- The newspaper 
AZ-ZAHF AL-AKHDAR, mouthpiece of the revolutionary committees, t y published an 
editorial entitled "The Moroccan King's Campaign of Oppression." in it, the paper 
analysed the deteriorating economic, military and political conditions under the 
reactionary monarchic regime. 


It said, "We are viewing with a mixture of sorrow, pain and horror the Moroccan king, 
the commander of the faithfuls' criminal war against the people of the Western Sahara, 
all in a bid to build the Moroccan throne.... 


There is much sorrow for the loss of tens of thousands of victims in the Western 
Saharan war....Anyone with revolutionary consciousness will be taken aback by the 
savagery of the Moroccan king's war against the Sahara under the false banner of 
annexing the Sahara to the ‘mother country.' 


Following an analysis of the conditions, causes and goals of the Western Sahara war, 
we can arrive at several conclusions. 


Firstly, economic ambitions: 


Since the sixties, the Morccan king, his majesty the amir of believers, who succeeded 
the throne with U.S. imperialism's help, had his eyes on the natural resources of the 
Sagui el Hamra and Rio de Oro. 


He always kept his ties with the Spanish Government that colonized the Sahara and built 
special relations with Spain and relied on Spanish technical skill to build his 
deterdorating economy and in a desperate attempt, since the seventies, to face the mass 
upheaval against his regime. The king was also hoping to get some military assistance 
through his relations with the Spanish Government.

He also tried, during that period, to avoid a confrontation with his neighbours -- Algeria 
and Mauritania, during late President Boumediene and Ould Dada of Mauritania. He became 
extremely hostile to the Algerian people, but stretched his bloody hands to Ould Dada. 

But luckily, the National Salvation Government had rescued the people of Mauritania and took 
the country out of this mess that was orchestrated by the king and implemented by the 
deposed Ould Dada. Such steps taken by Hassan are aimed at deepening the rift between 

the different Arab countries. 


Secondly, the artificial creation of the war to divert the Moroccan people's attention from 
domestic conditions: It is worth noting that the Moroccan king launched his criminal war 
to divert the Moroccan people's attention from the worsening domestic situation which 
threatens to explode. 


On the political level, there is growing anger and disdain against the king and his coterie 
for the following domestic and external reasons: 


-- The imprisonment of tens of thousands of political opponents for several years and 
their deprivation from the most basic human rights. The families of political prisoners 
detained in Kenitra's central prison sent an open letter to the Morrocan king's minister 
of justice calling on him to release their sons who have been convicted to long periods in 
jail stretching to life imprisonment for committing the "high treason 
own beliefs and differing with the king and his government's policies.

-- The corruption and corrupting influence of the king's entourage. 


-- The pro-Western, pro-American policy of the Moroccan regime. As a result of this 
policy, Morocco has been transformed into an advanced imperialist post with military 
installations in: 


1. Kenitra military base. The base was established during the second world war, but the 
Americans developed it during Hassan's reign and it is now being used by the U.S. Department 
of Defence, U.S. Air Force and the Sixth Fleet. 


2. (Bou Kanadel) military base. This base is the most vital to the Reagan administration 
which is constantly trying to spread its hegemony over the world's peoples and robbing them 
of their resources. The base, operated by the U.S. Navy, is used for radar and communica- 
tions. 


3. Sidi Benyahia base. The base has giant ammunition stores and an advanced early warning 
and communications station. 


4. Giant military barracks capable of accomodating more than 30,000 soldiers, and training 
grounds for mercenaries, or as they are called by the Americans "Special Forces," for anti- 
guerilla warfare, or, as is understood through our revolutionary ideology, for fighting 
against those who are struggling for the liberation of their people and their salvation. 


In accordance with an agreement signed on April 19, 1966, the (Al Nouaser) airport in 
Casablanca can be used in times of world crises (i.e. when the Sherefian family or other 
collaborationist reactionary regimes sympathetic to the U.S. are threatened) by the American 
administration. 


The Moroccan regime has also passed extraordinary laws placing these bases, which are 
part of Moroccan territory, under the jurisdiction of the U.S. Defense Department. 


Furthermore, there is public indignation regarding the presence of more than 3,400 military 
advisers who h-ve been granted diplomatic immunity. In other words, these advisers are not 
subject to Moroccan law.

The treacherous role of these bases has been proved in the past, for they were used on 
several occasions, including the Biafra war in Nigeria and in supporting the Congolese 
reactionary forces against Patrice Lumumba, liberator of the Congolese people from foreign 
colonialism and American hegemony. 


Such bases were used to receive the F-5 jets which were used during the Vietnam war and 
the Middle East war in 1973. The air base on Moroccan soil was also used to protect the 
Zairian regime in 1977 during the troubles in Shaba Province. 


The reason behind the Moroccan people's anger against the regime is because of the monarchic 
rule of the king and his two sons, who is just like other kings and sultans who usurped 
the authority from the people. 


The economic situation has become extremely acute and for the Moroccan people, the only 
way out is to get rid of the king. 


The economic situation was the reason behind the June 1982 strike which was called by 
Lebanese journalists "black June". Prior to the June uprising the government had put 
prices up and following the incidents the government was forced to reduce the increase 
to half its earlier level. 


Diverting the attention of the Moroccan Army from its true role. 


The Moroccan king's thinking is dominated by the fear of a military coup. In the early 
seventies, an abortive military coup took place in Skhirat and later, a plane carrying 
the Moroccan king was attacked. 


Because of these experiences, Hassan was left with the sole option of sending the army to 
the Western Sahara and attempting to convince them of the 'truth' of the cause they are 
fighting for. 


The Moroccan king therefore ordered Col Ahmad Dlimi to command the Moroccan forces to 
secure the political survival of his family and his throne. 


Wiping out groups opposed to the king within the army to prolong his survival. [sentence 
as received] 


Based upon these precepts, Hassan threw in his army into the Western Sahara to serve 
several purposes: 


1 - Divert its attention from its basic role in the holy Arab war against Israel. This 
purpose has been reinforced by the United States’ protection of Israel, for America can 
hardly expect one of its agent regimes to oppose its Zionist creation. 


2 - Using the army internally to crush the regime's opponents [word indistinct] and diverting 
it from playing its role in correcting Morocco's political economic and social paths. 


For these and other reasons, the Saharan war was embarked upon by the Moroccan king to 
annihilate the country's Arab people... 


On the social front, Moroccan society has a lot of social diseases such an inequality, 
prostitution and poverty. Let us take the Moroccan city of Casablanca, as a clear 

example of the class differences in Moroccan society. In one part of the city, you find 
places and villas as well as luxury hotels and night clubs. On the other side, you find 
the majority of the people in the city living in makeshift houses and huts, deprived from 
the most basic human needs. You find children in worn-out clothes and bare-footed. It 

is also a common scene in Morocco to find students at night studying in the streets of rich 
parts of the city because of the lack of electricity in their own area.

COMMENTARY RECALLS GULF OF SIDRA INCIDENT 


LD181506 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 18 Aug 82 


[Text] On this day last year, the American 6th Fleet began a 3-day naval military 
maneuver in the Mediterranean Basin during which it committed an aggressive operation 
against the security and stability of the people of the Jamahiriyah, contrary to 
international laws and norms as its forces violated the inviolability of the territorial 
waters of the Gulf of Sidra, which by law is under Libya's control. However, the large 
armies of America that are armed to the teeth with the latest weapons and equipment did 
not frighten the Libyan people and could not harm their unlimited dignity and 
nationalism. Libyans showed their traditional stand and their courageous sacrifices 
when the Libyan planes intercepted the American planes flying over the Gulf of Sidra and 
downing one military aircraft. 


This courageous stand enjoyed the support and solidarity of the world. Perhaps the huge 
formal and popular attendance witnessed by the international conference for solidarity 
with the people of the Jamahiriyah held 28-31 September 1981 is categorical proof of the 
extent of the confidence which the peoples and states entertain for the Great September 
Revolution and the high appreciation of its role as the vanguard of the liberation move- 
ment in the world, a fact which the leader of the revolution asserted at the very same 
conference when he addressed the participants, saying: Your solidarity with the Libyan 
people means they are right and it also means America is wrong and the aggressor; this 
is the strongest evidence and argument and the best testimony about the justness of the 
policy which the Libyan people are pursuing. 


It is not odd or unlikely that America, which surrounded the peoples with their military 
bases and spread on their territories the nuclear centers of fear, should commit the 
ugliest crimes against all stabilized, peaceful peoples, particularly against the Libyan 
people who back and support liberation movements and strengthen the causes of peoples 
still under the yoke of its imperialist hegemony. The American aggression on the Gulf 
of Sidra and the other American aggressive measures to hinder the major humanitarian 
role of the Great September Revolution will remain historic evidence of America's 
aggressive reality and a graphic picture of American terrorism for all peoples.
