SUBJECT: Topic for Discussion at DCI Breakfast Meeting 
with Secretary Haig on 19 January 1982 

Ghana - Libyan Activities in Ghana in the 
Wake of J. J. Rawlings Led Coup 

There is no question that the Libyans want to become 
involved in activities in Ghana following the overthrow of the 
Limann Government. Diplomatic relations between Tripoli and 
Accra, broken by ex-President Limann in November 1980 , have 
been reestablished. Jerry Rawlings turned down an offer of 
Libyan troop assistance, but he made a request for economic aid 
to a Libyan delegation that visited Accra on 9-10 January. A 
Ghanaian delegation is scheduled to visit Libya shortly to 
discuss detailed proposals. One of Rawlings' objectives is to 
seek an alternate oil supplier to Nigeria, which cut off oil to 
Ghana during the first Rawlings' coup in 1979. However, the 
Nigerian High Commissioner assured Rawlings that Nigeria would 
honor its commitments to provide oil to Ghana, realizing that 
Libya would be more than willing to replace Nigerian oil. 
Despite pressure from some of his more extreme advisers to move 
closer towards Libya, Rawlings said he would not favor direct 
Libyan involvement in Ghana. One obstacle to the spread of 
Libyan influence in Ghana is the lack of a substantial Islamic 

population there. 25X11 

To date, there is no evidence of Soviet complicity in events 
in Ghana.
