Summary (AI): A Philadelphia Inquirer column by Edwin Guthman (30 August 1981) discusses the 1977 Green Beret training mission to Libya, recruited by former CIA agents Edwin Wilson and Frank Terpil, and whether the CIA authorized it. It is about US intelligence and Libya's leader Khadafy, with little on Tripoli itself.
• Nine of the ten recruits were retired Green Berets, the elite commando element of the Army Special Forces.
• Thompson said he alerted Army counterintelligence officers at Fort Bragg after accepting the assignment.
• The Army told the New York Times that an informal review concluded the officers apparently acted in good faith, believing the CIA had authorized the mission.
• Wilson was reportedly selling his intelligence expertise, arms and explosives to Khadafy.
• Thompson said the Libyan military supply requests included training manuals and combat boots, which Army authorities told him to send.
• 1977-07: Ten American men, recruited as retired Green Berets and led by Master Sgt. Luke Thompson, travelled to Libya believing they were to train Libyan commandos under a covert operation they thought was CIA-authorized.
• 1977-04-26: CIA Director Stansfield Turner fired two middle-level CIA employees for providing Wilson with unauthorized assistance in shipping explosive devices to Libya.
• 1980-04: A District of Columbia grand jury indicted Edwin P. Wilson and Francis Terpil on charges of illegally exporting explosives, acting as unregistered agents for Libya, and conspiring to murder a Libyan defector in Egypt.
