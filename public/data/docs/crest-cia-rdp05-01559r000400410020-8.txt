[Question] Do you have a definite plan to end the problems in 
Chad? 

[Al-Qadhdhafi] My proposal is that the people of Chad should be 
free to decide on their own future, without intervention. 

[Question] Will you meet with President Mitterrand to discuss 
Chad? 

[Al-Qadhdhafi] There are continuous contacts, but the African 
Union must furnish the solution. 

[Question] Libya, too, seems to be seriously threatened by the 
United States, which holds your country responsible for a series 
of terrorist actions. What are the real reasons for this long held 
US. attitude? 

{Al-Qadhdhafi] Libya has been trying to unite all the Arabs and 
Arab countries into a strong power to work against the common 
enemy. The United States is scared and dislikes this. Thus, it 
reacts as you described. 

[Question] The recent mission by Whitehead, the U.S. deputy 
secretary of state, does not appear to have produced the.expécted 
results. How do you view the obvious dissension betweeri the 
Americans and the West Europeans?’ 

[Al-Qadhdhafi] The interests of West European countries differ 
completely from those of the United States. It was to be expected, 
therefore, that they could not agree. The United States, in its 
efforts to further itself, does not respect other countries’ interests. 
It is concerned only about the defense of Western Europe and 
NATO. This is why it has placed missiles in Western Europetand 
has filled the Mediterranean with fleets and nuclear arms. 

[Question] Despite all this, the West believes that you are 
adopting a friendly attitude toward the terrorists. In your opin- 
ion, how was this impression created, and how could you alleviate 
it? 

{Al-Qadhdhafi] We support the Palestinians’ struggle for their 
country’s liberation from the conquerors. The United States 
believes. that this support is a type of.terrorism.... If that issthe 
case, our: revolution, as well as everything we do, are terrorist 
actions. 

[Question] In the West there is great confusion, as well as 
ignorance, on the social development in your country and‘on its 
position in the world. Can you tell us how you see contemporary 
Libya? 

{Al-Qadlidhafi] We are trying to elevate the Libyan people’s 
standard of living. Before the revolution; the Libyan people:were 
living at subsistence level in huts and tents: All our efforts‘aim 
to raise our.people’s standard of living:as high as possiblé: 

[Question} What is Libya’s role at present in the Arab-world,

[Al-Qadhdhafi) Our efforts aim to unite all' the Arab states. This 
is not easily accomplished, as the approximately 20 Arab states 
have different political views and interests. As for now, these

terranean countries, such as Greece and Italy, toward Libya?

[Question] In Greece, there are differing views on whether the

{Al-Qadhdhafi] This is an issue that primarily concerns Greek

[Question] You recently said that Libya’s isolation from the West 
would lead your country toward the Soviet Union. The Green 
Book proposes the third path, between the socialist and capitalist 
models. According to this, should not Libya keep equal distance, 
at least ideologically, from the two superpowers? 
[Al-Qadhdhafi] According to the country’s ruling system and the 
Green Book, Libyan policy gives absolute authority to the people. 
Our ideology is a new one that will change all the world into a 
world of jamahiriyahs (that is, of people’s rule), without govern- 
ments, ministries, parliamentary deputies, police, armies, or 
violence. The people’s congresses will make decisions, which the 
people’s committees will implement. Our jamahiriyahs are based 
on the ancient Greeks system, that is, the people rule without a 
government or parties. 

[Question] How do you view Libya’s future? The price of oil is 
constantly dropping and the country’s oil reserves will be 
exhausted in the next 20 years. Thus Libya could be poorer and 
forced to make changes in its foreign policy. What would happen 
to the export of the Libyan “third path” model — Arab social- 
ism? And what about the slogan “Partners, not salaried work- 
ers”? 

[Al-Qadhdhafi] Libya has great prospects for development and 
adequate oil deposits. The oil issue, however, will not affect our 
economy directly, once we have developed an industry which 
offsets the reduction of the oil price. However, I believe that the 
Iran-Iraq war will cause a drop in the production of oil and a 
corresponding increase in price. Libya, whose oil deposits can last 
for over 100 years, does not intend to reduce the price of oil; on 
the contrary, Libya will attempt to increase the price by reducing 
production. This would cause an increase in demand and a 
corresponding increase in price, despite the pressure on Libya 
from the other oil-producing states. The Third World theory is 
not at all related to oil. 

[Question] What is your position on the fall of the dictatorship in 
Haiti and on the presidential elections in the Philippines? 
[Al-Qadhdhafi} Naturally, we have been very satisfied with the 
fall of dictatorship in Haiti. However, the government which has 
assumed power is not a people’s government. The revolution 
should continue until a completely democratic government is 
established. In the Philippines, an illegal government has been 
confirmed by fraudulent elections. Of course, this has been the 
result of activities by the United States, which considers the 
Philippines an area of great importance and does not want to lose 
it.
