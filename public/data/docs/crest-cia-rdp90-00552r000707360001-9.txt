Summary (AI): A 1981 New York Times article reports that Libyan student opponent Faisal Zagallai was shot in Fort Collins, Colorado, in October 1980 by a man the police say was an American mercenary recruited by the Libyan government. The article links the attack to Qaddafi and to the subsequent closure of the Libyan People's Bureau in Washington.
• Zagallai was on a Libyan list of 24 students to be killed, according to an FBI warning given about six months before the shooting.
• The article says this was the first known attempt by Qaddafi to kill an enemy in the United States and the first known use of an American mercenary for a killing.
• The Free Libyan Students Union reportedly claimed 2,000 members; Washington considered this exaggerated.
• JANA said Libya's World Revolutionary Forces had acted against Zagallai.
• 1980-10-14: Faisal Zagallai was shot in his apartment by a man the police later charged as an American mercenary recruited by the Libyan government; Zagallai survived but lost sight in one eye.
• 1981-04-22: FBI arrested Eugene Tafoya of Truth or Consequences, N.M., and charged him with attempting to kill Zagallai.
• 1981-05: Reagan administration ordered the Libyan People's Bureau in Washington closed and expelled all 27 of its diplomats.
