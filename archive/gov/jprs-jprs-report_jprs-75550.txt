Gafsa: Continued 
Paris DEMAIN L'‘AFRIQUE in French 10 Mar 80 p 35 
CArticle: "Gafsa: Last Episode?"] 


(Text] On 1 March, President Habib Bourguiba killed two birds with one stone: 
he appointed someone to take over the duties of his prime minister, Mr Hedi 
Nouira, 69 years old, who entered a Paris hospital on 26 February following 
a cerebral hemorrhage, and he found a replacement for his Minister of the 
Interior, Mr Othman Kechrid, guilty of not having had sufficient control 
over his troops at the time of the Gafsa affair. 


The ministry of the interior goes to Mr Driss Guiga, ambassador in Bonn, 
and Mr Mohamed Mzali, minister of national education, is appointed "govern- 
ment action coordinator." 


With a solid reputation for being a politician, Mr Mzali, a historian by 
training, belongs to the current favoring Arab-Muslim identity, which finds 
a certain following in Tunisia and is opposed to the out and out western- 
ization advocated by Mr Nouira. A modernist intellectual, he is said to 
have intervened personally last fall to prevent the imprisonment of 

Mr Kanouchi, the editor of MAJTANA (SOCIETE), the magazine of Tunisian 
integrists. 


To many observers, the choice of Mr Mzali represents a signal of Bourguiba 
to appease his restless Libyan neighbor. Mr Mzali is among those who have 
never had any quarrel with Colonel Qadhdhafi. Besides, the latter said 
himself on 17 February that "the situation of open defiance between his 
country and the Tunisian regime was ended (...) with the fall of Hedi 
Nouira..." To Colonel Qadhdhafi, Mr Nouira is the man who scuttled the 
merger between Tunisia and Libya signed in Djerba on 12 January 1974. 
However, this might also be Qadhdhafi's latest manoeuver to prevent his 
being cut off from the majority of Arab states. Not all of them have 
appreciated his donkey's kick [to the aging lion].

3. Production Sector's Financing Needs 


The gross national product representing one component of budget receipts from 
the production sector constitutes for the most part just that much revenue 
from the domestic sector. For its part, the state absorbs a porcion of it 
through indirect taxes (customs duties and other taxes). The remainder con- 
Sists of allowances fr depreciation, miscellaneous reserves, and reserve 
funds. In order to balance its accounts and finance a good portion of its 
gross capital and inventory, the production sector has had recourse to other 
economic agents *o the tune of over 1.2 billion LL. 


That amount was not all borrowed from the banks. A large share of the fi- 
nancing requirements was covered directly by savings in the domestic sector, 
by private establishments which finance themselves, or by the domestic sec- 
ter, which finances construction directly out of its own savings. 


In the absence of complete economic accounts for 1978, it is impossible to 
determine how the production sector's financing requirements evolved during 
that year.

[Text] Kyoto, 7 Apr KYODO--A top official of the Qatar General Petroleum 
Corporation said here Monday that a feasibility study being conducted on a 
newly found gas field has turned out to be favorable so that his country is 
certain to export liquefied natural gas (LNG) “o Japan in the future. 


Ali Mohammed Jaidah, managing director of the Qatar General Petroleum Corpor- 
ation, said the gas field, which he believed to be the biggest in the world, 
would tap 150 trillion cubic feet of natural gas. 


Jaidah is in Kyoto to attend the Sixth International Conference on Liquefied 
Natural Gas. The four-day meeting opened Monday. 


Qatar will exploit the mass gas deposit, Jaidah said. He added that his 
country would iefinitely export LNG to Japan. 


Qatar can also be expected to export 85,000 barrels per day of crude oil to 
Japan from Apri! on a direct deal (DD) basis, he said. 


Asked about a reported move to organize a group of natural gas producing na- 
tions for price cartel like the Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries 
(OPEC), he denied knowledge of such a step being taken. 


But Jaidah said OPEC might attempt to formulate a unified price mechanism 
among them for liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) first and then apply a simi- 
lar price system to LNG. 


Oniy four OPEC countries—Indonesia, Abu Dahbi, Algeria and Libya--are 
engaging in LNG busines at the present.
