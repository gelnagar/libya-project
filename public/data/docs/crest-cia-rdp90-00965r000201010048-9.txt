Summary (AI): A December 1985 Washington Post article reports that the U.S. protested to Moscow over Soviet SA-5 air-defence missiles being installed in at least two locations in Libya, and that the U.S. had drawn up precautionary military plans against Qaddafi. None of the locations named are in Tripoli.
• Soviet-made SA-5 long-range anti-aircraft missiles were reportedly being installed in at least two locations in Libya, expected to be manned by Soviet troops.
• The SA-5 can reach targets 95,000 feet up and has a range of about 150 miles.
• Reagan reportedly authorized the CIA to covertly undermine Qaddafi's regime, as reported by the Post the previous month.
• Qaddafi owed the Soviets an estimated $4 to $5 billion in debt.
• 1985-08: Precautionary US military planning against Libya initiated the previous summer, in case Libya attacked a neighbouring North African state or was responsible for a major terrorist incident; the Pentagon judged a direct operation could require six divisions or 90,000 men in the worst case, and the plan was never approved.
• 1985-07: An American was killed and 39 others held hostage for 17 days on hijacked TWA Flight 847. (1 killed)
• 1985-10-10: Qaddafi visited Moscow (10-12 October) seeking a friendship treaty, nuclear reactor assistance and debt relief; the visit reportedly went badly.
• 1981-08: Two US F-14 fighters shot down two Soviet-built Libyan fighters after a brief dogfight over the Gulf of Sidra.
