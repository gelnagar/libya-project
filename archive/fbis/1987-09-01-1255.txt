Says Rebellion ‘Deteriorating’ 
4JN291131 Khartoum SUNA in Arabic 1030 GMT 29 Aug 87 


[Text] Khartoum, 29 Aug (SUNA) — General Fawzi Ahmad 
al-Fadil, commander in chief of the People's Armed Forces, says 
the rebel movement has resorted to robbery and plunder, with its 
members turning into remnants and thieves who terrorize 
peaceful civilians more than they fight the Armed Forces. He 
stressed that the rebellion has receded considerably and is now 
deteriorating 


Addressing the graduation ceremony for a new class of the 
military academy on Thursday, his excellency said: “While we 
in the Armed Forces Command are fully convinced that a 
military solution is not the ideal one and that only a peaceful 
solution to the problem of the south can produce viable results, 
given the political and social circumstances surrounding the 
problem, we will nonetheless not have remnants of the rebellion 
disturbing security, killing the innocent, and disrupting construc- 
tion in the south.” 


The commander in chief urged public support for the Armed 
Forces so they can deal a fatal blow to the rebels and impress on 
their leadership or manipulators that hostilities are futile and 
that only dialogue serves the interests of the Sudanese people. 
His excellency commended the support that the Armed Forces 


enjoy among the Sudanese people, emphasizing that it provides 
the motive for further acts of heroism

SPLM's Garang Gives Press Conference in Nairobi 
£A30210) Nairobi SUNDAY STANDARD in English 
30 Aug 87 pi 

[Article by Ngumo wa Kuria: “We're Ready for Peace Talks, 
Says Col Garang™] 


[Text] The Sudan People’s Liberation Movement (SPLM) is 
ready to hold peace talks and exchange prisoners with the Khar- 
toum government. The leader of the SPLM, Col John Garang, 
said yesterday in Nairobi that the movement's door was open for 
peace negotiations with Khartoum to end the five-year war in the 
south. Our door is wide open for the talks, Col Garang told 
reporters during an interview. He was in transit 


The rebel leader, however, accused Prime Minister al-Sadiq 
al-Mahdi of crushing the peace process during August last year 
after the military wing of the movement, the Sudan People’s 
Liberation Army, SPLA, shot down a civilian aircraft that was 
overflying the SPLM territory. Said the colonel: Al-Mahdi 
slammed the door after the incident and branded us terrorists 


Following that incident, added Col Garang, the Khartoum gov- 
ernment slammed the door for any peace process. But we are 
ready, even at this moment, to resume the talks, because no war 
will end with war but with negotiations, Col Garang said 


He said his movement had forewarned Al-Mahdi against flying 
civilian aircraft in the south, but he would not listen so when this 
plane entered our territory we shot it down 


He emphasized that the war in the south was central to the 
collapse of the Numayri regime in April 1985 and now the 
disintegration of the coalition government of Al-Mahdi. 


Col Garang lashed out at Al-Mahdi's Al-Ummah Party, with the 
Democratic Unionist Party (DUP) of intransigence. The Al- 
Ummah Party has all along been intransigent because they make 
decisions in Khartoum and then they invite us. We do not want 
to be invited but we want participation, Garang told newsmen. 
The colonel sporting a well-kept beard and a cream suit, said two 
battalions broke away from the Sudanese National Army and 
formed the nucleus of the SPLA. 


On the recent release of four white hostages, Col Garang said 
President Moi was instrumental! in getting their release, adding 
that the Kenya Government played a major role in the release. 
He said in our statements about the hostages we thanked the 
government of President Moi and the people of Kenya for facili- 
tating the release. The SPLA leader added that the hostages were 
not involved in the war. Saying presently two white priests are 
held by the SPLA as well as hundreds of prisoners of war, Col 
Garang said his group is ready to exchange the prisoners with the 
Khartoum government through mediation of the international 
Red Cross committee 


Col Garang condemned some foreign countries for claiming that 
the SPLA was heartless for not allowing the provision of relief 
supplies in the south. We are fighting for the people and therefore 
we are concerned about their welfare. The question that the 
SPLA is denying access for famine relief does not arise, he said. 
Our detractors are the ones who are deliberately making the 
claim to try and confuse issues, he noted 


Col Garang said some of the problems facing the Sudan include 
the question of nationality, religion and the country’s debt crisis. 
We are the only country in the whole world with an association 
of amputees, observed the colonel, adding that the Al-Mahdi 
government had refused to compensate the amputees, he went on. 


Garang Blames Government for Peace Failure 
JN301136 Khartoum SUNA in Arabic 1015 GMT 30 Aug 87 


[Text] Khartoum, 30 Aug (SUN A) — Colonel John Garang holds 
the Sudanese Government repnnsible for the fumbling of peace 
endeavors and said that the prime minister has violated the Koka 
Dam agreement because he thinks that he can achieve a military 
victory to bring the SPLM to the negotiating table while it is still 
weak 


In a statement to the magazine Sudan Times quoted by the 
Khartoum newspaper Al-Ayyam today, Garang said that the 
government's military escalation has failed to realize any victory. 
On the contrary, it has led to the strengthening of the movement's 
position 


Garang claimed that his movement is in control of the whole of 
southern Sudan with the exception of the main cities. He 
affirmed the SPLM’s commitment to the Koka Dam agreement. 


General Fawzi al-Fadil, commander in chief of the Armed 
Forces, has declared that the rebel movement has begun to 
retreat and has turned into plundering groups.
