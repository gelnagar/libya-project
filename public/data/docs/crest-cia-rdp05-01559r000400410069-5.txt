Qadhdhafi Rhetoric 

TV Speech, 16 April 1986 

Qadhdhafi's rhetoric is confrontational, angry. He expresses 
satisfaction with the support he believes he has received from other Arab 
governments and European nations. Only one death/dying reference. 

On Reagan: "He should be put on trial as a war criminal and murderer of 
children. This is because he has issued the orders to his armed forces 
to kill our children." 

"We will not abandon the struggle for the liberation of Palestine. The 
raids will never make us abandon this whatever they might be. We will 
not abandon incitement for popular revolution, whatever raids they carry 
out. In fact we incite revolution, and we insist on revolution." 

"When the Americans attack Libyan territory, we will oppose them; we will 
chant slogans, and burning fire will start beneath their feet and 
(consume) their bodies. . . . We will fight them with 1 million fighters 
and millions of others are ready to fight on our side." 

"The murderers are Thatcher and Reagan, the murderers of children." 

"In appreciation of the international situation, and in appreciation of 
the stands of Italy, France, Spain, and Greece, these friendly countries, 
and also Malta, I decided not to include the Mediterranean region in the 
exclusion zone and not to escalate military operations in southern 
Europe. But Italy and Spain will have to stop the U.S. 6th Fleet from 
now on, from any presence in their ports and from launching raids from 
these ports, otherwise we will be forced to escalate the operations in 
self-defense." 

"Libyan radio must call on the Arab nation for an immediate unity." 

"I am calling for revolution, and he who stands in the way of revolution 
must be annihilated. Reagan can lift the protection of his children and 
family for we are not child-killers like him; we are responsible for the 
revolution and we incite it, and non one can make us change course." 

On Arab leaders: "Not a single day passes without them contacting me 
several times. They all call--I did not ask them--but they all called 
for the convening of an extraordinary Arab summit which could be held in 
a day or two, an extraordinary Arab summit, to stand by you, the Libyan 
people, against this flagrant aggression. . . . I believe that the Arab 
states which have an economic potential will participate with us in 
financing the battle . . . . Arab unity should emerge immediately."
