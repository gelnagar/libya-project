Summary (AI): A 1981 Los Angeles Times article (reproduced in a CIA reading room copy) describes a Pomona, California munitions dealer's shipment of 7,500 pounds of C4 explosive to Libya, and a terrorist training center set up in the servants' quarters of the Tripoli palace of former King Idris, run from 1976 by American ex-Green Berets and bomb experts.
• Shipment of 7,500 pounds of C4 plastique explosives, the first leg of a 2,000-mile trek to a desert bunker east of Los Angeles.
• Stockpile assembled at a cost of about $300,000 from private US and Canadian military surplus explosive stocks.
• The 20-ton C4 inventory was airlifted to Libya from Houston aboard a chartered DC-8 two months later.
• The Tripoli terrorist center was staffed by at least 30 Americans, and trained Libyan soldiers and Arab terrorists in commando, sabotage and assassination skills.
• Other Americans worked in a bomb factory disguising bombs as ashtrays, lamps, flower vases, and similar household items.
• 1969: Kadafi ousts King Idris (the palace later used for the terrorist center)
• 1976: Terrorist training center and bomb laboratory set up in the servants' quarters of the former royal palace, staffed by American ex-Green Berets and bomb experts
• 1977-06: One or more bombs from the center killed several Libyans and injured three American instructors (summer 1977)
