{Question) What do you think of the Iranian revolution? 


|\Anewer) When the revolution took place in Iran, Iraq welcomed it favorably, be- 
cause it saved the people from the dictatorial regime of the shah. Therefore, my 
country respects the choices that the Iranian people have made and want to make, 

The Iranian regime quickly forgot, however, its people's aspirations and it is pur- 
suing the same policy as the shah. Moreover, the Tehran regime has made the living 
eonditions of the Iranian people considerably worse, because it has made no decision 
in the economic, social and political field and it has only given rise to discontent. 
Authority is divided, too divided, and chaos prevails everywhere. In order to reduce 
this internal tendion, the Khomeini regime has sought to unload this chaos abroad by 
means of attempts at interference in Iraqi affairs and affairs of the Gulf countries 
and of the entire Arab nation. Again I point out that we are with the Iranian peo- 
ple in their struggle to choose the regime that pleases them. 


(Question) Libya has announced unification with Syria, a country with which it seems 
to me that you do not have the best relations. In an interview released some time 
ago, Sa'dun Hammadi stated: “We view with favor any initiative for the purpose of 
strengthening the ability of any Arab country for overcoming the difference between 
our military force and Israel's." What do you think of this unification and what 
repercussions can it have in the Arab world, if it does not fall through? 


|Anewer) By now, everyone knows that the Libyan regime is changeable and also its 
previous attempts with certain countries toward unity have failed. First, with 
Egypt, then with Sudan, then with Syria and immediately afterwards with Tunisia. 
They all were attempts at covering up its domestic crises. All the plans proposed 
by the Libyan regime are only plans written on sheets of paper, on which the Arab 
people have never been able to have any way of expressing themselves. The attempts 
and the previous experiments between those two regimes clarify the reason why they 
are in no position to create real unity in which conditions for the real pan-Arab 
etruggle can exist. 


Strengthening of Arab power in military terms occurs when a regime devotes all its 
efforts to serving the pan-Arab battle objectively and with coordination. We do not 
believe that this new attempt by the Libyan regime can really affect the struggle 
against Zionism. That same regime, for example, has not been keeping faith with the 
decisions made in the Ninth Summit in Baghdad, in 1978, in which it was decided to 
strengthen the defensive potentiality of Syria and Jordan. I should like to point 
out to the readers that Arab wiity is a great strategic objective of our Arab So- 
cialist Ba'th Party, but it must be based on democracy and have the support of the 
Arab people. Militant, and not tactical, unity.

What is your country's present policy with regard to this problem? 











\Anewer) Iraq's position with regard to the Zionist entity is well known, It is
