NAVON MAKES SPEECH AT WELCOMING CEREMONY FOR BEGIN 


TA292046 Jerusalem Domestic Television Service in Hebrew 1742 GMT 29 Mar 79 TA 


[Speech by President Yitzhaq Navon at the welcoming ceremony for Prime Minister Menahem 
Begin in the Knesset Plaza in Jerusalem on Begin's return from the United States--live ] 


(Text ] Mr Prime Minister, Mrs Begin, welcome back to your home, dear guests. This immense 
audience has gathered here to express to you, Mr Begin, to the Cabinet and the ministerial 
team and their aides, their deep feelings of gratitude not only for the signing of the 
peace but also for the long and tiring days that preceded it. You have striven toward the 
goal with stubborness, consistency and without tiring. You struggled against strong winds 
that blew in your face and wrestled also with yourself, inside, and everyone saw. Your 
faith has not dwindled even when the negotiations dangled between hope and failure. In 
putting your signature on the treaty, no doubt those decisive milestones in the history 

of your life were conjured up--the European vale of death, the prison and the cold steppes 
of Siberia, the underground, the Knesset and the utmost responsibility for the shaping of 
the fate of this amazing, courageous and peace-thirsty nation. 


Upon returning, you find here a mature people. There is unity and no outcry against peace 

in the camp; their hearts are with peace, as this is what we prayed for all the days, but 
they are apprehensive about its conditions and the degree of its stability. They are 

wearing their eyes out to see the secrets of the future, to find out what is contained there. 


The Egyptian president, Muhammad Anwar as-Sadat, who courageously dared break the wall of 
hatred around us once again--and justly so--elevated his ally, the Israeli mother. But 
she was not alone; besides her were her parents, her sons and her husband, her chosen 
representatives and ministers--all of them were allies to peace for scores of years. Their 
hands remained extended in the air in vain, until, next to peace, the Egyptian leader stood 
and found all those allies ready for it. All those allies have always called upon the 

Arab countries to put an end to the chains of bloodshed and have appealed them in poet 
Altermann's words: Arabia, this chain of bloodshed--it was not fate which forged it un- 
knowingly. Your ministers can bear witness to this--by a single stroke of one pen, one hint 
and it--the chain of bloodshed--is stopped and cut. It ends like an affair which has no 
basis and no goal. One sign, and the horror ends. Only a hair's breadth differentiates 
between an indescribable and nameless curse and the blessing of peace and an era of bloom 
unknown by the Semitic people until now. The people of this country--fruit pickers, 
builders, pavers, those who make the desert bloom--are 1th all their hearts prepared to 
zive real expression to the blessing of peace and to the era of bloom. 


Some things in peace are clear to everyone, yet there are things which will probably be 
clarified to us only in the course of building up peace. On this matter, let us accept 

the doctrine of Maimonides--that great rationalist--who, when he spoke about the prophecy 

of the days of the Messiah said: and all those things and their like--no man will know how 
they will be until they are. 


Gentlemen, these days the IDF will begin removing its forces from the Sinai Desert in the 
direction of its new positions. 


Ve. 30 Mar 79 


Officers and soldiers by the thousands will uproot what they have buried, will dismantle 
what they built and will return what they brought forward--but it is not a withdrawing 
army, but an advancing army, even though its direction of progress is not toward the 
enemy, but homeward. This is a courageous army and full of resourcefulness, which hates 
war but wins it. It knows the great desert with its valleys and mountains saturated 
in blood. It has already entered into it and has come out of it more than once. But 
this time it leaves it crowned with the greatest wreath of victory--the wreath of 
peace, which, in fact, is the reason and goal of all its wars. With the arrival of 
peace with Egypt, the IDF is not dismantling and its soldiers are not sent home. On 
the contrary, it needs ever increasing strengthening in quantity and quality, because 
it is the faithful guardian of peace and it is the answer to the voices which threaten 
us from east and north. 


I once again appeal to the distinguished kings and presidents of the Arab countries 

to listen to the bell of history, which today rings new sounds: The caravan of peace 
has gone on its way. Do not allow stones to be hurled at it. On the contrary, give 

it your blessing and join it, because the way of war has not solved anything, but added 
wreckage and graves. The new path is not free of obstacles, but only it involves the 
guarantee for a happy future for your sons and ours: for we all have the same father, 
for one God created us and he ordered them to live. They are entitled to know that 
their lives will no longer be sacrificed in endless battles. They are entitled to know 
that new horizons have been opened up to them, full of hope and creativity. 


The help of the U.S. President, Mr Jimmy Carter, will certainly be provided to this end 
with the same perseverence and devotion he gave the agreement with Egypt. From here 

we send our sincere gratitude to him for not having spared any effort until we reached 
the shores of our destination. 


Mr prime minister, I wish this to you: That just as you have witnessed great days, 

you will also reach the days of small things: housing for young couples, large families, 
aliyah, absorption and the character of the society--these are things which lack drama, 
but they are the guarantee of our real strength. And without the little things, there 
are no great things. 


We extend our congratulations to the Egyptian people, who have the ancient and great 
culture, with feelings of friendship and hopes that our meeting will write down in 
the history of the nations of the region a glowing page, a page which will bring back 
to all of us the glory of past days and will give greatness to coming days. 


Our prayer is that the peace on which we put our hands will be like a tree planted near 
brooks, which shall yield up its fruit in time and whose leaf shall not wither from now 
until eternity.

LEGAL ADVISER DISCUSSES MEMO OF UNDERSTANDING 
TA300557 Jerusalem Domestic Television Service in Hebrew 1900 GMT 29 Mar 79 TA 


[Interview in the studio with Israeli Foreign Ministry Legal Adviser Dr Me'ir Rosenne 
by Jerusalem television announcer Ya'aqov Ahime!'ir--live] 


[Text] [Ahime'ir] The Memorandum of Understanding between Israel and the United States 
was publicized in Washington and Jerusalem last night. Egypt regards the Memorandum of 
Understanding as a hostile document and, as far as it is concerned, it is null and void, 


The Memorandum of Understanding, signed in the United States by Moshe Dayan and Cyrus 
Vance, says among other things that if a violation of the peace treaty endangers 
Israel's security the United States will consider increasing its military presence in 
the area, The United States will urgently consider supplying Israel with emergency 
deliveries if they are needed, Also, the United States will be able to stop supplies 
of arms which will be directed against Israel. The United States will also act to 
guarantee the freedom of movement for planes and ships over and through the Strait of 
Tiran and Elat, 


One who has worked to achieve this Memorandum of Understanding and who has also played 
a very major role in the formulation of the peace treaty between Israel and Egypt is 
Dr Me'ir Rosenne, the Foreign Ministry legal adviser, 


[Question] What is your reaction to Egypt's announcement that it regards the Memorandum 
of Understanding between Israel and the United States as null and void? 


[Answer] First of all, let me correct something which was stated here: The negotiations 
on the Memorandum of Understanding were conducted by Israeli Foreign Minister Moshe 
Dayan and U.S. authorities at various levels, including the U.S, President, Now, with 
respect to your question: This memorandum contains nothing detrimental to Egypt, This 
memorandum or agreement between Israel and the United States, which is an open document 
and which has been given to the U.S, Congress to hold, refers to possible dangers of 
hostile activity against Israel, If you read it, you will see that Egypt's name is not 
mentioned in that agreement, It includes primarily four things: First, the renewal of 
all the commitments given by the United States in the past--in 1975 and prior to that, 
within the framework of agreements or any other form,,,. 


[Question, interrupting] The interim agreement? 


[Answer] The interim agreement, as well as in certain announcements or letters from the 
United States to Israel, 


Ve. 30 Mar 79 7 ISRAEL 


Second, the United States--since it was a partner to the negotiations and, by the way, 
in the Camp David accords it has also been clearly stated that the United States 

would be a partner to the arrangements which would be achieved in the Middle East--will 
take part--and this is what is stated in the agreement--in guaranteeing the implementation 
of the commitments that stem from the agreement, To this end, these actions which you 
have noted were mentioned: The increase of U.S. forces in the area, consultations with 
the parties on how to remedy this violation, etc, In addition, this agreement also 
includes things which do not refer to the peace treaty with Egypt. There is a U.S, 
commitment to take all necessary steps to insure that arms sold by the United States 
to a third party would not be used in attacks against Israel and a commitment by the 
United States to take all the necessary measures to prevent transfers of this kind, 


[Question] Is there not concealed in the memorandum of agreement--and I have read it--the 
suspicion and fear of Egyptian violations of the peace treaty with Israel? 


[Answer] As I said, Egypt is not mentioned, Since Israel is leaving the Sinai, it 
needs guarantees and one of them is the U.S, commitment to take necessary action to 
insure freedom of movement for ships and planes, etc, We must not forget that the state 
of war with Egypt has indeed ended, but we are in a state of war with Syria, with Jordan 
and with other hostile Arab elements which have not yet signed a peace treaty with 
Israel, 


[Question] Dr Rosenne, we have received the impression from reading this document that 
the U.S, commitment to Israel--mainly in the military sphere--is very great. What 
significant difference do you see between this document--called the Memorandum of Under- 
standing, and perhaps we should call it an agreement between Israel and the United 
States--and a military pact between the United States and Israel? And do you not, in 
fact, regard this document as a first step toward this? 


[Answer] I am glad you raised this question, First of all, to the best of my knowledge, 
in all the U.S, defense agreements there is an article which talks about the stationing 

of U.S, forces in the country which enjoys that guarantee, Well, this is not an agreement 
on defense guarantees, It is not a defense agreement between the United States and Israel, 


[Question] But the document says that the United States will quickly increase its 
presence in the area in the event... 


[Answer, interrupting] Yes, but nowhere does it say what is said in any defense pact: 
That an attack on the Philippines, for example, would be considered as an attack on the 
United States, Nor does it say what you have within the framework of NATO: That an 
attack on a NATO member would be considered an attack on the United States, It is 
stated here that, if the treaty is seriously violated or should there be violations of 
the freedom of navigation and the like, the United States would increase its presence, 
This has happened in the past without there being an agreement with Israel, during the 
Yom Kippur War, 


[Question] Well, then what is the difference between the document and a military pact 
between the United States and Israel? 


[Answer] This is not a military pact, It is an agreement intended to guarantee that all 
the commitments stemming from the treaty will be honored, Also, there is a formal matter 
involved here: A military pact requires ratification by the U.S. Senate by a two-thirds 
majority. This agreement is not an international convention and is not a defense pact, 
True, the U.S, involvement is increased here and it is correct to say that so far Israel 
has not had a legal document binding in such a far-reaching way,
