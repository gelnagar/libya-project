ext/ Minister of State for Foreign Affairs and Cooperation 
‘Hamed Boucetta last week returned from a long trip which 
led him successively to France, the Soviet Union, and Bul- 


garia. 


In the course of that trip the chief of Moroccan diplomacy 
led our delegations to major intergovernmental commissions 
with France, the Soviet Union, and Bulgaria, and had conver- 
sations with responsible officials of those countries con- 
cerning questions of common interest as well as major cur- 
rent international problems. 


In the interview which he was pleased to grant us, the min- 
ister evaluates those conversations, evokes the prospects 
for Moroccan relations with those countries, comments on 
the evolution of the Saharan question in the light of his 
discussions in the three capitals visited, and reiterates 
Morocco's position on numerous current problems. 


The minister particularly states that he met with great 
understanding with regard to our national cause, notably 
on the part of Soviet leaders, and considers that the USSR 
is prepared to strive for a peaceful solution of the con- 
flict in northwestern Africa. 


The text of the Boucetta interview follows: 


Quest ion7 Mr Minister, you have just visited Paris, Moscow, and Sofia,

finewer/ The three meetings, as you know, were held as sessions of the major

exchange viewpoints on international and bilateral political questions. 

In general, it can be said that the outcome of the three meetings is pos-

standing of the problem in all its details, so acted that the resolution

and spirit in which the problem must be resolved, that is, in the African
