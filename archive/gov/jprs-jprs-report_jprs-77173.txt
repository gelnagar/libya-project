CHAD'S CIVIL WAR SAID PART OF OVERALL U.S. PLAN 
Damascus AL~THAWRAH in Arabic 29 Nov 8 p 4 
[Article by Sayyah al-Sakhni } 


[Text] A new attempt to stop the fighting in Chad failed yesterday when Hissein 
Habre refused to go to the capital of Togo to sign an agreement previously approved 
by all the warring factions, including Habre himself, at the Lomi conference which 
was held early this month. 


Habre's absence, which surprised the four African nations that helped draw up the 
agreement, raised many questions about the real motives behind this stand which 
came as a surprise and placed Chad, again, on the verge of partition. It also 
intensified the civil war that had been going on for over 8 months, despite all 
the dangers that such intensification represents, threatening Chad's future and 
that of all the neighboring African countries. 


However, if we knew all the international powers that support Habre in this stand, 
we would easily realize the real reasons for his refusal to sign the agreement. 
He is famous for being America's number one man in Chad, and he implements its 
policy there with extreme accuracy and loyalty. The U.S. even brought Habre into 
the fold with Al-Sadat's Egypt and Israel by telling them to provide him with 
weapons, ammunition and men. 


Accordingly, the U.S., who is now getting ready to cause side wars in the area 
and preparing suitable conditions to ignite local disputes extending throughout 
the Arab world, is very interested in intensifying the Chad dispute and the 
eruption of civil war, in an attempt at distracting Libya from its real role in 
the national struggle and busying it by creating discord and trouble on its 
southern borders. 


Of course, what is going on in Chad is a small plan intended to plunge the whole 
area into wars and disruptions in order to cover up the larger and more 
comprehensive plan which is aimed at making the whole Arab region submissive 

to the American logic of [land] snatching. The details of this plan can be read 
clearly through the Iraq-Iran war, the Egyptian concentration of troops on the 
Libyan borders and, last but not least, through the moves of the Jordanian 
regime in preparation to implement its planned role.
