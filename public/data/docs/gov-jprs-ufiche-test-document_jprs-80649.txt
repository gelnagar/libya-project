FOR A longtime now, we have all been far too lenient with 
the tribe which rules over Saudi Arabia. While we have 
»een talking reasonably with them in Riyadh, their ears 
nave been open only to the messages of Washington. 


When we asked them to help liberate Jerusalem, their 
“nly action was to compromise Mecca. When the Leader of 
sur Revolution went to meet them in past years, in 
ittempts to mediate with them over the differences which 
divided Libyans, Palestinians and other progressive 
Arabs from them, his efforts were snubbed. 


Despite all the urging, despite all the messages and 
‘elegrams which have been sent to the Saudis, pleading 
with them to serve the Arab cause and turn away from 
their deals with the enemies of the Arab Nation, the 
saudis stood firmly against us and did everything within 
their power to prevent the realisation of the Arab dream. 

So, at this point, when the Saudis have finally shown 
their hatred of progress by their conniving with the 
Americans to sabotage the economy of countries like 
Libya, we are entitled to ask, by what means might we 
successfully restore an Arab identity to the errant Saudi 
tribe? 

They have embraced all the trappings of the west, as 
well as its political notions, thus we may ask whether their 
Arab identity would be restored merely by the trappings 
of Arabism? Put bluntly, is the wearing of the koufia and 
the iqal sufficient for people to call themselves true 
Arabs? 


One way 


Clearly, there is but one way to deal with such people. In 
setting out this way, we must remember that the house of 
Saud has, ever since it took power and gave its name to 
Arabia in 1932, served the interests of foreigners. The 
Saudis have for years been a tribe of agents, com- 
missioning agents, political agents and intelligence 
agents. pee 


The power of the house of Saud originally depended 
upon the support of the British, who paid off their debt for 
the earlier service of the Saudis by helping them to usurp 
power in the country. They were then, as now, literally the 
agents of a foreign power. 


Although ‘dignified’ with titles and responsibilities, the 
few hundred men who rule Saudi Arabia are all the ser- 
vants or the agents of oil companies, foreign military mis- 
sions, overseas political interests or espionage 
organisations. 


After the demise of British colonialism, and with 
America’s limitless appetite for the precious oil that lay 
beneath Saudi Arabia, America replaced the role of the 
British. Whiie the oil resources of the Saudis should have 
permitted them to make of their country a truly inde- 
pendent state, their servile mentality prohibited any such 
move, and they were content to remain the puppets and 
agents of the Arab Nation’s worst enemy. 


Given that the agent mentality is endemic to the Saudi 
tribe, one wonders why Arabs have not always treated 
them as such. Had we treated them with the ‘respect’ 
which agent regimes deserve, then maybe we might our- 
selves have managed to recruit them as ‘agents’ for the 
Arab Nation! 

Has our intelligence service, or before it that of Nasser’s 
Egypt or Syria, ever tried this novel approach? The 
answer is no, and the reason why is simple. 


It never entered our minds that we should be able to 
recruit a prince or a king, especially from a family which 
claims such a fierce bedouin ancestry, and which claims 
also a local monopoly over honour and intelligence. But 
the British and the Americans obviously never had such 
compunction. To them, the idea of recruiting a whole 
royal family to serve their interests one way or another 
was natural. 


Thus for decades now, the Saudis have acted as the 


agents of the foreigners who would stop at nothing to con- 
trol their oil wealth. Anyone who doubts the truth of this 
should recall Reagan’s remarks about not letting Saudi 
Arabia become ‘another Iran’. 


In the light of this, and the impact which Saudi treason 
has had upon the rest of the Arab Natio, we may ask 
ourselves the not totally light-hearted question, what 
would have happened if Crown Prince Fahd had been our 
agent, and not that of the Americans? Where would the 
Arab world stand if Fahd had served Arab, rather than 
American interests? 








Open surrender 


We may say at once that the present conflict over the 
Saudi flooding of world markets with cheap oil would 
never have taken place. But we may also say more.than 
this. For without Saudi financial support, many of the 
industries of the US would not survive the current reces- 
sion — these include war industries. 


Coupled with the diplomatic weight of an Arabia com- 
mitted to Arabism, it is not too much to say that perhaps 
the Lebanon would now be enjoying peace and security, 
that car bombs would not be exploding in Beirut and 
Damascus and that AWACS planes would not be able to 
assist the enemy strike at Baghdad. 


Had Fahd been ‘our’ agent, rather than ‘theirs’, the his- 
tory of the last few years would certainly have been very 
different. But it was not to be. Arab money resides in 
vaults in California, Switzerland and London, rather than 
in Algeria or Yemen. The Saudis, not content with launch- 
ing their own struggle against the Libyan Jamahiriya on 
the Americans’ behalf, teeter on the verge of open sur- 
render to Zionism. ; 


In such circumstances, we may ask ourselves whether it 
will always be this way, whether the Saudis may not one 
de~ be won over to our side, become ‘our’ agents? The 
an.wer is perhaps provided by the current joke which 
says that Fahd’s is the most expensive human head in 
history. The reason: he has never bothered to use it. 
Because Fahd’s actions are not a joke, we may wonder 
whethe~ there is not someone in the Arab camp prepared 
to purchase that head, no matter at what price.
