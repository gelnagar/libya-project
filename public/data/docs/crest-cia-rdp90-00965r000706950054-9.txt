Summary (AI): A June 1985 Washington Post article, citing intelligence sources, reports that the CIA backed the National Front for the Salvation of Libya (NFSL), led by former Libyan auditor general Mohammed Youssef Magarieff, which tried to assassinate Qaddafi in May 1984. A five-hour gun battle at Bab Aziziya barracks ended the attempt, and Qaddafi afterward reportedly executed up to 200 dissidents.
• Qaddafi reportedly executed perhaps as many as 200 dissidents after the failed plot and imprisoned thousands more.
• The Saudis reportedly provided at least $7 million to the NFSL.
• CIA reportedly trained NFSL recruits in Western Europe, Sudan and Morocco.
• Magarieff served as head of the Libyan comptroller's commission from 1972 to 1977 and defected in 1980 while ambassador to India.
• The NFSL was formed in 1981; its first public proclamation was dated Oct. 7, 1981.
• 1984-05-06: Ahmed Ibrahim Ahwas, leader of NFSL's military wing, was caught and killed trying to infiltrate Libya from Tunisia; this began the unravelling of the plot.
• 1984-05-08: Five-hour gun battle at the Bab Aziziya barracks ended in the death or capture of the NFSL rebels.
• 1981-10-07: NFSL's first public proclamation calling for Qaddafi's overthrow marked the group's birth.
