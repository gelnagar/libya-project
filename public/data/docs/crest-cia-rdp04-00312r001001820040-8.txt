Summary (AI): A 1981 Boston Globe article (reprinted in CIA reading room) describes how former CIA agents Edwin P. Wilson and Francis E. Terpil ran a 1976-1979 program in Libya that made explosives and trained terrorists, with American recruits working in a basement munitions workshop in the former royal palace complex south of Tripoli. The article also reports later Libyan plots and US indictments.
• The Libyan explosives workshop was in the basement of the servants' quarters of the former royal palace of King Idris (Mohammed Idirss El Senussi), about twenty miles south of Tripoli.
• Americans camouflaged small bombs as lamps, candles, briefcases and ashtrays there.
• About twenty-five thousand pounds of explosives and a Redeye missile were contracted for shipment to Libya.
• Wilson and Terpil were indicted by a federal grand jury in Washington; Brower pleaded guilty and got a five-year sentence.
• Libya was reported to have trained commandos who were believed responsible for the 1980 execution of eleven Libyan exiles in Europe (as stated in the article).
• 1976-08-13: Terpil, Wilson, Brower and others met at Intertechnology, Inc. offices and allegedly agreed to ship explosives to Libya without State Department approval (the 'conspiracy meeting').
• 1976-08-13: J.S. Brower and Associates shipped the first load of explosives to Tripoli, in black fifty-five-gallon drums labeled 'Industrial Solvent'.
• 1979-12: According to a federal investigators' report, the operation continued at least until late December 1979.
