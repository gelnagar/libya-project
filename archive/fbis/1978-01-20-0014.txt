AL-QADHDHAFI OFFERS EGYPT ‘HUNDREDS OF TANKS’ 


LD191546Y Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1400 GMT 19 Js 


(Text ] Tripoli--Col Mu'ammer al-Qadhdhafi, supreme commander of the armed forces, this 
morning paid a visit to the Military College. He discussed the affairs of the college 
and important military topics with the officers and students. During the visit Colonel 
al-Qadhdhafi stated that hundreds of tanks would be placed at the disposal of the 
Egyptian army if it decided to liberate the occupied Arab territories to preserve the 
honor of the Arab nation and to implement the resolutions and the principles of the 
United Nations which protect the right of people in fighting for their national and 
patriotic sovereignty. 


It should be mentioned that As-Sadat is about to complete an old plan to liberate the 
Occupied land with armed forces, for he had stated at a closed meeting of the Palestine 
National Council held in Cairo from l2 to 21 March 1977 that he asked for an opportunity 
to continue to deceive the Americans and theIsraelis until: 


1. He receives the weapons contracted with Britain and France which are to be paid for 
by Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and the Emirate; 


2. He completes the tunnel being cut under the Suez Canal, which he said will enable 
him to push hundreds of tanks to the east under the canal; 


3. He provides the necessary air cover for these tanks by receiving the aircraft under 
contract from France that are to be paid for by Saudi Arabia and some other Arab countries, 


As-Sadat also explained to the Palestine National Council that he is maintaining 700,000 
soldiers under arms despite his pretense of disbanding most of the Egyptian Army so that 
America does not cut off economic aid to Egypt. 


In light of the recent developments a question will remain unanswered about As-Sadat's 
recent visit to the occupied territory until he himself answers it: Is As-Sadat still 
continuing to work for the implementation of his initial plan about which he spoke to 
the Palestinian leaders in Cairo, or is he going to go back on it?

FURTHER MATERIALS ON ISRAELI-EGYPTIAN POLITICAL TALKS 
Pessimism on Resumption 
TA191515Y Jerusalem Domestic Service in Hebrew 1500 GMT 19 Jan 78 TA 


[Text] According to an assessment in Jerusalem, it will not be possible to resume de- 
liberations of the Political Committee. It is reported by our political correspondent 
Shalom Qital. This assessment holds that As-Sadat's move is based on three causes: 
First, As-Sadat hoped that he would get total and immediate consideration from Israel, 
meaning a total withdrawal from all territories and self-determination for the Palestin- 
ians; second is Egypt's contention that the Israeli communications media were painting a 
defamatory picture of Egypt in the eyes of the Arab world and presenting it as submis- 
sive to Israel's demands; and the third reason is As-Sadat's disappointment with the fact 
that the Americans have not applied strong pressure on Israel. 


Our political correspondent says that yesterday evening four of the six clauses in the 
declaration of principles between Israel and Egypt had been formulated. There was a 
small distance with respect to the two other clauses, but this could have been bridged 
within a few hours. However, As~-Sadat decided on his move without asking the Egyptian 
delegation in Jerusalem if there had been any progress. The decision came about as a 
result of an accumlation of other factors. 


Our political correspondent also says that changes for success in the negotiations would 
have been greater, according to assessments in Jerusalem, had Egypt opted in advance for 


a separate agreement and not asked for the participation of other Arab parties. 


Begin-Vance Press Conference 


TA191738Y Jerusalem Domestic Service in Hebrew 1700 GMT 19 Jan 78 TA 


[Text] Prime Minister Menahem Begin and U.S. Secretary of State Cyrus Vance held a 
lengthy meeting this afternoon. 


The prime minister said the Israeli Government hopes the secretary of state's mission 
to Cairo will be successful. Vance will leave tomorrow to meet with Egyptian President 
as-Sadat. In answer to a correspondent's question, the prime minister said that in 

its weekly meeting the cabinet would decide whether or not to continue the Israeli 
delegation's participation in the Military Committee. This will take place after it 
receives a report on the results of Vance's talks with As-Sadat. 


The prime minister said that it transpired from the talks of the Political Committee 
that there had been a great deal of progress on most clauses but that a few issues 
Still had to be discussed. The prime minister refused to elaborate on which issues 
there already had been agreement. 


The prime minister stressed that the framework of two committees--one a Political Com- 
mittee and the other, military--was very logical, and, therefore, in his opinion, if 
the talks should be renewed, then both should be renewed.

The prime minister emphasized Israel's desire to renew the talks if Egypt adopts a 
decision to this effect. The prime minister repeated his statement as forwarded to 
the Egyptian president that the settlements in the Rafah salient could not be uprooted 
and no government in Israel could make such a decision. In answer to a question, the 
prime minister stressed that the Egyptian president knew on which issues national con- 
sensus in Israel existed prior to his arrival in Jerusalem, The Egyptian president 
stated that Israel must withdraw from all conquered territories, that a Palestinian 
state should be established and that Jerusalem should be returned to the Arabs. He 
was told at the start that, with respect to these issues, Israel would not accede,

Asked by our correspondent if the miin pyoovlsm in continuing the momentum lies ina

Mr Vance was asked if in his opinion the Egyptians would agree to proposals the United
