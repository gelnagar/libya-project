LIBYA

AL-QADHDHAFI FORECASTS UNREST IN EGYPT, SAUDI ARABIA 
LD18150 London BBC Domestic Television Service in English 2100 GMT 17 Jan 79 LD 


[Text] Britain's past relations with the shah have been defended in the Commons by the 
Foreign Se-retary, Dr Owen, but he agreed that it had been a mistake to underestimate 
the mounting political opposition inside Iran, After the events there, other Muslim 
countries could face similar internal problems, a possibility examined by our diplomatic 
correspondent, John Simpson: [begin recording] 


[Simpson] Although the shah was finally brought down by a mass movement involving people 
of almost every kind in Iran, it began as a powerful combination of political leftwingers 
and religious fundamentalists. The same kind of alliance is forming elsewhere in the 
Muslim world, particularly in Western-inclined « untries, The govevnment which has given 
most support to the anti-shah movement has been Labya, and on Monday--the shah's last 
day in power--I traveled there to meet its leader, colonel Al-Qadhdhafi. I met him at 
a small camp in the desert where he had gone to rest and ponder on the idiosyncratic 
evolution he has brought to Libya. He strongly rejects what he sees as corruption of 
Western influence and he preaches the need to return to simpler virtues. He believes 
that Egypt will undergo the convulsions Iran has been through, that Saudi Arabia--another 
friend of the West--will follow. 


[Al-Qadhdhafi] It will come, too--to bring this thing to Seudi Arabia and to Egypt and 
other countries similar to this country, to Iran, 


[Question] When you say the time will come to bring it, does that mean it should be done 
from outside or to be done within the country? 


Vv. 19 Jan 79 NORTH AFRICA 


[Answer] No, no, It will be done from people itself from inside Saudi Arabia, inside, 
and Egypt as it is now being done inside the Iran from the people itself, by the people 
itself, by the masses. 


[Simpson] Colonel Al-Qadhdhafi denies giving money or arms to the shah's enemies. Wes- 
tern diplomats believe that days when he freely armed guerrilla movements like the IRA 
are now past. He himself says he is not in favor of the Soviet Union, but that mistakes 
the West has made in Iran and elsewhere in the Muslim world simply play into the hands 
of Russians. [end recording]
