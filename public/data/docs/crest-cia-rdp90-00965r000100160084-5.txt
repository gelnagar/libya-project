RECRUITING AMERICANS TO AID QADDAFI

Summary (AI): A 1981 Washington Post article describes how American former CIA agents, businessmen and military specialists (Edwin Wilson, Frank Terpil, Jerome Brower, Joseph Cappucci) were recruited to work for Qaddafi's Libya, mainly in arms and explosives dealings. Nothing in it is set in Tripoli itself.
• Brower was fined $5,000 and ordered to serve four months of a five-year prison term.
• Wilson reportedly had dozens of companies, with the article quoting a witness saying about 90 of 100 deals were legitimate.
• Brower supplied high explosives to Libya and Uganda, with knowledge and intent, and also supplied experts who taught customers how to use them.
• Federal investigators found no evidence of criminal activity by Cappucci and dropped the case.
• 1978-05: Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms investigators were told by an informant that one of Cappucci's employees was trying to obtain a large quantity of arms for export to Libya.
