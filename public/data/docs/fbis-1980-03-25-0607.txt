CHAD SITUATION DISCUSSED WITH NIGER DELEGATION 
LD242228 Tripoli Voice of the Arab Homeland in Arabic 2015 GMT 24 Mar 80 LD 


[Text] A spokesman at the Foreign Secretariat said that the Libyan Arab and Niger 
delegations held talks on the occasion of the visit of President Seymi Kountche to 
the Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriyah and reviewed the situation in Chad. 
They expressed their concern over this grave development in the internal situation 
in the Republic of Chad and agreed that the situation reached this stage because the 
Lagos agreement for national reconciliation has not been implemented. 


They stressed that their great interest in this deteriorating situation stems from 

their concern, in their capacity as states neighboring Chad and as members of the Lagos 
conference for national reconciliation, for peace and stability to prevail in that 
country. Proceeding from this concern, the two sides called for an immediate cease-fire 
and a halt to the bloodshed now taking place in Chad. They agreed on the need to call 
for the urgent convening of a new conference for national reconciliation in Chad. 


In this connection the two sides affirmed their adherence to the Lagos agreement for 
national reconciliation asa framework for finding an African solution to an African 
problem without foreign interference. They agreed that imperialist interference consti- 
tutues the only obstacle to solving the problem and preventing the establishment of 
peace and stability in Chad. This was underlined by all parties concerned in the 
agreement signed in Lagos on 23 August 1979 regarding national reconcialiation in Chad. 


Niger President's Statement 
LD241406 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 24 Mar 80 LD 


[Excerpt] Niger President Seyni Kountche and members of his delegation left Tripoli 
this morning at the end of his 3-day cordial visit to the Jamahiriyah. 


In a statement to a JANA correspondent on his departure he said: During the visit, we 
had very fruitful talks on the close relations and bilateral agreements. We always re- 
viewed the situation in the Arab region and the African Continent, as well as the 
international issues. 


He said of the situation in Chad: We have affirmed the need for stability and peace in 
Chad. In this respect, we have invited our brothers in Chad to stop the fighting. He 
pointed out that Libya and Niger, as two countries neighboring Chad and as members of 
the Chad peace committee, have worked to bring about the signing of national peace 
agreements in Chad and called on the disputing parties to respect the Lagos national 
peace agreements.

JANA EDITOR SAYS EGYPT CAMOUFLAGING NEW AGGRESSION 
LD241346 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 24 Mar 80 LD 


[Text] The JANA political editor has commented on recent statements by Lt Gen Ahmad 
Badawi, chief of staff of the defeated president's forces. He said the Libyans are 
right to inquire about the motvies behind these statements, particularly as they follow 
the provocative Egyptian military maneuvers which were staged at the Libyan border 
within the past few days and which were attended by Lieutenant General Badawi himself. 


The political editor added that the statements also come in the wake of the Egyptian 
authorities! decision to transfer Egyptian forces from the Suez Canal area to the border 
with Libya. The Israelis greatly welcomed this decision. 


The JANA editor added that this provides proof that the American arms which are being 
stockpiled by the regime of the defeated Egyptian president are intended for fighting 
the progressive revolutions in the Arab region and Africa and that a Great 1 September 
revolution, being the revolution most hostile to capitulation and imperialism, is a 
direct objective and target of these stockpiled arms in the arsenal of the regime of the 
defeated Egyptian president. This in turn points to a new aggressive action which the 
defeated Egyptian regime is camouflaging by such statements and which it plans to 

carry out on instigation of imperialism and Zionism.
