Summary (AI): A Boston Globe article reports that Eugene Tafoya, a former Green Beret charged in the October 1980 shooting of Libyan dissident Faisal Zagallai in Colorado, made three visits to Libya in 1980 and was possibly in contact with former CIA agent Edwin Wilson, who was in Libya. The case is linked to the US decision to close the Libyan embassy in Washington.
• Tafoya held in lieu of $1 million bond in Fort Collins, Colo.; no trial date set as of the article.
• Four passports in Tafoya's name were seized; one showed three 1980 visits to Libya, two before and one after the October shooting.
• Wilson's business card, for the Tripoli office of a Swiss-based company he controls, was found at Tafoya's residence.
• Wilson was detained in Malta in August 1980 at US State Department request and released within 48 hours.
• Tripoli reportedly announced it ordered the shooting of Zagallai.
• 1980-10-14: Faisal A. Zagallai, a Libyan graduate student at Colorado State University, was shot twice in the head by a man posing as a job recruiter; he survived but was blinded in one eye.
• 1980-04: A federal grand jury indicted former CIA agents Edwin P. Wilson and Francis E. Terpil for illegally supplying explosives and training terrorists for the Libyan government.
• 1980-04-22: Eugene A. Tafoya was arrested at his home after a pistol found near the shooting scene was traced to him.
• 1980-07: Tafoya spent about three months in Libya (July to September 1980) on official Libyan government business, per a passport marked 'mission'.
