Summary (AI): A May 1973 CIA weekly special report on Libya's arms procurement under Qadhafi since 1969: roughly $700 million in contracts, mainly from France and the USSR, with the UK and US no longer the main suppliers. It also notes Libya's armed forces are ineffective because it lacks trained personnel.
• Libya concluded agreements for almost $700 million worth of arms since the September 1969 coup; about $360 million had been delivered by the report date.
• France has sold Libya about $410 million worth of arms, including 110 Mirage III and Mirage 5 aircraft (contract worth at least $250 million) and helicopters.
• The USSR sold Libya nearly $130 million in arms, including agreements for 250 T-54/55 tanks, 130 BTR-60 APCs and 30 ZSU-23-4 self-propelled AA guns.
• Italy contracted to supply about $70 million in arms; the UK and US sales were limited to spares, small arms and non-lethal equipment after 1969.
• An Egyptian training mission of about 550 men accounted for 80 percent of foreign military advisers in Libya; about 1,300 Libyans trained in Egypt.
• Qadhafi's outlay for arms was about $330 per capita.
