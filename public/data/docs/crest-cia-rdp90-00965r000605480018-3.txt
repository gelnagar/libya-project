Summary (AI): A January 1986 Christian Science Monitor article (dated 1985 in the OCR) on Libyan students in the US who support Qaddafi. It covers the McLean, Virginia student office, FBI surveillance over an alleged assassination plot, Libyan visa and student counts, and the 1981 closure of the Libyan Embassy in Washington. None of it concerns Tripoli itself.
• The People's Committee for Students of the Libyan Arab Jamahiriya Inc. office in McLean, Va. was one of only two official Libyan offices in the US; the other was Libya's UN mission in New York.
• INS statistics cited 3,200 to 3,300 Libyan citizens in the US on visas, of whom 1,200 to 1,300 were students.
• Qaddafi's Jan. 2 warning said he would 'pursue US citizens in their country and streets' in retaliation for US sanctions after the Rome and Vienna airport attacks.
• The FBI investigated an alleged Libyan plot to assassinate anti-Qaddafi dissidents in exile in the US; officials said it helped foil assassination plots in four states.
• 1984-05: Two 36-year-old Libyan 'students' arrested in Philadelphia after buying three .45 caliber pistols and two bulletproof vests from an undercover FBI agent
• 1985-05: Farhat Tibar, administrative attaché at Libya's UN mission, expelled from the US after FBI linked him to a purported plot to assassinate anti-Qaddafi Libyans
• 1981: Libyan Embassy in Washington closed and US diplomatic relations suspended following attempted assassination of a dissident Libyan student at Colorado State University
