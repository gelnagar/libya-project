LIBYA

Further on Al-Qadhdhafi LE POINT Interview 
LD181649 Tripoli JANA in English 1403 GMT 18 Jul 83 


[Text] Tripoli, Shawwal 7, July 17, JAMAHIRIYAH NEWS 
AGENCY — The widely-read French magazine Le Point has 
interviewed the leader of the revolution, Colonel Mu’ammar 
Al-Qadhdhafi, on a number of Arab and African matters, par- 
ticularly on the recent developments in Chad and the attempt to 
internationalise the situation there. 


The leader said that what is happening in Chad is a struggle for 
power and that Libya is taking a neutral stand in this conflict. 
“We are very sorry for such grave developments concerning the 
weapons uses bv the rival group”. The leader said that it is 
believed that the weapons already captured in Abeche are 
Egyptian- and Israeli-made weapons and that the Libyan weap- 
ons exist in the city are old and were left for Goukouni Oueddei 
after the Libyan forces withdrawai from there. 


To the question of what would be Libya’s attitude to the possible 
sending of French forces, in the future to Chad, the leader said 
that it is a premature question, but in any case Libya would not 
overlook the situation that is taking place right on the borders. 
“There is no need for a European country in the heart of Africa 
and such an act would be considered a threat to Libya”. 


“We hope that a peaceful settlement would prevail over Chad 
and that Libya would contribute with all her efforts for a peaceful 
settlement among the conflicting parties. 


“Anyway, Habre does not have the legitimacy or power to rule 
Chad, which comprises 12 factions.” 


The leader added that Libya is in direct contact with the 
Organisation of African Unity (OAU) because Chad’s problem 
is of African concern. “Zaire should be tried for its unilateral 
action. By sending troops to Chad Zaire has challenged the 
legitimacy of the OAU”. 


The leader commented on France’s position regarding the Chad- 
ian issue, saying: “France has no right whatsoever to despatch 
arms or soldiers or even to talk about a country in the heart of 
Africa. Hence, France's intervention in Chad is unjustifiable and 
can only indicate that France continues to uphold the old form 
of colonialism, or the policy of imperialism”. 


Concerning the agreements by which the French Government 
justifies its intervention in Chad, the leader said: “These justi- 
fications insult French public opinion by completely overlooking 
it. Furthermore, the agreement between France and Chad is 
bound by aggression by a third foreign country against either of 
the signatories and does not warrant intervention on behalf of one 
faction against others as is the case in Chad now”. 


He pointed out the serious threats posed by kind of agreements 
France has established with the African states since these diverse 
from the internationally accepted forms. 


The leader added: “If such justifications are tenable, it would 
mean that by virtue of its agreements with other African states 
France will intervene to support any rebel against other legiti- 
mate parties, since Hissein Habre is himself a minister who has 
mutinied against the head of state. How is it justifiable to 
conclude a treaty with him against the head of state? France can 
only act in accordance with these treaties in the event of an 
outside aggression against Chad”. 


On the question of the attendance of Habre’s delegate of the 
OAU meeting in Addis Ababa, the leader said: “If we were to 
consider this a precedence then every rebel minister would 
receive recognition and this means that all the African heads of 
state occupy precarious positions and are in constant danger of 
having their positions taken by a rebel minister. Thus, should the 
defence minister of Zaire rebel, he would become the legitimate 
president in the country and Mobutu becomes the rebel. The 
minister becomes installed as president and is recognised as 
such”. 


Regarding France's cautions to Libya on the question of Chad 
the leader pointed out that President Mitterand had no right to 
caution Libya since the issue does not involve it. He said “France 
is a European country with a colonial past in Africa. Its past 
history in Chad is black and it is responsible for all the problems. 
As far as Libya is concerned it is an African border state which 
is connected by proximity of land, enjoying the same geograph- 
ical position and people. We have the same origins, half the 
Chadian people are of Libyan origin vice-versa. Furthermore, the 
majority of the Chadian people are Moslems like us. Thus how 
can the leader of a European state from the heart of Europe 
intervene between two countries so closely linked unless it is 
attempting to divert French public attention from the problems 
of Corsica and others faced by the French Government? 


“However, if the statements by France are intended for local 
consumption or to reassure the French-speaking countries, we 
have no objection.” 


In reply to the question on French policy in Africa since the 
assumption of power by Mitterrand, the leader said: “Mit- 
terrand’s policy is supposed to be better for, generally speaking, 
the policy of the socialists should have been much better, as had 
been our expectations when they assumed power in France. 
[sentence 2s received] However, the situation can improve in the 
future. France continues to adhere to its old colonialism, a policy 
to which neither we, nor the rest of the people, can adhere”.
