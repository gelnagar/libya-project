EDITORIAL ATTACKS CORRUPTION IN REVOLUTIONARY COMMITTEES 


London AL-ZAHAF AL-AKHDAR in English 22 Oct 82 p 7 


[Text ] 





ONE OF the central tasks of the revolutio- 
nary committees, who are the main tool of 
the popular revolution, is to agitate amongst 
the masses to eradicate the out-dated men- 
tality of pre-revolutionary times, which can- 
not absorb the fact that the masses have 
arrived at the pinnacle of power and hold all 
authority. 


One of the revolutionary committees’ tasks 
is therefore to uncover all such backward 
and reactionary types and to incense the 
masses against them. The revolutionary 
committees have therefore to work 
doggedly, day and night, to destroy the last 
remnants of the exploitative society, a 
society of master-slave relationships, aclass 
society. 

It is therefore necessary to strive and 
apply all the revolutionary slogans we chant 
so loudly against those who think they stand 
above the masses, against all those who try to 
act as representatives of the Libyan people 
and impose decisions emanating only from 
themselves, ignoring the authority of the 
people. 

The General people's committees are not 
representatives ofthe Arab people of Libya; 
nor can they deputise for them. They are not 
the ones who make the decisions, which are 
taken by the popular masses in their peo- 
ple’s congresses. All authority belongs to the 
people and no one has the right to represent 
the people. It is the people who have 
inherited authority and wealth and nobody 
other than the people is qualified to manage 
this authority and wealth. 

We repeat this today as the Arab Libyan 
people are celebrating the thirteenth 
anniversary of their great Revolution and as 
they announce the beginning of the great 
battle of confrontation with the Zionist- 
imperialist enemy, on the outcome of which 
the fate of our whole nation depends. 


We say this to remind these’ so-called 
‘gentlemen’ who raise themselves above the 





popular masses — excepting a minority who 
can be counted on the fingers of one hand 
and who work in silence and devotion in a 
truly revolutionary way — that the people of 
this country will not stand for any lessening 
of our revolutionary standards. 

This latter group have accomplished much 
in a short period and are busy trying to 
repair the economy and improve indus- 
trialisation projects, where it is necessary to 
purge corruption through bureaucrats 
ensconsed there. These brokers, these mer- 
chants and ex-merchants have been appoin- 
ted to positions in the people’s markets as a 
result of nepotism, or have been placed 
there by the enemies of the Revolution with 
the specific task of ruining our economy. 


Then there are those who import clothing 
from the West, clothing which leaves a lot to 
be desired, and those who control our daily 
bread who have managed to infiltrate the 
people’s markets evén after our popular 
masses marched on their exploitative shops. 

It is against this criminal group of petty- 
bourgeois exploiters that our revolutionary 
committees have to march, for it is they who 
are daily demeaning, even destroying the 
great achievements of the Revolution. They 
hope that by creating an economic situation 
of chaos and confusion, they will alienate 
the masses from the Revolution and get them 
to help re-introduce a society of explei- 
tation, or profit and monopoly capitalism. 


In this newspaper we have more than once 
tried to deal with those who try to lay siege to 
the Revolution and have asked them to 
change their ways. Our state of the masses is 
not a traditional state — it does not depend 
on some reformist regime making vague and 
shifty programmes meant to stave off the 
worst excesses of the exploiters. We live ina 
revolutionary state and the enemies of the 
Revolution must not forget this. 


Our trade bureaux abroad are one of the 
pillars of our socialist experiment. Their

the smallest possible cost to the people's

Instead of dispatching keen young

connections and mediation have taken over, 
so that various friends and relatives are 
given these posts to pay back favours by 
offering possibilities of easy enrichment. 
These officials were then given all sorts of 
facilities and amenities without con- 
sideration for their true personal capacities. 


Thus the decisions and recommendations 
of the Libyan people have been superceded 
by general secretaries who obey the wishes of 
some bourgeois families rather than faithfully 
execute the decisions of the people’s con- 
gresses. Right now, due to bad decisions by 
incompetent officials, mountains of impor- 
ted goods are dumped along the seashore 
where they rust and decay. Responsible for 
such waste — which amounts to stealing the 
people's resources — are those appointed by 
the people to manage the general economy 
and the light industrial sectors — and whose 
revolutionary beliefs in democracy, free- 
dom aad’social justice do not go beyond the 
doors of the television studio or the halls of 
the general people’s congresses. 

The role of trade officials and of the 
accountancy secretariat which has proved 
unable to stop the pilfering, needs assessing. 
It is up to the true revolutionary forces, the 
honest people within the secretariats, the 
banks and elsewhere to make the finance 
department accountable for its par- 
ticipation in the corruption of the economy, 
either by passively closing their eyes or by 
participating actively. We, for our part, will 
never cease to expose such wrong-doings
