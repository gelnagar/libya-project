(Text ] New York, October 9, JAMAHIRIYAH NEWS AGENCY--Addressing the U.N, General 
Assembly this morning, the foreign secretary said that in spite of the U.N, enormous 
efforts to achieve world peace and security and its belief in the people's right to 
self-determination and to freedom, a glimpse of the current international situation 
proves how far we are from achieving the goals from which the United Nations was 
establisiied and man's dreams of prosperity and justice. 


The foreign secretary pointed out that many nations are still under racist and 
colonialist rule, which insists upon its (?privileges) and moropolies and contimues to 
exploit the people and their resources, as well as preventing its effectiveness, 
require a revision of its charter so that equality and justice could prevail among its 
member-states regardless of these members! economic, political and military power.

The foreign secretary clarified in his speech that the drafting of the U.N, Charter was 
carried out under difficult international circumstances, following in the wake of bitter 
battles that have changed the balance of power. The charter came at a time, he said, 
when the independent states did not exceed 51, but the situation has altered now, 
changing with it the political and economic international scheme. 


The foreign secretary added that this impels us to revise the charter and cancel the 
privileges the superpowers granted themselves at the time. 


He indicated that the achievement of international peace and security will remain a 
landmark in human history and the United Nations, with all the people of the world 
under its domain, will continue to be responsible for the fulfillment of this objective 
which cannot come about except if all the people were politically and economically free. 


He said: "The declaration of human rights is still a document which is not respected 
in many parts of the world. The deplorable racism in its various forms contimes to 

be practised and the colonialist countries persist in their domination of the economies 
of developing nations through their direct monopolies or through their multi-national 
companies. More than three quarters of the world population are still living in extreme 
poverty, while the minorities continue to monopolize and manoeuvre the world economy to 
its advantage. He asserted that peace cannot be achieved as long as the world was 
divided into two parts--a majority, whose poverty increases daily, and a minority, whose 
wealth increases at the cost of the majority. 


In his address the foreign secretary spoke about the situation in the African Continent 
and said: "(?What) worries all of us is the continent, which suffered in the past from 
racial oppression exemplified in the slavery of a third of its population in other 
continents particularly in America, is still a huge area where racial discrimination is 
practised by colonialist white minority especially in the southern part", 


He pointed out that the continuation of the racist regime in the southern part of the 
continent to practise discrimination and collective annihilation of black majority 
represents a defiance to humanity and the United Nations. On this organization lies 
an historical responsibility to eliminate the plague of racism.

Dr, at-Turayki called on the United Nations to condemn the French policy and put a 

limit to the French intervention and military presence in African Reunion Island. The 
French Government is justifying its intervention by claiming it is for the protection 
of Central African children, Instead, we wished it had halted its arms exports to the 
racist regimes of southern Africa which used them to kill Atrican children, We wished 
so much that the French Government had sent its troops to Zimbabwe to put a limit to the 
racist regime there and prevent the killing of thousands of innocent Africans. 


How we wished that these troops had been sent to Palestine to defend men and human rights 
so that we could say that the French Government was sincere for once in the history of 
the French people to struggle for human rights. By sending its troops to invade Africa 
and kill innocent Africans and their children in the Sahara, Chad, and lately in Central 
Africa, France is violating the principles of the United Nations, the Organization of 
African Unity and even of the French Revolution. 


Referring to the situation in the Arab region, the foreign secretary said: "What we call 
the Middle East conflict is but that deriving from the installation by imperialist 

powers of a racist settler regime in the Arab region, and the attempts to find solutions to 
this problem, without understanding the Zionist racist regime, would only be partial 
interim settlements, which would not lead to real peace in the area." 


The foreign secretary went on to say: "Since the First World War when Britain had 

granted Palestine to the white racist Zionists emigrating from Europe, the Arab nation and 
the Palestinian people have waged long wars, The colonialists have also created and 
installed racist regimes in South Africa." The foreign secretary added: "The Arab 

nation had waged these wars and launched struggles in defence of its existence against the 
racist Zionist invasion, which was supported by imperialism. For this imperialism conceives 
the culture, economic and geographic potential possessed by the Arab nation as a consequent 
danger that could be posed by an Arab unity against its interests and Africa and Asia." The 
foreign secretary also emphasized that the Palestinian problem is first of all a homeland 
one, a problem of people who were expelled from their homeland in order to be granted 

[as received] to alien immigrants. The foreign secretary also referred to the recent peace 
efforts by imperialism, affirming thatsuch efforts are intended to legitimize occupation 
and enable the occupier to expand in the Arab territories, 


Dr. at-Turayki declared that the Arab nation is more capable to understand the meaning 

of peace, having been exposed to extermination and invasion by various forms of colonialism 
throughout the ages, Different parts of this nation were subjected to the Igalian, British, 
and French invasions, 


He said it was necessary in this respect to define the nature of the peace processes and 
the events currently taking place in the Arab region between the Zionist enemy and the 
Egyptian regime on the one hand, and between them and the United States on the other, He 
said the latter was assuming dangerously great responsibilities in the present attempts to 
establish the so-called peace, which in fact was designed to liquidate the Palestinian 
people and destroy its national ambitions and its very existence. 


In his address, the foreign secretary remarked that the U.S. actions constitute a grave 
precedent in international relations, as a big country is trying to compel a defeated 
state, namely Egypt to sign an agreement conceding the rights of a people on behalf of 
which the latter has no right to act--the Palestinian people. It is compelling Egypt 
to concede its sovereignty and to desert its national stand and its nation, history and 
civilization. 


He said the defeated Egyptian regime has signed, under the pressure of the United States of 
America, the capitulatory agreements with the Zionist racist occupation regime. These 
agreements have given up the Palestinian cause and violated the resolutions of the Arab 


league, the Organization of African Unity, the Islamic conference and the non-alignment 
movement,

The secretary also said that the defeated president has assumed the role of policeman in

The secretary explained that the oil-producing countries want to preserve their oil 
production as long as possible. If oil production continues at its present rate of 
7 billion tons a year, oil wells would drain in twenty years. The secretary also 
drew attention to the efforts of industrial countries to raise the prices of their 
oil up to the highest levels by OPEC. They intend by these policies to compensate 
for inflation and the devaluation of the dollar. 


During the first quarter of 1979, inflation rates in the U.S., and in Britain, France 
and West Germany were 13; 17.6; 12.4; and 9.6 percent respectively. The secretary 
indicated that inflation should not be dealt with by increasing oil prices. 


Dr. at-Turayki said statistics indicate that energy consumption was heavily concentrated 
in the advanced countries, which included only 30 percent of the total world population 
and were responsible for consuming 80 percent of the world energy consumption, while 

the developing countries which have 70 percent of the world population use (?less) 

than 20 percent of the energy. 


He said that the energy consumed by the U.S.A. and Western Europe, for the use of 
private cars only, almost equals the energy used for commercial purposes in the 
developing countries. Dr. at-Turayki declared that the consumption of Western countries 
was estimated at 74.470 barrels p.a. in 1976 while the rest of the world, the Eastern 
camp exclusive, has used only 18.497 million barrels. 


Dr. at-Turayki described the mass media campaign launched by the Western industrial 
countries against OPEC as a political concentrated campaign which was not based on 
true facts. 


The secretary said that OPEC countries are aware of these manoeuvres, that they would 
resist all forms of military, political and economic threats and interventions directed 
against our peoples, and that OPEC would confront the attempts which could lead to 
starvation, the rise in the prices of manufactured commodities and threats of oceupying 
the oil fields. The foreign secretary also explained that the value of assistance 
granted by OPEC to the developing countries amounted to 1229 [as received] million 
dollars by the end of 1978. In their meeting in June 1979 OPEC countries have also 
decided to raise their assistance to the developing countries by 800 million dollars, 
so that the special fund for the developing world would have a capital of 2.4 billion 
dollars. OPEC countries, Dr. at-Turayki maintained, have offered this assistance out 
of their solidarity with the developing countries and their conviction that the process 
of development is of common concern and that each country has to fulfill its responsibility 
in this respect. 


The secretary announced the Jamahiriyah's refusal to discuss the energy question aside 
from the major economic problems or outside the U.N. He considered the proposal of 
Group 77. 


For conducting comprehensive negotiations in accordance with a proposal by the non- 
alignment [as received] as an expression of the majority of the peoples who are 
interested in seeking total solutions to the international economic problems through 
the U.N. auspices. The secretary also expressed support for the declaration issued 

by Group 77 after their mesting on 28 September 1979, which reflected the attitudes of 
the developing countries towards the establishment of a new international economic 
order and their rejection of all forms of economic pressures and political threats from 
the developing countries. At the conclusion of his speech, the foreign secretary 
praised the achievements of the sixth non-alignment conference held in Havana and 
Cuba's efforts towards its success, affirming the commitment by the non-alignment 
movement to struggle against -imperialism, Zionism and colonialism, as well as to 

make efforts for establishing a new international economic order equitable towards 

the developing countries.
