OIL SALES TO FRANCE-—Kuwait will deliver 30,000 barrels of oil a day to France 
(375,000 tons for the last quarter). The two French companies will divide these 
amounts equally. If the contract is concluded "at the official price," Kuwait 
has demanded, in return, the purchase by the French companies, of 80,000 tons of 
propane (initially the Emirate wanted to sell 150,000 tons at $100 per ton) at a 
price $100 higher—including transport--than the going price for propane in 
Europe. That raises the price of Kuwait's petroleum by some two dollars per bar- 


rel if this propane is brought to France. [Excerpt] [Paris LE MONDE in French 
25 Nov 80 p 46]

MAURITANIA 


CLA. .S REPORTED BETWEEN PRO-IRAQ, PRO-LIBYA STUDENTS 
Paris LE MONDE in French 30 Dec 80 p 5 


ffext] Nouakchott (AFP)--"Subversive activities,” conducted in Mauritania 
by a "friendly country” not named but said to be Libya, according to observ- 
ers, were the subject of two official communiques published on Saturday, 

27 December, in Nouakchott. In the first, the Military Committee for Na- 
tional Safety (CMSN), which has been controlling Mauritania since 1978, de- 
plores "certain activities of a subversive nature conducted by a friendly 
country within Mauritanian territory.” 


A second communique, published by the Council of Ministers, denounces "the 
regrettable situation created by the actions of certain diplomatic represen- 
tatives of friendly and brotherly countries." The Council of Ministers says 
it has also analyzed “the worrisome and dangerous tendency of certain Mauri- 
tanian citizens to be in collusion with the exterior and to obey orders re- 
ceived from foreign powers through individual or group action.” 


These warnings are the aftermath of a wave of protestations "remote-con- 
trolled from abroad” in certain Nouakchott scholastic circles. The protes- 
tations were said to be the work of pro-Libyan and pro-Iranian individuals 
opposed to teachers considered to be Iraqi partisans. 


The movement originated at the Franco-Arab lycee of Nouakchott where a group 
of students struck to contest the professional capabilities of a Mauritanian 
professor who was educated in Iraq and of two Iraqi professors of natural 
sciences and mathematics. According to several witnesses, the strike degen- 
erated into a brawl between students some of whom were hostile, some favor- 
able, to the teachers. The police arrested about 100 students. The move- 
ment then spread to other institutions in the capital, and other motives 
were contrived by the striking students. The strike was interrupted only at 
the end of a week with the start of the year-end vacation on Wednesday, 

24 December. 


Mauritania is engaged in a high level of cooperation with Iraq which, more- 
over, it has supported in its conflict with Iran, These positions are not 
apt to please the supporters of the Imam Khomeyni, and Libya.

SULTANATE OF OMAN 


NEW FIVE-YEAR PLAN DETAILED 
Beirut THE ARAB WORLD WEEKLY in English 20 Dec 80 pp 12-18 


[Text] The new although limited role for the U.S.-backed Sultanate of Oman as 
protector and gendarme for the entrance to the Gulf to the extent that it could 
rightfully be called the Western nations’ watchful eye in the Gulf was reflected 
in its second Five Year Development Plan recently released. 


The Plan approved by Sultan Qaboos bin Said, shortly before the 10th anniversary 
marking the bloodless takeover of power by Qaboos in 1970, stressed defence and 
national security as a national priority. 


Allocations for defence and national security and related civil projects con- 
ceived for defence purposes will amount to RO 2,973 million over the Plan 
period (1981-85) which is over RO 800 million higher than development expendi- 
ture envisaged over the five years.
