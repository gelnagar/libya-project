COMMENTARY ON SAUDI SEVERING OF RELATIONS 


LD291417 Tripoli Domestic Service in Arabic 1230 GMT 29 Oct 80 


[Unattributed commentary] 


[Excerpts] Who is more important than he who rows against the current? How stupid are 
those who believe that historic, natural relations are severed by means of a decision 

or come to an end by a royal decree? The Arab people, from the ocean to the Gulf, are 
indivisible, whole, whatever the regional frontiers are, or wherever guards and gates 
are intensified, or however loud the ugly voice of royal decrees may be on the severence 
of relations. For the deep relationship of Arab fraternity has grown and settled 
throughout the history of this big homeland. It transcends the imaginary frontiers, 
decisions, crowns, flags and anthems, the governments which have dominated those masses 
and penetrated thrones and posts in order to create a distance between the masses and 
their freedom, cohesion and unity. 


Those governments and regimes realize through their rotten memtality that their first 
enemy is the fuse of the revolution and its sacred march. For the revolution and its 
concepts are a sweeping current and advance (?like) science as a rolling flood which 

knows no (?taboos) and only admits the strength of the people, their sovereignty and 

authority. 


Reactionary regimes have thrown themselves into the lap of colonialism, bringing it to 
their abode, cheering it, praising it and selling it the keys of the sanctuaries. They 
have established with colonialism what God has ordered to sever by means of documents 
and undertakings. They set themselves to antagonize every rimging word of truth calling 
for jihad against the enemy and taking up arms against him. Those puppets have set 
themselves to fill the air with their wailing and clowning as they felt they are 
approaching their end, and that there is neither escape nor shelter. They have felt 
that the voice coming from a distance hailing the people's rewolution is a certain 
expression of what is taking place in the minds of those masses who are tied to the 
fetters of injustice and persecution. The masses aspire to the day of the greater march 
and tremor in order to demolish the strongholds of injustice and persecution where acts 
of treason are perpetrated and justified. Then neither decrees nor royal decree will 

be of any use, nor will there be any use in sheltering behind ugly regional frontiers 
and throwing oneself into the lap of colonialism. 


The tide of the people and the flood of the revolution and masses will indeed move in order 
to bring to account those reactionary pockets and establish the one socialist, 
revolutionary Arab society, and revenge a dignity that was ignored, innocent souls 

killed, a history whose events were distorted and a faith that is crying to the God 

of the American Abrahah [Ethiopian ruler of Yemen in the 7th century who tried to attack 
the Ka'bah] and his new beasts who have severed what God has ordered to be maintained 

and have corrupted the earth. 


V. 30 Oct 80 ISRAEL 


NAVON, AS-SADAT ANNOUNCE RESULTS OF TALKS 
TA291130 Jerusalem Domestic Service in Hebrew 1100 GMT 29 Oct 80 


[Text] Following the meeting of Presidents as-Sadat and Navon at the Egyptian president's 
birthplace, the establishment of a joint headquarters for the peace process and the 

opening of the land border for a free transfer of goods has been officially announced. 
Among other issues in the framework of normalization that were summed up, there is to be an 
additional El Al flight to Cairo and an exchange of youth delegations. The two 

presidents also stated that they discussed issues pertaining to autonomy talks and 

several suggestions were formulated for future discussion. 


MINISTERS COMPLAIN NAVON EXCEEDED AUTHORITY 


TA291137 Tel Aviv DAVAR 29 Oct 80 p 1 


[By Daniyel Bloch] 


[Text] Cabinet ministers are claiming that President Navon exceeded his authority when 
he conducted political negotiations in his talks with President as-Sadat. According to 
these ministers, the president should not have entered into a discussion on the details 
of normalization and autonomy and he should have been content with a general exchange 

of remarks. President as-Sadat, for his own reasons, wanted to use President Navon to 
bypass the accepted negotiating channels, as well as to iighlight Prime Minister Menahem 
Begin's intransigence. According to conclusions reached by the Cabinet yesterday, Begin 
sent the president a cable presenting the Cabinet position: that President Navon has 

no authority to discuss the autonomy issue and that, as concerns the normalization 
issue, he should speak only about the implementation of agreements already achieved--the 
implementation of which has been delayed--and not about new issues. 


Governmental sources claimed that President as-Sadat is methodically "selling" the 
normalization issues on several occasions and each time he creates the impression that 

it is a new issue. However, in fact, most of the promises are not fulfilled in practice. 
The issues raised during the Navon-As-Sadat talk are those that As-Sadat had already 
guaranteed to implement several times in the past--to Dayan, Begin, Shamir and U.S. 
representatives--and on which he has not yet kept his word. 


The Cabinet decision was formulated politely and courteously toward the president. At 
the beginning of his cable to the president, Begin congratulated Navon on the success 

of his visit to Egypt. However, he gave him clear explanations as to how to prevent him 
from deviating from the government policy. Parliamentary observers predict that when 
the president returns from Egypt, the Likud will begin a campaign of criticism of the 
fact that Navon tried to conduct political negotiations, of the size and composition of 
his entourage--which contained many more than 19 people--and also of the way the trip 
was organized. 


BEGIN EXPRESSES REGRET OVER WEIZMAN'S ACTIVITIES 
TA281636 Tel Aviv ITIM in Hebrew 1520 GMT 28 Oct 80 
[Government Press Office Release] 


[Excerpt] Jerusalem, 28 Oct--In the Cabinet session the prime minister was asked about 

MK 'Ezer Weizman's activities in the United States. Following is Begin's answer as 
submitted to the Cabinet: "I am expressing my deep regret over MK 'Ezer Weizman's 
interference in the democratic election process in the United States. It is an outstanding 
rule that a citizen of a democratic country should in no way interfere in the election 
campaign of another democratic country. The Israeli Government still adheres to this 

rule. U.S. citizens, and only they, will choose their president according to their own 
convictions. Israel is glad that the American people, regardless of their partisan 
beliefs, supports it and its security."

COMMUNIQUE OFCEAO DEFENSE PACT SUMMIT CONFERENCE 
AB281802 Niamey Domestic Service in French 1200 GMT 28 Oct 80 


[Final communique of second summit conference of the CEAO Nonaggression and Defense Pact 
held in Niamey 27-28 October] 


[Text] The second conference of heads of state and governments of the Nonagression and 
Defense Pact, ANAD, composed of member countries of the West African Economic Community 
[CEAO] and Togo was held in Niamey on 27 and 28 October 1980 under the chairmanship 

of His Excellency Col Syni Kountche, chairman of the Supreme Military Council, head 

of state of Niger, and current chairman of the pact. Participants in this conference 

are: for the Republic of Senegal, His Excellency Mr Ieopold Sedar Senghor; president of 
Mauritania, His Excellency Lt Col Mohamed Khouna Ould Haydala, chairman of the Military 
Committee of National Salvation and head of state; for the Republic of Upper Volta, 

His Excellency Mr Joseph Issoufou Conombo, prime minister, representing His Excellency 
Gen El Hadj Aboubacar Sangoule Lamizana, president of the republic; for the Republic 

of Ivory Coast, His Excellency Mr Simeon Ake, minister of foreign affairs, representing 
His Excellency Mr Houphouet-Boigny, president of the republic; for the president of 

the Republic of Togo, His Excellency Mr Tevi-Benissan, minister of finance and economy, 
representing His Excellency Gen Gnassingbe Eyadema, president of the republic and head 

of state. The conference of heads of state and government of the Nonagression and Defense Pact 
between member states of the West African Economic Community heard a report from Dr Daouda Diallo, 
minister of foreign affairs and cooperation of the Republic of Niger and current chairman of the 
fourth ministerial council. The conference took note of the political will of all 

member states to ensure the security of our economic and social development. 


The conference of heads of state and government authorizes the secretary general to 
convene, as soon as possible, a meeting of a committee of experts to study the amend- 
ments and the precise details concerning the implementation of the pact in conformity 
with the political aspirations and security of member states. The results of the 
work of this committee will be examined by an extraordinary ministerial council, whose 
decision will be communicated to the current chairman of the conference of heads of 
state and government. 


The heads of state and government have decided to entrust the chairmanship of the con- 
ference of the Nonagression and Defense Pact to His Excellency Mr Leopold Sedar Senghor, 
president of the Republic of Senegal. The conference of heads of state and government 
of the pact congratulates Col (Tavaros de Souza), secretary general of the pact, for 

the work accomplished since his appointment and decided to reappoint him for another 
term of 3 years. 


Finally, the conference of heads of state and government of the Nonagression and Defense 
Pact of CEAO member states and Togo expresses its gratitude to His Excellency Col 

Seyni Kountche, chairman of the Supreme Military Council and head of state, and to the 
government and people of Niger for the warm welcome accorded all the delegations and for 
the facilities put at their disposal. 


Done in Niamey on 28 October 1980.

SENGHOR SPEAKS AT CLOSING SESSION OF SIXTH CEAO SUMMIT 


AB291524 Niamey Domestic Service in France 1200 GMT 28 Oct 80 

[Speech by Senegalese President Leopold Sedar Senghor at the closing session of the 
sixth summit conference of heads of state of the West African Economic Community, 
held in Niamey 27-28 October, in his capacity as the new chairman of the 
community--recorded ] 


[Excerpts] My dear colleagues, Mr Prime Minister, honorable leaders of delegations, 
honorable ambassadors and ministers, Honorable Secretary General of the CEAO [West 
African Economic Community], distinguished representatives of international institutions 
and sister organizations of the West African region, ladies and gentlemen, I will begin 
by carrying out the pleasant duty assigned to me by my colleagues and heads of dele- 
gations of thanking on their behalf President Seyni Kountche, the government and the 
people of Niger. [applause] 


We have come to the end of our meeting, a fruitful one as you said earlier. Since the 
application of our treaty, our organization has made remarkable progress, especially 

in the sphere of exchanges, in which the machinery of the Regional Cooperation Charter, 
CCR, seems to have gained better understanding. In fact, 190 companies were re- 

organized under it on 30 June 1979. In our concern to build a solid community we 

created the Solidarity and Intervention Fund for the Economic Development of the Community, 
FOSIDEC, in Bamako, in October 1978. 


With the creation of the organization chart which was subsequently adopted last October 
in Nouakchott, the CEAO made a decisive step. Though I would not like to redefine 
newer objectives, I would like to lay emphasis on two aspects which seem to play a 
fundamental role in the future of our community. 


The overall world economic situation is very harsh, and it is having an adverse effect 
on all eur states. Our desire to save our limited means and to avoid the dispersion of 
our efforts should lead us to [word indistinct] which our organization cannot exploit 
immediately in view of the situation it is in. In other words, to meet the basic needs 
of our organization we must carry out our most urgent objectives. We must run the 
community in the same rigorous manner that we run our states. 


We cite ECOWAS because of its dimension and the complementarity of the economies of 
member countries. It is an example of development. Finally, at the ECOWAS summit 
held in May 1980 in Lome, cooperation between the CEAO and ECOWAS was again discussed. 
[sentence indistinct] I have always advocated integration in concentraic circles, and 
I hope that through thought and cooperation our two secretariats will succeed in 
harmonizing the various projects of the two organizations. This harmonization is 
necessary and it can be implemented. The leaders of both organizations must therefore 
meet to technically define the data and modes of cooperation. 


Therefore, in the sphere of commercial exchange it is vital to establish a perfect link 
between the two schemes of liberalization, the customs and statistical nomenclature, 
customs laws and finally customs and commercial documents. 


In regard to production, the projects related to this sector must be drawn up in such 
a way that all worthless competition or duplication may be avoided by [words indistinct]. 
Industrial policies must be coordinated and harmonized. 


During my term of office I will pay great attention to these two preoccupations and 
orientations. We must once more avoid the preverse effects of the different policies 

we have already adopted. I am certain that youwillall give me your support. I am 

also counting on the technical support of our General Secretariat, which, with the 

help of its dynamic associates has indeed provided the CEAO not only with a soul but 
with a spirit of important and noteworthy achievements. Allow me to congratulate it 
warmly for these brilliant achievements. Finally, I take the opportunity to extend my 
warmest thanks to the international economic and financial institutions which continually 
give us their support. We are indeed grateful to them. Our community has set off on the 
right foot. We will help maintain its stride. Thank you. [applause]

[Excerpts] The prime minister, Dr Joseph Conombo, returned to Ouagadougou this after- 
noon from Niger, where he attended the sixth CEAO [West African Economic Community] 
summit conference which began yesterday and ended today. He was welcomed by cabinet 
members, directors and heads of services. Shortly after arrival, the prime minister 
answered questions by (Losseni Zogoume): 


[Begin recording] [Question] The sixth summit was expected to hold talks on the 
nonaggression and military assistance protocol, but the accord was sent to subcommittees 
for further consideration. We are aware that the talks on the protocol were put off. 
According to the new current president of the organization, President Senghor, the 
problem was put off because Mauritania raised an essential question. Could you tell us 
what the question was? 


[Answer] Just take a careful look at the map of Africa from east to west, at the 
situation of Mauritania, and at current developments and you will understand that before 
signing such an agreement within the CEAO as the Mutual Defense Pact, the possibilities 
of each country must be taken into account. Mauritania cannot (?afford) to do that. 

But the determination to be and remain a CEAO member state is a certainty. Therefore, 
we will have to find a solution enabling us to (?close ranks). [end recording] 


Traore Comment 


AB292017 Bamako Domestic Service in French 1630 GMT 29 Oct 80 


[Excerpts] The sixth conference of the CEAO [West African Economic Community] summit 
ended yesterday. The president of the republic, Gen Moussa Traore, who took part 
in the deliberations of this sixth conference, declared yesterday on his return from 
Niamey that a decisive stage for the CEAO has been passed. President Moussa Traore 
was interviewed by (Djibril Mboche): 


{Begin recording] As I had the occasion to stress in my speech in response to the open- 
ing speech of President Seyni Kountche, the current president of our organization, this 
Niamey conference has been a decisive phase in the development of the CEAO. It can be 
asserted that at this conference, decisions resulting from the evident desire to achieve 
the goals «f the organization were taken. Especially on the free circulation of goods 
from member countries without any qualitative restrictions, implementation of the pro- 
gram of water supply for villages and livestock raising, and the priority to be given 

to the integration of the various existing community institutions. 


[Question] Mr President, this sixth conference of CEAO also coincides with the second 
conference of the heads of state of the ANAD, the Nonaggression and Defense Pact, which 
links the CEAO countries and Togo. Could we know whether there has been a change in 
Mali's stand and what are the future prospects with respect to the implementation of 
this pact? 


[Answer] A change in Mali's stand? This is an unwavering stand by our country and our 
government. We have reaffirmed our stand on this question by asking our partners to 
agree that the Nonaggression and Defense Aid Pact be thoroughly examined and that this 
pact be accepted by all the parties. And what we agreed on in Niamey is that we are 
going to review this pact thoroughly in the interest of each of the CEAO member coun- 
tries. 


[Question] Thus, ANAD survives? 


[Answer] What is certain is that ANAD does not (?actually have the backing) to make it 
function. [words indistinct] [end recording]
