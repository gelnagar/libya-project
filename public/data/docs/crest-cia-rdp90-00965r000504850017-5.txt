Summary (AI): A Washington Post report says the Reagan administration ran a psychological "scare Gadhafi" campaign after the April 1986 U.S. air raid, hoping to provoke an irrational reaction from Libya's leader. It also describes U.S. naval maneuvers off Libya and European economic pressure on Libya. Tripoli appears only as a flight information region and as a bombing target in April 1986.
• White House plan, devised in early August 1986, aimed to scare Gadhafi into an irrational reaction and to embolden possible opponents in Libya.
• The plan followed a late-July intelligence report, later considered suspect, that Gadhafi had acted uncontrollably at an early July meeting with Yemeni leaders.
• Officials discussed and discarded creating harmless sonic booms over Libya with U.S. warplanes.
• USS Forrestal's planned port visit to Israel was canceled and the carrier ordered to remain near Libya after Sea Wind exercises with Egypt.
• Libya's dollar-denominated oil proceeds reportedly were cut about in half since last year.
• 1986-04-15: U.S. air raid (bombing) on Tripoli and Benghazi, referenced as the April 15 U.S. air raid that Gadhafi allegedly reacted to.
• 1986-07: Soviet-supplied Scud B missiles were fired at a barge in the gulf.
