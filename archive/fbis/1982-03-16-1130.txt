LIBYA

NATIONAL DEMOCRATIC RALLY CONDEMNS AL-QADHDHAFI 


LD132140 Riyadh Domestic Service in Arabic 1800 GMT 13 Mar 82 


[Text] The Libyan National Democratic Rally rejected the call by Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi, 
head of the ruling regime in Libya, for a dialogue between him and Libyans abroad. It 
described the call as a mere propaganda attempt by which Al-Qadhdhafi wants to find a way 
out of the growing isolation he placed himself in and the domestic and international 
impasses in whichhe placed his regime because of: his erroneous actions and policies and 
his irresponsible adventures. 


In a statement issued in London last night, the rally denounces the terrorist activities 
of Colonel al-Qadhdhafi and the assassinations carried out on his orders against a large 
number of Libyans throughout the world. It affirms the illegitimacy of the Al-Qadhdhafi 
regime because it is founded on oppression, tyranny, terror, suppression of freedom and 

violation of the most fundamental rights of man. 


The rally points out that Al-Qadhdhafi's call on Libyans abroad to meet with him is no 
more than an attempt to give his regime a shining appearance, particularly in the eyes 

of world public opinion and to erase the stigma of terrorism which he has earned as a 
result of his adventures and terror squads he sends to chase Libyans abroad. The rally's 
statement says the Libyan Arab people are living under the yoke of Al-Qadhdhafi's regime 
of terror and injustice, which has no respect for man's life, freedom and dignity. 


The statement says the Libyan people's rejection of the unjust rule of Al-Qadhdhafi can 
be seen from the overcrowded prisons and concentration camps filled with political pris- 
oners and other detainees from various sectors of the public. 


The statement relates the Libyan colonel's ettempts to silence his opposition from various 
factions, including his execution on 9 April 1977 of a large number of university students 
and his execution of scores of army officers in the same month, and his assassination of 
many honorable men through torture in detention camps and his physical liquidation of 
Libyans inside and outside Libya. Al-Qadhdhafi admitted such acts in his official 
speeches and these acts have taken, in some cases, the form of official resolutions 
adopted by the revolutionary committees run by the Libyan colonel. 


The Libyan rally's statement challenged Al-Qadhdhafi to give the oppressed Libyan people 
the right to express their views in complete freedom on the legitimacy of Al-Qadhdhafi's 
regime, or allow the holding of a truly free plebscite through which Libyan people could 
choose their representatives, and accept the holding of truly free elections through which 
Libyan people would elect a constituent assembly to draw vp a constitution for Libya in 
place of the current arbitrary conditions in which all powers are centered in a moody 
individual who obeys no laws or restrictions and who is responsible to no authority 
despite [as heard] his absolute powers. 


The Libyan rally's statement also says basic opposition to the regime of Al-Qadhdhafi 
stems from the Libyan Arab people and that external opposition is no more a reflection 
from internal opposition, and it is not for it to help make Al-Qadhdhafi's face shine 
and give him an Arab and international certificate of good conduct while he violates 
the most fundamental rights and freedoms of citizens and also violates the sanctity of 
their religion, faith and holy things.

The statement affirms the Libyan people's determination, both inside and outside Libya, 
to face up to Al-Qadhdhafi's attempts and adventures which are carried out at the expense 
of Libyan people, his persistence in squandering the people's wealth and in creating 
sedition between the people and sisterly Arab countries. 


The statement points out that Libyans abroad did not leave their country because of their 
personal problems and conditions, as Colonel al-Qadhdhafi claims, but because of conditions 
in their homeland, problems the people face there and their basic freedoms and human rights 
which are harmed. By calling on Libyans abroad to meet with him, Al-Qadhdhafi wants to 
transform matters into personal questions to negate the national character to these 
questions. 


The statement affirms the Libyan National Rally's anxiousness to work for lifting 
oppression and injustice from the shoulders of the Libyan people and for giving them the 
chance to live as they like and to exercise their basic rights to a free honorable life. 


Opposition Leader Cited 
LD141930 Riyadh Domestic Service in Arabic 1800 GMT 14 Mar 82 


[Text] One of the leaders of the Libyan opposition has revealed that seven Libyan organi- 
zations have been formed abroad which seek to overthrow Al-Qadhdhafi's rule and that they 

have been active since 1976. He said an agreement has been reached between these organi- 

zations on this objective despite differences in political orientations. 


This Libyan leader, who declined to give his name, said in an interview to AR-RIYAD that 
these various organizations had agreed on one objective: to put an end to Al-Qadhdhafi's 
rule, to remove the injustice besetting the Libyan people, to use as before Libya's 
economic resources for its development, welfare and prosperity instead of squandering 
them on empty dreams of dominating the world and instead of disseminating crazy ideas such 
as Al-Qadhdhafi's idea that he is charged with the mission of conveying civilization to 
mankind. 


This Libyan leader revealed the growing opposition activity inside Libya and said that 
Libyan opposition abroad had discovered a number of documents proving that Al-Qadhdhafi's 
intelligence is spreading its agents everywhere in the world to hunt down and catch members 
of the opposition. This leader said Al-Qadhdhafi has been squandering the money of the 
Libyan people to fulfill his own personal ambitions of dominating the world, causing 
trouble to the security of neighboring states. He said training camps still exist in 
Libyan territories. 


SAUDI ROYAL FAMILY SPLIT OVER FAHD POLICIES 


LD151528 Tripoli JANA in Arabic 1400 GMT 15 Mar 82 


[Text] Riyadh, 15 Mar (SPA) -- A diplomatic report has disclosed that deep differences 
have sprung up between Prince Fahd ibn ‘Abd al-'Aziz and a number of other princes opposed 
to the policy of the Saudi regime. Prince Fahd is involved in capitulation projects and 
is totally submissive to the orders and instructions of the U.S. administration. 


The report obtained by JANA discloses that Prince Salman, the governor of Riyadh region 
and Fahd's brother, pointed his weapon at one of the princes during a meeting last week 
in Riyadh and hurled abuse at him when the prince strongly criticized Fahd's aspirations 
and his attempts to control the affairs of the country. The prince held Fahd responsible 
for the disturbances sweeping the eastern regions because of the worsening living condi- 
tions and the deliberate disregard for the citizens of these regions.

AL-QADHDHAFI PHONES BENDJEDID; MEETING SET 3 APR 


LD152216 Tripoli Voice of Arab Homeland in Arabic 2115 GMT 15 Mar 82 


[Text] The brother leader of the revolution today telephoned President Chadli Bendjedid. 
It is known that it has been decided to hold the first joint meeting of the General 
People's Congress in the jamahiriyah and the Algerian Council of Ministers on 3 April 
within the context of the unity steps between the two sister countries. 


The brother leader visited Algeria from 18-23 January for a unity visit to sister Algeria 
when it was agreed to hold regular meetings between the General People's Committee and 
the Algerian Council of Ministers. 


RELATIONS DISCUSSED WITH BULGARIAN OFFICIAL 


LD130952 Tripoli JANA in English 0825 GMT 13 Mar 82 

[Text] [Place not given] 13 March (JANA) -- The under-secretary for technical affairs 
in the People's Bureau for Foreign Liaison met yesterday the deputy premier and the 
chairman of the cultural commission in the Socialist Republic of Bulgaria. 


The meeting discussed the friendly relations between the Jamahiriyah and Bulgaria and 
ways of consolidating them.
