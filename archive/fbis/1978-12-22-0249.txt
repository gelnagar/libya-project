LIBYA

AL-QADHDHAFI; 'REVOLUTION NO LONGER ISOLATED! 


LD201740Y Tripoli JANA in English 1435 GMT 20 Dec 78 LD 


{Text ] Tripoli, December 20, JAMAHIRIYAH NEWS AGENCY--Col Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi, leader 
of the Great lst of September Revolution and secretary general of the General People's 
Congress, has affirmed; "The revolution in Libya is no longer isolated, as is the case 
with other coups [as received] and revolutions staged in the world which start on a broad 
basis and then shrink until they become embodied in one individual, then they wither away 
with the collapse of a certain individual," 


Colonel al-Qadhdhafi pointed out: "There is a new generation of revolutionaries who have 
started to appear now and to join the revolutionary committees, the people's congresses, 
and the vocational congresses in the schools and institutes. These facts are cause for 
much rejoicing and optimism,"

through struggle, hard work and sweat. 


AL-QADHDHAFI DEFINES HIS VIEW ON TERRORISTS 
DW201128Y Bonn DIE WELT in German 20 Dec 78 p 4 Dw 


(Manfred Schell report on a talk between Libya's general secretary of the General People's 
Congress Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi and six correspondents from the Federal Republic in 
Tripoli, presumably on 18 December] 


{(Excerpt] Al-Qadhdhafi arrives at 2145 hours, wearing a patterned shirt, light grey 
trousers, and a dark jacket. He speaks Arabian only--a secretary of the Europe Depart- 
ment who had studied in Hamburg is translating. The bilateral relations are good, 
Al-Qadhdhafi opines, time and again interrupting his replies with pauses and a fleeting 
smile. But it cannot be denied that the Palestinian problem is being differently judged. 
Probably it is "historical reasons" which have led to a situation on the German side 
where the right of the Palestinians is not understood, 


Asked about cross connections between the PLO and the RAF [Rote Armee Fraktion--German 
terrorist gang], Al-Qadhdhafi said that he knows nothing about that, nor about the motives 
of the German terrorists, but then making a differentiation after all: "Why has the PLO 
been admitted to the United Nations Organization? It is not logical to say that the 
United Nations Organization admits an organization which is terrorist. Why has the RAF 
not admitted?" The different assessment of the Palestinian issue, however, will not pre- 
vent a cooperation of the two countries "in the field of security policy." [quotation 
marks as received] 


Asked by DIE WELT where he draws the line between liberation movements and terrorists, 
Al-Qadhdhafi replied: "What matters in the case of the former is the liberation of an 
occupied country and the return of an expelled people. A terrorist merely sees his 
personal gain. He is an end to himself." When a hint was made to his private visit 
to Germany, Al-Qadhdhafi answered; "I have never been in Germany."
