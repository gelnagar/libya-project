ARAB COUNTRIES AGREE TO CONVENE SUMMIT 


LD210030 Tripoli Voice of Arab Homeland in Arabic 2315 GMT 20 Jun 82 


[Text] JANA has learned from sources in the People's Bureau for Foreign Liaison that 
Steadfastness and Confrontation Front countries and Tunisia, Morocco, the UAE, Kuwait, 
Qatar, Mauritania, Lebanon and the YAR have agreed to the urgent convening of an Arab 
summit as called for by the Jamahiriyah.

AL-QADHDHAFI: U.S. MANEUVERS WILL RESULT IN WAR 


AU181804 Vienna Domestic Service in German 1600 GMT 18 Jun 82 


[Udo Bachmayr report on an Austrian political science delegation's recent visit to Libya 
and interview with Colonel al-Qadhdhafi in the Surt desert camp -- date not given] 


[Excerpt] After a drive of several hundred meters through the military desert camp, the 

car stopped in front of a desert tent several meters from a bungalow. A friendly Al-Qadhdhafi 
aide welcomed us and requested us to sit on the carpeted tent floor. Two bodyguards of 
Al-Qadhdhafi appeared ahead of the revolutionary leader, who arrived wearing a green- 
patterned shirt with a closed round collar and light green corduroy pants. He was carrying 

a paper with the questions we had submitted to him. He laid the paper on the floor, extended 
his hand to us with a friendly smile and immediately started with words of praise for 

Austria -- how important his visit there was, important also for a better international 
climate, stating how much he respects the Austrian people. 


Then the game of questions and answers began, a game that subsequently far exceeded the 
submitted questions. To a question about his readiness for rapprochement with Europe, which 
became perceptible during his visit to Austria and also signaled his readiness for rapproche- 
ment with the United States, he did not reply directly. He simply pointed out that Ronald 
Reagan had improved somewhat in the past few months; he said by some 25 percent without 

going into more details. In the tent under the scorching sun of the Libyan desert, 
Al-Qadhdhafi remained loyal to Libya's generally intransigent policy toward the United States. 


To my question as to what extent Libya, in view of the maneuvers announced for this fall 
in the area of the Gulf of Sidra -- an area which Libya claims for itself for security 
reasons -- sees itself exposed to danger, Al-Qadhdhafi assumed an unmistakeable position: 
If the Americans hold their maneuvers, it will mean war. We are fearful for peace but we 
are not afraid of facing the Americans, and we hope that the Americans will not commit 
themselves to too disastrous a mistake. This could imply a military conflict, a struggle 
between us and the Americans and it could be that another power will also intervene. It 
could be that the Russians will intervene. But Al-Qadhdhafi sees the greatest danger of a 
world crisis coming from the Middle East. Israeli policy will ultimately, Al-Qadhdhafi 
said, lead with certainty to the third world war. 


CORRESPONDENT DESCRIBES TRIPOLI MILITARY PARADE 


AB181015 Lagos NAN in English 0950 GMT 18 Jun 82 
[By Kunle Akande] 


[Text] [No dateline received] Recently, more than 50,000 jubilant Libyan demonstrators 
marched to the city square in Tripoli waving banners and changing "We shall not surrender," 
"Bravo Jamahiriyah (revolution)," and “Libya, heart of Arab land." They were acclaiming 
the socialist regime of "brother leader Colonel Mu'ammar al-Qadhdhafi," as they fondly call 
their leader, at a national ceremony commemorating the "era of the masses." 


The "era of the masses" started in March 1977, eight years after the overthrow of the 
monarchy in Libya. Its ideology is that "political power is concentrated in the hands of 

the people without deputation or representation at any level." It claims that representation 
is a denial of participation. In other words, policy decisions are made by the people even 
at the expense of the speed and consistency of the government.

One of the highlights of the ceremony was the parade of military hardware which ranged 
from the most sophisticated jet fighter to the deadliest tanks on the world market. 


As the parade began, the first tank units rumbled by, while an announcer explained in 
Arabic the purpose and capabilities of each equipment, which was made up mostly of 
Russian trucks. Then the soldiers, including boys and girls in their early teens, 
marched into view with rifles pressed against their sides. "It is doubtful," said 

an observer, "if most of these youngsters know the sensitivity of the weapons they are 
carrying." However, there had been no incidence of deaths resulting from accidental 
shootings. 


The most spectacular part of the parade was display of Mirage jet fighters for which 
Colonel al-Qadhdhafi is reportedly "keenly interested." The jet fighters swooped so 
low that many of the jubilant demonstrators had to block their ears as daring para- 
troopers landed in a rare display of individual agility. In another show, a four- 
column-wide procession of jets swept along in a parallel line, trailing plumes of green 
smoke across the sky. 


According to reports, the Libyan Air Force now has 20 MiG-21 fighters, 100 MiG-23's 
and 17 TU-22 bombers, all Soviet-made. It also has 2,400 Soviet tanks, including T-72 
models and three Soviet-made submarines. 


Said a French journalist at the African soccer championship finals in Benghazi: '"We 
might not be very correct to assert that Al-Qadhdhafi is about the most dangerous man 
in the world, but arming his 3.9 million people to the teeth proves us right." 


Obviously moved by the display, the 38-year-old Libyan leader referred to the celebrations 
as a demonstration of the "triumph of the heart of Arab's homeland over America." 

He said: "The founding of the Jamahiriyah and the establishment of the people's authority 
are some of the most serious transformations of the modern era, because it is the first 
time the masses are freed of their jailers and emancipated from their heavy chains." 


In a more forceful tone, he went on: "We on this glorious day invite the Arab masses 
to stage the massive popular revolution to annul and destroy regional laws which some 
Arab reactionary regimes use to protect themselves." 


"We declare from here with a powerful voice, which fears no one, our total backing with 
all our resources, of the Arab forces facing persecution from the Atlantic to the Gulf." 


"Our main task is to mobilize more than one million of our citizens of both sexes in 
an armed state of readiness to confront and counter America and its agents.'"' He then 
specifically made reference to Saudi Arabia, Sudan, Morocco and Jordan as the agents. 


"The United States tried to stifle this voice which is a thorn in the side of imperialism, 
reaction and Zionism, because it knows the revolution will spread from Libya, and the 
winds of Jamahirayah, and the winds of socialism, do not have artificial borders." 


Naturally, some Nigerians would have expected Al-Qadhdhafi in his characteristic manner 
to carp at Nigeria, because of that country's action against the "Peoples’ Bureau” 

last year, but surprisingly enough, he sympathized with Nigeria over its present oil 
debacle. "Under orders from America," he said, "Saudi Arabia has launched an economic 
war of starvation against the Libyan, Algerian, Kuwaiti, and Nigerian peoples, as well 
as others." But the validity of this assertion has been disproved by Saudi Arabia's 
backing for Nigeria at the recently concluded OPEC meeting, without which Nigeria would 
have suffered some greater setbacks.

After the two-hour speech, Al-Qadhdhafi left the rostrum, but for another one hour his 
people were still chanting "Al Fatah Jamahiriyah (long live the revolution)." 


The show seemed somewhat like an orchestrated mass movement in rabid support of the 
country's socialist-oriented ideology. As one diplomat commented, "someday, these people 
will become so well-programmed like a computer, that all you need to do is to press a 
button and they are set in motion." 


The ceremony was a personal triumph for Al-Qadhdhafi who had tailored the country's consti- 
tution to his personal specifications, as reflected in the "Green Book," and the Islamic 
laws. Little wonder then, that unlike in Nigeria where such national events are rounded 
off with large doses of beer in hotels, pubs and beer parlors, Libyans merely turned in 
home to play accordions and tamborines.
